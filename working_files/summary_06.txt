A top Mexican university used AI to prevent exam cheating. Now thousands of admissions are in doubt
============================================================

The National Autonomous University of Mexico has suspended enrollment and ordered a new in-person exam for thousands of applicants following a widespread cheating scandal involving artificial intelligence.

### Key Points
- UNAM invalidated approximately 3,000 of 150,000 admissions tests after detecting an unprecedented surge in perfect scores.
- Investigations revealed students used AI tools, unauthorized companions, and identity theft to bypass the university's digital surveillance system.
- The university contracted Territorium Life to manage the online testing platform, which faced criticism for its reliance on automated monitoring.
- Approximately 58,000 applicants must now retake an in-person control exam to secure their spots for the upcoming semester.
- Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum has called for an official investigation into the integrity of the admissions process.

### Why it Matters
This scandal highlights the growing tension between digital accessibility in education and the reliability of AI-based remote proctoring systems. As a primary engine for social mobility in Mexico, UNAM’s struggle to maintain academic integrity threatens to disrupt the educational futures of tens of thousands of students.
