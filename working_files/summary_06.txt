Tesla’s Make-or-Break Cybercab Had a Quiet Debut
============================================================

Tesla has launched its driverless Cybercab in Austin, Texas, marking a critical step in the company's transition from an automotive manufacturer to an AI and robotics-focused enterprise.

### Key Points
- Tesla’s Cybercab is a two-seater vehicle designed without a steering wheel or foot pedals, currently operating on a limited robotaxi network in Texas.
- The company has registered 45 Cybercabs for its network, while its broader autonomous fleet has collectively traveled 1 million driverless miles.
- Tesla faces significant regulatory hurdles, as it has not yet filed for federal exemptions required to operate vehicles lacking traditional driving controls.
- Unlike competitors like Waymo and Zoox that utilize lidar and radar, Tesla relies exclusively on camera-based vision systems to power its autonomous technology.
- Tesla plans to allow customers to purchase their own Cybercab fleets by the end of 2026, pending necessary legal and safety rule changes.

### Why it Matters
The success of the Cybercab is central to Elon Musk’s strategy to pivot Tesla into an AI-driven robotics company with a valuation exceeding current market leaders. Whether Tesla can overcome regulatory barriers and technical competition will determine if the company can achieve its ambitious vision for autonomous transportation and mass-market robotics.
