Plans for a data center threaten scenic wilderness and dark skies in West Virginia
============================================================

Residents and environmental groups are challenging plans for a massive data center in West Virginia, citing concerns over light pollution, environmental degradation, and a lack of local oversight.

### Key Points
- Fundamental Data LLC plans to build the Ridgeline data center, a facility potentially spanning 10,000 acres, on a reclaimed coal-mining site in Tucker County.
- The project includes a 785-megawatt power plant primarily fueled by natural gas, with diesel backup, to support artificial intelligence and cloud computing infrastructure.
- A state law passed in 2025 exempts high-impact data centers from local zoning, noise, and lighting restrictions, effectively removing municipal guardrails for the development.
- An analysis by the Northern Virginia Astronomy Club suggests the facility could increase local sky glow by 528%, threatening the region's status as a dark-sky destination.
- Opponents, including the grassroots group Tucker United, have filed legal challenges against air-quality permits, citing potential public health risks and environmental hazards.

### Why it Matters
The conflict highlights the growing tension between the urgent national demand for AI infrastructure and the preservation of rural economies built on tourism and outdoor recreation. As states prioritize rapid tech development, the debate over local control and environmental accountability serves as a critical test case for future industrial expansion in protected wilderness areas.
