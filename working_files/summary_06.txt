US stocks: Dow, S&P slip as investors eye Mideast talks, earnings
============================================================

The Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 reached record highs this week, driven by strong corporate earnings and optimism regarding a potential de-escalation in the Iran conflict.

### Key Points
- The S&P 500 saw 84.8% of reporting companies exceed analyst earnings expectations, significantly higher than the 68% historical average since 1994.
- U.S. crude oil prices rose 2.75% to $77.29 per barrel, while Brent crude increased 3.83% to $82.49 per barrel.
- SpaceX shares closed higher despite the expiration of a lockup period that allowed early investors to sell their holdings.
- An Iranian parliamentary committee is currently reviewing a bill that could restrict "hostile" vessels from transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
- U.S. Treasury yields declined as easing inflation concerns reduced market expectations for an immediate interest rate hike from the Federal Reserve.

### Why it Matters
Strong corporate performance and cooling inflation expectations are currently offsetting geopolitical tensions and macro-economic uncertainty. This resilience suggests that investors are prioritizing fundamental earnings growth over headline-driven volatility as they await further guidance on Federal Reserve interest rate policy.
