Meta’s Muse Could Upend All Kinds of Businesses. It Hasn’t Yet
============================================================

Meta’s agentic AI tool, Muse, is seeing limited commercial adoption despite high user engagement, prompting major corporations to begin optimizing their websites for future automated shopping integration.

### Key Points
- Meta reports over 10 million users for Muse in the U.S. and Canada, with more than 25 percent utilizing the tool for shopping tasks.
- Major companies including DoorDash, Hilton, and Ally Financial report that current transaction volumes originating from AI agents remain insignificant.
- Chipotle and other retailers are actively updating website code to resolve technical issues that prevent AI agents from successfully completing customer orders.
- Meta plans to eventually monetize Muse by charging businesses transaction fees, potentially creating a revenue stream that could surpass its advertising business.
- Bank of America and other financial institutions have issued security warnings regarding the risks of sharing user credentials with third-party AI assistants.
- Amazon and Insurify have shifted their strategies, moving from blocking Muse to exploring ways to integrate or collaborate with external AI shopping agents.

### Why it Matters
While current transaction volumes remain low, businesses are proactively adjusting their digital infrastructure to prepare for a potential shift toward AI-driven commerce. This transition highlights a growing tension between the convenience of automated shopping and the need for companies to maintain brand loyalty, data security, and profit margins.
