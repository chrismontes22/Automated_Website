Momentum builds to ban Madison Square Garden's 'surveillance empire'
============================================================

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s administration and a majority of the City Council are backing legislation to ban private businesses from using facial recognition technology on customers.

### Key Points
- The proposed legislation prohibits public-facing businesses from using biometric scans, including facial, iris, voice, fingerprint, or gait recognition.
- The bill allows consumers to sue businesses for violations, with potential damages reaching $5,000 per intentional or reckless breach.
- Madison Square Garden owner James Dolan is a primary target of the legislation due to his use of facial recognition to bar specific attorneys from venues.
- Supporters cite risks of data misuse, including potential sharing with federal immigration authorities and documented cases of misidentification leading to false arrests.
- The bill currently has 28 co-sponsors, securing a majority in the 51-member City Council, though it still requires support from Speaker Julie Menin.

### Why it Matters
This legislation represents a significant shift in digital privacy regulation by attempting to curb the unchecked use of biometric surveillance in private commercial spaces. If passed, it would set a major precedent for consumer rights and corporate data practices in large urban markets.
