Fixing a Bricked Framework Laptop
============================================================

A Framework Laptop 13 user successfully repaired a bricked motherboard after a failed BIOS 3.20 update, highlighting significant gaps in the company's repairability and support policies.

### Key Points
- A user’s Framework Laptop 13 became unresponsive following a recommended BIOS 3.20 update, a failure reported by multiple users on company forums.
- Framework support refused to provide a free repair or technical guidance, citing the expiration of the one-year warranty and requiring a CA$500+ motherboard replacement.
- The user bypassed the manufacturer's refusal by manually flashing the BIOS chip using a CH347 programmer, pogo pins, and a custom-extracted firmware image.
- The incident coincided with a data breach at Framework’s business intelligence provider, Metabase, which exposed customer names, addresses, and contact information.
- The user criticized Framework for lacking basic BIOS recovery features, using non-repairable WSON chip form factors, and failing to provide necessary technical documentation.

### Why it Matters
This incident challenges the "right to repair" reputation of Framework, as the company’s refusal to provide documentation or recovery tools forces users to perform risky, manual hardware interventions. It underscores a growing tension between consumer expectations for long-term device support and the reality of proprietary firmware limitations in modern modular hardware.
