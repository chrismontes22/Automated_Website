No evidence of data breach after Chinese-made component found in Navy drones, MoD says
============================================================

The UK Ministry of Defence confirmed that Chinese-made components found in new Royal Navy K3 Scout sea drones did not result in any data breaches or security compromises.

### Key Points
- Routine vulnerability assessments revealed that drone cameras were transmitting heartbeat signals to a Chinese IP address.
- The Ministry of Defence stated that no sensitive military data or systems were accessed or transmitted during the incident.
- Kraken, the supplier of the 20 K3 Scout drones, confirmed the Chinese components originated from a third-party camera manufacturer.
- NATO nations maintain strict policies banning Chinese-made components in military equipment due to ongoing espionage and national security concerns.
- The security lapse has been addressed, though the incident highlights the difficulty of removing Chinese technology from global defense supply chains.

### Why it Matters
This incident underscores the significant challenges defense agencies face in auditing complex global supply chains for hidden foreign components. It highlights the persistent tension between sourcing cost-effective technology and maintaining the rigorous security standards required for sensitive military equipment.
