SK Hynix is in talks with Intel to make memory chips in the US, Reuters reports
============================================================

SK Hynix is currently negotiating a potential deal to manufacture memory chips in the United States, possibly by leasing space at Intel’s semiconductor production site in Ohio.

### Key Points
- SK Hynix is considering a joint venture or lease agreement with Intel to establish its first domestic memory chip production facility in the United States.
- The company is currently building a $4 billion advanced packaging plant in West Lafayette, Indiana, which does not include front-end chip manufacturing.
- US Commerce officials have suggested that foreign chipmakers could face significant tariffs if they do not expand their manufacturing operations within the country.
- SK Hynix holds a dominant market position in high-bandwidth memory, a critical component currently experiencing supply shortages due to high demand from AI data centers.
- Any final agreement involving this technology must be reviewed by the South Korean government under the Industrial Technology Protection Act.

### Why it Matters
Establishing domestic memory production would help the United States secure its semiconductor supply chain and reduce reliance on overseas manufacturing for critical AI hardware. This move could significantly lower geopolitical risks for major cloud providers while increasing operational costs for SK Hynix due to higher American labor and construction expenses.
