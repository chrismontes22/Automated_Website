Apollo blames social engineering for a four-day breach in July
============================================================

**Private equity firm Apollo Global Management has notified individuals that hackers accessed sensitive personal information, including Social Security numbers, following a social engineering incident in early July.**

### Key Points
- Unauthorized access to Apollo cloud platforms occurred between July 6 and July 10, 2026.
- Stolen data includes names, dates of birth, contact details, home addresses, and Social Security numbers.
- Apollo is offering affected individuals 24 months of credit monitoring services through Cyberscout, a TransUnion company.
- The breach impacts residents in seven jurisdictions, including California, New York, and the District of Columbia.
- No specific threat actor has claimed responsibility, and Apollo reports no evidence of the stolen data being misused for fraud yet.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing vulnerability of major financial institutions to sophisticated social engineering attacks that bypass traditional security protocols. As firms like Apollo manage nearly $1 trillion in assets, such breaches underscore the significant risks posed to the personal data of employees and investors alike.
