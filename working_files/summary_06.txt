Why the AI guys built tech that terrifies them
============================================================

Leading AI executives, including Sam Altman and Dario Amodei, are racing to develop advanced artificial intelligence while simultaneously expressing deep concerns about the technology's potential for existential catastrophe.

### Key Points
- Technology journalist Kevin Roose explores the industry's "race to the top" in his new book, *The AGI Chronicles*.
- AI leaders justify rapid development by arguing that "the good guys" must achieve superintelligence first to prevent less responsible actors from doing so.
- Many Silicon Valley insiders assign high probabilities to "P(doom)," the statistical likelihood that AI will cause human extinction or societal collapse.
- Proposed safety measures include international inspection regimes and industry-wide pacts to slow the development of frontier models.
- Despite recent signals from the Trump administration favoring self-regulation, national security concerns may eventually drive federal oversight of AI labs.

### Why it Matters
The tension between the rapid commercialization of AI and the existential fears of its creators highlights a critical governance vacuum in the technology sector. As these companies hold immense power without formal federal oversight, the industry's ability to self-regulate remains the primary, albeit uncertain, safeguard against potential global risks.
