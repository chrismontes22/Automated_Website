The 3-2-1 backup rule everyone repeats is missing a step, and I found out the hard way
============================================================

The traditional 3-2-1 backup rule is increasingly viewed as insufficient, prompting experts to recommend the 3-2-1-1-0 strategy to ensure data integrity and protection against modern ransomware threats.

### Key Points
- The original 3-2-1 rule suggests keeping three data copies on two media types, with one stored offsite, but fails to account for file corruption or restoration failures.
- A 2024 Backblaze survey found that while 84% of US adults back up data, only 15% feel certain their files are securely recoverable.
- The updated 3-2-1-1-0 rule adds an immutable or air-gapped copy to prevent ransomware from deleting backups and mandates zero errors through regular testing.
- Cybersecurity agencies like CISA warn that organizations often discover backup failures only during critical incidents, highlighting the necessity of periodic restore verification.
- Tools such as FreeFileSync can assist in creating disconnected, air-gapped backups to ensure at least one copy remains unreachable by network-based attacks.

### Why it Matters
Relying solely on the quantity of backup copies creates a false sense of security that can lead to permanent data loss during a system failure or cyberattack. Implementing verification and immutable storage transforms a passive backup habit into a reliable recovery strategy, ensuring that data is not only saved but actually usable when needed.
