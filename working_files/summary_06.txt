Seattle Times and Newsday sue OpenAI and Microsoft for infringement
============================================================

The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging their journalism was used to train AI models without proper authorization.

### Key Points
- The plaintiffs allege that OpenAI and Microsoft used their copyrighted reporting to train AI models without permission or compensation.
- The lawsuit seeks the destruction of all AI models and training datasets that incorporate the publishers' proprietary journalistic content.
- Microsoft is named as a defendant because its Copilot service utilizes OpenAI’s underlying technology to generate responses.
- The publishers claim that AI chatbots reduce website traffic and subscription revenue by providing answers directly to users.
- This legal action joins a growing list of nearly 400 local newspapers currently suing the two tech companies over intellectual property concerns.

### Why it Matters
This litigation highlights the escalating conflict between major media organizations and AI developers regarding the unauthorized use of intellectual property for machine learning. The outcome could force a fundamental shift in how AI companies source training data and potentially establish new legal precedents for digital copyright protections.
