‘Hostile, but hooked’: What’s behind the US-China trade truce extension?
============================================================

Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump have extended a bilateral trade truce, though deep strategic rivalries over technology, rare-earth minerals, and geopolitical tensions persist.

### Key Points
- The US and China extended an October 2025 trade truce for two months, delaying potential bans on Chinese rare-earth metal exports until January 10.
- Current trade remains strained, with Chinese goods facing 36.5 percent tariffs and US exports to China taxed at 31 percent.
- Tensions are fueled by competition in artificial intelligence, US sanctions on Russian oil buyers, and an $11.1 billion US arms sale to Taiwan.
- The US faces a $40 trillion national debt, increasing the economic risk if the fragile trade truce collapses and triggers further inflationary shocks.
- China maintains significant leverage by controlling 60 percent of global rare-earth deposits and 90 percent of processing capacity.

### Why it Matters
The ongoing gridlock between the world's two largest economies creates significant global market uncertainty and threatens the stability of critical technology supply chains. While the temporary truce prevents immediate escalation, the lack of a long-term resolution leaves businesses and investors vulnerable to sudden shifts in trade policy and geopolitical volatility.
