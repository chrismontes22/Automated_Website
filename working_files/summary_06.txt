Bluesky’s new CEO wants a big tent, not a bubble
============================================================

**Bluesky CEO Toni Schneider aims to scale the social platform and its underlying AT Protocol by fostering a diverse, interoperable ecosystem that functions like the open web.**

### Key Points
- Toni Schneider has transitioned from interim to permanent CEO of Bluesky, succeeding founder Jay Graber, who is now the company's chief innovation officer.
- The platform operates as both a consumer-facing social app and the foundation for the AT Protocol, an open standard currently powering over 1,000 interoperable applications.
- Bluesky is actively decentralizing control of the protocol by handing off core components, such as identity directories, to independent entities like the IETF and a new Swiss nonprofit.
- The company plans to introduce monetization features this year, focusing on affiliate-style models that reward creators and developers within the broader "ATmosphere" ecosystem.
- Future development is prioritizing private, membership-based communities to complement the existing public-facing social network.

### Why it Matters
By positioning Bluesky as an open-protocol "on-ramp" rather than a closed walled garden, Schneider is attempting to build a social media future that resists the fragmentation and algorithmic manipulation seen on platforms like X or Facebook. This long-term strategy seeks to create a sustainable, user-owned network where developers can build independent social experiences that remain connected through a shared, interoperable standard.
