Privacy advocates call on Maryland to investigate data brokers
============================================================

A coalition of privacy and civil rights groups has filed a formal complaint urging Maryland’s attorney general to investigate data brokers for allegedly violating state privacy laws.

### Key Points
- The complaint targets companies including Penlink, Thomson Reuters, Motorola, Insight LPR, LexisNexis, and Flock Safety for allegedly selling geolocation data without proper warrants.
- Advocates claim these brokers are violating Maryland law by selling sensitive personal information to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for surveillance purposes.
- Maryland’s privacy law, updated in 2024, prohibits the sale of precise geolocation data and restricts data sharing with immigration enforcement agencies without a warrant.
- Companies named in the complaint, including Penlink and Thomson Reuters, have denied the allegations and maintain that their business practices comply with all applicable regulations.
- The complaint was authored by the Georgetown University Law Center’s Technology Law Clinic on behalf of 12 organizations, including We Are CASA.

### Why it Matters
This case highlights the growing tension between state-level privacy protections and the federal government's use of the "data broker loophole" to conduct surveillance without judicial oversight. If successful, an investigation could set a significant legal precedent for how states can restrict law enforcement access to commercially purchased personal data.
