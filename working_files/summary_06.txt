Two more newsrooms join the case against OpenAI and Microsoft
============================================================

The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed a joint lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging the companies illegally bypassed paywalls to scrape copyrighted journalism for AI training.

### Key Points
- The lawsuit claims OpenAI and Microsoft methodically circumvented digital paywalls to harvest news content for generative AI model development.
- Plaintiffs allege the unauthorized use of their articles reduces website traffic and advertising revenue while causing AI models to hallucinate false information.
- The legal action joins ongoing litigation involving the New York Times and approximately 400 other local newspapers against the tech giants.
- The complaint highlights potential violations of the EU’s general-purpose AI code, which explicitly prohibits signatories from bypassing subscription-based access restrictions.
- Unlike the plaintiffs, other media organizations including the Associated Press and Vox Media have opted to license their archives to OpenAI.

### Why it Matters
This litigation challenges the legal boundaries of fair use regarding how AI companies acquire training data from subscription-based digital publishers. The outcome could force a shift in how tech firms negotiate licensing agreements and comply with international copyright standards for web scraping.
