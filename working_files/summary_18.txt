Honda Wants to Put a Robotic Lab Assistant on Future Commercial Space Stations
============================================================

Honda is partnering with space infrastructure firm Redwire to develop an automated robotic system designed to manage laboratory tasks on future commercial space stations in low Earth orbit.

### Key Points
- The collaboration combines Redwire’s STAARK motion platform with Honda’s advanced robotic hand technology featuring tactile and force sensors.
- The system aims to automate mundane laboratory maintenance, such as inventory management and sample handling, to save valuable astronaut time.
- Future commercial space stations, including those planned by Axiom Space and Vast, will require automation due to smaller crew sizes and uncrewed periods.
- The technology is being showcased at the International Astronautical Congress in Antalya, Turkey, which runs through October 9.
- NASA plans to deorbit the International Space Station by 2030, shifting the focus toward private, automated orbital research facilities.

### Why it Matters
Automating routine laboratory tasks is essential for the economic viability of smaller, next-generation commercial space stations that cannot support large, full-time human crews. This partnership highlights the growing competition to provide critical infrastructure for the emerging commercial space economy as private entities replace government-run research outposts.
