Is Pavel Durov a free-speech champion or an unaccountable tech baron?
============================================================

Russia’s Federal Security Service has charged Telegram founder Pavel Durov with facilitating terrorism, escalating a long-standing conflict over platform accountability and the regulation of encrypted messaging services.

### Key Points
- The FSB accuses Durov of failing to block a dating chatbot used by Ukrainian agents to recruit 46 Russian citizens for sabotage and arson.
- Russia’s financial watchdog, Rosfinmonitoring, has officially added Durov to its register of extremists and terrorists.
- If convicted of the terrorism-related charges, the Dubai-based billionaire faces a potential prison sentence of 15 years or more.
- Telegram previously faced a Russian court-ordered ban in 2018, which was lifted in 2020 after the company agreed to cooperate on security matters.
- Durov remains under separate judicial scrutiny in France regarding allegations that Telegram fails to adequately combat organized crime and illicit content.

### Why it Matters
The legal actions against Durov highlight a growing global trend where governments are increasingly challenging the autonomy of cross-border communication platforms. This conflict forces a choice between maintaining strict user privacy and complying with national security mandates, potentially reshaping how digital infrastructure is governed worldwide.
