Millions of Android TV boxes came with malware, and Google's certification missed it
============================================================

Google has dismantled a massive botnet involving 1.3 million Android set-top boxes manufactured by Videostrong, which contained preinstalled XCPE malware designed to facilitate ad fraud and data theft.

### Key Points
- The XCPE backdoor was embedded directly into the firmware of devices manufactured by Videostrong.
- Infected set-top boxes were used to conduct ad fraud, steal data, and route unauthorized traffic through users' home networks.
- Videostrong bypassed Google’s security certification by using obfuscation to trigger the malware only after the device's initial boot.
- Google has officially terminated its partnership with Videostrong and banned the company from producing certified Android devices.
- The tech giant has updated its security review processes to better detect malicious code hidden within device firmware.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights significant vulnerabilities in the supply chain for consumer electronics and raises concerns regarding the efficacy of Google’s hardware certification standards. It serves as a reminder that even officially certified devices can pose serious security risks, potentially compromising user privacy and home network integrity.
