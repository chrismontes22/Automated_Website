Democrats Are Failing the Waymo Test
============================================================

Waymo’s expansion of driverless robotaxis faces significant political resistance in Democratic-led cities, sparking a national debate over balancing technological safety benefits against potential labor market disruptions.

### Key Points
- Waymo currently operates autonomous vehicles in California, Arizona, Florida, Georgia, Tennessee, and Texas, while facing legislative blocks in many blue states.
- Research from the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety indicates Waymo vehicles are involved in 68% fewer police-reported crashes and 81% fewer injury-related crashes than human drivers.
- Labor unions, including the Teamsters and various rideshare driver groups, are leading the opposition, citing concerns over job displacement and the impact on professional drivers.
- Critics highlight "edge case" failures, such as vehicles idling during power outages or obstructing emergency responders, as significant safety and operational hurdles.
- Washington, D.C. is considering a compromise bill that would authorize robotaxis while implementing per-mile taxes to fund public transit and workforce training for displaced drivers.

### Why it Matters
The debate over autonomous vehicles serves as a critical test for modern progressivism, forcing a choice between protecting the status quo for labor groups and embracing technology that could drastically reduce traffic fatalities. Successfully integrating this technology requires a regulatory middle ground that addresses legitimate safety and economic concerns without stifling innovation that offers clear societal benefits.
