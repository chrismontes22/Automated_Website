the hidden landscape of the digital world: what it takes to keep technology running
============================================================

The digital "cloud" relies on extensive physical infrastructure, including data centers and semiconductor manufacturing, which consume significant energy, water, and raw materials while generating substantial electronic waste.

### Key Points
- The International Energy Agency projects data center electricity consumption could reach 950 terawatt-hours by 2030, up from 485 terawatt-hours in 2025.
- Semiconductor manufacturing is highly resource-intensive, with 28 surveyed companies reporting 789 million cubic meters of water withdrawals and 149 billion kilowatt-hours of energy use in 2023.
- Global electronic waste reached 62 billion kilograms in 2022, with only 22.3 percent formally recycled, a figure projected to rise to 82 billion kilograms by 2030.
- Data centers generate significant waste heat, which some experimental projects are attempting to repurpose for district heating to improve overall energy efficiency.
- Efficiency gains in hardware are often offset by the rapid growth in demand for computationally intensive services like artificial intelligence.

### Why it Matters
The rapid expansion of digital infrastructure creates a tangible environmental footprint that challenges the perception of the cloud as an immaterial space. Understanding these physical demands is essential for developing sustainable technology policies that account for resource scarcity and long-term ecological impact.
