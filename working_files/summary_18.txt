Why Were Trump Officials Trying to Hack Into a Voting Machine?
============================================================

Former Trump administration officials reportedly pressured the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency to covertly hack voting machines to potentially invalidate unfavorable election results during his second term.

### Key Points
- Political appointees at the Department of Homeland Security directed CISA staff to obtain and breach voting machines without notifying the manufacturers.
- CISA employees resisted the directive, viewing the request as a violation of the agency’s core mission to protect election infrastructure from cyber threats.
- The operation was never executed, partly due to the termination of key personnel by the Department of Government Efficiency.
- Over 1,000 CISA employees have been removed during the current administration, representing approximately one-third of the agency's total workforce.
- The president’s 2027 budget proposal seeks a $700 million funding reduction for CISA, which could lead to the loss of 900 additional staff positions.

### Why it Matters
These reports highlight a significant tension between political leadership and the nonpartisan agencies tasked with maintaining the integrity of national election systems. If federal resources are used to undermine rather than protect voting infrastructure, it could permanently erode public trust in democratic processes and institutional oversight.
