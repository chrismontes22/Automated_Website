San Francisco's car crime has plunged. How much credit does Flock deserve?
============================================================

San Francisco has seen significant declines in car thefts and break-ins, with city officials attributing the success to increased police patrols and the deployment of Flock Safety surveillance technology.

### Key Points
- Car thefts in San Francisco dropped nearly 59% and break-ins fell over 82% between January and August 2022 and the same period in 2026.
- The San Francisco Police Department utilizes Flock Safety’s AI-powered cameras to identify vehicles and track suspects in real time.
- A preliminary study by University of South Carolina researchers linked the deployment of Flock cameras to an 11% reduction in car thefts across 216 agencies.
- Privacy advocates, including the ACLU, warn that these systems create a "surveillance industrial complex" and have been linked to instances of unauthorized police tracking.
- San Francisco recently implemented stricter data retention policies, limiting access to license plate records after 30 days to mitigate potential misuse.

### Why it Matters
The debate over Flock Safety highlights the tension between leveraging high-tech surveillance to combat urban crime and protecting individual civil liberties from potential government overreach. As San Francisco attempts to balance these interests with new oversight legislation, its model may serve as a critical test case for other municipalities navigating the ethics of modern policing.
