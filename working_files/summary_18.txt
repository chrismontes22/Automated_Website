China Builds Legal Machinery To Flip Civilian Economy Into War Mode
============================================================

China is implementing a revised national defense mobilization law on October 1, granting the government authority to redirect civilian economic and industrial resources toward military needs during conflicts.

### Key Points
- The law mandates that companies in sectors like energy, technology, and manufacturing stockpile resources and prepare for rapid transition to wartime production.
- Local governments gain the power to requisition civilian assets, including equipment, data, and software, if national defense requirements are not met.
- New exit restrictions effective September 15 allow authorities to block Chinese citizens from leaving the country over potential technology-transfer or export control violations.
- The legislation applies to organizations and individuals within China, raising concerns that foreign-owned subsidiaries could be subject to state requisition during a crisis.
- The policy formalizes "military-civil fusion," a strategy aimed at integrating civilian technological development with national defense modernization.

### Why it Matters
This shift creates significant operational and geopolitical risks for multinational corporations that maintain manufacturing hubs or supply chains within China. The potential for state seizure of assets during a conflict may accelerate efforts by Western firms to decouple from the Chinese market to protect intellectual property and infrastructure.
