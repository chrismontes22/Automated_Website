What happened to 145 Nasdaq companies after the $1 warning letter
============================================================

A study of 145 Nasdaq-listed companies receiving minimum bid price deficiency notices in early 2025 reveals that while most remain listed, shareholders typically face significant long-term value losses.

### Key Points
- Approximately 75% of companies remained listed 16 to 22 months after receiving their first deficiency notice, though 37 firms were delisted, acquired, or declared bankruptcy.
- Nearly 59% of the companies executed at least one reverse stock split, with a median ratio of 1-for-20, to regain compliance with Nasdaq’s $1.00 minimum bid requirement.
- Companies that did not perform a reverse split saw a median stock price increase of 41.2%, while those that utilized splits experienced a median decline of 83.0%.
- Recovery success was highly correlated with the initial stock price; companies trading above $0.80 were significantly more likely to recover than those trading below $0.50.
- New SEC rules implemented in January 2025 eliminated compliance periods for companies falling below $1.00 shortly after a reverse split, accelerating potential delisting.

### Why it Matters
Receiving a Nasdaq deficiency notice often serves as a negative indicator for long-term shareholder value rather than a temporary hurdle. Investors should recognize that while reverse splits can prevent immediate delisting, they frequently fail to address the underlying financial issues that caused the stock price to decline.
