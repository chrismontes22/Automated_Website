What Your iPhone Actually Protects On Wi-Fi, And What It Doesn’t
============================================================

Apple’s Private Wi-Fi Address feature protects iPhone users by randomizing hardware identifiers, but it does not encrypt data traffic, leaving users vulnerable on unsecured public network connections.

### Key Points
- Apple’s Private Wi-Fi Address feature prevents network operators from tracking devices by randomizing MAC addresses across different Wi-Fi connections.
- This privacy tool does not encrypt data, meaning unencrypted traffic on public hotspots remains visible to other users on the same network.
- The Federal Trade Commission warns that public Wi-Fi can expose sensitive information like logins, messages, and account activity to malicious actors.
- Using a VPN complements Apple’s built-in protections by encrypting data traffic, ensuring that information remains unreadable even on insecure networks.
- When selecting a VPN, users should prioritize services that offer verifiable no-logs policies, independent security audits, and minimal impact on connection speeds.

### Why it Matters
While Apple provides robust background privacy tools, users often mistakenly assume these features offer total protection against all network-based threats. Understanding the distinction between device identification and data encryption is essential for maintaining security when accessing the internet in public spaces like airports or cafes.
