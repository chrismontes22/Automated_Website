Weapons makers and SpaceX in government's key investment fund portfolio
============================================================

Australia’s Future Fund reported significant growth in its semiconductor holdings while diversifying into aerospace, despite facing scrutiny over its continued investments in global weapons manufacturers and surveillance technology.

### Key Points
- The Future Fund’s semiconductor portfolio value rose 73% to $8.3 billion, driven by massive gains in Micron Technology and SK Hynix.
- Holdings in the "Magnificent 7" tech stocks declined by $1.4 billion, with the fund selling shares in most companies, including Tesla and Meta.
- The fund acquired 40,185 shares in SpaceX following its June NASDAQ debut and increased its stake in Palantir Technologies to 680,569 shares.
- Investments in defense and aerospace firms grew, with holdings in Thales and Elbit Systems increasing by 64% and 128% respectively.
- Unlike Norway’s sovereign wealth fund, which excludes companies like Boeing and Elbit Systems, the Future Fund maintains a narrower exclusion list focused primarily on tobacco.

### Why it Matters
The Future Fund’s investment strategy highlights the tension between maximizing sovereign wealth returns and addressing ethical concerns regarding defense and surveillance technology. As the fund continues to hold assets restricted by other global wealth managers, it faces ongoing pressure from activists and lawmakers to align its portfolio with stricter human rights and social responsibility standards.
