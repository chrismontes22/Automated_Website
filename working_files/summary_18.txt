The 10-Year Treasury Yield Just Hit 5.21%, Its Highest Level Since 2007, and It’s Dragging Up Mortgages, Car Loans, and Credit Cards With It
============================================================

The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield reached a 16-year high of 5.21%, triggering significant increases in borrowing costs for mortgages, credit cards, and consumer loans across the country.

### Key Points
- The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.21%, marking its highest level since 2007 and pushing average 30-year mortgage rates to 7.45%.
- Markets currently estimate a 70% probability of another Federal Reserve interest rate hike occurring in October.
- Goldman Sachs projects hyperscalers will invest $800 billion in capital this year, potentially rising to $1.1 trillion by 2027.
- A recent five-year Treasury auction experienced its weakest investor demand since 2018 as bond sell-offs continue.
- Analysts cite rising federal deficits and ongoing geopolitical instability as primary factors driving the increased term premium for U.S. debt.

### Why it Matters
Rising Treasury yields directly increase the cost of capital for businesses and consumers, potentially slowing economic activity as borrowing becomes more expensive. This trend reflects a complex tug-of-war between massive corporate investment in artificial intelligence and concerns over long-term fiscal sustainability and government debt levels.
