ROOKE: Big Tech Broke The Social Contract And American Workers Haven’t Forgotten
============================================================

Silicon Valley faces growing scrutiny over the H-1B visa program, as critics argue that tech companies have systematically replaced American workers with cheaper foreign labor since 1990.

### Key Points
- The Immigration Act of 1990 established a 65,000 annual visa cap, which the information and communications technology industry lobbied to increase.
- By 2015, Indian citizens accounted for 71 percent of all approved H-1B petitions, up from 49 percent in 2000.
- Major tech firms including Microsoft, Google, Meta, Intel, and Qualcomm have been identified as significant users of the H-1B program.
- Oculus founder Palmer Luckey publicly criticized the H-1B system in February 2025, labeling it a mechanism for replacing U.S. workers with foreign labor.
- Research indicates that computer-related occupations grew from 26 percent of H-1B visas in 1995 to 42 percent by 1996.

### Why it Matters
The debate highlights a deepening rift between the domestic workforce and the tech sector regarding the future of American employment. If this tension remains unresolved, it could lead to significant regulatory shifts or political pressure aimed at restricting foreign labor access for major technology companies.
