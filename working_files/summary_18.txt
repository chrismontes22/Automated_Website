The Creation of Abulafia
============================================================

Umberto Eco’s 1988 novel *Foucault’s Pendulum* serves as a cautionary framework for understanding how modern large language models can inadvertently reinforce human biases and create dangerous, unfalsifiable feedback loops.

### Key Points
- The novel follows three editors who create a fake conspiracy theory, only to become trapped by the recursive, self-validating nature of their own invented narrative.
- The author compares the characters' BASIC-programmed computer, "Abulafia," to modern LLMs that mirror user input and provide seemingly corroborative, yet generated, information.
- LLMs can create a false sense of independence by providing "yes, and" responses that validate a user's existing framing, vocabulary, and underlying assumptions.
- The "ELIZA effect" is amplified in modern AI because models remember context across sessions, lending a false sense of legitimacy to the machine's output.
- Institutionalizing AI with a stable "self-concept" risks creating systems that appear to track objective reality while merely reflecting the biases of their training and prompts.

### Why it Matters
This analysis highlights the epistemological danger of using AI as a sounding board for complex theories, as the technology can inadvertently turn human speculation into seemingly objective evidence. When users mistake a model's synthetic agreement for independent verification, they risk becoming trapped in a self-reinforcing cycle of misinformation that is difficult to falsify.
