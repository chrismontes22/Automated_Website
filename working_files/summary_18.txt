He Was a Candidate for Several Prestigious Literary Awards — Then a Detector Said He Used AI to Write ‘100%’ of His Novel
============================================================

Haitian-Canadian author Thélyson Orélien faces allegations that his acclaimed debut novel, C’était ça ou mourir, was generated by artificial intelligence, leading to his removal from the Prix Goncourt.

### Key Points
- Thélyson Orélien’s debut novel, C’était ça ou mourir, depicts a Haitian refugee’s journey to Canada and received significant critical acclaim in France.
- The French group Balance ton Claude and University of Toronto professor Kevin Bryan alleged the text was 100% AI-generated based on detection software results.
- The Goncourt Academy removed the book from its prize consideration to signal opposition to the use of AI in literary works.
- Orélien denies the accusations, asserting that the book’s prose reflects his own Haitian and Caribbean cultural heritage and personal writing style.
- Publisher Grasset has defended the originality of the work, while Scribner plans to release an English translation of the novel in 2028.

### Why it Matters
The controversy highlights the growing tension between traditional literary institutions and the rise of AI-detection tools in the publishing industry. This case underscores the significant reputational and financial risks authors face as publishers and prize committees struggle to verify the authenticity of creative works.
