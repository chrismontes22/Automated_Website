Researchers found a way to eavesdrop on headphones from 30 meters away, and encryption can't stop it
============================================================

Researchers at Hong Kong universities have developed InjectEave, a technique using radio signals to extract audio and data from common electronic devices through their vulnerable analog hardware components.

### Key Points
- The InjectEave method forces analog components like amplifiers and converters to leak signals by injecting electromagnetic waves between 0 MHz and 9 MHz.
- Researchers successfully recovered audio from devices including Sony, Apple, HP, and Philips products at distances of up to 30 meters, even through walls.
- The attack was tested on 11 commercial products, including wired headphones, VoIP phones, and smart-home appliances like fans and lamps.
- Equipment used in the study included a USRP B210 software-defined radio, a spectrum analyzer, and an RF power amplifier to extend the signal range.
- The technique bypasses standard digital security measures like encryption because the vulnerability exists within the physical analog hardware path.

### Why it Matters
This research highlights a significant security vulnerability in everyday consumer electronics that cannot be patched through software updates or encryption. Because the flaw resides in the physical hardware, manufacturers may need to implement better shielding or filtering to prevent unauthorized data extraction.
