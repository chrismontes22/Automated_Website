NY Gov. Hochul once resisted a data center pause. Now it’s central to her campaign.
============================================================

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has implemented a one-year moratorium on large-scale data centers to establish new environmental regulations, marking a significant shift in her administration's technology policy.

### Key Points
- The executive order pauses new data center projects consuming 50 megawatts or more of energy, enough to power approximately 45,000 homes.
- Governor Hochul intends to use the year-long window to develop an environmental impact statement addressing water usage, noise levels, and energy demand.
- The administration proposes that future data centers provide $1 million in local community benefits for every megawatt of power consumed.
- Republican opponent Bruce Blakeman criticizes the move as anti-business, arguing it stifles artificial intelligence innovation and creates a hostile environment for technology companies.
- A Siena University poll indicates that New York voters support the moratorium by a 2-to-1 margin, despite few ranking the issue as a top priority.
- At least 15 other states are currently considering similar legislative bans or moratoriums on large-scale data center development.

### Why it Matters
This policy shift highlights the growing tension between the massive energy requirements of the artificial intelligence industry and the infrastructure concerns of local communities. By balancing regulatory oversight with economic development, the state's approach could serve as a national model for how governments manage the rapid expansion of high-demand digital infrastructure.
