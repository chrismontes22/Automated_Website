Now That The Shoe Is On The Other Foot, Microsoft And Epic Games Suddenly Agree That AI Is Theft
============================================================

Major video game publishers like Microsoft and Epic Games are aggressively issuing DMCA takedowns against AI-generated game ports while simultaneously utilizing generative AI in their own development processes.

### Key Points
- Microsoft and Epic Games have filed numerous DMCA notices against browser-based ports of titles including *Fortnite*, *Minecraft*, *Call of Duty*, and *Halo*.
- Indie developers, such as the creators of *Slay the Spire 2*, *Celeste*, and *RimWorld*, have also been caught in the crossfire of these mass copyright enforcement actions.
- Epic Games is reportedly using Anthropic’s Claude AI for an upcoming Disney/Marvel project, despite Anthropic’s history of legal issues regarding training data.
- Microsoft has committed $80 billion annually to AI infrastructure, with Activision utilizing generative AI for artwork in recent *Call of Duty* releases.
- Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney recently defended the company's stance, arguing that training AI on copyrighted content can constitute fair use under current legal interpretations.

### Why it Matters
The industry is currently grappling with a "doom loop" where major publishers utilize AI to cut costs while simultaneously weaponizing copyright law to suppress AI-driven fan projects. This creates a double standard that disproportionately harms independent developers who lack the legal resources to defend their intellectual property against both unauthorized AI ports and corporate litigation.
