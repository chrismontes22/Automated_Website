Laptops Sealed Computers Shut, ARTISAN ALTO Snaps Them Back Open
============================================================

The ARTISAN ALTO AT-101 is a modular, vintage-inspired personal computer designed by Leo Botinelly that allows users to upgrade hardware capabilities through swappable, toolbox-style expansion cartridges.

### Key Points
- The AT-101 features a distinct vermilion trapezoidal shell with ivory keycaps and exposed cooling louvres.
- Users can expand system functionality by inserting cartridges for graphics, sound, retrocomputing, or dedicated AI processing.
- The system includes a dedicated AI module called COMPANION that keeps user data and processing local rather than cloud-based.
- Physical controls include five multifunction creative keys and a dedicated rocker switch for pausing or rewinding active tasks.
- The chassis is designed for easy maintenance, featuring a single-screw service clamp and accessible memory slots for hardware tinkering.
- Rear connectivity includes USB-C, HDMI, LAN, and USB-A ports to manage cable clutter while supporting modular hardware additions.

### Why it Matters
This concept challenges the industry trend of sealed, non-upgradable consumer electronics by prioritizing user repairability and hardware customization. By shifting toward a modular architecture, the device offers a sustainable alternative that allows users to evolve their computing power without replacing the entire machine.
