Trump Taking Stake In Corporations Opens Door For Dems To Exploit When They Regain Power
============================================================

The Trump administration’s acquisition of $27.7 billion in private company stakes across multiple industries has established a precedent that could enable future Democratic administrations to expand federal corporate influence.

### Key Points
- Since January 2025, the Trump administration has secured 39 ownership stakes in mining, technology, energy, and defense firms.
- The government acquired a 10% stake in Intel, a move critics note lacks an explicit exit strategy or the emergency context typical of past federal interventions.
- Investments are largely facilitated through the CHIPS and Science Act, which provides $52 billion in subsidies to boost domestic semiconductor manufacturing.
- Left-leaning policy groups and politicians, including Senator Bernie Sanders and Governor Gavin Newsom, are exploring how to leverage these ownership models for climate and labor goals.
- Economists warn that these interventions may distort capital markets by prioritizing companies with government ties over those with the strongest commercial performance.

### Why it Matters
This shift in federal policy fundamentally alters the relationship between the government and the private sector by normalizing state ownership outside of traditional financial crises. By establishing this framework, the current administration may inadvertently provide a roadmap for future leaders to exert direct control over corporate operations and industrial priorities.
