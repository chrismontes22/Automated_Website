Midterm election results will slow data center buildout, experts predict
============================================================

Recent midterm election results across the United States have empowered local and state officials to implement stricter regulations and moratoriums on the rapid expansion of new data centers.

### Key Points
- Voters approved 38 ballot measures restricting data center development across states including California, Illinois, Ohio, and New York.
- Texas, which represents up to 30% of planned U.S. data center capacity by 2030, faces potential construction delays following the gubernatorial election.
- Georgia Governor-elect Keisha Lance Bottoms plans to sign an executive order pausing new data center construction and reviewing existing tax incentives.
- Analysts from Yorkville Ives & Co. and GLJ Research suggest these political shifts will likely slow the development of hundreds of planned AI-focused facilities.
- Semiconductor and hardware stocks declined on Wednesday as investors reacted to the prospect of increased permitting costs and regulatory hurdles.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward stricter oversight could increase development costs and extend project timelines for the infrastructure essential to the artificial intelligence industry. While this may temper rapid sector growth, economists suggest it could also prevent market overheating and improve the long-term efficiency of capital investment.
