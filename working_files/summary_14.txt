Space Data Centers Proposed by Big Tech Could Be “Catastrophic,” Experts Warn in FCC Petition
============================================================

Environmental advocacy groups have petitioned the FCC to halt satellite licensing, citing concerns that orbital data centers could cause catastrophic atmospheric pollution and permanent damage to the night sky.

### Key Points
- Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility, Earthjustice, and DarkSky International filed the petition to mandate environmental reviews for satellite megaconstellations.
- The petition warns that millions of satellites burning up upon re-entry release toxic metals and pollutants into the stratosphere, potentially damaging the ozone layer.
- NOAA-sponsored research has already detected spacecraft-derived metals within sensitive stratospheric aerosols, though the long-term climate impacts remain largely unknown.
- Critics argue that companies like SpaceX and Cowboy Space Corporation have failed to provide adequate environmental mitigation plans for their proposed orbital infrastructure.
- The groups invoke the National Environmental Policy Act, asserting the FCC must assess cumulative environmental risks before granting further satellite deployment licenses.

### Why it Matters
This petition highlights a growing conflict between the rapid expansion of space-based technology and the preservation of Earth's atmospheric and astronomical integrity. If successful, the request could force a significant regulatory shift, requiring tech companies to prove their projects are environmentally sustainable before receiving federal approval.
