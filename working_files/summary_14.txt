Treasury crackdown exposes crypto's role in $2 million Hamas fundraising network
============================================================

The U.S. Treasury Department has sanctioned five individuals and entities for operating a fundraising network that funneled over $2 million to Hamas using cryptocurrency and fraudulent humanitarian charities.

### Key Points
- The Treasury sanctioned a Hamas military-wing member, two France-based individuals, and two charities for facilitating illegal financial transfers.
- The network raised more than $2 million between 2020 and 2026, with $1.5 million collected following the October 7, 2023, attack on Israel.
- Funds were moved through purported humanitarian efforts, with hundreds of thousands of dollars transferred via cryptocurrency to Gaza-based commander Saleem Abdallah Saleem al-Zaq.
- Sanctions freeze all assets under U.S. jurisdiction and prohibit Americans from conducting business with the designated parties.
- Foreign financial institutions that knowingly facilitate significant transactions for these entities now face potential secondary sanctions risk.

### Why it Matters
This action highlights the ongoing efforts by U.S. regulators to dismantle sophisticated financial networks that exploit digital assets to fund militant organizations. It underscores the government's strategy of targeting specific facilitators to disrupt terrorist financing while acknowledging that such groups continue to rely on a mix of conventional and crypto-based payment systems.
