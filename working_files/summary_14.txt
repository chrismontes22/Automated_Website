Mexican cartel crypto farm seized in mountain raid — 300 GPUs, satellite links, and industrial transformers tapped hydroelectric power
============================================================

Mexican drug cartels are increasingly establishing clandestine cryptocurrency mining operations in remote regions to launder illicit funds and diversify their income streams through sophisticated, electricity-intensive digital infrastructure.

### Key Points
- Mexican authorities recently seized a clandestine crypto farm in Tlaola, Puebla, containing 300 GPUs, satellite equipment, and power infrastructure.
- This facility marks the fourth such operation discovered near the Presa de Necaxa hydroelectric dam since early 2023.
- Criminal organizations, including the Cartel Jalisco Nueva Generación (CJNG), are reportedly stealing electricity from the local grid to power mining activities.
- Cybersecurity firm SILIKN reports that the use of cryptomining for money laundering in Mexico increased by at least 55.8% last year.
- Cartels are targeting remote, cartel-controlled areas to hide the significant noise and power consumption associated with large-scale mining operations.

### Why it Matters
The integration of cryptocurrency mining into cartel operations represents a significant evolution in how organized crime groups launder money and evade international financial oversight. As these illicit activities expand globally, they pose growing challenges for law enforcement and energy infrastructure security in vulnerable regions.
