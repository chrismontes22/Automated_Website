Danish university DTU breach exposes data of up to 200,000 people
============================================================

The Technical University of Denmark has disclosed a significant data breach involving its identity management system, potentially exposing the personal information of approximately 200,000 current and former users.

### Key Points
- Hackers accessed the DTUBasen identity and access management system using compromised credentials to download data spanning over two decades.
- Exposed information includes Danish civil registration numbers (CPR), home addresses, job titles, and contact details for next of kin.
- The breach potentially impacts anyone who has been an employee, student, guest, or external partner of the university since 2003.
- DTU is notifying affected individuals via the e-Boks system but cannot confirm the exact scope of the stolen data.
- The university advises users to monitor for identity fraud, place credit alerts on their CPR numbers, and remain vigilant against targeted phishing attempts.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the severe risks associated with centralized identity management systems that store long-term historical data. The exposure of sensitive government-issued identification numbers creates a long-term threat of identity theft and sophisticated social engineering attacks for the affected individuals.
