'This technology turns every router into a potential means for surveillance': Report claims Wi-Fi devices could 'quietly identify' people with nearly 100% accuracy
============================================================

Researchers at KASTEL have developed a method to identify individuals using standard, unmodified Wi-Fi routers, achieving nearly 100% accuracy without requiring cameras or specialized sensing equipment.

### Key Points
- KASTEL researchers at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology successfully identified 197 volunteers using only standard Wi-Fi beamforming feedback signals.
- The system functions by analyzing radio wave propagation to create images of people, effectively turning routers into invisible surveillance tools.
- Identification remains possible even if individuals do not carry Wi-Fi-enabled devices, as the system utilizes signals from other active devices in the vicinity.
- Unlike CCTV or video doorbells, this wireless surveillance method leaves no visible trace, making it difficult for subjects to detect or avoid.
- Researchers are urging the inclusion of privacy safeguards in the upcoming IEEE 802.11bf wireless standard to prevent potential misuse.

### Why it Matters
This technology transforms ubiquitous wireless networks into an invisible, comprehensive surveillance infrastructure that operates without user consent or specialized hardware. The findings highlight a significant privacy risk, prompting calls for industry-wide security standards to protect individuals from covert tracking in public and private spaces.
