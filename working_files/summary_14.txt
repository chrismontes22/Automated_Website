Open Channels FM: Who Controls Our Software, Open Channels News Week in Review
============================================================

The r/OpenSource subreddit bans AI-generated content, highlighting a broader industry debate over software control, infrastructure sustainability, and the shifting landscape of the open-source ecosystem this week.

### Key Points
- The r/OpenSource subreddit implemented a ban on AI-assisted projects, prompting some users to migrate to the new r/TrueOpenSource community.
- Tesco is reportedly considering a migration from VMware to the open-source Proxmox platform following a 175% price increase dispute with Broadcom.
- Linux Foundation research indicates that while AI can reduce coding time by 52%, it also introduces a significant volume of low-quality pull requests.
- Mistral AI previewed "LeChonk," a trillion-parameter open-weight model designed for specialized sectors like cyber defense, finance, and engineering.
- A Danish government pilot program to replace Microsoft Office with Collabora ended after six months due to persistent formatting and interoperability issues.

### Why it Matters
These developments underscore a growing tension between corporate software control and the demand for independent, sustainable infrastructure. As organizations and users prioritize autonomy, the ability to inspect and modify technology is becoming a critical factor in long-term digital strategy.
