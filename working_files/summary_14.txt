Media Briefing: Overheard from publishers at Digiday London Media Strategies Town Hall
============================================================

Publisher executives at Digiday’s London Town Hall discussed evolving engagement metrics, the risks of blocking AI crawlers, and the uncertain future of agentic media buying in journalism.

### Key Points
- The SPUR coalition, now representing over 80 publishers including The New York Times and The Economist, is developing standards to track AI usage of content.
- Industry leaders are shifting focus from simple page views to complex engagement data, such as dwell time and conversion rates, to better measure audience value.
- Publishers are debating the long-term impact of blocking AI bots, weighing potential content scarcity against the risk of losing search visibility and relevance.
- Media companies are exploring agentic media trading to reclaim ad revenue from intermediaries, though many view the technology as an unproven, experimental phase.
- Financial Times data highlights the scale of the scraping challenge, with one trialist account recently attempting to access one million articles via 700,000 IP addresses.

### Why it Matters
As AI fundamentally alters how audiences discover and consume information, publishers are struggling to establish new economic models and standardized metrics for success. The industry's ability to secure fair compensation for content and maintain direct relationships with readers will determine the long-term financial viability of professional journalism.
