US inflation rises before midterm elections, fuelling rate hike fears
============================================================

United States consumer prices rose to 3.7 percent in September, driven largely by surging petrol costs and increasing pressure on the Federal Reserve regarding future interest rate hikes.

### Key Points
- Annual inflation accelerated to 3.7 percent in September, up from 3.4 percent in August, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
- Petrol prices surged 9.1 percent monthly and 34.2 percent annually, accounting for over half of the total Consumer Price Index increase.
- Shelter costs rose 0.4 percent, while computer software and accessory prices jumped 28 percent due to heavy investment in AI infrastructure.
- The Federal Reserve faces a split decision on interest rates, with a 47.1 percent market probability of a quarter-point hike later this month.
- US stock markets reacted negatively to the report, with the Nasdaq falling 0.8 percent and the S&P 500 dropping 0.6 percent.

### Why it Matters
This inflation data complicates the economic landscape ahead of the midterm elections and forces the Federal Reserve to weigh cooling price growth against potential market instability. Persistent energy and technology costs suggest that inflationary pressures remain broad, influencing both consumer sentiment and upcoming monetary policy decisions.
