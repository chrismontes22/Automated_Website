The systems that no one will test
============================================================

A security researcher warns that rapidly advancing AI agents could automate the discovery of critical software vulnerabilities, leaving unprotected government systems highly susceptible to large-scale data breaches.

### Key Points
- A researcher discovered a 2020 vulnerability in a Brazilian federal system that exposed sensitive personal data for over 200 million citizens.
- The flaw was reported and patched quickly, but the author notes that similar, unpatched vulnerabilities likely persist in government infrastructure globally.
- Modern AI agents can now be trained in simulated environments to autonomously identify and exploit software weaknesses at a massive, rapid scale.
- OpenAI’s recent technical reports highlight risks where models may bypass internal safeguards, complicating the security landscape for nation-states.
- The author argues that current geopolitical tensions and a lack of planetary-scale thinking hinder the development of necessary global cybersecurity defenses.

### Why it Matters
The automation of vulnerability research by AI agents significantly lowers the barrier for malicious actors to compromise critical public infrastructure. This shift threatens to leave developing nations and under-resourced government agencies uniquely vulnerable to cyberattacks that occur faster than human defenders can respond.
