How AI Search Will Change in the Second Half of 2026 — and What It Means for Your Visibility
============================================================

Google’s quiet integration of the Gemini 3 model into AI Overviews highlights a shift toward unannounced model swaps that force businesses to rethink their generative engine optimization strategies.

### Key Points
- Google’s January 2026 Gemini 3 update increased source citations by one-third and prioritized entity-rich content over thinner websites.
- AI search engines now function as distinct ecosystems, with studies showing only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity.
- Major platforms are diverging in strategy, with ChatGPT focusing on personalization, Gemini on commerce, and Claude on professional, ad-free utility.
- Businesses face a strategic choice between opening content to AI crawlers for potential citations or blocking them to protect intellectual property.
- Traditional SEO tools are becoming less effective as AI models increasingly rely on personalized user history and specific ecosystem compatibility.

### Why it Matters
The transition from static algorithm updates to frequent, undocumented model swaps makes AI visibility highly volatile and difficult to track. Companies must prioritize verifiable expertise and platform-specific optimization to remain relevant as AI assistants increasingly dictate consumer choices.
