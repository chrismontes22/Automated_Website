How HSBC’s Sharia fund invested millions in Israeli war-linked firms
============================================================

HSBC’s Islamic Global Equity Index Fund faces scrutiny after investing over $130 million in companies linked to the Israeli military, raising concerns regarding its Sharia-compliant investment standards.

### Key Points
- The HSBC fund invested in five companies linked to the Israeli military, including Palantir, RTX, Analog Devices, Honeywell, and Rolls-Royce.
- Investments included $38.3 million in Palantir and $35.7 million in RTX, a major manufacturer of missile and air-defense components.
- The fund, which manages over $8 billion, is marketed as ethically aligned with Islamic principles and overseen by an independent committee of scholars.
- Experts note a "critical gap" in screening processes that focus on a company's primary sector rather than the end-use of its products in military supply chains.
- HSBC has pledged to review the matter with independent advisors to determine if the fund’s current approach meets investor expectations.

### Why it Matters
This controversy highlights a significant disconnect between technical financial screening and the ethical expectations of investors seeking Sharia-compliant products. It raises broader questions about the transparency of Islamic finance governance and whether current industry standards effectively prevent capital from supporting military activities and civilian harm.
