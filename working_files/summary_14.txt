Linux creator Linus Torvalds has signaled that kernel maintainers will accept AI-generated code, provided the submissions meet quality standards and avoid low-effort, automated "drive-by" patches.

### Key Points
- Linus Torvalds confirmed that kernel maintainers will no longer prioritize identifying the origin of code, focusing instead on technical merit.
- The policy shift addresses concerns regarding the influx of low-quality, automated contributions often associated with generative AI tools.
- Developers are encouraged to maintain high standards for patches, as the kernel project remains wary of "drive-by" submissions that lack human oversight.
- This stance reflects a broader industry trend of integrating AI-assisted development while attempting to mitigate the risks of unverified or buggy code.

### Why it Matters
This decision marks a significant shift in how one of the world's most critical open-source projects manages the integration of generative AI into its development workflow. By prioritizing code quality over authorship, the Linux kernel team sets a pragmatic precedent for other large-scale software projects balancing innovation with security.
