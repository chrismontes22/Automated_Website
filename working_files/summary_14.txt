Hackers hijack Egypt highway billboard and Malaysian screens to show porn
============================================================

A hacking group exploited software vulnerabilities in digital advertising infrastructure to display pornographic content on a major highway billboard in Giza, Egypt, on October 29.

### Key Points
- The hacking group, known as ‘Leakers$’, claimed to have identified thousands of exposed digital advertising systems globally through automated scans.
- The incident occurred on the 26 July Corridor in Giza, involving a billboard operated by Arabian United, a joint venture between United Media Services and Arabian Contracting Services.
- Egypt’s Interior Ministry confirmed the breach, attributing the incident to the hacking of an unnamed international advertising company’s website.
- The hackers reported gaining unauthorized access to display systems in multiple countries, including Iraq, Malaysia, Brazil, and China.
- Arabian United operates a network of 1,926 billboards in Egypt, including 239 digital installations, and has suspended the affected screen pending an investigation.

### Why it Matters
- This breach highlights significant cybersecurity vulnerabilities within the rapidly expanding digital infrastructure of public advertising networks. It raises urgent questions regarding the oversight of third-party software and the lack of standardized security protocols for systems operating in highly visible public spaces.
