How did the Manchester Airports Group cyberattack take place, and what data was exposed in the 8.7 million customer records? The experts weigh in
============================================================

Manchester Airports Group has confirmed a significant data breach affecting 8.7 million customers, exposing personal information including email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registrations, and residential postcodes across three airports.

### Key Points
- The breach impacts customers of Manchester, London Stansted, and East Midlands airports who used parking, lounge, Fast Track, or Wi-Fi services.
- Stolen data includes email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registrations, and postcodes, though banking and financial information remains secure.
- Manchester Airports Group has contained the incident and is working with authorities to investigate the unauthorized access.
- Experts warn that the exposed data enables highly convincing, personalized phishing and smishing scams targeting travelers.
- Affected individuals are advised to remain vigilant against suspicious communications and avoid clicking links in unexpected messages.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing vulnerability of non-operational airport services, which now serve as primary targets for cybercriminals seeking to exploit traveler data. The breach underscores a critical need for the aviation industry to prioritize data minimization and robust network segmentation to protect customers from sophisticated, AI-enhanced extortion campaigns.
