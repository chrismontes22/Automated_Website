The Business Case For SEO Is Changing; Budget Justifications Should Too via @sejournal, @cshel
============================================================

Organizations should shift from funding SEO as a single acquisition channel to classifying search investments by their specific economic purpose, such as revenue protection or infrastructure maintenance.

### Key Points
- Traditional SEO budgets often fail because they evaluate diverse technical and operational tasks solely against incremental organic traffic metrics.
- Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) initiatives often overlap with existing SEO fundamentals, making separate budgets redundant and potentially inefficient.
- Companies should categorize spending into four pillars: revenue generation, revenue protection, capability maintenance, and uncertainty reduction through experimentation.
- Technical foundations, such as structured product data and site architecture, provide shared value across both conventional search and AI-powered discovery platforms.
- Financial justification for SEO projects should be based on the specific business problem addressed rather than the marketing acronym attached to the work.

### Why it Matters
Reframing search budgets allows executives to make more informed resource-allocation decisions by distinguishing between growth-focused initiatives and essential operational maintenance. This approach prevents the common mistake of cutting critical infrastructure funding simply because organic traffic metrics are experiencing temporary declines.
