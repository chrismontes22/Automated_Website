I disabled one Windows USB feature and my peripherals have never felt more responsive
============================================================

Windows users can improve peripheral responsiveness and prevent connectivity issues on desktop PCs by disabling the USB selective suspend power-saving feature within the system's advanced power settings.

### Key Points
- USB selective suspend is a Windows power-saving feature that cuts power to peripherals like mice, keyboards, and webcams during inactivity.
- The setting often causes wake-up delays, cursor stuttering, and connection failures for external SSDs, USB hubs, and Bluetooth dongles.
- Users can disable the feature globally by navigating to Control Panel, Power Options, and adjusting the Advanced power settings.
- Specific devices can be managed individually by unchecking power-saving options within the Device Manager properties menu.
- Internal Wi-Fi cards may also experience connectivity drops, which can be mitigated by disabling PCI Express Link State Power Management.

### Why it Matters
Disabling these aggressive power-saving settings eliminates unnecessary hardware latency and prevents the frustration of having to manually reconnect peripherals after brief periods of inactivity. This optimization is particularly beneficial for desktop users who rely on stable connections for external drives, docks, and high-bandwidth devices.
