Tech industry leaders and security experts are addressing critical challenges in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and hardware development as companies navigate rising infrastructure costs and evolving digital threats.

### Key Points
- Over 100 technology companies are warning of imminent AI-driven cyberattacks while debating responsibility for funding defensive security measures.
- Meta has introduced the MTIA 400 chip for AI training and ad serving, while Nvidia and Cerebras face scrutiny over real-world performance metrics.
- Microsoft is addressing a zero-day vulnerability in on-premises SharePoint, and EQT has acquired a majority stake in cybersecurity firm Acronis for $3.5 billion.
- Google is mandating stricter memory management for Android applications to combat rising hardware costs and limited RAM availability.
- The DEF CON conference is expanding its Franklin project to harden critical infrastructure, following successful security testing in its voting village.

### Why it Matters
These developments highlight a growing tension between the rapid deployment of AI technologies and the underlying need for robust, cost-effective infrastructure and security. As hardware costs rise and cyber threats become more sophisticated, businesses must balance innovation with the practical realities of maintaining secure and efficient digital systems.
