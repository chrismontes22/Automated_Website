It’s Not Just RAM: Windows Licenses Are Also Pushing Up PC Prices
============================================================

PC manufacturers may increase consumer laptop and desktop prices by up to 5% following reports that Microsoft has raised Windows 11 OEM licensing costs by 10%.

### Key Points
- Reports from Taiwan’s Economic Daily News indicate Microsoft increased Windows 11 OEM license fees by 7% to 10%.
- PC makers like Asus, Acer, and Framework have already cited rising component and licensing costs as reasons for recent hardware price hikes.
- Manufacturers typically purchase Windows licenses in bulk at discounted rates, but these costs are now being passed on to consumers.
- Rising prices for essential hardware components, including RAM, SSDs, and motherboards, are further contributing to the increased cost of new computers.
- Microsoft has declined to comment on the reported licensing fee adjustments.

### Why it Matters
Rising software licensing costs combined with expensive hardware components threaten to make new computers significantly less affordable for budget-conscious consumers. This trend could dampen consumer demand and force manufacturers to adjust their pricing strategies throughout the upcoming fiscal quarters.
