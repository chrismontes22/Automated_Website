We’ve spent billions defending software. It’s time to protect execution
============================================================

Cybersecurity experts are advocating for a shift toward hardware-based oversight to monitor processor instructions, aiming to secure critical infrastructure against vulnerabilities that software-only defenses currently fail to prevent.

### Key Points
- The United States recorded a record 3,322 data breaches in 2025, with 80% attributed to cyberattacks.
- Memory-safety vulnerabilities, such as buffer overflows, account for approximately 70% of reported security bugs at companies like Microsoft and Google.
- Verizon’s 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report identified vulnerability exploitation as the leading initial access vector, responsible for 31% of all breaches.
- Anthropic reported that AI models, such as Claude Opus 4.6, are now capable of identifying and validating hundreds of high-severity vulnerabilities in open-source software.
- Implementing an independent hardware layer allows processors to enforce security rules and stop prohibited behaviors before they result in successful exploits.

### Why it Matters
Moving the security boundary from software to hardware provides a necessary backstop for critical systems that cannot rely on perfect code. This approach helps mitigate the risks posed by AI-accelerated attacks, which currently allow hackers to identify and exploit vulnerabilities faster than human teams can patch them.
