I’m a gamer and a parent. The KIDS Act makes my kids less safe.
============================================================

The proposed KIDS Act aims to enhance child safety online but faces criticism for potentially mandating widespread collection of sensitive biometric data and government-issued identification from young users.

### Key Points
- The KIDS Act mandates age verification across various online platforms, including gaming services and social media.
- Critics argue the legislation forces companies to collect sensitive data like government IDs and biometrics, increasing the risk of large-scale data breaches.
- The bill is criticized for applying a one-size-fits-all regulatory framework to diverse digital experiences, ranging from console gaming to educational platforms.
- Opponents suggest that existing parental control tools and private software solutions are more effective than government-mandated age verification systems.
- Historical data from similar laws in the United Kingdom shows that third-party age-checking providers have previously suffered security breaches exposing user identity documents.

### Why it Matters
The legislation highlights a growing tension between government efforts to regulate digital safety and the fundamental cybersecurity principle of data minimization. By incentivizing the mass collection of sensitive personal information, the bill may inadvertently create new security vulnerabilities that threaten the privacy of children it intends to protect.
