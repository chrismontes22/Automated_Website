These Students Thought They Were Making an Investment in Their Future. They Spent Everything They Had. Then the Worst Happened.
============================================================

The sudden liquidation of the cybersecurity boot camp provider IronCircle has left hundreds of students with significant debt and few job prospects despite partnerships with prestigious universities.

### Key Points
- IronCircle, formerly known as ThriveDX and HackerUSA, abruptly shut down in June after eight years of operation.
- The company charged students up to $18,900 for courses while failing to provide promised job placement support or accurate career data.
- More than 20 universities partnered with the firm, earning millions in revenue while providing the program with a veneer of institutional legitimacy.
- Internal data from three partner universities showed only 35% of graduates secured employment within six months of completing the program.
- Students remain liable for private loans and tuition payment plans despite the company’s liquidation and the cessation of all coursework.

### Why it Matters
These partnerships allowed a for-profit entity to leverage the reputations of accredited universities to attract students without being subject to the same regulatory oversight as traditional degree programs. The collapse highlights the risks of "brand renting" in higher education, where institutions prioritize revenue over the vetting of third-party vocational training providers.
