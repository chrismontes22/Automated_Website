Hackers defrost fridges at hundreds of French supermarkets, Bloomberg says
============================================================

Hackers compromised refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets, including Intermarché and Super U locations, forcing stores to discard large quantities of perishable food after forced defrosting.

### Key Points
- Attackers remotely set refrigeration units to 50°C for six hours, causing widespread spoilage of meat, frozen goods, and vegetables.
- Approximately 150 Intermarché stores and nearly 100 Coopérative U locations were confirmed as targets, with additional reports involving Leclerc stores.
- The affected cooling systems are managed by the Danish supplier Danfoss, which previously released firmware fixes for security vulnerabilities identified by researchers in August.
- Cybersecurity firm Claroty’s Team82 previously warned that thousands of Danfoss controllers remained reachable via the internet with potential administrative access flaws.
- Retailers and Danfoss have not yet confirmed the specific method used to breach the systems or whether the known vulnerabilities were exploited.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a growing trend of cyberattacks that prioritize physical operational disruption over traditional data theft. As critical infrastructure becomes increasingly connected, these breaches pose significant financial risks to retailers and threaten the stability of food supply chains.
