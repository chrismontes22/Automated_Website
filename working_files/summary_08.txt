Automakers Want Congress to Ban Chinese-Made Cars, Citing Data Privacy
============================================================

The Alliance for Automotive Innovation is urging Congress to implement a comprehensive ban on Chinese-made vehicles, software, and hardware to address national security and unfair trade concerns.

### Key Points
- The Alliance for Automotive Innovation (AAI) requested a total ban on the import, sale, and manufacture of Chinese automotive technology.
- The proposed restrictions align with the bipartisan "Connected Vehicle Security Act of 2026," which targets China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea.
- AAI CEO John Bozzella cited concerns regarding intellectual property theft, government-backed subsidies, and potential surveillance risks.
- Chinese electric vehicles currently face high U.S. tariffs, though their global popularity has surged due to significant state-funded price advantages.

### Why it Matters
This push for a permanent ban highlights the intensifying intersection of national security policy and the global automotive supply chain. If enacted, these measures would solidify a protectionist stance that could fundamentally reshape the U.S. electric vehicle market and future trade relations with China.
