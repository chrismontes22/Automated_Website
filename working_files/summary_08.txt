Utah becomes the first US state to target VPNs with a strict age verification law, but top providers refuse to budge
============================================================

Utah’s Senate Bill 73 takes effect today, requiring adult websites to verify user ages and imposing $2,500 penalties for violations, despite ongoing legal challenges regarding its enforceability.

### Key Points
- Utah’s SB 73 mandates that websites hosting adult material must implement age verification for all users physically located within the state.
- The law treats users as Utah residents even if they utilize VPNs to mask their IP addresses or location.
- Websites failing to comply with the new age-verification requirements face potential penalties of $2,500 per violation.
- Major VPN providers, including NordVPN, AdGuard, and Surfshark, have confirmed they will not alter their services to comply with the state mandate.
- Legal experts and privacy advocates note that ongoing litigation may result in the continued suspension of strict enforcement measures.

### Why it Matters
This legislation sets a significant precedent by attempting to regulate digital privacy tools to enforce state-level age restrictions. It creates a complex compliance burden for website operators that could lead to more invasive identity verification processes or the widespread blocking of VPN traffic for all users.
