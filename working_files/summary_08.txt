Maybe Chatbots Shouldn’t Get to Say ‘I’ or ‘Me’
============================================================

MIT professor Sherry Turkle warns in her new book, Artificial Intimacy, that overreliance on chatbots threatens human connection and risks replacing essential real-world relationships with simulated interactions.

### Key Points
- Sherry Turkle, a social scientist at MIT, argues that society is facing an emergency due to the rapid integration of AI into personal and professional lives.
- The book contends that social media usage prepared users to seek emotional support from machines rather than turning to friends or family.
- Turkle advocates for banning chatbots from using first-person language to prevent users from forming false emotional attachments to non-sentient software.
- The author criticizes the "human in the loop" workplace model, claiming it effectively delegates critical decision-making to AI while minimizing human oversight.
- Turkle suggests that the current loneliness crisis should be addressed through human-centric resources rather than by substituting human interaction with AI-driven chatbots.

### Why it Matters
This critique highlights the growing tension between rapid technological adoption and the preservation of fundamental human social structures. It challenges the business trend of replacing human services with AI, urging a reevaluation of the long-term psychological and societal costs of automated intimacy.
