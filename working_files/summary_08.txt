Voice Of Goku Responds To AI Stealing Her Voice: ‘I can’t Forgive Them…It Makes Me Really Angry, It’s No Different Than Theft’
============================================================

The Japan Actors Union has released new guidelines requiring explicit consent and royalty payments for the use of generative AI to replicate the voices of professional performers.

### Key Points
- The Japan Actors Union (JAU), representing approximately 2,600 performers, issued its first formal guidelines to combat the unauthorized use of AI voice synthesis.
- Companies must now obtain explicit permission from actors before using their voices for machine learning, deep learning, or any AI-generated audio recordings.
- The union classifies authorized AI voice replication as a performance, mandating that companies provide salaries or royalties to the original voice actors.
- Prominent voice actors, including Dragon Ball’s Masako Nozawa and Kenjiro Tsuda, have publicly criticized unauthorized AI usage as a form of theft.
- These guidelines arrive as the Japanese government faces increasing pressure to establish national legal frameworks regarding copyright infringement and generative AI technology.

### Why it Matters
These guidelines establish a significant precedent for labor rights in the entertainment industry by formalizing the protection of vocal identity against unauthorized AI replication. As Japan navigates the intersection of technology and copyright law, this union-led initiative could influence future national regulations and corporate policies regarding AI training data.
