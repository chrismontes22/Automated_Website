Winner of scientific imagery contest was AI generated
============================================================

Nikon has disqualified researcher Ning Xu from its Small World In Motion competition after his winning microscopy video was found to contain unauthorized generative AI elements.

### Key Points
- Nikon disqualified Ning Xu’s video of cilia in a child with primary ciliary dyskinesia for violating rules against generative AI.
- Microscopy experts challenged the video's scientific accuracy on social media, noting that the cilia and nuclei did not behave realistically.
- Dr. Xu stated he rendered regions of the video to improve presentation rather than to provide an accurate anatomical reconstruction.
- The microscopy community issued an open letter expressing concern that the misleading footage could distort public understanding of the rare disease.
- Vietnamese researcher Nguyen Nam Nhat was named the new first-place winner for his video of a roundworm encountering a single-celled organism.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing tension between artistic presentation and scientific integrity in the era of generative artificial intelligence. It underscores the necessity for rigorous verification processes in scientific competitions to ensure that visual data remains an accurate representation of reality rather than a fabricated interpretation.
