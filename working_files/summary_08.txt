At least 12 states face cyberattacks on their water systems, sources say
============================================================

Cyberattacks targeting water and wastewater utility systems have been reported across at least 12 U.S. states, prompting federal investigations into potential foreign interference and infrastructure security vulnerabilities.

### Key Points
- Reports indicate cyber intrusions have affected water utilities in at least 12 states, though no widespread service disruptions have occurred.
- Intelligence sources identify Iran as the primary suspect behind the attacks, which aim to disable alarms or compromise system passwords.
- The FBI is leading the investigation and advising utilities to disconnect systems from the internet and prepare for manual operations.
- Michigan State Police are coordinating with federal agencies to ensure local municipal water systems secure their infrastructure against further threats.

### Why it Matters
These incidents highlight critical vulnerabilities in essential public infrastructure that could pose significant risks to public health if automated systems are successfully compromised. The situation underscores an urgent need for municipal utilities to adopt robust cybersecurity protocols to protect vital water supplies from state-sponsored digital threats.
