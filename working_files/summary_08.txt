Trump declares 100 percent tariffs on many drones and all aircraft parts
============================================================

President Donald Trump has announced new tariffs of up to 100 percent on imported drones and aircraft parts, aiming to bolster domestic manufacturing and address national security concerns.

### Key Points
- Drones with thermal cameras and those weighing over 25kg face a 100 percent tariff, while smaller consumer drones are subject to a 25 percent levy.
- Companies can avoid these tariffs by committing to domestic production of drones and aircraft components within the United States.
- Reduced tariff rates of 10 to 15 percent apply to imports from the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan, and the UK under specific origin requirements.
- The administration cited national security vulnerabilities, data privacy risks, and the need for increased domestic production capacity as primary justifications for the policy.
- The Secretary of Commerce is authorized to establish a new program designed to incentivize investment in U.S.-based drone manufacturing facilities.

### Why it Matters
These tariffs significantly increase costs for consumers and businesses that rely on foreign-made drone technology for agriculture, emergency response, and commercial operations. The policy reflects a broader shift toward protectionist trade measures intended to reduce reliance on foreign supply chains, despite ongoing legal challenges regarding the executive authority to impose such duties.
