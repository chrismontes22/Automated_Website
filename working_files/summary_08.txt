Amazon's HR Lead Uses AI Tool to Write 100,000 Lines of Code - 25 Years After She Last Coded
============================================================

Amazon HR leader Beth Galetti reports using internal AI tools to write 100,000 lines of code, highlighting the company's broader push to integrate generative AI into employee workflows.

### Key Points
- Amazon HR leader Beth Galetti utilized the company's internal AI coding tool, Kiro, to develop applications after a 25-year hiatus from programming.
- Over 1,500 Amazon employees participated in an internal "Everyone Can Build" competition, resulting in significant process automation and productivity tools.
- Amazon provides all staff with free access to AI training and covers the cost of one annual AWS AI certification exam.
- The company’s focus on AI-driven productivity follows significant corporate workforce reductions, including 16,000 job cuts announced seven months ago.

### Why it Matters
Amazon is positioning AI as a tool to empower non-technical staff to build software, potentially increasing internal efficiency and operational agility. However, this strategy coincides with ongoing corporate layoffs, reflecting a broader industry trend of prioritizing AI investment while restructuring traditional human-led roles.
