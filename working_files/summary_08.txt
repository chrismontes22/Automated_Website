Is AI the new China Shock?
============================================================

The massive global investment surge in artificial intelligence now rivals China’s post-2001 capital spending boom, signaling a transformative macroeconomic shift that will impact labor markets and global industries.

### Key Points
- U.S. hyperscalers including Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet are projected to spend approximately $800 billion on capital expenditures in 2026.
- Global AI-related capital investment for hardware like chips and data centers is estimated to reach $10 trillion to $15 trillion over the next decade.
- The International Energy Agency forecasts that global data center electricity consumption will double by 2030, reaching 945 terawatt hours.
- Unlike China’s manufacturing-led boom, the AI surge is driven by market forces and focuses on compute, software, and cognitive labor efficiency.
- Early U.S. labor data indicates that job categories with high AI exposure are experiencing faster deterioration compared to low-exposure sectors.

### Why it Matters
This investment cycle represents a fundamental macroeconomic regime change that extends far beyond the technology sector to influence global GDP, power demand, and asset prices. Policymakers and investors must prepare for significant societal shifts, as the transition risks mirroring the political and economic polarization that followed the previous era of global industrial expansion.
