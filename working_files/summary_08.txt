If AI kills us, don’t blame the machine
============================================================

Leading artificial intelligence executives from OpenAI and Anthropic are facing scrutiny as internal warnings about potential existential risks clash with aggressive industry efforts to commercialize powerful new models.

### Key Points
- OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Anthropic leadership have publicly warned that rapid development of large language models could threaten cybersecurity and global stability.
- Former Anthropic employee Jacob Coxon resigned, citing concerns that the company is recklessly gambling with human safety by ignoring civilizational risks.
- OpenAI data scientist Evan Hubinger stated he believes there is a greater than 10% chance that artificial intelligence could cause human extinction within the next decade.
- Critics argue that these catastrophic warnings may serve as marketing tactics to drive investment while companies simultaneously pitch AI tools to replace human labor.
- Experts emphasize that AI models are code-based tools rather than autonomous beings, meaning responsibility for safety failures rests with the developers, not the software.

### Why it Matters
The tension between AI companies' apocalyptic warnings and their pursuit of rapid market expansion raises critical questions about corporate accountability and the regulation of emerging technologies. If these tools cause real-world harm, the industry's tendency to anthropomorphize AI as an uncontrollable force may fail to shield developers from legal and ethical liability.
