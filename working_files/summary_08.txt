Should you ask AI about voting? Election officials are cautious
============================================================

Election officials are debating the risks and potential benefits of using artificial intelligence chatbots to provide voters with accurate information ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

### Key Points
- Election officials warn that chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude may provide outdated, hallucinated, or geographically incorrect voting information.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are partnering with organizations like Democracy Works to direct users toward official, authoritative election resources.
- Ohio has implemented an internal AI tool called "Eva" to assist election workers by summarizing state procedures and manuals.
- Secretaries of state in Maine and other regions remain cautious, citing cybersecurity concerns and the potential for AI-generated misinformation.
- Meta reports that it actively collaborates with election associations to identify and correct inaccurate voting data on its platforms.

### Why it Matters
The reliance on AI for election information creates a tension between technological convenience and the necessity of verified, official data. If chatbots fail to provide accurate details, it could lead to voter disenfranchisement or confusion, forcing election offices to prioritize public education on trusted information sources.
