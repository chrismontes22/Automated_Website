Can Germany revive its economy before the AfD gains ground?
============================================================

Germany is pivoting its industrial strategy toward high-tech sectors like artificial intelligence and quantum computing to combat economic stagnation and the rising influence of the far-right AfD party.

### Key Points
- Germany’s GDP grew by only 0.2% last year, while exports to China dropped 9.7% in 2025 due to increased competition and high domestic costs.
- The government’s new high-tech agenda prioritizes six sectors, including biotechnology, microelectronics, and climate-neutral mobility, to stimulate future growth.
- Venture capital investment in Germany surged 44% to €5.8 billion in the first half of 2026, with over 3,000 new startups founded during that period.
- Defense spending is projected to reach €120 billion in 2026, providing a significant financial boost to dual-use technologies like drones and advanced sensors.
- Experts suggest that Germany can maintain global competitiveness by focusing on specialized industrial niches and "choke points" rather than entire value chains.

### Why it Matters
This strategic shift represents a critical attempt to modernize Germany's aging industrial model and stabilize the political landscape amid growing populist sentiment. Successfully transitioning to a startup-driven, high-tech economy is essential for maintaining the nation's long-term prosperity and global market relevance.
