The last generation of fully upgradeable PCs is being built right now
============================================================

The rise of soldered RAM and integrated hardware is ending the era of modular PC upgrades as manufacturers prioritize performance, power efficiency, and AI-driven processing capabilities.

### Key Points
- Major manufacturers, including Intel, Apple, and Qualcomm, are increasingly soldering memory directly onto processor packages to improve signal bandwidth and power efficiency.
- High-performance requirements for AI workloads and integrated graphics are forcing even modular-focused companies, like Framework, to adopt soldered LPDDR5X memory.
- The shift toward sealed, non-upgradeable designs is moving from thin laptops into desktop-class hardware, mini PCs, and all-in-one systems.
- Cloud gaming services like Nvidia GeForce Now and Xbox Cloud Gaming are further reducing the consumer necessity for high-end, upgradeable local hardware.

### Why it Matters
This transition marks a fundamental shift in personal computing from modular, long-term investments to "finished" appliance-style devices. Consumers will likely face shorter product lifecycles, as the inability to upgrade individual components forces users to replace entire machines to keep pace with evolving software and AI demands.
