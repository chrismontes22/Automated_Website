Defeating Satellite Spoofing with Galileo’s Encryption
============================================================

The European Space Agency successfully demonstrated the first cryptographically-secured position fix for the Galileo satellite navigation system, effectively resisting sophisticated spoofing attacks during testing in Norway.

### Key Points
- The European Space Agency (ESA) utilized its Signal Authentication System (SAS) to maintain a stable receiver lock during spoofing trials.
- Testing occurred during the annual Jammertest event in Andøya, Norway, which evaluates GNSS resilience against jamming and signal hijacking.
- The SAS technology uses encrypted spreading codes transmitted via the E6-C pilot signal to verify the authenticity of satellite data.
- Galileo satellites transmit decryption keys on a separate signal, allowing receivers to correlate data and confirm the satellite’s true pseudorange.
- This security measure addresses the inherent vulnerability of weak GNSS signals, which are susceptible to high-power interference from malicious actors.

### Why it Matters
This development marks a significant shift toward securing global navigation infrastructure against increasingly common and sophisticated signal-spoofing threats. If successfully scaled, this cryptographic authentication could become a standard requirement for critical industries relying on precise timing and location data.
