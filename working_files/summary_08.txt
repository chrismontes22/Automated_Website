AI Has Turned Software Security Into a Race You Can’t Afford to Lose
============================================================

The rapid adoption of AI in software development has created a critical security vulnerability, as automated tools now allow attackers to exploit code faster than human teams can verify.

### Key Points
- Nation-state actors are using AI agents to identify vulnerabilities and execute exploits, with Anthropic reporting AI performing up to 90% of the work in recent breaches.
- CrowdStrike data shows the average time for an intruder to move through a network dropped to 29 minutes, with some attacks occurring in under 30 seconds.
- AI-enabled cyberattacks increased by 89% in one year, and 42% of exploited vulnerabilities were utilized before public disclosure or patches were available.
- Research indicates that reliance on AI for coding has led to less stable releases and a decline in essential code maintenance and refactoring.
- Experts recommend "AI-Unified Release Assurance," a discipline requiring continuous verification that software matches business intent and remains secure against active threats.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward AI-driven development has turned speed into a baseline requirement rather than a competitive advantage, leaving companies exposed to automated, high-velocity threats. Organizations must now prioritize rigorous, automated verification processes to ensure that rapid software releases do not inadvertently provide attackers with an easy entry point.
