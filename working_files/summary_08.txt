Meta CTO says employees should use AI productivity gains to do more work — not take more time off
============================================================

Meta Chief Technology Officer Andrew Bosworth rejected employee requests to use AI-driven productivity gains for additional vacation time, emphasizing that extra capacity should be redirected toward product development.

### Key Points
- Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth dismissed staff requests to revive "Meta Days," a former holiday program, during an internal Q&A session in early July.
- Bosworth argued that increased efficiency from AI should be used to build more features for the company's billions of daily users.
- The company currently provides employees with four weeks of vacation annually, supplemented by two flexible "choice days."
- Bosworth criticized the inquiry as a poor career strategy, though he later apologized for his harsh tone toward the employee.
- The exchange highlights a growing industry debate over whether AI-driven productivity should lead to reduced working hours or increased output.

### Why it Matters
This exchange underscores a fundamental tension in the tech industry regarding how companies will distribute the efficiency gains generated by artificial intelligence. While some advocates suggest AI could lead to shorter work weeks, leadership at major firms like Meta appear focused on leveraging these tools to accelerate product development and maintain competitive output.
