Crypto for Advisors: The CLARITY Act failed, but the rules came anyway
============================================================

Following the failure of the CLARITY Act in the U.S. Senate, financial regulators are independently establishing new frameworks to govern digital assets and tokenized securities for market participants.

### Key Points
- The SEC issued an "Innovation Exemption" allowing specific venues to trade tokenized U.S.-listed stocks onchain using automated market makers.
- The CFTC is providing regulatory relief to software providers and updating guidance regarding blockchain-based recordkeeping and tokenized investments.
- SEC exemptions for tokenized stocks require tokens to carry full shareholder rights, including voting and dividends, rather than just synthetic exposure.
- The SEC's new market test is limited to five years, with trading volume caps and no margin allowed for participants.
- Major financial institutions and firms like Stripe, Circle, and Robinhood are continuing to develop blockchain-based infrastructure despite the lack of comprehensive federal legislation.

### Why it Matters
Regulatory actions are currently filling the void left by stalled congressional legislation, providing a temporary pathway for firms to integrate blockchain technology into financial services. While these measures offer immediate operational clarity, they lack the long-term legal certainty of federal law, which remains a critical factor for institutions committing significant capital to digital infrastructure.
