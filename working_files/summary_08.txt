How the Military Will Hack the Election
============================================================

Task Force Raider, a secretive military unit based in San Antonio, is conducting covert cyber operations to influence public discourse under the guise of protecting U.S. election security.

### Key Points
- Task Force Raider operates from a secure facility in San Antonio, Texas, under the oversight of Cyber Command’s Joint Force Headquarters.
- The unit utilizes offensive cyber capabilities, including cellular blackouts and malware analysis, to disrupt foreign and domestic information systems.
- Documents reveal the task force has deployed over 56,000 psychological operations messages and millions of text and voice communications to influence foreign audiences.
- Military personnel engage in "persona operations," creating fake online identities to monitor and interact with social media users.
- The mission prioritizes "sensitive activities" that protect the government from embarrassment, raising concerns about the regulation of legitimate public debate.

### Why it Matters
The expansion of military cyber operations into the domestic political sphere challenges long-standing prohibitions against federal involvement in elections and raises significant First Amendment concerns. By blurring the lines between foreign influence defense and the regulation of public speech, these activities risk establishing a precedent for government control over digital discourse.
