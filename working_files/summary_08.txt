Calgary’s quantum defence bub signals secure communications in Canada
============================================================

Canada has launched the $20.3 million Quantum Defence Innovation Secure Hub in Calgary to accelerate the development of quantum-secure communications and technologies for national defence applications.

### Key Points
- The federal government is providing $20.3 million in funding over two years to support the new Calgary-based facility.
- Operated under the BOREALIS framework, the hub is led by a University of Calgary consortium to bridge academic research and military deployment.
- Research priorities include quantum-secure communications, quantum sensing for GPS-independent navigation, quantum algorithms, and hardware assurance.
- This facility is the third Defence Innovation Secure Hub (DISH) established in Canada, following previous initiatives for maritime and uncrewed systems.
- The project aims to protect government and military infrastructure against future "harvest now, decrypt later" cyber threats posed by quantum computing.

### Why it Matters
This initiative addresses the urgent need to secure national communications against future quantum-enabled cyberattacks that could render current encryption methods obsolete. By fostering domestic expertise, Canada aims to maintain technological sovereignty and develop dual-use innovations that could eventually benefit civilian sectors like finance and healthcare.
