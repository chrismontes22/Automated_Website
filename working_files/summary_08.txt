HDMI carries far more than a picture, and these 5 features prove it
============================================================

HDMI cables provide more than just video transmission by utilizing hidden data channels to manage audio, device control, network connectivity, display configuration, and content protection security protocols.

### Key Points
- Audio Return Channel (ARC) and eARC allow TVs to send audio back to soundbars, supporting up to 37 Mbps for lossless formats like Dolby Atmos.
- Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) enables a single remote to manage up to 15 connected devices, though manufacturers often market it under proprietary brand names.
- HDMI Ethernet Channel (HEC) provides a 100 Mbit/s network connection between devices, though it has seen limited adoption compared to other HDMI features.
- Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) allows source devices to automatically detect a display's resolution, refresh rate, and color capabilities during the initial handshake.
- High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) enforces encryption and authentication to prevent the unauthorized interception of protected 4K and HDR media content.

### Why it Matters
Understanding these background protocols explains why HDMI simplifies home entertainment setups while occasionally causing complex connectivity or "handshake" errors. These integrated features reduce cable clutter by consolidating power, network, and control signals into a single, standardized interface.
