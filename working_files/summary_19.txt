That Weird 7-Pin S-Video Port on Old Laptops
============================================================

Early 2000s laptops frequently featured a proprietary 7-pin S-video port that offered composite video and digital audio capabilities, though manufacturers often implemented inconsistent pinout standards across different hardware models.

### Key Points
- The 7-pin mini-DIN connector, often labeled S-video+, provided composite video and S/PDIF digital audio alongside standard 4-pin S-video signals.
- Most manufacturers, including Dell, utilized a consistent pinout configuration to allow for expanded connectivity via specialized adapters.
- Apple’s PowerBook G4 deviated from the industry standard by omitting audio pins and reassigning the composite signal to pin 5.
- The lack of universal pinout standards during this era created significant compatibility challenges for users attempting to utilize the port's full functionality.

### Why it Matters
This historical hardware inconsistency highlights the ongoing industry struggle to balance port versatility with standardized user expectations. While modern USB-C ports have replaced these legacy connectors, they introduce similar confusion regarding which data and power standards are supported by a specific physical interface.
