The Metric Is Not The Mission: When The Reckoning Reaches The Courts
============================================================

The ninth installment of the "Metric Is Not the Mission" series examines how litigation and judicial accountability may force Big Tech to reform beyond traditional legislative efforts.

### Key Points
- The series argues that Big Tech companies rarely change profitable business models unless faced with significant legal, financial, or reputational consequences.
- Unlike prospective legislation, court proceedings focus on retrospective evidence, internal corporate knowledge, and specific design decisions regarding user harm.
- Child safety is identified as the primary catalyst for this shift, as it forces platforms to defend specific features against claims of foreseeable harm.
- The author suggests that moving accountability into the courts shifts the burden of explanation away from lobbyists and toward internal corporate documentation.
- Broader societal interests, including privacy, autonomy, and social cohesion, are presented as the next frontiers for potential legal challenges against platform power.

### Why it Matters
This shift toward litigation represents a fundamental change in how digital platforms are held accountable for their business practices. By moving beyond lobbying and regulatory compliance, courts can force companies to address the specific consequences of their design choices and internal knowledge.
