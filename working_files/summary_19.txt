Microsoft’s new Copilot signals a shift in their AI strategy for Windows & enterprise. Are we past the gimmick phase?
============================================================

Microsoft has unveiled a major evolution for Copilot, transforming it into a unified workspace within Microsoft 365 that integrates documents, tasks, and automation across the entire productivity suite.

### Key Points
- Microsoft introduced three new Copilot experiences: Home for dashboard-style task management, Code for non-developer app creation, and Autopilot for autonomous project management.
- The platform is powered by Microsoft IQ, a unified intelligence layer that connects data from Dynamics 365, Power Platform, and other business systems.
- Copilot Home features a "Today" command center to track missed emails, meetings, and upcoming tasks, entering private preview in October.
- The new "Code" feature allows users to build lightweight apps and workflows using plain language, with integration into the GitHub Copilot ecosystem.
- Microsoft is implementing "FinOps for AI" to help organizations manage costs through flexible billing options and automated task optimization.

### Why it Matters
This update signals a strategic shift where Microsoft positions Copilot as the central operating layer for all professional productivity rather than just an add-on tool. By embedding AI directly into the workflow, the company aims to reduce app-switching and automate routine logistics, fundamentally changing how businesses manage knowledge and human expertise.
