Intel says PC market is ‘a tale of two kingdoms’ with mainstream ‘taking a beating’ — VP suggests a split between mainstream and enthusiast sockets across the industry
============================================================

Intel executive Robert Hallock anticipates a permanent industry shift toward separate mainstream and enthusiast processor sockets to address rising manufacturing costs and a starkly divided consumer PC market.

### Key Points
- Intel vice president Robert Hallock describes the current PC market as a "tale of two kingdoms," split between budget-constrained buyers and high-end enthusiasts.
- Rising supply chain and component costs are forcing manufacturers to pass expenses to consumers, leading to higher average selling prices.
- Intel is reportedly planning a socket transition, potentially moving to LGA 1700 for mainstream users and LGA 1954 for upcoming enthusiast-grade Nova Lake processors.
- Companies are increasingly using short-term pricing concessions, such as deep discounts on Arrow Lake Refresh chips, to maintain sales volume during economic volatility.
- Industry leaders suggest that maintaining a unified platform is becoming unsustainable, necessitating product stack concessions to keep hardware affordable for the mainstream segment.

### Why it Matters
This strategic shift signals a long-term departure from universal motherboard compatibility, which could increase the total cost of ownership for PC builders and limit future upgrade paths. By segmenting the market, Intel and other hardware manufacturers aim to protect profit margins on premium products while attempting to prevent the entry-level desktop market from becoming entirely unaffordable.
