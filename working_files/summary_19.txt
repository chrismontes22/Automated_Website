Homelabs After VMware: Containers Are Growing, but VMs Are Not Going Away
============================================================

Modern homelab architecture requires a strategic balance between containers and virtual machines, as each technology serves distinct operational needs rather than acting as a universal replacement.

### Key Points
- Containers like Docker Compose offer efficient, lightweight deployment for single-application services such as media servers or password managers.
- Virtual machines remain essential for workloads requiring kernel isolation, different operating systems, or testing enterprise-grade platforms like VMware vSphere.
- Proxmox VE serves as a versatile foundation by managing both Linux containers and virtual machines within a single, unified environment.
- Lightweight Kubernetes distributions like k3s are ideal for learning platform engineering but may introduce unnecessary complexity for simple, single-node setups.
- Broadcom continues to offer a free version of ESXi 8.0 for home users, maintaining its relevance for those studying professional virtualization stacks.
- Effective data protection requires dedicated backup procedures for persistent volumes, as filesystem snapshots do not replace comprehensive disaster recovery strategies.

### Why it Matters
Choosing the right deployment layer prevents overengineering and ensures that homelab infrastructure aligns with specific learning or service goals. By matching the tool to the objective, users can maintain stable, manageable environments that leverage the unique strengths of both containerization and virtualization.
