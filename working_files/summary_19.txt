Weekend reads: Mega-journal on hold at PubMed; 1,400 retractions in one day; ‘I Used AI to Fake a Scientific Paper’
============================================================

Retraction Watch reports a busy week of academic integrity challenges, including multiple journal retractions, legal developments for Elsevier, and ongoing concerns regarding AI-generated content in scientific research.

### Key Points
- A federal judge dismissed a $1 billion defamation lawsuit filed against the academic publisher Elsevier.
- The Retraction Watch Database now tracks over 68,000 retractions, while the Hijacked Journal Checker has surpassed 450 entries.
- Springer Nature and Google DeepMind have partnered to develop an AI-powered assistant to aid in the peer review process.
- The Dartmouth provost resigned following allegations that his academic work was produced using artificial intelligence.
- Scientific Reports is currently under reevaluation by the National Library of Medicine, resulting in a temporary hold within the PubMed database.

### Why it Matters
These developments highlight the growing tension between rapid technological advancement and the maintenance of rigorous standards in scholarly publishing. As AI tools become more prevalent, the scientific community faces increased pressure to address issues of data integrity, authorship transparency, and editorial accountability.
