Federal judge strikes down Utah's controversial VPN age verification rules
============================================================

U.S. District Judge David Barlow issued a preliminary injunction blocking Utah’s SB 73, a law that attempted to restrict VPN usage for age verification on adult content websites.

### Key Points
- Judge David Barlow ruled that Utah’s SB 73 likely violates the U.S. Constitution by targeting VPNs for age verification.
- The lawsuit was initiated by Aylo, the parent company of Pornhub, to challenge the state's aggressive digital mandates.
- The court determined that "perfect geolocation" is technically impossible, making the state's requirements for platforms to identify user locations unfeasible.
- Cybersecurity experts and companies like Proton and Surfshark argued that VPNs are essential security tools rather than mere loopholes for bypassing content restrictions.
- The injunction temporarily halts the enforcement of the law's VPN-specific provisions while the broader legal challenge continues.

### Why it Matters
This ruling establishes a significant legal precedent by affirming that state governments cannot force private companies to undermine essential cybersecurity infrastructure to enforce local regulations. It serves as a warning to other states that legislative attempts to restrict digital privacy tools may face constitutional challenges regarding free speech and user security.
