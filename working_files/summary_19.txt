Former FTX Europe chief targets U.S. tokenized stock trading with new SEC exemption
============================================================

Nasdaq-listed Perpetuals.com, led by former FTX Europe executive Patrick Gruhn, is seeking to launch a U.S. tokenized stock marketplace under a new SEC regulatory innovation exemption.

### Key Points
- Perpetuals.com filed a notice to operate a tokenized securities venue using the SEC’s five-year innovation exemption for public blockchain trading.
- The platform plans to use permissioned automated market maker liquidity pools on the Kronos X layer-2 blockchain to trade stocks against USDC.
- This initiative follows a similar proposal by the OKX–ICE joint venture to offer round-the-clock trading for over 60 tokenized U.S. stocks.
- Trading could potentially commence in mid-November, pending the completion of necessary compliance, security reviews, and technical preparations.
- CEO Patrick Gruhn previously co-founded Digital Assets AG, which developed early stock-linked tokens for exchanges like Binance and FTX before the latter's collapse.

### Why it Matters
This development signals a significant shift toward integrating traditional equity markets with blockchain infrastructure under formal U.S. regulatory oversight. By utilizing the SEC's new exemption, these firms aim to provide investors with tokenized shares that maintain full shareholder rights, potentially bridging the gap between decentralized finance and regulated securities trading.
