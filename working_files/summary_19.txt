OpenAI sociopath Sam Altman says it's "terrible" that your PC, Xbox, etc. have RAM that could be used for his AI
============================================================

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is facing criticism for suggesting that consumer-owned computing hardware is inefficient, advocating instead for a centralized cloud-based model for global AI resource allocation.

### Key Points
- Sam Altman argued on the Cheeky Pint podcast that personal computers waste limited DRAM supplies by remaining underutilized.
- The OpenAI CEO envisions a future where intelligence functions as a metered utility, similar to electricity or water services.
- Critics argue that the current supply constraints on hardware are driven by massive, unproven investments in generative AI infrastructure.
- Microsoft recently cited RAM supply issues as a factor impacting its Xbox financial performance during the current AI hardware boom.
- Industry observers warn that this shift could lead to a monopoly where tech giants control compute access through subscription or ad-based models.

### Why it Matters
This debate highlights a growing tension between the tech industry's push for centralized cloud-based AI and the consumer preference for local hardware ownership. If compute becomes a metered utility, it could fundamentally change how users access technology and increase long-term reliance on major cloud providers.
