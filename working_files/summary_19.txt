ChatGPT just blocked its most controversial writing feature
============================================================

OpenAI has updated ChatGPT to block user requests for generating text that mimics the specific writing styles of famous authors, following ongoing legal pressure regarding copyright and intellectual property.

### Key Points
- ChatGPT now rejects prompts asking it to write in the style of specific living or deceased authors.
- The restriction follows high-profile copyright lawsuits against AI companies from organizations like the Authors Guild.
- Anthropic recently reached a record $1.5 billion settlement with authors over claims of unauthorized training data usage.
- Competitor chatbots, including Google Gemini and Anthropic’s Claude, currently continue to fulfill requests for author-specific stylistic mimicry.
- Users can bypass the new restriction by describing an author's stylistic traits rather than naming the individual writer.

### Why it Matters
This shift reflects the growing legal and ethical pressure on AI developers to protect copyrighted creative works from being replicated by generative models. As litigation continues, companies are increasingly adjusting their software to mitigate liability and avoid further accusations of profiting from protected literary content.
