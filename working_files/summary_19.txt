Big Tech can cut methane … cheaply!
============================================================

As artificial intelligence data centers drive a massive surge in natural gas demand, major technology companies face increasing pressure to mandate ultra-low methane emissions from their energy suppliers.

### Key Points
- Data center power demand is projected to skyrocket from 7 gigawatts in 2024 to 189 gigawatts, significantly increasing reliance on natural gas.
- Methane is 80 times more potent than carbon dioxide at trapping heat, making its reduction the fastest method to limit near-term global temperature increases.
- Industry experts state that half of all methane leaks can be eliminated at no net cost by replacing outdated equipment and stopping gas venting.
- Major tech firms, including Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, xAI, and OpenAI, have yet to commit to sourcing ultra-low methane “differentiated gas” for their operations.
- New satellite technology is currently exposing large-scale methane leaks, particularly within the Permian gas basin in Texas.

### Why it Matters
The rapid expansion of AI infrastructure threatens to accelerate climate change unless technology giants leverage their massive purchasing power to force cleaner energy standards. By requiring utilities to provide ultra-low methane gas, these companies could effectively decarbonize a significant portion of the U.S. energy sector at a minimal cost.
