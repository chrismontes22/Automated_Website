‘California Never?’ Utopian city faces setbacks from industrial flight
============================================================

The California Forever development project faces significant uncertainty after losing its primary industrial investor, Saronic, which relocated a $3.2 billion autonomous shipbuilding facility to Texas due to local opposition.

### Key Points
- The California Forever project aims to build a new city for 400,000 residents in Solano County, featuring 170,000 homes and 500,000 projected jobs.
- Defense technology firm Saronic withdrew its $3.2 billion autonomous shipyard project from the development, citing regulatory delays and local political resistance.
- California has lost over 610,000 industrial jobs since 2000, with many manufacturing firms choosing states like Texas, Arizona, and Tennessee for lower costs and fewer regulations.
- While California remains a hub for defense startup capital, analysts note that companies frequently move production facilities out of state once products reach the manufacturing stage.

### Why it Matters
The collapse of the Saronic deal highlights California's ongoing struggle to retain industrial manufacturing despite its status as a global leader in technology and innovation. This trend suggests that without significant policy shifts to streamline development, the state risks losing the high-skilled blue-collar jobs necessary to diversify its economy beyond software and AI.
