"This was not a Creative Assembly developed game": Total War boss points the finger at external partner for Shogun 2 Complete Edition disaster
============================================================

Creative Assembly and Sega have removed the Total War: Shogun 2 Complete Edition from Steam following widespread player backlash regarding technical issues and the inclusion of AI-generated art.

### Key Points
- Creative Assembly vice president Roger Collum apologized for the release, citing a lack of internal oversight regarding the unnamed external development partner.
- The studio confirmed it prohibits the use of generative AI for creating game assets and art, though it utilizes such tools for debugging and prototyping.
- Sega and Creative Assembly have restored the original version of Total War: Shogun 2 to Steam while investigating the failed Complete Edition launch.
- Collum urged the community to refrain from harassing individual developers, warning that abuse could lead to reduced transparency from the studio in the future.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing tension between game publishers and players regarding quality control in outsourced development and the ethical use of generative AI. The studio's public response underscores the reputational risks companies face when external projects fail to meet the standards of their core franchises.
