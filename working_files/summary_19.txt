It's so hard to finish an idea that is not yours (and suggested by AI)
============================================================

Integrating artificial intelligence into personal note-taking systems like Obsidian risks diluting original insights and diminishing the cognitive benefits of the deliberate, human-led process of building a "second brain."

### Key Points
- Avoid cluttering personal vaults with AI-generated summaries, which can obscure original thoughts and create "AI slop" that hinders long-term knowledge retrieval.
- Use AI tools sparingly for specific tasks like finding related notes or performing vector searches, rather than for automated tagging or organizational connections.
- Maintain a clear distinction between human-authored content and AI-generated text by using quotes or separate folders, such as a dedicated resource folder under the PARA method.
- Prioritize manual note-taking and link creation, as the process of connecting ideas is essential for deep learning and developing unique, long-term insights.
- Leverage local plugins like Obsidian Smart Connections or Omnisearch to maintain privacy and efficiency without relying on external AI models for core thinking.

### Why it Matters
Over-reliance on AI to generate or organize personal knowledge bases threatens to replace the critical thinking process that defines a true "second brain." By prioritizing human-curated content, users ensure their notes remain a valuable, authentic repository of personal conviction rather than a collection of generic, machine-generated data.
