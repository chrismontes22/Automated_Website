North Korean IT Workers Used Stolen US Identities to Earn $3 Million
============================================================

The U.S. Department of Justice has charged 14 individuals and seized 187 devices in a crackdown on a North Korean scheme placing fake IT workers into American companies.

### Key Points
- Operation Lonestar Roundup involved FBI searches of over 40 Texas residences used as "laptop farms" to facilitate remote work for North Korean intelligence agents.
- The scheme targeted more than 70 U.S. companies across 17 states, including firms in the healthcare, cybersecurity, and manufacturing sectors.
- North Korean operatives generated approximately $3 million in illicit revenue, which was funneled back to sanctioned entities controlled by the regime.
- The threat group, tracked by CrowdStrike as Famous Chollima, increasingly recruits U.S. facilitators to bypass security measures and subcontract tasks to third-party foreign nationals.
- The DOJ has released specific email indicators and hiring red flags to help employers identify and mitigate the risk of North Korean insider threats.

### Why it Matters
This operation highlights a significant national security risk where foreign intelligence agents exploit remote hiring practices to infiltrate Western corporate networks and siphon funds. Companies must now implement more rigorous identity verification and background screening to prevent state-sponsored actors from gaining persistent access to sensitive intellectual property and internal systems.
