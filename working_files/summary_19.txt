Conti Hacker Who Built Malware and Attacked Victims Gets Four-Year Sentence
============================================================

A US federal court sentenced Ukrainian national Oleksii Lytvynenko to four years in prison for his role in the Conti ransomware operation, which extorted over $150 million globally.

### Key Points
- Oleksii Lytvynenko pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud for his active participation in the Conti ransomware group between 2021 and 2022.
- The Conti operation targeted organizations across 47 U.S. states and 31 foreign countries, resulting in victim payouts exceeding $150 million.
- Lytvynenko developed malware "loaders" and personally held stolen data from at least 12 victimized companies in the U.S. and abroad.
- Irish authorities arrested Lytvynenko in July 2023 at his home in County Cork, where they discovered active cybercrime tools on his computer.
- The defendant was extradited to the United States in October 2025 to face charges following a multi-year investigation by federal authorities.

### Why it Matters
This sentencing highlights the ongoing efforts of U.S. law enforcement to prosecute international cybercriminals who facilitate large-scale ransomware attacks. It serves as a reminder that even after major criminal syndicates like Conti dissolve, participants remain targets for global legal accountability.
