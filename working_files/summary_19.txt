Why WebKit Did It; Apple’s Car In The Ads Race
============================================================

Apple is intensifying its privacy restrictions on ad tech vendors following recent research linking extended identifiers and malicious ad-based exploits to significant security risks for iPhone users.

### Key Points
- A September study by the New Jersey Institute of Technology, DeepSee.io, and Adalytics found that extended identifiers enable persistent user-level tracking without cookies.
- Cybersecurity reports from Report URI and iVerify revealed that attackers are using expired domains and ad tags to steal keychain passwords from iPhones.
- Confiant and CitizenLab documented zero-click spyware, allegedly linked to Candiru, delivered via targeted ads to spy on journalists.
- Apple TV reported double-digit viewership growth for its inaugural Formula 1 season, outperforming US connected TV benchmarks for brand lift.
- Retailers including Ulta, Lowe’s, and Home Depot are integrating data from Flock’s vehicle-tracking camera network into their retail media and loss-prevention strategies.

### Why it Matters
These developments highlight a growing tension between the ad tech industry's desire for granular tracking and Apple's aggressive push toward platform-level privacy and security. As brands navigate these restrictions, the success of Apple’s "balanced" advertising model suggests that scarcity and high-quality placements may become more valuable than traditional, high-volume ad strategies.
