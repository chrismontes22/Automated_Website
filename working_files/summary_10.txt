McDonald’s and Walmart Are Part of the Widening Fray Over Surveillance Pricing
============================================================

Walmart and McDonald’s are facing public and regulatory scrutiny over concerns that artificial intelligence could enable surveillance pricing, a practice where companies adjust costs based on individual consumer data.

### Key Points
- Walmart CEO John Furner publicly denied plans to use AI for personalized pricing following backlash over recent patent filings for digital shelf technology.
- McDonald’s has faced media criticism regarding its implementation of AI systems that could potentially lead to variable pricing across different restaurant locations.
- New Jersey passed legislation effective in 2027 that prohibits retailers from using personal data to determine individual product pricing.
- The Federal Trade Commission is currently soliciting public comments on surveillance pricing policies to address concerns regarding consumer transparency and fairness.
- Consumer advocacy groups warn that algorithmic pricing may disproportionately impact low-income families by identifying and exploiting their specific purchasing needs.

### Why it Matters
The rise of AI-driven pricing models creates significant tension between corporate profit strategies and consumer privacy rights. As regulators and lawmakers increase oversight, companies must navigate the fine line between standard market-based dynamic pricing and potentially discriminatory practices that could trigger legal action.
