I’m quitting Kindle, and Amazon’s new DRM update proves why you should too
============================================================

Amazon’s latest Kindle software update has sparked backlash by closing a popular Libby library-loan loophole and tightening DRM restrictions, prompting users to reconsider their loyalty to the platform.

### Key Points
- Amazon implemented an on-device digital timer that automatically locks Libby library books once the loan period expires, even in airplane mode.
- New DRM protocols restrict users from managing or backing up purchased ebooks using third-party software like Calibre.
- The update affects older hardware, including the Kindle Oasis, limiting the ability of users to preserve or archive their digital libraries.
- Critics argue Amazon prioritizes subscription services like Kindle Unlimited and publisher contracts over user-requested features like custom wallpapers and better file organization.
- Competitors such as Kobo and Boox are gaining traction by offering more flexible, open-format support and greater control over hardware and software.

### Why it Matters
These changes highlight the growing tension between corporate digital rights management and the consumer expectation of true ownership over purchased media. As Amazon continues to restrict device functionality through silent updates, users are increasingly shifting toward open-ecosystem alternatives that offer more autonomy and long-term control over their digital libraries.
