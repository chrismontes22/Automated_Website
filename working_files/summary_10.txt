Will Linux phones become a viable alternative to the Android/iOS duopoly?
============================================================

Linux phones are poised to become a viable alternative to the Android and iOS duopoly as hardware performance improves and software development matures through unified desktop-mobile stacks.

### Key Points
- The Rockchip RK3588S processor provides sufficient performance for a functional Linux smartphone, overcoming previous limitations of low-power embedded chips.
- Mobile interfaces like Phosh and Plasma Mobile have gained stability and widespread adoption by leveraging existing desktop software stacks like GNOME and KDE.
- Qualcomm’s "Dragonwing" IoT processors, such as the QCM6490, offer up to 15 years of support, enabling longer device lifespans than standard Android hardware.
- Collabora is actively upstreaming mainline Linux support for the RK3588S, reducing reliance on proprietary firmware blobs and improving long-term maintainability.
- The industry shift toward locking bootloaders on Android devices is driving privacy-conscious users to seek open-source alternatives that offer greater control.

### Why it Matters
The emergence of more powerful, long-supported hardware and standardized software interfaces creates a sustainable path for a niche Linux phone market. This shift offers users a viable escape from the surveillance-heavy business models and planned obsolescence currently dominating the mainstream mobile industry.
