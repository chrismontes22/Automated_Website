Hackers steal children's data after company used 'Password1' to protect old account
============================================================

The Information Commissioner’s Office has reprimanded Ogi Networks Limited and Data Cymru after a weak password led to a cyber-attack exposing personal data of 3,300 children.

### Key Points
- Hackers accessed 42GB of data on November 1, 2024, by exploiting a 12-year-old test account protected by the password "Password1."
- The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) found both organizations breached UK GDPR rules by failing to audit dormant accounts or implement effective security measures.
- The breach compromised personal information related to two projects supporting children and families, though no evidence suggests the data was further misused.
- Ogi Networks Limited and Data Cymru have since implemented multi-factor authentication and established a formal, legally compliant data processing contract.
- The ICO investigation highlighted a critical lack of clear responsibility between the IT service provider and the client regarding system security and account management.

### Why it Matters
This incident underscores the severe risks posed by poor password hygiene and the failure to decommission dormant accounts in outsourced IT environments. It serves as a critical reminder for organizations to maintain clear contractual accountability and rigorous security audits to protect sensitive personal data.
