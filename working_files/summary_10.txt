AI Slop Is Ruining Cute Animals on the Internet
============================================================

The proliferation of AI-generated imagery is eroding public trust in animal-related content, complicating legitimate rescue efforts and enabling new forms of scams targeting pet owners and nonprofits.

### Key Points
- Scammers are using AI-generated photos of missing pets to extort money from desperate owners, as seen in the ongoing search for a cat named Brooklyn.
- The nonprofit We Animals faces increasing skepticism, with viewers falsely accusing their authentic photojournalism of being AI-generated, forcing the group to adopt new verification technologies.
- Experts like philosopher Oscar Horta warn that synthetic animal content can distort public perception of wildlife rescue and hinder critical fundraising for animal welfare.
- New regulations in California and the EU now mandate that major AI image generators embed invisible tags to identify synthetic content for social media platforms.
- Researchers at New York University are advocating for AI developers to implement guidelines that discourage the creation of misleading or harmful animal-related imagery.

### Why it Matters
The rise of AI-generated "slop" threatens the credibility of animal welfare organizations that rely on authentic imagery to secure donations and public support. As synthetic content becomes harder to distinguish from reality, the resulting skepticism may undermine genuine efforts to address animal suffering and environmental crises.
