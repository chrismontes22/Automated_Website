Meta lawsuits: Is social media facing a global legal reckoning?
============================================================

A major multistate trial begins in California this week, where Meta faces allegations that its social media platforms are designed to be addictive and harmful to young users.

### Key Points
- Twenty-nine US states are suing Meta, alleging the company intentionally designed platforms to encourage addictive behavior and failed to protect adolescent users.
- The trial, which is expected to last seven weeks, could result in up to $1.4 trillion in damages if the plaintiffs are successful.
- Attorneys general are seeking court-ordered changes, including stricter parental verification, removal of image filters, and modifications to "dopamine-manipulating" algorithms.
- Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and the head of Instagram are expected to testify during the proceedings.
- This case follows a recent New Mexico ruling that ordered Meta to pay $567 million for failing to protect children on its platforms.

### Why it Matters
This trial represents a significant legal challenge that could force Meta to fundamentally alter its core product design and algorithmic recommendation systems. A ruling against the company may set a global precedent, influencing how tech giants operate and manage user safety in jurisdictions worldwide.
