Treasury crackdown exposes crypto's role in $2 million Hamas fundraising network
============================================================

The U.S. Treasury Department has sanctioned individuals and charities for operating a fundraising network that funneled over $2 million to Hamas using cryptocurrency and fraudulent humanitarian donations.

### Key Points
- The Treasury sanctioned a Hamas military-wing member, two France-based individuals, and two charities for facilitating the illicit funding network.
- The operation raised more than $2 million between 2020 and 2026, with $1.5 million collected following the October 7, 2023, attack on Israel.
- Funds were transferred to Gaza-based Hamas commander Saleem Abdallah Saleem al-Zaq, with hundreds of thousands of dollars moved via cryptocurrency.
- Sanctions freeze all assets held under U.S. jurisdiction and prohibit American citizens and businesses from engaging in transactions with the designated parties.
- Foreign financial institutions that knowingly facilitate significant transactions for these entities now face the risk of secondary U.S. sanctions.

### Why it Matters
This action highlights the ongoing efforts by U.S. authorities to disrupt the integration of digital assets into terrorist financing networks. It underscores the regulatory focus on closing loopholes in both conventional and cryptocurrency-based financial systems used by militant groups.
