North Korean WaterPlum hackers infected 30,000 devices worldwide
============================================================

A joint international advisory reports that the North Korean hacking group WaterPlum compromised 30,000 devices globally, stealing over $10.7 million in cryptocurrency to fund state weapons programs.

### Key Points
- WaterPlum targeted job seekers through the "Contagious Interview" campaign, using malicious npm packages and fake coding tests to infect devices.
- The group successfully exfiltrated funds or credentials from more than 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets across 100 countries between December 2025 and July 2026.
- Identified malware families used in the attacks include BeaverTail, InvisibleFerret, OtterCookie, OtterCandy, and StoatWaffle.
- Attackers utilized AI face-swapping software during interviews to impersonate legitimate candidates and gain access to corporate networks.
- Japanese authorities recently dismantled a North Korean "laptop farm" used to facilitate these fraudulent IT worker operations and financial transfers.
- The FBI and international partners link WaterPlum to North Korea's 313 General Bureau, which supports the regime's weapons research and production.

### Why it Matters
These attacks demonstrate how state-sponsored actors leverage recruitment platforms and remote work trends to infiltrate corporate networks and steal sensitive intellectual property. The integration of stolen identities and AI-driven deception poses a significant threat to global cybersecurity, requiring companies to implement stricter verification processes for all new hires.
