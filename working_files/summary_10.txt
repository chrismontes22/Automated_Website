The biggest flaw with the Galaxy Watch 9 has nothing to do with the watch itself
============================================================

Samsung restricts critical health-tracking features on its Galaxy Watch series to users who own a Samsung smartphone, limiting access to essential monitoring tools for many Android device owners.

### Key Points
- Samsung Health Monitor requires a Galaxy smartphone to access blood pressure, ECG, sleep apnea, and irregular heart rhythm monitoring.
- These health features are unavailable on non-Samsung Android devices, even if the user owns a compatible Galaxy Watch model.
- The exclusivity is enforced through the Galaxy App Store, which is only accessible on Samsung-branded mobile hardware.
- Competitors like Google, OnePlus, and Xiaomi allow their wearable health features to function across various Android smartphones.
- Users have resorted to unofficial, potentially insecure workarounds to bypass these software restrictions on non-Samsung phones.

### Why it Matters
This ecosystem gatekeeping forces consumers to purchase specific hardware to access health features they have already paid for in their wearable devices. It creates a significant barrier for users who prefer non-Samsung Android phones, potentially limiting their access to important medical screening tools.
