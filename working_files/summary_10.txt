Fake Interview of Software Engineer Led to Binance Mega-Heist
============================================================

Binance revealed that a sophisticated, months-long social engineering campaign targeting its software engineers resulted in a massive digital heist involving XRP, SOL, and OP cryptocurrency assets.

### Key Points
- Attackers used fake recruitment interviews and malicious code repositories to compromise the work computer of a Binance software engineer in September.
- The breach utilized zero-day malware designed to evade endpoint security tools by replacing legitimate development components with malicious versions.
- Hackers gained access to internal AI tools and stolen credentials to compromise additional employees, including Wallet DevOps and Software engineers.
- On October 29, attackers triggered malicious scripts to drain 233.46 million XRP, 970,500 SOL, and 44.2 million OP from Binance hot wallets.
- Binance has since rebuilt its wallet systems, decommissioned compromised credentials, and enhanced behavioral anomaly detection to prevent future unauthorized access.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the severe risks posed by targeted social engineering against high-privilege employees, even within organizations with established security protocols. It underscores the critical need for companies to balance developer productivity with strict controls over software execution and internal access permissions.
