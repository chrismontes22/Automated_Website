The College Students Finding New Ways to Cheat With AI
============================================================

College students are increasingly using advanced AI tools to bypass academic integrity measures, forcing universities to choose between invasive surveillance technologies and fundamental changes to their teaching methods.

### Key Points
- Students are utilizing autonomous AI agents and coding tools to automate homework and bypass platforms like WebAssign and Turnitin.
- Institutions including Princeton, Oberlin, and the University of Chicago are responding by banning technology or ending unsupervised exams.
- Universities are investing in detection software like GPTZero and Draftback, though students frequently manually edit AI outputs to evade these systems.
- Some educators, such as UCLA’s Danielle Carr, are shifting toward "reading jail" environments where students complete all work under direct supervision.
- Princeton and other schools are increasingly relying on campus network logs to track student access to generative AI platforms during exams.

### Why it Matters
The ongoing arms race between AI-assisted cheating and detection technology highlights a growing disconnect between traditional academic requirements and student engagement. If universities cannot prove the intrinsic value of their coursework, they risk losing the battle for academic integrity regardless of how much they invest in surveillance.
