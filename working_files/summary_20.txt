UK’s Police National Legal Database Reveals Data Breach
============================================================

The Police National Legal Database has confirmed a significant data breach involving the personal information of thousands of British police officers and criminal justice professionals across the UK.

### Key Points
- The breach, identified on July 26, exposed names and work email addresses of staff from all 43 police forces in England and Wales.
- The extortion group ExfilSquad claimed responsibility for the incident, alleging they possess 135,000 records totaling 1.9GB of data.
- Compromised information also includes contact details for individuals who previously submitted inquiries to the Ask the Police service.
- PNLD officials stated that no passwords, security credentials, or confidential data regarding victims, witnesses, or offenders were accessed during the attack.
- The National Crime Agency and specialist cybersecurity organizations are currently investigating the breach and the group's claims.

### Why it Matters
The exposure of professional contact details provides cybercriminals with a targeted directory to launch sophisticated spear-phishing and social engineering campaigns against law enforcement personnel. This incident highlights the ongoing vulnerability of public sector databases and reinforces the UK government's firm stance against paying extortion demands to cyber adversaries.
