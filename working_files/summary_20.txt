Report Links Pirate IPTV to Hezbollah TV, Calls for U.S. Site-Blocking
============================================================

A new report from the Digital Citizens Alliance claims that pirate IPTV services are distributing content from sanctioned terrorist organizations and state broadcasters to audiences in the U.S.

### Key Points
- The "Hostile Signals" report found 17 of 25 tested pirate IPTV services carried channels linked to groups like Hezbollah, Hamas, and the Houthis.
- Security firm risk3sixty identified specific platforms, including Lion OTT and Wish IPTV, that provide access to banned outlets like Al-Manar and Al-Masirah.
- The report argues that these piracy platforms act as a "back door" for content that the U.S. government previously removed from satellite and broadcast networks.
- Proponents of the report are using these findings to advocate for new U.S. site-blocking legislation, such as the American Copyright Protection Act.
- The study acknowledges that many of these channels remain freely accessible to U.S. viewers via the broadcasters' own websites, which operate outside American jurisdiction.

### Why it Matters
This report attempts to shift the piracy debate from intellectual property theft to national security by linking illegal streaming services to terrorist propaganda distribution. If successful, this framing could provide the political momentum necessary to pass controversial site-blocking legislation that has previously stalled in Congress.
