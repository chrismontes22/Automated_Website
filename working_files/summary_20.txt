United’s CEO Naps At Work, Limits Meetings To 4 Hours A Day And Says His Job Is To “Think Hard”
============================================================

United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby is shifting his leadership strategy by prioritizing deep thinking, employee communication, and customer experience over traditional spreadsheet-based decision-making and aggressive cost-cutting measures.

### Key Points
- Kirby emphasizes "thinking hard" over working hard, maintaining a schedule that includes eight hours of sleep, daily reading, and limited meeting times.
- The CEO now views spreadsheet-driven conclusions as "toxic," arguing that leaders must instead question the underlying assumptions of any mathematical analysis.
- Following advice from former United CEO Oscar Munoz, Kirby has changed his management style to express appreciation for employees more openly.
- United is integrating artificial intelligence to streamline operations, contributing to a planned 8% reduction in management headcount between 2023 and 2026.
- Kirby advocates for a "risk of ruin" investment philosophy, favoring strategic bets that offer significant upside without threatening the airline's long-term financial stability.
- The airline is focusing on technology and reliability to reduce the need for staff to apologize to passengers, aiming to make travel stress-free from departure.

### Why it Matters
This shift represents a significant evolution for a leader once defined by his reputation as a data-obsessed "spreadsheet guy" at US Airways and American Airlines. By prioritizing customer-facing investments and employee morale, Kirby is attempting to differentiate United in a competitive industry that often relies on aggressive cost-cutting.
