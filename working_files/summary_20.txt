Don’t Let AI Speak for the Dead
============================================================

New startups are offering "generative ghost" AI chatbots that simulate deceased loved ones, raising significant ethical concerns about the commercialization of grief and the long-term impact on memory.

### Key Points
- Startups are creating AI chatbots trained on a deceased person's emails, social media, and voice recordings to allow users to continue conversations.
- These "grief tech" services often operate on a subscription model, creating a financial incentive to keep users engaged in their grief rather than healing.
- Critics argue that outsourcing remembrance to an app risks weakening an individual's internal capacity to process loss and maintain personal memories.
- The "continuing bonds theory" suggests that healthy grieving involves integrating the deceased into one's own internal life rather than relying on external digital simulations.
- There is a risk of permanent loss if the technology company folds, changes its business model, or increases subscription prices.

### Why it Matters
The rise of grief-tech creates a fundamental conflict between the psychological necessity of processing loss and the profit-driven goals of the attention economy. By shifting the act of remembering from an internal human process to a digital transaction, these services may inadvertently hinder the natural healing process and create a dependency on corporate-controlled platforms.
