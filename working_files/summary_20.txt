The Gold Coast is done with being 'the Glitter Strip', so what's next?
============================================================

The Gold Coast is looking to Austin, Texas, as a model for economic reinvention, aiming to move beyond its "Glitter Strip" tourism reputation toward a sophisticated, creative-led economy.

### Key Points
- Brand Gold Coast is leading a strategic effort to shift the city's identity from a cheap tourism destination to a hub for business, technology, and film.
- Austin successfully transformed into "Silicon Hills" by leveraging its "Keep Austin Weird" culture to attract major tech firms like Apple, Google, and Tesla.
- The Gold Coast’s film industry currently generates $420 million annually, producing blockbusters such as *Thor*, *Aquaman*, and *Elvis*.
- Local officials aim to transition from hosting international productions to developing domestic intellectual property to ensure long-term economic sustainability.
- Gold Coast median house prices have risen over 80% since 2020, reaching $1.2 million, raising concerns about affordability and displacement similar to Austin's experience.

### Why it Matters
This transition represents a critical attempt to diversify the Gold Coast's economy and attract high-value investment beyond traditional tourism. However, the city faces the challenge of fostering growth while managing the risks of gentrification and rising living costs that often accompany rapid urban development.
