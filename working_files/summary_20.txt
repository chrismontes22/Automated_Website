Experts uncover bizarre new malware that spooks users with jump scares and screams
============================================================

Security researchers at Group-IB have identified KGB RAT, a sophisticated malware-as-a-service platform that combines traditional credential theft and crypto hijacking with unusual psychological harassment tactics for victims.

### Key Points
- KGB RAT is a Remote Access Trojan available via subscription models ranging from $150 monthly to $800 annually.
- Core features include remote desktop access, keylogging, file management, and automated cryptocurrency wallet address hijacking.
- The malware incorporates "fun settings" such as fake Blue Screen of Death (BSOD) errors, jump scares, and screamer functions.
- Developers offer a $500 network-spreading add-on to facilitate automatic propagation across compromised systems.
- The platform uses Telegram bots for infection notifications and employs advanced obfuscation to bypass security software.
- Operators market the tool as legitimate software, complete with fake customer reviews and instructional videos for users.

### Why it Matters
The emergence of KGB RAT highlights a growing trend where cybercriminals weaponize psychological distress alongside traditional data theft to maximize victim impact. By positioning malicious tools as legitimate software, developers are lowering the barrier to entry for inexperienced threat actors, increasing the overall risk to enterprise and personal network security.
