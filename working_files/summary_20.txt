Capcom data breach exposes 11.7 million account records
============================================================

Japanese video game developer Capcom confirmed a data breach involving unauthorized access to 11.7 million CAPCOM ID accounts, exposing email addresses and system login data for global users.

### Key Points
- Attackers accessed 11,774,247 records containing email addresses, account creation dates, and recent IP addresses.
- Personal details including gender, date of birth, and nicknames were exposed for 43 specific accounts.
- Capcom confirmed that no payment card information or customer passwords were compromised during the October 19 incident.
- Unauthorized parties successfully changed passwords for 196 accounts, which the company has since reset.
- The developer reported the breach to the Osaka Prefectural Police and Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission.
- Capcom has established regional support channels and online inquiry forms to assist affected players worldwide.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the persistent cybersecurity risks facing major gaming publishers as they manage massive databases of user information. While financial data remained secure, the exposure of millions of email addresses increases the risk of targeted phishing campaigns against the company's player base.
