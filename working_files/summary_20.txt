South Korea weighs fourth delay on its crypto gains tax, pushing it to 2029
============================================================

South Korea is considering a fourth postponement of its cryptocurrency gains tax, potentially delaying the implementation until 2029 to align with international data-sharing timelines from the United States.

### Key Points
- The proposed delay would push the start date from January 2027 to January 1, 2029.
- Officials fear domestic exchange users would face unfair tax burdens due to a lack of U.S. crypto trading data before 2029.
- The current tax plan includes a 20% levy on net income, plus a 2% local tax, following a 2.5 million won deduction.
- Parliament must approve a formal amendment to the law, with a final decision expected by early December.
- The delay aligns with the OECD’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, which schedules U.S. data participation for 2029.

### Why it Matters
This potential delay highlights the significant regulatory challenges governments face in tracking cross-border digital asset activity without global data-sharing synchronization. By aligning with the U.S. timeline, South Korea aims to prevent domestic investors from being disproportionately targeted while international reporting standards remain fragmented.
