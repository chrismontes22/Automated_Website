An Indiana Court of Appeals judge recently criticized a trial transcript for containing numerous errors, suggesting that improperly proofread artificial intelligence tools likely caused the significant inaccuracies.

### Key Points
- Judge Jeffrey L. Marchal identified multiple typos, misattributed statements, and incorrect names within the official court transcript.
- The court noted that motions and closing arguments were wrongly attributed to the trial judge and bailiff rather than the attorneys.
- The memorandum suggests these errors stem from a court reporter failing to verify the output of an automated speech-to-text system.
- The judge emphasized that professionals must rigorously proofread any documents generated or assisted by artificial intelligence tools.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing risks of relying on automated transcription software within the legal system where accuracy is paramount. It serves as a warning that failing to verify AI-generated content can compromise the integrity of official court records and judicial proceedings.
