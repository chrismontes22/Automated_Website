How Trump Left America Vulnerable to Cyberattacks
============================================================

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency faces significant operational challenges and declining trust from state officials as it navigates political scrutiny and rising cyber threats from Iranian-linked actors.

### Key Points
- The Trump administration reduced CISA’s budget and cut its staff by one-third, leaving the agency without a Senate-confirmed director.
- Suspected Iranian hackers recently targeted U.S. water systems in over a dozen states, including a temporary shutdown in Braham, Minnesota.
- Democratic state officials are increasingly bypassing CISA, relying instead on private contractors and local law enforcement due to concerns over political bias.
- The administration has replaced key personnel with figures focused on election integrity investigations, straining relationships with state-level election administrators.
- Acting Director Nicholas Andersen indicated that some critical support services for election officials may not resume until 2027.

### Why it Matters
The erosion of trust between CISA and state officials creates a dangerous intelligence gap as the U.S. faces heightened cyber threats from foreign adversaries. This breakdown in federal-state cooperation leaves critical infrastructure and election systems more vulnerable to disruption during a period of significant geopolitical instability.
