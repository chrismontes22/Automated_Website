South Carolina: From Slaves to Bots
============================================================

The deployment of humanoid robots at a BMW plant in South Carolina highlights growing concerns regarding the economic impact of automation and the rise of a jobless boom.

### Key Points
- BMW recently introduced humanoid robots at its manufacturing facility in Spartanburg, South Carolina, to perform manual labor tasks.
- South Carolina maintains a "right-to-work" status with only 2.7 percent union membership, limiting worker leverage during technological transitions.
- A "jobless boom" is currently affecting the S&P 500, where companies are increasing profits through AI adoption while simultaneously curtailing new hiring.
- Approximately 40 percent of political campaigns this year are addressing AI and data center expansion as major voter concerns.
- Critics argue that tech industry leaders are prioritizing automation-driven profits while remaining indifferent to the displacement of human workers.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward AI-driven automation is fundamentally altering the American labor market by decoupling corporate profitability from job creation. This trend intensifies public skepticism toward current economic structures and is increasingly shaping political discourse as voters demand accountability for the social costs of technological displacement.
