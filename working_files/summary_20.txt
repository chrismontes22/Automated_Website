Homeowner comes home to discover his native pollinator garden has been damaged, discovers neighbor's trespassing $3,800 robotic mower is the culprit, repeated attempts to stop it crossing their shared boundary fail—homeowner takes more drastic measures
============================================================

A homeowner is facing a dispute with a neighbor after installing $95 of metal edging that damaged a $3,800 robotic mower that repeatedly trespassed onto his private property.

### Key Points
- A neighbor’s robotic mower repeatedly crossed property lines, damaging a native pollinator garden despite multiple attempts to recalibrate the device's boundary settings.
- The homeowner installed metal landscaping edging entirely within his own property line to protect his garden after documenting the mower's intrusions with a doorbell camera.
- The neighbor is now demanding the homeowner cover repair costs and a rental fee for the mower, claiming the edging created an unexpected hazard.
- The homeowner is currently weighing whether to pay for repairs to maintain peace or refuse payment based on the mower's history of trespassing.

### Why it Matters
This conflict highlights the growing legal and social complexities surrounding the accountability of automated consumer technology in residential settings. It underscores the necessity of clear communication and established liability standards when autonomous devices cause property damage or interact with neighboring landscapes.
