UK Police Data Faces Long-Standing Microsoft Cloud Security Concerns
============================================================

A 2017 internal document reveals that UK police officials were warned about significant security risks and potential US government access when migrating sensitive criminal data to Microsoft Azure.

### Key Points
- A 2017 risk assessment signed by former City of London police commissioner Ian Dyson identified vulnerabilities in Microsoft Azure regarding potential US government access to sensitive UK police data.
- The data stored on Microsoft’s cloud includes criminal records, victim statements, and classified information from over 40 UK police forces.
- Experts warn that the US CLOUD Act allows American authorities to compel US-based companies to provide data stored abroad, potentially bypassing UK data sovereignty.
- Despite warnings, every UK police force now utilizes Microsoft’s cloud services, with the government spending at least £1.9 billion annually on Microsoft software.
- Security experts note that current logging systems may be insufficient to detect if a data breach by unauthorized parties has already occurred.

### Why it Matters
This situation highlights a critical tension between national data sovereignty and the reliance on global cloud infrastructure providers for essential public services. It raises urgent questions about whether government agencies can effectively secure sensitive information when legal frameworks allow foreign authorities to access data stored by multinational corporations.
