Wells, BofA, Citi back upcoming stablecoin
============================================================

Twenty-one global financial institutions, including Bank of America, Citi, and Goldman Sachs, have announced plans to launch a new company supporting a regulated U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoin by 2027.

### Key Points
- The consortium includes major banks such as Capital One, Wells Fargo, UBS, Deutsche Bank, and Santander.
- The initiative aims to launch a U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoin by early 2027, with future plans to expand into other G7 currencies like the euro.
- The project focuses on providing a secure, compliant digital money solution for wholesale, institutional, and retail cross-border payments and asset settlements.
- The new entity will be established by the end of 2024, positioning it ahead of the January 2025 effective date for the Genius Act digital-asset regulations.
- JPMorgan Chase is notably absent from the project, continuing its independent strategy involving its proprietary JPM Coin and blockchain technology.

### Why it Matters
This initiative represents a significant shift as traditional banks move to capture the growing market for digital assets and prevent the migration of commercial deposits into private stablecoins. By establishing a bank-governed alternative, these institutions aim to maintain their influence over global payment systems while leveraging blockchain technology for more efficient cross-border settlements.
