AI use leads UC Berkeley math professor to see 'no point' in grading homework
============================================================

UC Berkeley professor and Fields medalist Richard Borcherds has stopped grading student homework for accuracy, citing the widespread use of artificial intelligence tools among his mathematics pupils.

### Key Points
- Richard Borcherds now grades homework based solely on completion because AI makes verifying individual student work for correctness impractical.
- The decision follows OpenAI’s recent progress in using AI agents to address the complex Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness mathematical problem.
- UC Berkeley has tested AI detection software like Turnitin and Rumi but concluded these tools are currently inadequate for campus-wide standardization.
- The university leaves AI policy to individual faculty discretion, providing resources through the campus AI Hub to help instructors draft syllabus statements.
- Borcherds expressed uncertainty regarding the future of mathematics as a career path, noting that AI advancements are rapidly outpacing human capabilities.

### Why it Matters
This shift highlights the growing tension between traditional academic assessment and the rapid integration of generative AI in higher education. As AI tools become capable of solving advanced mathematical problems, universities face significant challenges in maintaining academic integrity and defining the future value of human expertise.
