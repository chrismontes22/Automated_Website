Unwinding the great tech power grab
============================================================

Growing movements like Neo-Luddism and Permacomputing are challenging Big Tech’s dominance by encouraging digital refusal, community-led technology development, and the restoration of democratic accountability through grassroots political organizing.

### Key Points
- The "Summer of Ludd" event in New York City highlights a cultural shift toward rejecting pervasive technology in favor of public, human-centric interaction.
- Tech companies have consolidated power by substituting genuine political organizing with "clicktivism," evading regulation, and replacing public infrastructure with private, extractive services.
- Resistance strategies include "refusing" tech, "remaking" tools through community-led projects like the School for Poetic Computation, and "rewilding" the internet via decentralized platforms.
- Cooperative governance models, such as The Drivers Cooperative and social.coop, are being implemented to give users and workers direct ownership over digital platforms.
- Experts argue that reclaiming power requires moving beyond individual tech habits to build durable, collective institutions capable of exerting political pressure.

### Why it Matters
These movements represent a critical transition from passive digital consumption to active, organized resistance against the unchecked influence of surveillance capitalism. By rebuilding civic muscles and local institutions, these efforts aim to restore democratic control over the essential technologies that currently shape modern life and governance.
