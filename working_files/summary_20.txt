Unraveling the Pentagon’s quantum problem
============================================================

The Pentagon must upgrade its high-value systems to post-quantum cryptography by 2030 to defend sensitive data against future quantum computing threats and "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks.

### Key Points
- Executive Order 14412 mandates that federal agencies implement post-quantum cryptography for high-value assets by December 31, 2030.
- Military branches must transition to digital signatures using post-quantum algorithms by 2031 to ensure long-term network security.
- Experts emphasize that creating a comprehensive inventory of all IT assets, applications, and third-party dependencies is the critical first step toward compliance.
- Rapid modernization is required to overcome shortened maintenance windows for mobile platforms like naval ships, which now often have only 45 days for upgrades.
- Achieving "cryptographic agility" is essential for maintaining mission capabilities while transitioning to new, quantum-resistant security architectures.

### Why it Matters
This transition is vital because adversaries are currently harvesting encrypted data to decrypt it once quantum technology matures, potentially exposing long-term national security secrets. Successfully meeting these deadlines requires a fundamental shift in how the military manages its IT supply chain and infrastructure modernization.
