Why AI Hasn’t Triggered Mass Layoffs — Yet
============================================================

Harvard University experts analyze the long-term labor market impact of artificial intelligence, noting that while current job disruption remains limited, significant workforce displacement may occur within decades.

### Key Points
- Harvard economists estimate AI could eventually displace 3 million workers, potentially exceeding the impact of the early 2000s "China shock" on the U.S. labor market.
- Research from Harvard Business School and Accenture indicates that while 41% of work tasks are currently automatable, only one-third of corporate AI experiments succeed.
- Experts warn that companies often fail to provide formal training, leading employees to use AI tools inefficiently rather than boosting actual productivity.
- Economists suggest that policymakers should prepare for potential labor shifts by reforming tax schemes, unemployment insurance, and worker retraining programs.
- Business leaders are encouraged to view human capital as a strategic asset, as employees possess tacit knowledge that AI cannot currently replicate.

### Why it Matters
The transition toward AI-integrated workflows presents a fundamental challenge to traditional entry-level hiring and long-term workforce development. While companies currently struggle with implementation, the eventual adoption of specialized AI agents may force a significant restructuring of the labor market that requires proactive government intervention.
