YouTuber escapes the modern AI tech market by recreating his 1996 PC
============================================================

Tech YouTuber TrashBench recreates his childhood 1996 PC build to revisit the era of early gaming, highlighting the technical challenges and simplicity of mid-nineties hardware configurations.

### Key Points
- The build features an Intel Pentium MMX 200 MHz CPU on an Epox Pronix EP-P55-BT motherboard with 32 MB of RAM.
- Graphics were handled by an S3 Trio32 card, later upgraded to a Matrox Mystique 3D for basic 3D acceleration.
- Storage relied on a noisy 2 GB Western Digital Caviar 22100 hard drive, which required manual jumper settings to manage BIOS capacity limits.
- The system utilized a Sound Blaster 16 sound card and a Quickshot QS-123 joystick for titles like Need for Speed and Microsoft Flight Simulator 98.
- Manual configuration of motherboard jumpers was required to set CPU speed and voltage, a common but risky task during that hardware generation.

### Why it Matters
This retro project highlights a growing consumer desire for the simplicity and ownership associated with early computing hardware. It reflects a broader cultural pushback against modern tech industry practices, such as telemetry, subscription models, and perceived predatory consumer exploitation.
