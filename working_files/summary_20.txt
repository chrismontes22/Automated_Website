New parents in the United States are increasingly targeted by a $60 billion baby product industry that leverages social media algorithms to exploit parental exhaustion and insecurities.

### Key Points
- The U.S. baby product market is projected to grow 40% by 2033, driven largely by online sales and premium, tech-focused items.
- Targeted advertising often begins during pregnancy, with companies using data tracking to deliver personalized ads for sleep aids, monitors, and courses.
- Many parents report feeling pressured to purchase expensive items like the $1,995 Snoo bassinet or $179 sleep courses to achieve "perfect" parenting outcomes.
- The lack of federal paid family leave in the U.S. leaves many mothers isolated and sleep-deprived, making them more susceptible to digital marketing promises.
- Several major baby websites, including Babylist, have faced legal scrutiny over allegations of sharing user data with third-party advertisers without consent.

### Why it Matters
The aggressive marketing of baby products capitalizes on the lack of community support and the intense pressure for parents to optimize their children's development. This cycle of consumption often creates unnecessary financial and emotional strain, despite expert consensus that most purchased gadgets have little impact on long-term child outcomes.
