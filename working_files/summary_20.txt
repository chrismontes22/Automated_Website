Your favorite free mobile game could be sharing your data with Russia and China, Proton warns
============================================================

A new study by Proton reveals that most popular free Android games harvest personal user data and transmit it to ad-tech companies in foreign nations, including China and Russia.

### Key Points
- Proton analyzed the top 100 free Android games, finding that most transmit sensitive personal information to foreign ad-tech firms.
- In the U.S. alone, games sharing data with Chinese entities recorded 15.8 million downloads during a single month.
- Trackers in these apps can collect device types, usage habits, payment information, and precise physical location data.
- Popular titles like Subway Surfers, Block Blast!, and Disney Solitaire were identified as containing multiple data-harvesting trackers.
- Experts recommend using VPNs with built-in tracker blockers or purchasing ad-free versions of games to mitigate privacy risks.

### Why it Matters
This report highlights the significant privacy risks inherent in the "free-to-play" business model, where user data is often the primary currency. By funneling personal information to foreign ad-tech networks, these apps create potential vulnerabilities for both individual users and national security.
