Bond yields surge to fresh two decade highs as oil prices hit $105 per barrel
============================================================

U.S. Treasury yields reached multi-decade highs on Thursday as rising oil prices and persistent inflation concerns prompted investors to anticipate further interest rate hikes from the Federal Reserve.

### Key Points
- The 30-year U.S. Treasury bond yield hit 5.47%, marking its highest level in 22 years, while the 10-year yield climbed to 5.18%.
- Average 30-year fixed mortgage rates rose to 7.37%, the highest level recorded since May 2024.
- International Brent crude closed at $106.60 per barrel, contributing to a 73% increase in diesel prices since the start of the Iran war.
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent conducted a $4 billion bond buyback program, though the intervention failed to curb the upward trajectory of long-term yields.
- Federal Reserve officials, including John Williams and Anna Paulson, signaled that additional interest rate tightening may be necessary to combat rising inflation.

### Why it Matters
The surge in bond yields and energy costs creates significant headwinds for consumer borrowing and broader economic stability. As global sovereign debt markets react to these shifts, the Federal Reserve faces increasing pressure to balance aggressive inflation control with the risk of stalling economic growth.
