Taiwan indicts 10 for smuggling US military-grade chips to China, parts routed to missile and radar programs using forged Taiwan defense institute orders
============================================================

Taiwanese prosecutors have indicted 10 individuals for allegedly smuggling restricted U.S.-made military-grade chips from Texas Instruments and Analog Devices to Chinese research units by falsifying export documentation.

### Key Points
- Prosecutors allege defendants used the name of Taiwan’s National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology to bypass U.S. export controls on high-speed military-industrial chips.
- The diverted components, which include radar and communication parts, were falsely labeled as "Made in Taiwan" to deceive customs officials and reach Chinese military end-users.
- Three primary defendant groups reportedly generated over NT$336 million in illicit profits through the scheme.
- Charges against the 10 individuals include fraud, forgery, and money laundering, with prosecutors seeking prison sentences ranging from two to 10 years.
- The U.S. Commerce Department prohibits the export of these specific analog and mixed-signal chips to China, Hong Kong, and Macau due to their potential military applications.

### Why it Matters
This case highlights significant vulnerabilities in the global supply chain for sensitive military technology and the ongoing challenge of enforcing U.S. export controls through third-party distributors. The incident risks straining trade relations between Taiwan and the United States, potentially prompting stricter legislative oversight and tighter export regulations on high-tech components.
