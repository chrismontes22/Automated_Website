Your Second Skill Was Never a Parachute
============================================================

Artificial intelligence is fundamentally shifting the labor-to-capital ratio, threatening to devalue human expertise and create a prolonged period of economic uncertainty for professionals across various industries.

### Key Points
- Historical precedents, such as the 1349 Statute of Labourers, demonstrate that governments struggle to legislate against shifting market realities and labor scarcity.
- Technology does not immediately eliminate jobs but instead reprices them, creating a dangerous gap between corporate cost-saving timelines and the decades required for human labor reallocation.
- Specialized domain expertise often acts as a "specific factor," making it difficult for displaced workers to pivot to new roles without significant loss of income or status.
- Economic multipliers mean that technology-funded job losses extend far beyond the tech sector, impacting local service economies like retail, hospitality, and property markets.
- The "paradox of thrift" suggests that individual efforts to save money during transitions can collectively worsen economic downturns by reducing aggregate demand.

### Why it Matters
The current transition driven by AI creates a significant mismatch between the speed of corporate profit capture and the slow, painful process of human career adjustment. Because this shift is occurring in a way that benefits those in power, there is little political incentive to build the necessary "bridges"—such as social safety nets or retraining infrastructure—to support workers during the decades-long crossing.
