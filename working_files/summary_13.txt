Apple Will Check Public iCloud Links for Harmful Content Starting in iOS 27.3
============================================================

Apple will begin scanning public iCloud links for harmful content in upcoming software updates for iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS, scheduled for a public release this December.

### Key Points
- Apple will monitor files and photos shared via the "Anyone with the link" option in iCloud Drive and iCloud Photos.
- The policy applies to iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, macOS 27.3, and visionOS 27.3, which are currently in developer beta.
- Content identified as harmful may be removed, and Apple reserves the right to disable associated user accounts or contact law enforcement.
- Files shared publicly are no longer end-to-end encrypted, even for users who have enabled Advanced Data Protection.
- The new scanning protocols specifically target public links rather than files shared with restricted groups or "Send a Copy" transfers.

### Why it Matters
This update marks a significant shift in Apple's privacy policy by removing end-to-end encryption for publicly shared iCloud content to facilitate automated safety checks. Users must now be aware that files accessible via public links are subject to Apple's content moderation and potential account-level enforcement actions.
