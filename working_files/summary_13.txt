Weekend reading: are bonds discounting a robot revolution?
============================================================

The rapid expansion of AI-driven corporate debt is potentially crowding out government bond markets, raising concerns about long-term fiscal stability and the future of tax-based state funding.

### Key Points
- Major tech firms, including Google, Amazon, and Meta, are issuing significant debt to fund data center expansion for AI development.
- Rising long-term government bond yields, such as the UK 30-year, reflect investor anxiety over sticky inflation and ballooning national deficits.
- US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent noted that corporate AI spending is currently "yield-agnostic," prioritizing rapid infrastructure growth over borrowing costs.
- Analysts suggest a "crowding-out" effect where capital flows into corporate AI bonds, forcing government yields higher to attract remaining investors.
- Speculation persists that AI-driven economic shifts could reduce the future tax base, complicating the ability of governments to service long-term debt.

### Why it Matters
The potential displacement of government debt by corporate AI investment signals a shift in how global markets perceive long-term economic risk. If investors begin to doubt the state's ability to tax future AI-driven productivity, the resulting volatility in bond markets could fundamentally alter borrowing costs and fiscal policy worldwide.
