I stopped wanting a $600 gaming router after these three simple tests
============================================================

Expensive gaming routers often fail to improve connection speeds or reduce ping because federal regulations and hardware limitations restrict their performance compared to standard, lower-cost home networking equipment.

### Key Points
- Federal law limits router transmission power to 4W, meaning expensive models cannot broadcast a stronger signal than budget alternatives.
- High-gain antennas focus signal direction rather than increasing speed, and extra spatial streams are useless if your devices lack the hardware to utilize them.
- Quality of Service (QoS) settings, which prioritize gaming traffic, are often available on standard routers but remain disabled by default.
- Premium router pricing primarily covers faster processors, more memory, and multi-gig ports, which are only necessary for high-speed fiber connections and many simultaneous devices.
- Users should run a bufferbloat test and check their device's spatial stream capacity before considering a hardware upgrade.

### Why it Matters
Understanding these technical limitations prevents consumers from overspending on marketing-heavy "gaming" hardware that offers no tangible performance benefits for most home setups. A simple Ethernet cable or adjusting existing router settings often resolves latency issues more effectively than purchasing a high-end device.
