Quote of the day by Julian Assange: 'If you want a vision of the future, imagine Washington-backed Google Glasses strapped onto vacant human faces — forever'
============================================================

WikiLeaks founder Julian Assange famously critiqued Google’s corporate vision in a New York Times op-ed, warning that consumer technologies like Google Glass could facilitate mass surveillance states.

### Key Points
- Julian Assange criticized the book "The New Digital Age," authored by former Google executive chairman Eric Schmidt and Jared Cohen.
- Assange compared the adoption of Google Glass to George Orwell’s dystopian vision of a boot stamping on a human face.
- The critique argued that emerging consumer gadgets threaten individual independence and personal privacy.
- Although Google Glass was a commercial failure, modern concerns regarding mass surveillance have renewed interest in Assange’s warnings.

### Why it Matters
Assange’s perspective highlights the ongoing tension between rapid technological innovation and the erosion of individual privacy rights. His warnings remain relevant as modern digital trends and consumer devices continue to raise significant questions about data collection and state surveillance.
