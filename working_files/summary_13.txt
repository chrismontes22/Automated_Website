‘I don’t remember the case number leave me alone': US cops ignore Flock camera oversight, probing nationwide surveillance network with bogus reasoning
============================================================

An investigation by the Electronic Frontier Foundation reveals that police are abusing the Flock automated license plate reader network by providing spurious justifications for tracking citizens.

### Key Points
- The Electronic Frontier Foundation found police officers using nonsensical or profane justifications, such as "LMAO" or "idiot," to search the 82,413-camera network.
- Hackers successfully compromised a single Flock camera, extracting 1.6 million images and 27,000 video clips over a 21-day period using a stolen encryption key.
- Despite documented misuse, police departments have largely avoided disciplining officers, opting instead for informal counseling or failing to meet investigation deadlines.
- Public opposition is growing, with nearly half of Americans opposing the network and cities like Columbus, Ohio, pausing their use of the technology.

### Why it Matters
The widespread misuse of the Flock system highlights significant gaps in oversight and data security for mass domestic surveillance tools. These vulnerabilities raise urgent questions regarding the balance between public safety initiatives and the protection of individual civil liberties.
