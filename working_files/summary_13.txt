Your car’s smartest features are opening doors for hackers
============================================================

Researchers from the University of Gothenburg and Chalmers University of Technology report that modern connected vehicles face increasing cybersecurity threats from external software, cloud services, and third-party applications.

### Key Points
- Researchers analyzed 413 software vulnerabilities in connected vehicles identified between January 2022 and September 2024.
- External applications, including websites and cloud services, now account for over one-third of all identified automotive security weaknesses.
- Approximately 61% of documented vulnerabilities are classified as high or critical severity, a significant increase from historical data.
- A December 2024 incident exposed the GPS locations and personal data of 800,000 Volkswagen Group vehicles due to unsecured third-party cloud credentials.
- Security flaws often originate in third-party software libraries integrated into vehicles during the manufacturing process.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward remote, software-based vulnerabilities transforms vehicle security from a simple theft-prevention issue into a critical matter of driver safety and personal privacy. As cars become increasingly connected, manufacturers must prioritize cybersecurity throughout the entire development lifecycle to prevent potential exploitation of safety-critical systems.
