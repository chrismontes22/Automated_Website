Trump administration pushes for food assistance card upgrade to fight fraud
============================================================

The Trump administration has proposed a new rule requiring states to transition to chip-enabled EBT cards to combat widespread electronic skimming and theft within the SNAP program.

### Key Points
- The USDA proposal mandates that all states adopt chip-enabled EBT cards and requires retailers to upgrade payment terminals to support tap-to-pay technology.
- Electronic skimming of SNAP benefits resulted in an estimated $470 million in losses last year, according to data from the app Propel.
- The USDA Office of Inspector General projects that SNAP theft could reach $233 million between 2025 and 2026 without improved security measures.
- Currently, fewer than one-third of U.S. states have transitioned to more secure card technology, largely due to the high production costs of chip-enabled cards.
- The USDA estimates that implementing these security upgrades will prevent at least $1.5 billion in benefit theft over the next decade.
- The public has 60 days to submit comments on the proposal, with the government considering a two-year implementation deadline for states.

### Why it Matters
This initiative aims to modernize outdated payment infrastructure to protect vulnerable households from losing essential food assistance to criminal activity. While the transition imposes significant administrative costs on states, the federal government views the investment as a necessary step to safeguard taxpayer-funded benefits from ongoing fraud.
