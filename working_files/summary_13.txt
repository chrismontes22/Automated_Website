Community banks sue OCC over trust bank charters of crypto firms
============================================================

The Independent Community Bankers of America has filed a lawsuit against the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, challenging its authority to grant national trust charters to cryptocurrency firms.

### Key Points
- The Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA) filed the lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia.
- The suit alleges the OCC exceeded its congressional mandate by allowing crypto companies to bypass standard banking requirements like FDIC insurance and capital standards.
- Plaintiffs argue that these trust charters provide crypto firms with federal credibility without requiring compliance with the Community Reinvestment Act.
- The OCC, under former leadership, has conditionally approved numerous applications from digital asset companies seeking to expand their U.S. service offerings.
- National trust charters currently prohibit firms from accepting traditional deposits or issuing commercial loans.

### Why it Matters
This legal challenge highlights a growing tension between traditional financial institutions and the expanding regulatory footprint of digital asset companies. The court's ruling could force federal regulators to tighten oversight of crypto firms or potentially revoke the pathways currently used by these companies to gain federal banking status.
