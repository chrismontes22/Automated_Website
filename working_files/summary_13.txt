Digital Sovereignty: What It Is, What It Could Be
============================================================

Digital sovereignty represents a growing global movement to reduce reliance on foreign technology infrastructure by empowering nations and individuals to maintain control over their own digital ecosystems.

### Key Points
- Digital sovereignty is defined as the capacity to control one's digital destiny, though interpretations vary significantly between governments, civil society, and indigenous groups.
- European initiatives, such as Germany’s Sovereign Tech Agency, focus on funding open-source software to reduce dependency on American cloud infrastructure and hardware.
- Global majority nations often view digital sovereignty as a means to protect data from foreign surveillance, geopolitical sanctions, and the influence of major tech corporations.
- Critics warn that some states may misuse the concept of sovereignty to justify internet censorship, surveillance, and the fragmentation of global digital access.
- The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) advocates for a model centered on user autonomy, interoperability, and end-to-end encryption rather than creating new state-controlled "walled gardens."

### Why it Matters
The push for digital sovereignty reflects a fundamental shift in how nations and individuals perceive the risks of relying on a small number of dominant, mostly U.S.-based technology providers. By prioritizing open-source infrastructure and data portability, stakeholders aim to build a more resilient internet that protects user privacy while mitigating the geopolitical vulnerabilities inherent in centralized digital systems.
