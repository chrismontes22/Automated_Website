Eight Bits of Trust; eMudhra's CA keys cracked in milliseconds
============================================================

Security researchers discovered that eight eMudhra certificate authority private keys were generated with extremely weak values, allowing anyone to easily compromise encrypted traffic across various software platforms.

### Key Points
- Eight eMudhra intermediate certificates, created in April 2025, used trivial private keys that could be brute-forced in milliseconds.
- The affected certificates include six TLS issuing CAs and two code-signing CAs, all of which were cross-signed by Sectigo.
- Major browsers like Chrome and Firefox have already blocked the compromised certificates, but non-browser TLS clients remain vulnerable.
- Many automated systems, including CI/CD pipelines, IoT devices, and command-line tools like curl, do not check revocation or Certificate Transparency logs.
- The vulnerability stems from a human error during the key generation ceremony, where a script mistakenly used weak keys instead of secure ones.

### Why it Matters
This incident exposes a critical flaw in the global trust ecosystem, as non-browser software often lacks the automated security updates required to mitigate such widespread certificate compromises. Because many embedded devices and legacy systems cannot be easily patched, they remain susceptible to traffic interception and malicious code execution until the certificates expire in 2039.
