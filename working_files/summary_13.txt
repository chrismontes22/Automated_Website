A guide to six regulatory risks shaping games in 2026
============================================================

European video game studios face an increasingly complex regulatory landscape as the EU implements new laws governing artificial intelligence, consumer protection, digital services, and child safety standards.

### Key Points
- The EU AI Act now mandates transparency for generative AI, requiring machine-readable labels for AI-generated content and clear disclosure of interactive AI systems.
- The Consumer Protection Cooperation Network (CPC) has established seven principles for virtual currencies, demanding transparent real-world pricing and the elimination of deceptive monetization practices.
- Under the Digital Services Act (DSA), games hosting user-generated content are classified as online platforms, requiring robust safety measures and reporting tools for minors.
- National gambling laws remain fragmented across Europe, with varying restrictions on loot boxes and "staking-like" mechanics that often trigger age-rating adjustments.
- Upcoming legislation, including the Digital Fairness Act and the EU KIDS Act, aims to further restrict addictive design patterns and mandate strict child-safety defaults by 2028.

### Why it Matters
These overlapping regulations force developers to integrate legal compliance into the earliest stages of game design to avoid costly retrofitting or significant enforcement penalties. Failure to adapt to these evolving standards risks both legal liability and reputational damage as European authorities prioritize the protection of vulnerable users and digital transparency.
