Staying ahead of the Chicoms: Beijing’s semiconductor bottlenecks are fading away
============================================================

China is rapidly reducing its reliance on foreign semiconductor technology by building domestic capabilities across its supply chain, challenging the long-term effectiveness of Western export control strategies.

### Key Points
- Sinolytics data shows the share of semiconductor technology bottlenecks in China dropped from 60% to 40% over the last three years.
- Domestic suppliers like SiCarrier are increasing production of etching and deposition tools to support advanced logic and memory chip manufacturing.
- Chinese photoresist producers have begun qualifying products for use by major firms like ChangXin Memory Technologies and Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation.
- China remains heavily dependent on imports for high-energy ion implanters and metrology systems, with 306 of 448 imported machines coming from the U.S. in 2025.
- Domestic substitution often shifts dependence upstream, as local equipment makers frequently rely on imported components and specialized materials.

### Why it Matters
- China’s accelerating industrial localization suggests that broad export controls are becoming less effective at maintaining a long-term technological advantage for the United States. Relying on these restrictions may inadvertently weaken the U.S. industrial base by reducing revenue for domestic research and development.
