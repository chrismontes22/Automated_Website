Tim Cook: Apple 'Reluctantly Raised Prices' Due to '100-Year Flood on Memory Pricing'
============================================================

Apple CEO Tim Cook confirmed that the company raised prices for Mac and iPad models in June due to unprecedented, exponential increases in global memory component costs.

### Key Points
- Apple implemented price hikes for Mac and iPad hardware during June to offset surging memory expenses.
- Tim Cook described the current memory market volatility as a "100-year flood" regarding pricing trends.
- The company evaluates pricing strategies by balancing unit sales, total revenue, and profit margins over the long term.
- Apple expects elevated memory costs to persist through the September quarter, though inventory stockpiles may mitigate the impact.
- Analysts anticipate potential price increases for the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro and foldable models launching this September.

### Why it Matters
These price adjustments highlight how supply chain volatility and component shortages can directly impact consumer hardware costs for major technology firms. Investors and consumers are now closely watching whether these inflationary pressures will extend to the upcoming iPhone product lineup.
