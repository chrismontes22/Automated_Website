Very cold war: Who’s attacking Europe’s supermarket freezers?
============================================================

Cyberattacks targeting internet-connected refrigeration systems across Europe and the United States have caused widespread food spoilage and equipment damage, prompting investigations into potential efforts to disrupt food supplies.

### Key Points
- Hackers are remotely accessing vulnerable refrigeration controllers to raise temperatures, causing food loss and, in some cases, fires in supermarket units.
- Major retailers including Delhaize, Carrefour, Spar, Coop, IKEA, and SuperValu have reported incidents across Belgium, Luxembourg, France, Sweden, and Ireland.
- U.S. military facilities, including bases in Arizona and Rhode Island, reported similar freezer malfunctions that the Pentagon suspects were deliberate cyberattacks.
- Cybersecurity experts attribute the vulnerabilities to outdated software, lack of secure network management, and the use of easily guessable passwords on remote-access controllers.
- Belgian authorities have launched a criminal investigation into the attacks, though no group has claimed responsibility for the disruptions.

### Why it Matters
These attacks highlight the growing vulnerability of critical infrastructure as essential systems like food refrigeration are increasingly digitized without adequate security protocols. The incidents threaten the stability of the food supply chain and demonstrate how cyber-physical attacks can cause significant financial losses and undermine public trust in essential services.
