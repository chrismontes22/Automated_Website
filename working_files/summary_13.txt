Anthropic seems to be testing a potential crackdown on vibe-coded ports of existing games
============================================================

Anthropic has implemented new system instructions within its Claude Code tool that explicitly prohibit the AI from recreating specific existing video games, even under different names.

### Key Points
- The restriction was discovered in the Claude Code binary v2.1.314 and is actively enforced in the Opus 5.5 model.
- Instructions explicitly state: "Don't build a recreation of a specific existing game, even if it is renamed or called original."
- Testing indicates the policy does not currently ban legitimate reverse engineering, decompilation, or static recompilation projects.
- Anthropic utilizes a remote configuration mechanism called "tengu_heron_brook" to deploy these behavioral updates to its models.
- Some developers have reported inconsistent refusals when using Claude for projects involving digital board-wargame modules.

### Why it Matters
This policy shift highlights the growing tension between AI-assisted software development and intellectual property protection in the gaming industry. Developers relying on AI for complex porting or recompilation projects now face increased uncertainty regarding how strictly these guardrails will be enforced in future updates.
