Privacy's Defenders Podcast: No Privacy Without Encryption
============================================================

The landmark legal case Bernstein v. Department of Justice established that computer code is protected speech, fundamentally shaping modern internet privacy and the widespread use of digital encryption.

### Key Points
- The 1995 lawsuit challenged federal regulations that classified encryption software as a restricted war munition.
- Plaintiff Dan Bernstein, a UC Berkeley graduate student, sued after being barred from publishing his "Snuffle" encryption algorithm.
- Attorney Cindy Cohn represented Bernstein on behalf of the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) to defend First Amendment rights.
- The court ruling successfully prevented the government from requiring licenses for the publication of cryptographic code.
- The case is the subject of the second episode of the "Privacy’s Defenders" podcast, hosted by Cindy Cohn.

### Why it Matters
This legal precedent ensures that developers can create and share encryption tools without government censorship, which remains the foundation for secure global communication. Without this ruling, the digital privacy standards currently protecting personal data and international commerce would be legally prohibited.
