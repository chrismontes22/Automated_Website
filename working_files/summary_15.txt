DisplayPort's free adaptive sync beats HDMI's licensing mess, and here's why it matters
============================================================

DisplayPort remains a superior, open-standard alternative to HDMI 2.1 for PC users, offering royalty-free Adaptive Sync technology and higher bandwidth capabilities despite HDMI's broader dominance in consumer electronics.

### Key Points
- DisplayPort utilizes the royalty-free Adaptive Sync standard, which provides Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) functionality similar to HDMI 2.1 without restrictive licensing fees.
- DisplayPort 2.1 supports up to 80 Gbps of bandwidth, significantly outperforming the 48 Gbps limit of HDMI 2.1 for high-resolution and high-refresh-rate gaming.
- The HDMI Forum maintains strict, proprietary control over its specifications, recently restricting public access and complicating Linux-based implementations.
- HDMI remains the de facto standard for televisions and game consoles, creating a compatibility gap for users who prefer the technical advantages of DisplayPort.
- Active adapters and USB-C Alt-Mode can bridge the gap between standards, though VRR performance remains inconsistent when converting signals between DisplayPort and HDMI.

### Why it Matters
DisplayPort offers a more flexible and powerful ecosystem for PC enthusiasts, but the industry's reliance on HDMI creates a fragmented experience for users connecting computers to televisions. Understanding these differences helps consumers choose the right hardware to avoid performance bottlenecks and compatibility issues in high-end display setups.
