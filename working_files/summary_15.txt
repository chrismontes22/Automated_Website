They're a world leader in using drones for healthcare. But drones can't do it all
============================================================

Rwanda is leveraging Zipline’s drone delivery network to provide emergency medical supplies, significantly reducing maternal mortality rates while highlighting the limitations of technology in addressing broader rural healthcare infrastructure.

### Key Points
- Rwanda utilizes Zipline drones to deliver blood, vaccines, and medicine to hospitals, reaching any location in the country in under 30 minutes.
- The U.S. government recently awarded a $150 million grant to expand Zipline’s operations across Rwanda and four other African nations.
- Despite the drone program's success, the U.S. has simultaneously cut $200 million in funding for NGOs providing essential food and health aid in rural Rwanda.
- Maternal mortality in Rwanda has dropped from approximately 1,000 deaths per 100,000 births to 87 over the last 26 years.
- Experts warn that high-tech solutions cannot fully replace the need for basic infrastructure, such as reliable electricity, roads, and staffed medical facilities.

### Why it Matters
The shift in U.S. foreign aid strategy prioritizes private-sector innovation over traditional NGO support, creating a complex landscape for developing nations. While drone technology provides critical emergency access, it remains an incomplete solution that cannot address systemic issues like malnutrition, staffing shortages, and the lack of basic clinical equipment.
