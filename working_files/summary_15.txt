Scientists are using AI to design new viruses. Should they be?
============================================================

Researchers have successfully used artificial intelligence to design entirely new bacteriophages, highlighting a significant advancement in biotechnology that currently outpaces existing global biosecurity regulations and safety oversight.

### Key Points
- Researchers published a study in *Science* detailing the creation of novel viruses that are over 90 percent similar to existing bacteriophages targeting *Escherichia coli*.
- The AI model used is open-source and has already been adapted for additional tasks since its initial preprint release in September 2025.
- Experts warn that future AI models could potentially design pathogens capable of infecting complex life or assist nonexperts in creating novel bioweapons.
- Proposed safety measures include a tiered access system for training data and improved screening protocols for DNA and RNA sequence orders.
- Current biosecurity relies heavily on voluntary compliance from researchers and suppliers rather than mandatory, standardized government regulations.

### Why it Matters
This development demonstrates that AI-driven biological design is advancing rapidly, creating a critical gap between technological capability and effective regulatory oversight. Establishing formal governance is essential to harness the medical potential of synthetic biology while preventing the accidental or intentional creation of dangerous pathogens.
