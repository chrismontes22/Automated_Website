North Korean 'Contagious Interview' gang hits 30,000 businesses across the world with malware following fake interviews
============================================================

North Korean threat actors have compromised over 30,000 devices across 100 countries through the "Contagious Interview" campaign, successfully stealing more than $10 million in cryptocurrency from unsuspecting victims.

### Key Points
- The four-year "Contagious Interview" campaign, also known as Operation DreamJob, is attributed to North Korean state-sponsored hackers.
- Operatives use fake personas, AI-generated media, and stolen personal data to infiltrate IT, healthcare, and blockchain organizations.
- Attackers utilize "laptop farms" in foreign countries to mask their true locations and bypass corporate IP-based security measures.
- The campaign has successfully targeted 7,000 individuals and businesses, resulting in over $10 million in total cryptocurrency theft.
- Law enforcement agencies from the U.S., Japan, Germany, and Australia advise verifying applicant certifications, IP addresses, and contact details to prevent infiltration.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a sophisticated evolution in social engineering where state-sponsored actors exploit global labor shortages to gain direct access to corporate networks. Businesses must now implement more rigorous identity verification processes to protect sensitive infrastructure from these increasingly deceptive hiring-based cyberattacks.
