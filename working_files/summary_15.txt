Data Centers Are Causing a Huge Surge in Incredibly Toxic Chemicals Polluting the World
============================================================

The rapid expansion of global data centers is driving increased production of toxic forever chemicals, or PFAS, which are essential for cooling systems, AI chips, and cable insulation.

### Key Points
- Data centers rely heavily on PFAS for fire suppression, circuit boards, and cooling infrastructure to support continuous operations.
- Frequent hardware replacements in data centers lead to the disposal of PFAS-laden components, increasing environmental contamination risks.
- The top ten global producers of PFAS are reportedly increasing production quotas to meet rising demand from the data center industry.
- Environmental concerns extend to physical spills, such as a recent 5,000-gallon diesel leak at an Equinix facility in Secaucus, New Jersey.
- Advocacy groups like ChemSec are calling for government-mandated phaseouts to prevent a surge in persistent chemical pollutants.

### Why it Matters
The reliance on PFAS in critical digital infrastructure creates a long-term environmental hazard as these chemicals accumulate in the food chain and human bodies. This trend highlights a growing conflict between the rapid scaling of artificial intelligence and the need for sustainable industrial regulation.
