Why quantum scales on compute-per-watt, not qubit count
============================================================

The quantum computing industry must prioritize compute-per-watt and supply chain development over qubit counts to ensure economic viability and address the escalating power demands of modern AI infrastructure.

### Key Points
- UN researchers project that AI-driven data center power and water consumption will double by 2030.
- Current quantum development focuses on qubit counts, which fail to reflect the total cost of ownership or actual economic returns.
- Superconducting qubit processors currently dedicate over 90% of chip surface area to wiring, limiting efficiency and scalability.
- Achieving economic viability requires a hundredfold reduction in cost per qubit through volume manufacturing and standardized parts.
- A "Quantum Open Architecture" model, involving specialized companies for processors, cryogenics, and control electronics, is essential for scaling production.

### Why it Matters
The transition from laboratory proof-of-concepts to industrial-scale manufacturing is critical for quantum computing to eventually alleviate the massive energy strain caused by AI workloads. If the industry fails to prioritize economic metrics like compute-per-watt, it risks remaining a collection of expensive, niche demonstrations rather than a transformative, power-efficient computing utility.
