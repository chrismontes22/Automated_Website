Switzerland's Federal Government Tests Open-Source Alternatives to Microsoft on 3,000 Computers
============================================================

The Swiss federal government has launched a pilot program to transition 3,000 workstations to the open-source openDesk suite, aiming to reduce reliance on Microsoft 365 by late 2027.

### Key Points
- The pilot program follows a successful proof-of-concept involving 172 employees testing the German-developed openDesk collaboration platform.
- Switzerland’s Cyber Command plans to fully replace Microsoft 365 with openDesk across its systems by October 2026 to enhance data security.
- Officials cite concerns over US cloud legislation, operational dependency on foreign vendors, and rising proprietary software licensing costs as primary drivers for the shift.
- If the current pilot proves successful, the government may expand the migration to cover all 54,000 federal administration workstations.
- During the testing phase, the open-source software is running in parallel with existing Microsoft applications to ensure service continuity.

### Why it Matters
This transition highlights a growing trend among European governments to prioritize digital sovereignty and reduce reliance on major US-based technology providers. By moving to open-source alternatives, Switzerland aims to mitigate risks related to foreign data access and long-term software cost volatility.
