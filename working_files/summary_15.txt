Why education IT teams must protect both human and machine identities
============================================================

UK educational institutions face heightened cybersecurity risks during the back-to-school season as AI-enhanced phishing and unmanaged machine identities expand the attack surface for hackers targeting sensitive data.

### Key Points
- UK government data shows 27% of higher education institutions experience weekly cyberattacks, significantly higher than the 14% reported by primary schools.
- AI-driven phishing campaigns now mimic official communications from IT helpdesks and student funding offices, making them increasingly difficult for staff and students to identify.
- Non-Human Identities (NHIs), including API keys, service accounts, and AI agents, often lack proper oversight, creating hidden vulnerabilities in modern EdTech environments.
- Only 45% of higher education institutions currently comply with all 10 steps of the UK government’s cyber security guidance.
- Security experts recommend enforcing multi-factor authentication, auditing privileged access, and maintaining a comprehensive inventory of all machine identities to mitigate these risks.

### Why it Matters
The education sector remains a primary target for ransomware and data theft due to its vast stores of personal information and often underfunded IT infrastructure. By failing to secure both human and non-human digital identities, institutions risk significant operational disruption and the compromise of sensitive research and financial data.
