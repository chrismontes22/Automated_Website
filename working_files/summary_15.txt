Microsoft warns hotel/airport Wi-Fi is being hijacked to steal your login
============================================================

Microsoft has issued a security warning regarding the CaptiveCrunch attack campaign, where Russian hackers exploit public Wi-Fi networks to steal user credentials and install malware on devices.

### Key Points
- The hacker group Storm-2945, linked to the Russian Foreign Intelligence Service, is identified as the primary actor behind the CaptiveCrunch campaign.
- Attackers manipulate DNS queries on public Wi-Fi networks to redirect users to sophisticated phishing sites that mimic official Microsoft login portals.
- Compromised captive portals at locations like hotels and airports are used to intercept OAuth codes and capture sensitive device credentials.
- Malware deployed during these attacks includes Trojans capable of recording keystrokes, hijacking cameras, and exfiltrating private files from infected systems.
- Microsoft is currently investigating commonalities in network management systems to determine how attackers gain initial access to these public infrastructure points.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a significant vulnerability in public Wi-Fi infrastructure that could lead to widespread account takeovers and long-term device surveillance. Users should prioritize private mobile hotspots or encrypted VPN connections to mitigate the risk of credential theft and unauthorized remote access.
