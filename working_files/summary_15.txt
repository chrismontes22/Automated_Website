Security Affairs newsletter Round 592 by Pierluigi Paganini – INTERNATIONAL EDITION
============================================================

The latest Security Affairs newsletter highlights a surge in global cyberattacks, including critical infrastructure breaches, ransomware campaigns, and significant legal settlements involving major technology companies like Meta.

### Key Points
- The UK’s Manchester Airports Group (MAG) suffered a breach exposing the personal data of 8.7 million customers.
- U.S. authorities seized China-linked hacking platforms QScan and QTRouter used to target domestic critical infrastructure.
- Meta agreed to pay up to $18 billion to settle lawsuits regarding the impact of social media on children.
- A critical zero-day vulnerability in PaperCut software is under active exploitation, prompting an emergency patch release.
- Researchers discovered a remote code execution flaw in Unitree G1 humanoid robots that allows unauthorized root access via Bluetooth.
- Operation Jackal resulted in 58 arrests globally to dismantle organized crime groups involved in money laundering and scams.

### Why it Matters
These incidents demonstrate the escalating threat landscape facing both private corporations and national critical infrastructure providers. As state-sponsored actors and cybercriminals refine their tactics, organizations must prioritize vulnerability management and robust security protocols to protect sensitive user data and operational integrity.
