User-made maps from Steam top-seller Meccha Chameleon have been found to contain malware, just as the game's official Discord server got hacked
============================================================

The indie hit game Meccha Chameleon has addressed a security breach involving malware-infected Steam Workshop maps and a compromised official Discord server, prompting a mandatory game update for players.

### Key Points
- Independent researcher Feint discovered malware droppers embedded within user-created Steam Workshop maps, specifically Laser Tag Neon and Chroma Grid Arena.
- Developer Lemorion_1224 confirmed that a system engineer’s PC was compromised, allowing hackers to bypass Discord two-factor authentication and ban official staff.
- The developer released patch version 3.1.0 on Saturday to resolve the vulnerability that allowed Workshop maps to execute malicious code.
- Players who downloaded affected maps prior to the patch are advised to perform full system malware scans to ensure their devices remain secure.
- The official Meccha Chameleon Discord server has been restored following the temporary takeover by unauthorized actors.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the significant security risks associated with user-generated content platforms, even for highly popular titles that have sold millions of copies. It serves as a critical reminder for both developers and players to maintain rigorous cybersecurity hygiene when interacting with community-driven game modifications.
