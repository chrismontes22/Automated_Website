Karaoke Giant Reveals Data Breach Impacting Eight Million
============================================================

Japanese karaoke operator Daiichi Kosho reports a potential data breach at supplier Nippon Columbia Group that may have exposed personal information for over 8.7 million customers.

### Key Points
- The breach potentially affects 8.72 million customers, including 5.6 million Big Echo users and 3.5 million DK Dining patrons.
- Compromised data may include names, genders, dates of birth, email addresses, and telephone numbers.
- Nippon Columbia Group discovered a malware infection on an employee's PC between October 1 and October 2, 2026.
- Daiichi Kosho has advised customers to remain vigilant against phishing attempts via email, SMS, or phone calls.
- The company has established a toll-free support line to assist customers concerned about their personal data security.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a growing trend of large-scale data breaches across Japanese corporations, following recent attacks on major railway and car-rental firms. It underscores the persistent cybersecurity vulnerabilities within the Japanese private sector and the ongoing challenges organizations face in securing sensitive customer information held by third-party suppliers.
