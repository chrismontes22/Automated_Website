Can Trump Save Public Perception of Data Centers?
============================================================

Despite President Donald Trump’s vocal support for data center expansion, public opposition remains high as critics argue that Big Tech companies must improve their local community engagement strategies.

### Key Points
- A May 13 Gallup poll indicates that 70% of Americans oppose the construction of data centers within their local communities.
- Former White House strategist Steve Bannon and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argue that tech companies have failed to justify the local economic and utility impacts of these facilities.
- In July, 23 governors signed the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, which aims to shield residents from increased energy costs caused by data center operations.
- Vice President JD Vance noted that public backlash often stems from concerns that data centers increase utility bills rather than contributing power back to the grid.
- Proponents cite Quincy, Washington, as a success story where data center tax contributions helped reduce the local poverty rate from 29% to 6%.

### Why it Matters
The tension between national AI infrastructure goals and local community resistance highlights a significant public relations challenge for the technology industry. If companies fail to demonstrate tangible local benefits, they risk continued political friction and potential regulatory hurdles that could impede the broader expansion of AI-related infrastructure.
