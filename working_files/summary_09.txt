India’s drone moment has arrived
============================================================

India is aggressively expanding its domestic drone manufacturing ecosystem to achieve strategic autonomy and defense self-reliance, aiming to become a global hub for unmanned aerial systems by 2030.

### Key Points
- India is investing billions in indigenous drone programs, including a $3.6 billion order for Medium Altitude Long Endurance (MALE) drones and a $2 billion tactical drone procurement.
- The government is mandating over 60% indigenous content for new military drones, focusing on critical components like motors, batteries, and software to reduce import dependence.
- Major industrial players including HAL, Tata, L&T, and Adani Defense are leading the production of advanced systems like the Nagastra-1 and the Ghatak combat vehicle.
- The "Bharat Drone Stack" and Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes are being used to support startups and small businesses in scaling local manufacturing and R&D.
- India is leveraging international partnerships, such as the INDUS-X initiative with the U.S., to integrate cybersecurity standards and expand export potential to the Global South.

### Why it Matters
The rapid development of an indigenous drone industry is critical for India to maintain national security amid evolving geopolitical threats and the proven effectiveness of low-cost, autonomous systems in modern warfare. By shifting from foreign imports to domestic production, India aims to secure its supply chain while positioning itself as a significant competitor in the projected $23 billion global drone market.
