G7 Urges Fast-Track on Quantum-Safe Cybersecurity Rules
============================================================

The G7 nations have issued a formal call to action urging public and private sectors to accelerate the transition to post-quantum cryptography to mitigate future security threats.

### Key Points
- The French National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI) is leading the G7 initiative to address the near-term risks quantum computing poses to current public-key encryption.
- Organizations are advised to inventory cryptographic assets and prioritize the protection of critical data systems as part of a phased, risk-based transition strategy.
- The G7 outlines five core priorities, including raising awareness, developing national PQC strategies, and fostering public-private partnerships to advance quantum-safe technologies.
- ANSSI plans to cease vetting products lacking quantum-safe encryption by 2027, with mandatory PQC requirements for certain security products expected by 2030.
- The initiative is supported by all G7 member states, the European Commission, and the EU Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA).

### Why it Matters
This directive signals a major shift in global cybersecurity policy by categorizing quantum computing as an immediate threat rather than a distant technical challenge. By standardizing these requirements, the G7 aims to force a market-wide migration that protects critical digital infrastructure from future decryption capabilities.
