AI opens new era in cognitive studies of wild primates
============================================================

Researchers from Emory University and Georgia Tech developed CapuchinAI, a portable, automated system that uses facial recognition and touchscreens to conduct cognitive testing on wild capuchin monkeys.

### Key Points
- The system uses a Raspberry Pi, a touchscreen, and a 3D-printed food dispenser to present cognitive tasks to monkeys in their natural habitat.
- CapuchinAI identifies individual monkeys with 97% accuracy using a machine-learning model trained on field footage.
- Field tests in Costa Rica’s Taboga Forest Reserve successfully engaged 16 wild capuchins, who learned to interact with the interface for food rewards.
- The open-source project provides a scalable, low-cost blueprint for researchers to study primate cognition without the limitations of laboratory environments.

### Why it Matters
This technology bridges the gap between controlled laboratory experiments and the complex, unpredictable social environments where primates naturally evolved. By automating data collection, researchers can now quantify individual cognitive differences across large wild populations, providing deeper insights into how environmental factors shape animal intelligence.
