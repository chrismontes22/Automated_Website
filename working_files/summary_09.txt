France’s Goncourt prize drops a novel over claims AI wrote it
============================================================

The Académie Goncourt has removed Thélyson Orélien’s novel C’était ça ou mourir from its prestigious literary prize selection following allegations of plagiarism and significant artificial intelligence usage.

### Key Points
- The Académie Goncourt disqualified the debut novel after analyses suggested the text was largely generated by artificial intelligence.
- Plagiarism accusations surfaced regarding Orélien’s previous works, including a 2012 short story and a 2013 opinion piece.
- The anonymous X account "Balance ton Claude," linked to essayist Samuel Fitoussi, first raised the AI-generation claims on September 21.
- Author Thélyson Orélien denies using AI, attributing the book's stylistic rhythms to his Haitian and Caribbean linguistic heritage.
- Publisher Grasset released the novel in August, with translation rights sold to publishers in over 20 countries.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing tension between traditional literary institutions and the rise of generative AI in creative writing. It forces publishers and prize committees to establish new standards for authenticity and intellectual property in an era where AI-assisted content is increasingly difficult to verify.
