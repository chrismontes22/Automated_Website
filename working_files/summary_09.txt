Glyph Lefkowitz: Who Is Open Source About?
============================================================

Open source maintainers must move beyond the "gift economy" narrative to acknowledge the implicit, long-term ethical obligations and complex power dynamics they share with their project users.

### Key Points
- Maintainers often release software for self-interested reasons, including reputation, professional influence, skill development, and shared maintenance burdens.
- The relationship between maintainers and users creates an implicit trust, requiring developers to avoid malicious code and maintain operational security.
- Users rely on software for their livelihoods, creating a moral expectation that maintainers will communicate changes and avoid breaking critical functionality.
- Current accounting practices often fail to recognize the business value of open source, leading to underfunded maintenance and project instability.
- The rise of AI-generated code has intensified user-maintainer friction, highlighting the urgent need for better community management and feedback infrastructure.

### Why it Matters
Redefining the open source relationship as a nuanced, two-way exchange rather than a one-sided gift helps maintainers set clearer boundaries and manage user expectations. By formalizing these implicit obligations, the software ecosystem can reduce toxic interactions and build more sustainable, long-term project governance.
