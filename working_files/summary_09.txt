Why stocks have held up as yields surge to 24-year highs
============================================================

Global stock markets remain resilient near record highs despite a significant bond market sell-off that pushed the 10-year US Treasury yield to its highest level since 2002.

### Key Points
- The 10-year US Treasury yield spiked to 5.33%, while the 30-year Treasury yield reached a 24-year peak of 5.64%.
- The S&P 500 remains within 2% of its all-time high, supported by strong corporate earnings and optimism surrounding artificial intelligence investments.
- Atlanta Fed data projects 3.7% economic growth for the third quarter, signaling that the economy is currently robust enough to withstand higher interest rates.
- Analysts note that the bond market's recent move has been relatively orderly, preventing the type of systemic shock that typically triggers a stock market correction.
- Mega-cap technology stocks continue to drive index performance, masking broader weakness where the median S&P 500 stock is down 15% from its 52-week high.

### Why it Matters
The current divergence between rising bond yields and buoyant equity prices suggests investors are prioritizing strong economic growth and AI-driven earnings over traditional interest rate concerns. However, market experts warn that this disconnect is historically unsustainable and could lead to a significant market correction if bond volatility increases.
