Israel’s economy prospers despite years of war, but prices worry voters
============================================================

Israel’s economy has demonstrated unexpected resilience, achieving significant growth driven by a booming technology sector despite the ongoing financial strain of multi-front military conflicts and regional instability.

### Key Points
- Israel’s GDP grew by 2.9 percent in 2025, with the Bank of Israel projecting 4 percent growth for 2026 and 5.5 percent for 2027.
- The tech sector, which accounts for one-fifth of economic activity, attracted a record $26.2 billion in direct foreign investment last year.
- Military costs have reached approximately 350 billion shekels ($114.6 billion), with proposed defense budgets rising to 9 percent of GDP.
- Unemployment remains low at 2.8 percent, though inflation in food prices and a high cost of living continue to impact individual households.
- The TA-125 stock market index has surged over 110 percent, and the shekel reached a three-decade high against the US dollar in May.

### Why it Matters
The country's economic performance highlights a decoupling between macroeconomic growth and the fiscal burden of sustained military mobilization. While the tech sector provides a robust buffer, the long-term sustainability of public finances remains a critical challenge for the next government as it balances defense spending with domestic economic pressures.
