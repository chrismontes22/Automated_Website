Cybersecurity’s identity crisis: why trust can no longer begin and end at login
============================================================

Cybersecurity experts warn that stolen credentials now allow attackers to bypass traditional defenses, necessitating a shift toward continuous behavioral monitoring rather than relying on one-time authentication protocols.

### Key Points
- Phishing remains the primary cyber threat, impacting 85% of businesses that experienced a security breach according to the UK's Cyber Security Breaches Survey 2025.
- Attackers frequently use stolen login credentials and authentication tokens purchased on the dark web to gain legitimate access to corporate and public sector systems.
- Modern security strategies are moving toward Zero Trust models, which require continuous verification of user behavior rather than granting permanent access after an initial login.
- Advanced machine learning tools now analyze digital fingerprints and behavioral patterns to detect anomalies that indicate an account has been compromised by an unauthorized user.
- Organizations are encouraged to treat identity as a dynamic system, regularly auditing permissions and access rights to prevent lateral movement within their digital infrastructure.

### Why it Matters
Relying on static passwords and single-point authentication is no longer sufficient as attackers increasingly blend into normal operations using valid credentials. Adopting continuous trust models is essential for business resilience, as it shifts the focus from preventing initial entry to identifying malicious activity in real time.
