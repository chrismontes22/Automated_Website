U.S. restrictions on Chinese drones are leaving police departments in the lurch
============================================================

New federal restrictions and tariffs on Chinese-made drones are creating significant financial and operational challenges for U.S. law enforcement agencies that rely on affordable, high-performance DJI technology.

### Key Points
- Approximately 80% to 90% of U.S. law enforcement agencies currently utilize DJI drones for tactical operations, search and rescue, and public safety monitoring.
- The Trump administration has imposed tariffs ranging from 15% to 100% on foreign drones to encourage domestic manufacturing and address national security concerns.
- American-made drone alternatives often cost two to three times more than comparable Chinese models while sometimes offering inferior flight time and thermal imaging capabilities.
- The FCC is considering reclassifying drones with advanced features like thermal imaging as "military grade," which could effectively ban many models currently used by police.
- DJI denies allegations of data security risks and forced labor, maintaining that its products are secure and that technical evidence does not support the bans.

### Why it Matters
These restrictions force cash-strapped police departments to choose between abandoning essential aerial programs or passing significant replacement costs onto local taxpayers. While the federal government aims to secure the domestic supply chain, the current lack of affordable, high-quality American alternatives threatens the operational capabilities of thousands of local law enforcement agencies.
