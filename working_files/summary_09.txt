The mystery of a 'little red book' about insurance startup Corgi
============================================================

AI insurance startup Corgi faces scrutiny after reports of a controversial internal booklet promoting aggressive workplace culture, age-based hiring biases, and extreme, round-the-clock work expectations for employees.

### Key Points
- The 17-page booklet, titled "The Mission at Corgi," contains inflammatory statements, including claims that "Boomers are slow and bad" and mandates to fire anyone who refuses to work weekends.
- Corgi, valued at $4 billion as of July, denies creating the book, attributing it to an external investor despite reports from former employees who saw it in the office.
- The company has raised over $378 million since its 2024 founding and is known for its "hardcore" work culture, including CEO Nico Laqua’s public stance against weekends off.
- The text describes the insurance industry in combative terms, specifically targeting established competitors like Chubb and framing the startup's mission as a "war."
- Internal policies mentioned in the book include providing employees with office mattresses and a philosophy that prioritizes young workers aged 19 to 30.

### Why it Matters
The controversy highlights the growing tension between "hardcore" Silicon Valley startup culture and modern workplace standards regarding age discrimination and employee well-being. As Corgi scales with significant venture capital backing, these revelations raise questions about the sustainability and ethical implications of extreme corporate environments.
