Teen hackers tell BBC how police are helping them use their skills for good
============================================================

The National Crime Agency’s Cyber Choices program is working to divert talented teenagers away from criminal hacking by redirecting their technical skills toward legitimate cybersecurity career paths.

### Key Points
- The Cyber Choices scheme has received over 1,150 referrals in eight years, with nearly 600 cases occurring within the last three years.
- Program officers provide one-to-one mentorship to help young hackers transition from illicit activities to professional training and university qualifications.
- Authorities report that many young offenders are neurodiverse and initially begin hacking out of curiosity rather than criminal intent.
- Teenage hackers have been linked to significant 2024 and 2025 cyber-attacks against major organizations, including Transport for London and MGM Grand.
- Critics express concern that teaching advanced cyber skills to individuals already prone to hacking could inadvertently increase their capacity for future criminal activity.

### Why it Matters
This initiative highlights the growing challenge of preventing young, tech-savvy individuals from being recruited into global cyber-crime syndicates. By intervening early, authorities aim to transform potential security threats into a skilled workforce for the cybersecurity industry.
