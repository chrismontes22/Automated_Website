American investors are touring China’s robot factories to size up the competition
============================================================

Silicon Valley venture capitalists are increasingly traveling to China to evaluate the rapid advancements of Chinese robotics and hardware firms, aiming to assess competitive threats to their portfolios.

### Key Points
- Chinese companies Unitree and AgiBot accounted for 71% of global humanoid robot shipments last year.
- China controls 63% of the global supply chain for key humanoid-robot components, according to the Mercator Institute for China Studies.
- Investors from firms including Eclipse, G2 Venture Partners, and Founders Fund are conducting on-the-ground research to gauge China's manufacturing and data-collection capabilities.
- The Chinese government has established a national policy to deploy humanoid robots across its economy by 2027.
- US investors are paying up to $10,000 for curated tours that include factory visits and meetings with Chinese technology executives.

### Why it Matters
These excursions signal a shift in how American investors perceive the global technology landscape, moving from dismissal to active concern regarding China's industrial dominance. By witnessing China's integrated supply chains and data-collection scale, venture capitalists are now incorporating geopolitical risk and competitive benchmarking into their long-term investment strategies for US robotics startups.
