Lean LaunchPad – The Next Generation
============================================================

Stanford University has overhauled its Lean LaunchPad curriculum to integrate artificial intelligence, replacing traditional minimum viable products with rapid hypothesis testing and a focus on securing design partners.

### Key Points
- The course replaces the "Minimum Viable Product" (MVP) with "Initial Untested Products" (IUP) and "Minimal Tested Canvases" (MTC) to accelerate discovery.
- Students are now required to secure "Design Partners"—stakeholders who commit resources like data or workflow access—as the primary metric for customer validation.
- The curriculum is split into two phases: four weeks of deep-dive stakeholder and problem mapping, followed by five weeks of rapid deployment and testing.
- New AI-specific metrics, such as "Product-Outcome Fit," replace generic product-market fit goals to better align with modern software, hardware, and life science development.
- Students must now account for "learning debt," LLM inference costs, and model portability as core components of their business model economics.

### Why it Matters
This curriculum shift acknowledges that AI has fundamentally altered the speed of product creation, necessitating a move away from slow, iterative prototyping toward rigorous, evidence-based validation. By prioritizing real-world design partners over theoretical product demos, the course prepares entrepreneurs to navigate the high-stakes, AI-driven market landscape more effectively.
