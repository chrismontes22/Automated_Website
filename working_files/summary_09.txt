Most of Us Have Been Cyberattack Targets, Consumer Reports Finds. AI Isn’t Helping
============================================================

A new report from Consumer Reports, Aspen Digital, and the Global Cyber Alliance reveals that 90% of Americans have encountered digital scams or cyberattacks amid rising privacy concerns.

### Key Points
- Research indicates that 29% of U.S. adults now lack confidence in personal data privacy, a significant increase from 16% in 2025.
- The percentage of Americans confident in the security of their sensitive information has dropped from 48% to 32% over the past year.
- Experts warn that artificial intelligence is enabling scammers to execute personalized, large-scale fraud more efficiently and at a lower cost.
- Common consumer defense strategies include using multifactor authentication, enabling automatic software updates, and scrutinizing suspicious emails or text messages.
- Policy experts are calling for federal legislation, such as the SCAM Act or SAFE Platforms Act, to hold companies accountable for platform abuse.

### Why it Matters
The rapid integration of AI into cybercrime is outpacing individual security efforts, shifting the burden of protection from consumers to corporations and lawmakers. Without stronger federal guardrails and corporate accountability, the erosion of public trust in digital privacy will likely continue to accelerate.
