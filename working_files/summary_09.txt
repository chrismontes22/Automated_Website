At colleges, the AI boom means everyone wants to dabble in computer science
============================================================

Universities across the United States are rapidly expanding artificial intelligence curricula for non-computer science majors as students seek to gain essential technical literacy for an evolving job market.

### Key Points
- Enrollment in traditional computer and information science programs has declined by over 8% since spring 2025 as entry-level software developer job postings decrease.
- Institutions including Virginia Commonwealth University, Purdue, and Harvard are implementing new AI minors, graduation requirements, and specialized workshops for students outside of STEM fields.
- Northwestern University is streamlining prerequisites and adding an AI major to accommodate high demand from students in diverse disciplines like music and the humanities.
- Community colleges, such as Johnson County Community College, are accelerating course development to offer non-credit AI certificates and occupation-specific training in weeks rather than years.
- Educators are balancing the need for AI literacy with concerns that over-reliance on automated tools may hinder students' fundamental understanding of their chosen professional fields.

### Why it Matters
Integrating AI education across all disciplines prepares graduates to remain competitive as machine learning increasingly disrupts traditional career paths. This shift reflects a broader institutional recognition that AI proficiency is becoming a foundational skill comparable to basic literacy or mathematics.
