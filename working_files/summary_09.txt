Hackers Hid Vasilek Backdoor in VMware Tools During 2-Year Russian Healthcare Intrusion
============================================================

The Belarus-aligned hacktivist group Cyber Partisans maintained a long-term, stealthy presence within a Russian healthcare organization’s network to facilitate potential espionage and future supply chain attacks.

### Key Points
- Cybersecurity firm Solar identified the intrusion, which began in early 2024 and remained undetected until December 2025.
- The attackers used a custom backdoor called Vasilek, which leverages Telegram for command-and-control and data exfiltration.
- Persistence was maintained by masquerading malicious files as legitimate VMware Tools components to evade security detection.
- Unlike previous destructive campaigns by the group, this operation prioritized long-term access over immediate system disruption.
- The threat actors utilized various tools, including DNSCat2 and the Evlx utility, to manage infected hosts and clear Windows event logs.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a strategic shift by hacktivist groups toward long-term espionage within critical infrastructure rather than immediate sabotage. By compromising a healthcare entity with extensive trusted relationships, the attackers gained a high-value foothold that could be leveraged to infiltrate secondary medical institutions.
