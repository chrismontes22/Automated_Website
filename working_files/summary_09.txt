Russia's next tool to fight VPNs appears to be AI — and the numbers suggest it's scarily effective
============================================================

Russian authorities are exploring the integration of artificial intelligence into their national censorship infrastructure to identify and block virtual private networks with a projected 95% effectiveness rate.

### Key Points
- The proposal appeared in a draft national plan for AI implementation through 2030, aiming to analyze traffic patterns for 70% of targeted VPN services.
- Russia plans to expand its traffic-filtering capacity 2.5-fold to 954 terabits per second by 2030 using existing technical systems for countering threats.
- AI-driven detection would evaluate packet size, timing, and session duration, though industry experts warn of potential false positives affecting legitimate corporate traffic.
- A separate mandate requires 5G smartphones to support the domestic "Kuznyechik" encryption algorithm by 2032, potentially forcing global manufacturers to create Russia-specific hardware.

### Why it Matters
These developments signal a significant escalation in Russia's efforts to enforce digital isolation and maintain strict control over internet traffic. By mandating domestic encryption standards and automating censorship, the state creates substantial operational hurdles for global technology companies and limits secure communication options for users.
