The Meta Settlement Will Put Limits on Instagram for Teens. They’re Still Vulnerable
============================================================

Meta has reached a court-approved settlement with state attorneys general to implement significant parental oversight and usage restrictions on Instagram and Facebook to protect teenage users from harm.

### Key Points
- Meta agreed to pay up to $16.7 billion over 10 years to settle lawsuits regarding the impact of its platforms on children.
- New features include mandatory daily time limits, overnight access blocks, and muted notifications during school hours for teen accounts.
- Parents will receive alerts regarding suspicious account activity and gain the ability to set default non-algorithmic feeds for their children.
- The settlement requires Meta to strengthen age-verification systems, though critics warn these measures may compromise user privacy and free expression.
- US District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers officially approved the settlement, which avoids a lengthy federal civil trial in Northern California.

### Why it Matters
This settlement represents a major regulatory shift in how social media giants manage youth safety, moving toward mandatory friction and parental control features. While the agreement forces Meta to alter its flagship products, experts remain divided on whether these changes will effectively reduce online harm or simply shift the burden of enforcement onto caregivers.
