Why was there an 'evil’ Delta airlines Wi-Fi network? The experts weigh in
============================================================

A passenger on a Delta flight departing from the DEF CON hacker conference in Las Vegas allegedly launched a malicious "evil twin" Wi-Fi attack against fellow travelers.

### Key Points
- The attacker used a deauthentication attack to jam legitimate onboard Wi-Fi for 30 minutes.
- A fake network named "Delta WiFi Fast" was broadcast to trick passengers into connecting.
- The incident was reported by flight crew via ACARS messages after passengers raised concerns.
- Delta confirmed that aircraft operating systems remained secure and flight safety was never compromised.
- Experts warn that such attacks, which can harvest credentials via phishing, are federal crimes.
- The FBI is reportedly involved in investigating the incident following the flight's departure from Las Vegas.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the significant security risks posed by untrusted public networks, particularly for business travelers who may inadvertently expose sensitive corporate data. It serves as a reminder that users should utilize always-on VPNs and remain vigilant against suspicious login prompts when accessing Wi-Fi in transit.
