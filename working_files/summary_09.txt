Unpublished novel's $2.4m deal pulled over AI concerns
============================================================

A debut novel’s $2.4 million licensing deal with Minotaur/Macmillan was canceled after literary agents could no longer verify the manuscript's authorship amid concerns regarding AI-generated content.

### Key Points
- Literary agent Sandy Hodgman withdrew the unnamed novel from international markets after failing to substantiate claims that the work was written without AI assistance.
- The cancellation follows reports that the self-published hit *Daggermouth* may be 60 percent AI-generated, according to analysis by the detection tool Pangram.
- A study of 14,000 Kindle ebooks found that one in five titles contained at least 25 percent AI-generated text.
- Industry experts suggest that publishers may be failing to adequately vet manuscripts, prioritizing market buzz and financial projections over editorial review.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a growing crisis of trust and quality control within the publishing industry as AI-generated content becomes increasingly difficult to distinguish from human writing. It forces publishers to confront the financial and reputational risks of acquiring high-value manuscripts without rigorous verification of their origins.
