Verification Is Not Knowledge: Science needs more than answers
============================================================

The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into scientific research risks prioritizing verified outputs over human understanding, potentially undermining the critical accountability and social processes that sustain scientific knowledge.

### Key Points
- Northwestern University professor Jessica Hullman argues that scientific progress requires human judgment, not just the automated verification of results.
- The "value-free ideal" incorrectly suggests that scientific truth is purely procedural and independent of ethical or social context.
- Mathematician Terry Tao and others warn that relying on AI-generated solutions without human comprehension creates risks if the underlying logic is flawed.
- Scientific knowledge relies on transformative criticism, where researchers hold one another accountable for claims and methodologies.
- Current AI models lack the persistence and identity required to act as truly accountable epistemic agents within the scientific community.

### Why it Matters
Treating scientific truth as a collection of verified facts rather than a social, value-driven practice threatens to erode the human expertise necessary to interpret and build upon new discoveries. If researchers lose the ability to critically engage with AI outputs, the scientific community risks losing the capacity to distinguish between meaningful knowledge and mere data.
