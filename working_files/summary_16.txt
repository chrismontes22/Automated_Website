The hackers protecting America’s water supply
============================================================

Volunteer hackers from the DEF CON Franklin initiative are partnering with rural U.S. water utilities to address critical cybersecurity vulnerabilities and implement essential network security improvements for local communities.

### Key Points
- Many small, rural water utilities rely on outdated, insecure technology and lack the dedicated staff or funding required for modern cyberdefense.
- The DEF CON Franklin project connects cybersecurity experts with utility operators to perform basic, high-impact tasks like network mapping and setting up multifactor authentication.
- The initiative gained trust by partnering with established rural water associations to vet volunteers and bridge the gap between technical experts and utility operators.
- Organizers emphasize that while volunteer efforts provide immediate relief, long-term security requires sustained federal and state funding rather than just donated software.
- The program is named after Benjamin Franklin, drawing inspiration from his creation of the first volunteer fire department to foster civic responsibility in infrastructure protection.

### Why it Matters
Small water utilities often lack the resources to defend against sophisticated cyberattacks, creating significant risks for public health and essential infrastructure. While volunteer-led "cyber civil defense" provides a vital stopgap, the reliance on unpaid labor highlights a broader need for systemic government investment to ensure national water security.
