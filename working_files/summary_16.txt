UK government officials prioritized meetings with AI datacenter executives over environmental groups while industry leaders and security experts navigate a rapidly evolving landscape of infrastructure and cyber threats.

### Key Points
- UK ministers faced criticism for excluding environmental advocates from discussions regarding the development of designated AI Growth Zones.
- Anthropic executives argued that global investment in AI infrastructure must reach $800 billion to sustain future technological progress.
- Apollo Global Management reported that AI-driven financial gains remain concentrated within the "Magnificent Seven" tech companies rather than the broader market.
- Microsoft issued emergency guidance after failing to fully patch a zero-day vulnerability currently targeting on-premises SharePoint servers.
- Private equity firm EQT acquired a majority stake in Swiss cybersecurity company Acronis at a valuation exceeding $3.5 billion.
- The DEF CON security conference expanded its Franklin project to include hackers focused on hardening critical national infrastructure.

### Why it Matters
The concentration of AI influence among a few major corporations and the persistent vulnerability of critical infrastructure highlight significant risks for global markets and digital security. These developments suggest that while AI investment is accelerating, the benefits and security burdens remain unevenly distributed across the technology sector.
