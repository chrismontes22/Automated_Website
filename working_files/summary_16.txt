Banks are now trying to offload the AI data center debt they just underwrote
============================================================

Major banks including JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley are offloading AI-related data center debt as they reach internal exposure limits and face cooling investor demand for tech infrastructure loans.

### Key Points
- JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, SMBC, and MUFG are seeking risk transfers to move AI-linked debt off their balance sheets.
- Banks have struggled for six months to distribute $38 billion in construction debt tied to Oracle data centers in Texas and Wisconsin.
- Oracle’s credit rating was downgraded to BBB- by S&P, sitting just one notch above junk status due to high AI spending.
- Demand for AI-related corporate bonds has weakened, with order books shrinking significantly since February.
- Yields for bonds leased to smaller firms like CoreWeave are trading at a premium compared to those leased to major hyperscalers.

### Why it Matters
The difficulty banks face in offloading these loans suggests that the massive capital requirements for AI infrastructure are beginning to strain traditional lending capacity. If credit markets continue to tighten, smaller AI firms may struggle to secure the funding necessary to sustain their rapid growth and capital-intensive operations.
