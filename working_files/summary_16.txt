Meet the robots programmed by kids to speak their Indigenous languages
============================================================

Danielle Boyer, a member of the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians, is empowering Indigenous youth to preserve their native languages by teaching them to build custom SkoBots.

### Key Points
- Danielle Boyer created the SkoBot, a customizable robot designed to help Indigenous children learn languages like Anishinaabemowin.
- The robots use prerecorded audio files rather than generative AI to ensure linguistic accuracy and prevent the creation of fake words.
- Through her nonprofit, Boyer has mentored hundreds of Native American children, including 12-year-old Holden Hoy, in robotics and programming.
- SkoBots are designed to resemble woodland creatures and are decorated with traditional Native American floral patterns.
- The project aims to increase student engagement in STEM fields while protecting sacred cultural knowledge from exploitation by large technology companies.

### Why it Matters
This initiative provides a culturally grounded alternative to mainstream AI tools that often misrepresent or hallucinate Indigenous languages. By putting the technology directly into the hands of youth, the project fosters both technical literacy and a deeper connection to ancestral heritage.
