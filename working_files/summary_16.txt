You Still Can’t Buy Airless Tires Even After A Decade… But You Can 3D Print One
============================================================

YouTuber Berm Peak successfully developed and tested functional 3D-printed airless bicycle tires, demonstrating that consumer-grade technology can outperform long-delayed commercial efforts from major industry players like NASA and Michelin.

### Key Points
- Berm Peak designed and printed modular airless tires using flexible TPU filament on a Bambu Lab H2D consumer 3D printer.
- The design utilizes dovetail joints and screws to assemble segments, allowing for home production without specialized industrial manufacturing equipment.
- Field testing revealed that while the front tire performed well on trails, the rear tire experienced structural failure under high-impact mountain biking conditions.
- The project highlights that airless tire technology is currently accessible to hobbyists, despite years of stalled commercial development from major corporations.
- Liquid tire sealant remains the industry standard for puncture prevention, offering a more practical and proven solution for most cyclists.

### Why it Matters
This development challenges the narrative that airless tire technology requires massive corporate R&D budgets or NASA-level partnerships to achieve functional results. It suggests that consumer-grade additive manufacturing is rapidly closing the gap between experimental prototypes and real-world utility, potentially disrupting traditional cycling supply chains.
