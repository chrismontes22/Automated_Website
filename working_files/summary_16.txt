Race to update services' computer systems after cyber-security flaw found
============================================================

Global organizations are scrambling to patch critical security vulnerabilities in the PHP programming language, which powers approximately 70% of websites, to prevent potential remote code execution attacks.

### Key Points
- The "php2shell" vulnerability allows unauthenticated attackers to remotely execute code on servers running affected versions of the PHP language.
- Cloudflare reported a surge in exploitation attempts, rising from thousands to millions within hours of the vulnerability being publicly disclosed.
- The UK government took 32 services offline as a precautionary measure, while other entities like Wikipedia and Orange also experienced temporary disruptions.
- PHP is a foundational web development language used by major platforms including WordPress, Drupal, and Laravel.
- Cybersecurity experts from the NCSC and NCC Group warn that the window for organizations to apply patches has shrunk to hours or days.

### Why it Matters
Because PHP underpins a vast majority of the internet, these vulnerabilities pose a systemic risk to global digital infrastructure and business continuity. Organizations must prioritize rapid patching to mitigate the threat of remote exploitation, as the speed at which attackers weaponize such flaws continues to accelerate.
