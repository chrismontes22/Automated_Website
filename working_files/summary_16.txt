The hidden tax on enterprise AI: Why data architecture is the ROI problem nobody budgeted for
============================================================

Enterprise AI initiatives often fail to deliver measurable returns because companies underestimate the hidden costs of data gravity and the complexity of managing distributed data architectures.

### Key Points
- A 2025 MIT NANDA report found that 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots fail to produce a measurable impact on profit-and-loss statements.
- "Data gravity" creates a compounding tax on AI programs, as moving large datasets incurs significant costs in bandwidth, security, and operational maintenance.
- Centralized storage strategies often conflict with data sovereignty, regulatory requirements, and existing application dependencies.
- Successful AI deployment requires a "context layer" that allows models to access and reason about data where it resides rather than forcing consolidation.
- Organizations should evaluate data ownership, synchronization, and regulatory restrictions before committing to major infrastructure investments.

### Why it Matters
Many companies treat AI as a compute-heavy challenge, ignoring the long-term operational tax of maintaining data pipelines and governance. By shifting focus from data consolidation to providing AI with accurate, distributed context, businesses can avoid costly architectural failures and improve the reliability of their AI-driven decisions.
