Proposed anti-Flock bills could spell trouble for license plate readers
============================================================

US lawmakers have introduced bipartisan legislation to restrict automated license plate reader networks amid growing concerns regarding data privacy, surveillance accuracy, and the potential for civil rights abuses.

### Key Points
- The proposed "Stop Flock Abuse Act" mandates data encryption, ten-day retention limits, and strict audit requirements for government agencies using license plate reader technology.
- The "Ban Flock Act" seeks to prohibit federal agencies from using these systems and would withhold federal grant funding from state and local governments that deploy them.
- Concerns were highlighted by a Senate hearing where a Florida resident was wrongfully arrested and detained for 13 days due to an inaccurate AI-enabled plate-reader match.
- Flock Safety, a leading supplier of these systems, currently operates approximately 120,000 cameras across the United States, though the total size of connected networks remains debated.
- Proposed regulations aim to prevent the sale of location data to third parties and prohibit the integration of facial-recognition technology into existing plate-reader networks.

### Why it Matters
These legislative proposals signal a shift in the national debate, moving automated license plate readers from a local procurement issue to a significant civil liberties concern. The outcome of these bills will determine whether government agencies can continue building searchable, long-term records of individual movement and daily routines.
