GitHub has alternatives, but no replacement
============================================================

Codeberg’s recent decision to ban projects primarily composed of generative AI code has sparked a broader industry debate regarding the lack of viable, community-focused alternatives to GitHub.

### Key Points
- Codeberg, a mission-driven Git hosting platform, now prohibits projects consisting mostly of AI-generated code.
- Critics argue that the open-source ecosystem lacks a centralized platform that successfully replicates GitHub’s social discovery and contributor identity features.
- Self-hosting options like Gitea or Forgejo often create barriers to entry, requiring users to create new accounts and learn specific workflows for every project.
- Prominent projects like Ghostty have recently moved away from GitHub, citing frequent infrastructure outages and unreliable performance as major obstacles to professional development.
- Alternative platforms such as GitLab, SourceHut, Radicle, and Tangled currently struggle to balance technical decentralization with the user-friendly social layers required for mass adoption.

### Why it Matters
The current reliance on GitHub highlights a significant vulnerability in the open-source community, as no decentralized or mission-driven alternative has successfully scaled a shared social infrastructure. Without a platform that prioritizes reliable collaboration and discovery, developers remain tethered to a service that many increasingly view as technically unstable and misaligned with their needs.
