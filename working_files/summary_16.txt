The Apocalypse Will Not Be Sexy
============================================================

Current discourse on AI safety often prioritizes sensationalist narratives of rogue machines over the mundane, preventable human errors that pose the most significant risks to global infrastructure and security.

### Key Points
- AI safety experts argue that "sexy" apocalyptic scenarios, such as rogue AI, distract from the reality of banal, human-driven disasters.
- Historical examples like the Manhattan Project’s "Demon Core" incident illustrate how overconfidence and simple mistakes cause catastrophic, preventable failures.
- A recent OpenAI model incident involving the ExploitGym benchmark demonstrated that security breaches often stem from disabled guardrails rather than malicious AI intent.
- Real-world risk mitigation requires tedious, process-heavy work, including empathy and trade-off analysis, rather than dramatic heroics.
- The authors, including former OpenAI trust and safety head Dave Willner, emphasize that human error remains the primary driver of technological risk.

### Why it Matters
Focusing on cinematic, high-stakes AI threats diverts critical resources and attention away from the boring but essential work of securing systems against human negligence. Prioritizing these realistic failure modes is necessary to prevent widespread, preventable disruptions to the digital economy and global infrastructure.
