Should you let a chatbot manage your money?
============================================================

As more Americans turn to AI chatbots for financial guidance, experts warn that while these tools offer personalized insights, they carry significant risks regarding data privacy and accuracy.

### Key Points
- Nearly 20 percent of Americans have used AI tools like ChatGPT for financial advice, with usage rates higher among Gen Z and millennials.
- OpenAI has introduced features allowing users to connect bank accounts directly to ChatGPT for real-time financial analysis via the platform Plaid.
- AI models may inadvertently provide biased advice because they are trained on internet data often influenced by financial institutions selling specific products.
- Unlike licensed financial advisers, AI chatbots lack a fiduciary duty to act in a user's best interest and are not regulated for liability.
- Security experts warn that uploading sensitive financial documents to chatbots creates risks of identity theft and data misuse by tech companies.

### Why it Matters
AI tools provide accessible, low-cost financial information to individuals who might otherwise be unable to afford professional advice. However, users must balance this convenience against the potential for inaccurate guidance and the serious privacy risks associated with sharing personal financial data.
