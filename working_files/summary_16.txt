LA for showbiz, NYC for finance: talent clusters still matter in a world shrunk by telecom connectivity
============================================================

The traditional dominance of major American industry hubs like Los Angeles, Silicon Valley, and New York is shifting as rising costs and remote work encourage talent to relocate.

### Key Points
- High costs, tax incentives in other regions, and remote work trends are challenging the long-standing economic dominance of traditional industry clusters.
- Silicon Valley remains the primary hub for global AI innovation, while New York continues to attract major financial firms like Citadel despite affordability concerns.
- Emerging secondary hubs, such as Atlanta for film and Texas for finance, are gaining viability as talent seeks alternatives to expensive coastal cities.
- California’s proposed wealth taxes and quality-of-life issues in major cities are testing the "stickiness" that has historically kept top talent and businesses concentrated.
- Economic clusters provide unique benefits, including professional networking, knowledge sharing, and innovation, which are difficult to replicate in decentralized environments.

### Why it Matters
The potential decline of these industry clusters could reduce tax revenues and public service quality in major cities while slowing the pace of national innovation. If the United States loses its ability to concentrate top talent, the broader economy may become less competitive on the global stage.
