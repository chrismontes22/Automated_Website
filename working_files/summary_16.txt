How AI Is Splitting the Right
============================================================

The American political right is currently divided into three distinct factions regarding artificial intelligence, creating a complex internal debate over the technology's rapid development and future regulatory landscape.

### Key Points
- Skeptics, including Senator Josh Hawley and Governor Ron DeSantis, advocate for strict safeguards to protect workers and communities from Big Tech's influence.
- Techno-optimists, represented by figures like David Sacks and Marc Andreessen, prioritize rapid innovation to maintain a competitive advantage over China.
- A large, moderate group of lawmakers generally favors pro-business policies but remains open to minor concessions on issues like data center energy consumption.
- The debate is influenced by diverse interests, including Christian social thought, national security concerns, and the economic goals of firms like OpenAI and Nvidia.
- Public opinion remains largely wary of AI, with many voters expressing concerns about job displacement and the potential risks of unchecked technological growth.

### Why it Matters
This ideological split within the Republican Party creates significant uncertainty for the future of federal AI policy and the regulatory environment for major tech companies. As the administration navigates these competing factions, the outcome will determine whether the U.S. pursues a path of aggressive, unrestricted development or one defined by protective oversight.
