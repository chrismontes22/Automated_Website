How Israeli firm’s ‘zero-click’ malware attack poses global security risk
============================================================

Researchers have identified a sophisticated "zero-click" spyware campaign by the Israeli firm Candiru that infects devices through malicious advertisements without requiring any user interaction or file downloads.

### Key Points
- Citizen Lab discovered that 45 Al Jazeera staff devices were targeted in June 2025 using spyware that bypasses traditional security by exploiting browser vulnerabilities.
- The Israeli company Discover-Tech facilitated the attacks by posing as a legitimate advertiser to win automated ad auctions and redirect users to infected servers.
- Malicious advertisements were served via major networks including Google, Yandex, and Beeswax, appearing on devices linked to NATO, the UN, and various national defense ministries.
- Once installed, the spyware can capture keystrokes, record microphone and webcam input, and extract sensitive data like emails, messages, and saved credentials.
- The US government previously blacklisted Candiru in 2021 for its role in the illegal surveillance of journalists and activists across 10 countries.

### Why it Matters
This discovery exposes a critical structural vulnerability in the global online advertising ecosystem, which allows malicious actors to deliver code to high-profile targets without needing direct contact. It highlights an urgent need for stricter vetting of ad-tech vendors and improved regulatory oversight to prevent the weaponization of automated advertising auctions.
