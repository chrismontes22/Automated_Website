What Would You Do If Your Employer Could Destroy the World?
============================================================

Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon and several other industry veterans have publicly resigned from top AI labs, citing concerns over reckless development and existential risks posed by artificial intelligence.

### Key Points
- Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic in September 2024, protesting the company's aggressive pursuit of autonomous artificial general intelligence.
- Former employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind report a shift toward corporate secrecy and a prioritization of competitive speed over safety.
- Critics argue that internal "safety" teams are often ineffective, leading some researchers to form independent organizations like AVERI and the Coalition of Concerned AI Staff.
- Many researchers face a "systemic bias" toward staying in labs due to high compensation, access to proprietary compute resources, and the prestige of working at the frontier.
- Internal debates persist regarding whether employees can exert more influence by agitating from within or by speaking out as independent whistleblowers.

### Why it Matters
The exodus of experienced researchers highlights a growing internal crisis within the AI industry regarding the ethical implications of rapid model scaling. As these companies transition from research-focused nonprofits to profit-driven conglomerates, the tension between competitive market pressures and long-term existential safety remains a critical point of failure.
