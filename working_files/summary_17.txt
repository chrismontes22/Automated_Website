Act like it is science
============================================================

Economist Tyler Cowen challenges critics of recent AI security incidents to provide empirical, data-driven forecasts regarding the long-term financial impact of potential cybersecurity threats on the industry.

### Key Points
- Tyler Cowen argues that current concerns regarding AI security breaches often lack rigorous, scientific evidence to support their pessimistic outlooks.
- Critics are asked to outline specific methodologies for estimating future cybersecurity costs associated with AI vulnerabilities over the next two to three years.
- The proposal suggests creating numerical cost projections under various regulatory and safety scenarios to allow for future verification.
- Cowen questions the conviction of skeptics by asking if they have shorted stocks or assets in response to their stated fears about AI risks.
- The request aims to shift the public discourse from subjective anxiety toward a more objective, evidence-based analysis of technological risks.

### Why it Matters
This call for empirical rigor highlights a growing tension between speculative fears regarding AI safety and the need for quantifiable economic risk assessment. By demanding specific predictions, the proposal encourages a more disciplined approach to evaluating how emerging technologies impact market stability and corporate security expenditures.
