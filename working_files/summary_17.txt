Drones are the latest buzzy tech aimed at stopping a school shooting. They’re part of a billion-dollar industry
============================================================

School districts across the United States are increasingly adopting advanced security technologies, including specialized drones, to neutralize active shooter threats and improve emergency response times on campus.

### Key Points
- The school security industry is estimated to be worth between $3 billion and $4 billion annually, covering tools ranging from metal detectors to tactical drones.
- Campus Guardian Angel produces drones capable of breaking through windows, communicating via speakerphone, and disorienting armed suspects within 60 seconds.
- Drones are currently deployed in Florida and Georgia schools, with recent expansions into Colorado and potential interest from districts in Texas.
- Legislative efforts like Florida’s Alyssa’s Law have mandated silent panic alarm systems in schools across at least 13 states to accelerate police response.
- Experts note that while physical "hardening" measures are popular, there is limited data on the long-term effectiveness of newer AI and drone technologies.

### Why it Matters
The rapid growth of the school security market reflects a shift toward technological solutions as parents and administrators seek to minimize response times during active shooter incidents. However, critics argue that prioritizing physical hardening over mental health and preventative measures may negatively impact the psychological well-being of students while relying on unproven tools.
