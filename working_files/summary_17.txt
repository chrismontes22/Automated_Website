Can enterprise AI pay for the boom?
============================================================

Enterprise AI adoption is currently driven by individual task automation, but long-term economic viability depends on businesses successfully integrating these tools into their core operating models.

### Key Points
- Enterprise AI investment is currently characterized by high infrastructure spending and "FOMO," with many firms prioritizing competitiveness over immediate, measurable financial returns.
- Productivity gains are currently concentrated in individual tasks, such as coding and customer service, rather than broad, firm-wide income statement improvements.
- The "productivity J-curve" suggests that significant organizational benefits often lag behind initial technology adoption as companies undergo necessary, time-intensive workflow redesigns.
- Experts emphasize a shift toward a "shared accountability model," where businesses must balance AI-driven execution and judgment with human oversight for compliance and security.
- Successful monetization is increasingly moving away from seat-based pricing toward usage-based models that reflect the actual value generated by AI-driven workflows.

### Why it Matters
The transition to an AI-driven economy requires businesses to move beyond simple task automation and fundamentally rewire their internal processes to achieve sustainable growth. If enterprises cannot translate these early productivity gains into repeatable, self-funding revenue before infrastructure supply catches up, the industry faces a significant risk of a market correction.
