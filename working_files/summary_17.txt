Tech CEOs are writing 6,500-word manifestos. Who’s actually reading?
============================================================

Tech CEOs like Mark Zuckerberg and Dario Amodei are publishing lengthy manifestos to justify AI investments, though critics argue these essays lack the clarity of Warren Buffett’s leadership.

### Key Points
- Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word essay on AI, while Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei released a 13,000-word piece titled "Machines of Loving Grace."
- Trump Media is charging high-frequency trading firms up to $100,000 monthly for early access to Donald Trump’s Truth Social posts.
- Canva lowered its revenue growth forecast to 20% due to high AI-delivery costs and a strategic decision to rebuild its product architecture.
- U.S. consumer prices rose 0.1% in July, with KPMG economist Diane Swonk describing the current economic landscape as a "mess" despite inflation relief.

### Why it Matters
These long-form manifestos highlight a growing disconnect between tech founders and the public, as CEOs struggle to build trust while justifying massive AI expenditures. Effective communication remains a critical leadership challenge, as companies must prove their business models are sustainable before they can win over skeptical regulators and consumers.
