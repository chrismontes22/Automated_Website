‘VPNs at Risk’: Tech Coalition Pushes Back on Piracy Blocking Duties for VPNs
============================================================

The i2Coalition released a new report warning that forcing VPN providers to block pirate websites threatens neutral internet infrastructure and risks harming legitimate users and corporate networks.

### Key Points
- The "VPNs at Risk" report argues against using general-purpose internet infrastructure as a tool for copyright enforcement.
- French courts recently ordered major providers, including NordVPN and ExpressVPN, to block pirate sports streaming domains.
- Legal appeals regarding these blocking orders are currently pending before France’s Supreme Court, the Cour de cassation.
- The report highlights that VPNs share underlying technologies with corporate networks, making it difficult to restrict traffic without collateral damage.
- U.S. legislative proposals like the American Copyright Protection Act (ACPA) could mandate that VPNs with over 100,000 users implement site-blocking measures.

### Why it Matters
This debate highlights a growing tension between copyright enforcement efforts and the preservation of a neutral, open internet. If lawmakers successfully mandate blocking at the VPN level, it could set a precedent that forces infrastructure providers to monitor and restrict user traffic, potentially undermining privacy and security for all users.
