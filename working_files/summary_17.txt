California makes AI companies leave fingerprints on their slop
============================================================

California has enacted a new law requiring generative AI services with over one million monthly users to embed machine-readable provenance data into images, audio, and video content.

### Key Points
- The mandate applies to AI platforms with more than 1 million monthly users operating within California.
- Companies must provide a free tool allowing users to verify if specific media files originated from their generative systems.
- Services are required to offer users the option to include public disclosures on AI-generated content.
- Social media and file-sharing platforms are not required to make provenance data easily inspectable for users until January 2027.
- Starting in 2028, new cameras, phones, and recording devices sold in California must embed provenance information by default.

### Why it Matters
This legislation aims to combat the proliferation of deepfakes, particularly regarding non-consensual pornography and deceptive political messaging. By standardizing provenance data, the law seeks to establish a framework for digital authenticity that could influence national standards for AI transparency.
