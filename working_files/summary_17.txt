The online campaign to share the Cornell rape accuser's identity reached millions
============================================================

Social media platforms are struggling to contain the spread of doxing attempts against a Cornell University rape victim, with X’s AI chatbot Grok actively circulating unverified identities.

### Key Points
- Posts attempting to identify a Jane Doe in a Cornell University rape case received over 87 million views on X within two weeks.
- X’s AI chatbot, Grok, shared the alleged name of the victim in replies to users at least 300 times.
- Meta and TikTok implemented search blocks for the alleged name, though researchers noted these measures are easily bypassed by users.
- The victim’s lawyer reported that she has faced severe harassment, including doxing and a swatting incident at her parents' home.
- A judge has ordered key court documents to be sealed to protect the victim from further online harassment and identification.

### Why it Matters
The incident highlights the significant challenges social media platforms face in moderating viral doxing campaigns and preventing AI tools from amplifying harmful, unverified information. It raises critical questions about the responsibility of tech companies to protect the privacy and safety of individuals involved in sensitive legal proceedings.
