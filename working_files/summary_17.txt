Germany’s most popular party targeted by unknown hackers
============================================================

Unknown hackers have leaked over 420,000 internal emails from Germany’s Alternative for Germany (AfD) party, exposing sensitive personal documents and private correspondence of senior party leadership figures.

### Key Points
- The breach involved 281 gigabytes of data, including bank statements, identity documents, and invoices belonging to officials like Alice Weidel and Alexander Gauland.
- The AfD confirmed a third-party hosting provider was compromised in September but claimed current federal email systems remain secure.
- The leaked files include an identity card belonging to Bundestag deputy leader Markus Frohnmaier, according to reports from the newspaper Bild.
- The AfD currently leads national polling with 30% support, significantly outpacing the CDU/CSU bloc despite ongoing efforts by political rivals to pursue a formal party ban.
- German authorities and intelligence agencies have previously investigated the party for alleged extremism, though a court blocked a formal designation earlier this year.

### Why it Matters
The leak provides potential evidence for activists and political opponents currently campaigning to outlaw the AfD on grounds of extremism. This incident intensifies the volatile political climate in Germany as the party continues to gain electoral momentum despite efforts by the establishment to isolate it.
