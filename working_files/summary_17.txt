Kaushik Basu: Is an AI-pocalypse inevitable? Not if we curb Big Tech’s concentrations of wealth
============================================================

Prominent technology leaders and scholars are increasingly warning that rapid advancements in artificial intelligence could pose existential risks to humanity, necessitating urgent global coordination and regulatory intervention.

### Key Points
- Anthropic alignment lead Evan Hubinger estimated a greater than 10% probability that artificial intelligence could cause human extinction within the next decade.
- Industry figures including Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, and Bill Gates have publicly advocated for slowing the development of advanced AI models.
- Experts warn that "recursive self-improvement" allows AI to evolve beyond human control, creating unpredictable and potentially dangerous outcomes.
- Game theory suggests that unilateral corporate development creates a "prisoner's dilemma," making industry-wide and international cooperation essential for safety.
- Economists argue that taxing extreme wealth is necessary to prevent a small group of individuals from using AI to establish global authoritarian control.

### Why it Matters
The rapid development of AI presents a dual threat of existential catastrophe and the consolidation of unprecedented power by a small elite. Addressing these risks requires international cooperation and economic policy shifts to ensure that technological progress does not undermine democratic stability or human safety.
