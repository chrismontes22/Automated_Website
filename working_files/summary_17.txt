AI’s ‘opportunity divide’ could deepen global inequality, UN official warns
============================================================

A senior United Nations official warns that the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence risks deepening global socioeconomic inequalities by widening the digital infrastructure gap between nations.

### Key Points
- Tomas Lamanauskas of the International Telecommunication Union estimates that $2.8 trillion in investment is required by 2030 to bridge the global digital infrastructure divide.
- Africa and Latin America currently account for only 3.3 percent of global data center capacity, while 2.2 billion people worldwide still lack internet access.
- Generative AI adoption rates currently stand at 29 percent in developed nations compared to 16 percent in developing countries.
- AI-driven automation could displace 90 million jobs while potentially creating 170 million new roles, though 40 percent of workplace skills may change by 2030.
- Only seven percent of African languages are currently represented in AI translation tools, limiting accessibility for millions of potential users.

### Why it Matters
The growing AI divide threatens to reverse decades of economic progress by leaving nations with limited connectivity and infrastructure behind in the global digital economy. Ensuring equitable access to technology is essential to prevent the erosion of tax bases and living standards in emerging markets.
