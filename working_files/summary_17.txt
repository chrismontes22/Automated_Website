[Eugene Volokh] Court Notes Apparent AI-Generated Errors in Court Reporter's Transcript
============================================================

The Indiana Court of Appeals criticized a trial transcript riddled with errors, suggesting that the improper use of generative artificial intelligence likely compromised the document's accuracy and reliability.

### Key Points
- Judge Felix of the Indiana Court of Appeals identified numerous typos, misattributed statements, and incorrect names within the official trial transcript for Williams v. State.
- The court noted that errors included attributing a state motion to the trial judge and a defense objection to the bailiff.
- The appellate panel explicitly suggested that generative AI tools were likely used to prepare the transcript without adequate human oversight or proofreading.
- Indiana Appellate Rule 28(B) mandates that court reporters must certify the accuracy of all transcripts submitted for legal review.

### Why it Matters
This case highlights the growing risks of integrating unverified AI tools into legal documentation processes where precision is a fundamental requirement for justice. It serves as a warning to legal professionals that reliance on automated transcription must be balanced with rigorous human verification to maintain the integrity of the judicial record.
