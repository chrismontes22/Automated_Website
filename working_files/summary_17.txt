Life Imitates Art: The $6.3 Billion Binance Hack
============================================================

Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, suffered a record-breaking $6.3 billion theft after attackers compromised a DevOps engineer's laptop to bypass internal security controls and drain funds.

### Key Points
- Attackers utilized an Ethereum EIP-7702 feature to force the exchange's hot wallet to sign 50 unauthorized transactions in a single event.
- Security firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute the breach to the North Korean-linked Lazarus Group, also known as TraderTraitor.
- Approximately $3 billion in stablecoins, including USDT and USDC, were frozen by issuers like Tether and Circle following the incident.
- Roughly $2.4 billion in native Bitcoin and Ethereum remains unrecoverable due to the decentralized nature of those blockchains.
- Binance pledged to cover the remaining losses using its own reserves, claiming the theft represents only about 4% of its total holdings.

### Why it Matters
The incident highlights a fundamental vulnerability in crypto exchanges where centralized human management often overrides the security promises of decentralized blockchain technology. By relying on corporate balance sheets and centralized asset freezes to recover funds, the industry is increasingly functioning like the traditional financial systems it originally sought to replace.
