Proposed anti-Flock bills could spell trouble for license plate readers
============================================================

US lawmakers have introduced bipartisan legislation to restrict automated license plate reader networks amid growing concerns over data privacy, system inaccuracies, and the potential for government surveillance.

### Key Points
- Automated license plate readers (ALPRs) capture vehicle location and time data, creating searchable records of individual travel patterns.
- The proposed "Stop Flock Abuse Act" mandates data encryption, ten-day retention limits, and strict oversight for government agencies using ALPR technology.
- The "Ban Flock Act" seeks to prohibit federal agencies from using ALPR systems and would withhold federal funding from local governments that utilize them.
- Concerns regarding system accuracy were highlighted by a case where a Florida resident was wrongfully arrested for 13 days due to an ALPR data error.
- Flock Safety, a major industry supplier, currently operates approximately 120,000 cameras across the United States.

### Why it Matters
These legislative proposals signal a shift in the national debate regarding the balance between public safety technology and individual civil liberties. The outcome could fundamentally change how law enforcement agencies collect, store, and share location data on private citizens.
