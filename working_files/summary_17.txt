Apple takes down billboard after accusations of normalizing ‘electronic babysitting’
============================================================

Apple has removed a controversial billboard in Milan featuring a toddler holding an iPhone after Italian regulators and citizens raised concerns regarding the normalization of electronic babysitting.

### Key Points
- The advertisement was part of Apple’s "Relax, it’s iPhone" campaign, which highlights device durability in risky scenarios.
- Italy’s children’s rights watchdog, AGIA, received complaints that the image promoted inappropriate screen time for young children.
- The agency referred the matter to the Italian Competition Authority and the national communications regulator for further review.
- Apple replaced the original advertisement on via Melchiorre Gioia with a new billboard promoting the iPhone’s "Find My" tracking capabilities.
- The company has not issued a formal public statement regarding the removal of the campaign imagery.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing sensitivity of global brands toward local cultural standards regarding technology use and child development. It serves as a reminder that marketing campaigns emphasizing product durability can face significant regulatory and public backlash when interpreted as promoting unhealthy digital habits.
