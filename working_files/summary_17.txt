This copy-paste scam surged 517% in six months, enable this setting to let Chrome catch it
============================================================

Google has introduced a new security feature in Chrome designed to detect and warn users about malicious commands associated with the rapidly growing ClickFix social engineering scam.

### Key Points
- ClickFix attacks trick users into copying and running malicious terminal commands disguised as fake error messages or CAPTCHA prompts.
- ESET reported a 517% surge in ClickFix detections over a six-month period, making it the second most common attack vector by H1 2025.
- The new defense analyzes clipboard content and uses Gemini to evaluate webpage intent before alerting users to potential threats.
- Users must manually enable the Enhanced Protection setting within Chrome’s Privacy and security menu to activate these specific safeguards.
- The protection functions across Windows, macOS, and Linux systems to prevent the unintentional installation of malware.

### Why it Matters
This update provides a critical layer of defense against social engineering tactics that exploit user trust to bypass traditional system security. By integrating AI-driven analysis into the browser, Google aims to mitigate the rising threat of command-based malware delivery that targets unsuspecting individuals.
