After years of resisting touchscreen laptops, Apple just proved Windows PCs had a point
============================================================

Apple has officially introduced a touchscreen MacBook Pro, reversing its long-standing public stance against integrating touch interfaces into its laptop lineup after years of dismissing the technology.

### Key Points
- Apple previously argued that vertical touchscreens were ergonomically fatiguing and inferior to indirect input methods like trackpads.
- The company’s shift follows a history of similar reversals, including the adoption of larger iPhone displays and keyboard accessories for the iPad.
- Competitors like Microsoft have utilized touchscreen technology in Windows laptops for over a decade, experimenting with various form factors and hybrid designs.
- The move highlights how corporate messaging often frames product limitations as design philosophies rather than temporary engineering or strategic decisions.

### Why it Matters
This reversal serves as a reminder that corporate product roadmaps are fluid and often driven by evolving market conditions rather than fixed technological truths. Consumers should evaluate hardware based on utility rather than adopting company-led narratives that dismiss features simply because they are not yet available on a specific platform.
