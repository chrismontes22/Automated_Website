Major Video Doorbell Companies Are Being Sued for Privacy Violations. Here’s Why
============================================================

Amazon Ring and Google Nest are facing multiple class-action lawsuits alleging that their video doorbells’ facial recognition features collect biometric data from non-consenting individuals in violation of privacy laws.

### Key Points
- Lawsuits including *Fennessy v. Google* and *Sigwalt v. Amazon* challenge the use of "familiar-face" technology to create biometric faceprints.
- Plaintiffs argue that these devices capture and analyze images of neighbors and passersby without obtaining explicit consent.
- Ring and Nest have already disabled facial recognition features in jurisdictions like Illinois and Texas due to strict biometric privacy regulations.
- The legal complaints cite various state statutes, including Virginia’s Computer Crimes Act and California privacy laws, to contest data collection practices.
- Google previously settled a Texas biometric data lawsuit for $1.4 billion in 2025, highlighting the significant legal risks associated with facial recognition.

### Why it Matters
These lawsuits highlight a growing legal conflict between consumer home security features and individual privacy rights regarding biometric data. If the plaintiffs succeed, tech companies may be forced to restrict or remove facial recognition capabilities across the United States to avoid further litigation.
