Taiwan says AI agents helped hack its government in four days
============================================================

Taiwan’s Ministry of Digital Affairs reported that hackers utilized autonomous AI agents to infiltrate government agencies and steal sensitive personnel records during a four-day campaign in July.

### Key Points
- The cyberattack targeted multiple government bodies, including the Ministry of Justice and a nuclear-safety agency, starting around July 20.
- Attackers employed AI agents like "Open Claw" to accelerate credential theft and data extraction, completing tasks in four days that previously required weeks of human labor.
- Officials confirmed that the affected agencies have addressed the breaches and investigated the attack methods, though no specific threat actor was identified.
- Cybersecurity firm Dream initially identified the campaign, which highlights how AI lowers the barrier to entry for sophisticated digital espionage.
- Taiwan remains a primary testing ground for advanced cyber-techniques due to its high level of digitization and ongoing geopolitical tensions.

### Why it Matters
The use of AI agents as an accelerant for cyberattacks fundamentally shifts the defensive landscape by drastically compressing the time available for security teams to detect and respond to breaches. This evolution forces organizations to rethink their cybersecurity strategies as the cost and technical expertise required to launch state-level attacks continue to decline.
