US charges 14 over North Korean IT worker scheme at more than 70 companies
============================================================

The U.S. Department of Justice has charged 14 individuals for operating a sophisticated scheme where North Korean IT workers infiltrated over 70 American companies using stolen identities.

### Key Points
- Prosecutors allege the workers earned $3 million to fund North Korean state activities while working remotely in sectors like healthcare, telecommunications, and manufacturing.
- The indictment names 14 defendants, including two U.S. nationals from Pennsylvania and Texas and 10 individuals based in China, on charges of wire fraud and money laundering.
- Between 2019 and 2025, the group utilized "laptop farms" in the U.S. to mask their overseas locations and bypass corporate security protocols.
- The FBI recently seized 187 devices from over 40 locations in Texas suspected of facilitating these remote work operations.
- Security firm CrowdStrike identifies the group behind these activities as "Famous Chollima," noting their use of malware and AI tools to target software developers.

### Why it Matters
This scheme highlights significant vulnerabilities in remote hiring practices that allow foreign actors to infiltrate sensitive U.S. corporate networks. Companies must now implement more rigorous identity verification and device monitoring to prevent state-sponsored entities from siphoning revenue and accessing proprietary data.
