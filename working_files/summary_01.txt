Massive GovTech breach leaks 42 million documents — including thousands packed with personal information
============================================================

A misconfigured database linked to GovTech firm CitySourced exposed 42 million documents and 450,000 files containing sensitive personal information, including names, contact details, and internal municipal complaint records.

### Key Points
- Cybersecurity researcher Jeremiah Fowler discovered the exposed database, which contained records dating from 2016 to 2026.
- The repository included 450,000 JSON files containing personally identifiable information (PII) such as email addresses, phone numbers, and postal addresses.
- CitySourced, a company acquired by Granicus in 2022, secured the database following the disclosure.
- The exposed data included images of code violation reports and internal logs, alongside potentially malicious .exe and .sdk files.
- Experts recommend that organizations implement tenant isolation, encryption, and strict access controls to prevent similar cloud storage misconfigurations.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the persistent risk of data exposure caused by misconfigured cloud storage within the government technology sector. Such leaks provide cybercriminals with the necessary information to conduct targeted phishing attacks, potentially leading to identity theft or further security compromises for affected residents.
