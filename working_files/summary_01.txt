I gave my smart home its own DNS and finally stopped my privacy settings from breaking it
============================================================

Implementing separate DNS policies for smart home devices and personal computers allows users to maintain aggressive privacy protections while preventing IoT connectivity issues caused by over-blocking.

### Key Points
- Network administrators can use Pi-hole or AdGuard Home to assign distinct DNS filtering policies to specific device groups or isolated 2.4 GHz IoT networks.
- Creating a dedicated IoT DNS lane prevents smart devices from losing connectivity due to blocked telemetry or authentication endpoints required for basic functionality.
- Using local DNS records, such as the RFC 8375-compliant .home.arpa suffix, simplifies internal network management by replacing hard-coded IP addresses with human-readable hostnames.
- Analyzing query logs for a specific device class makes it easier to identify and whitelist necessary domains without compromising the security of the broader network.
- Maintaining a secondary resolver and implementing firewall rules for port 53 ensures consistent policy enforcement and prevents devices from bypassing local DNS settings.

### Why it Matters
Segmenting network traffic by device type balances the need for robust privacy with the operational requirements of cloud-dependent smart home hardware. This architectural approach reduces maintenance overhead by eliminating the need to constantly adjust global blocklists when individual appliances fail.
