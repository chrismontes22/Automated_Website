Say Hello to RatHat, a New AI-Powered Malware Invading the Android Ecosystem
============================================================

Security researchers have identified RatHat, a sophisticated AI-powered malware targeting Android devices that gains administrative control by tricking users into granting excessive accessibility permissions through fake app downloads.

### Key Points
- Zimperium discovered RatHat, which mimics the Google Play Store to trick users into downloading malicious versions of legitimate apps like Google Chrome.
- The malware exploits Android accessibility permissions to enable Wireless Debugging and grant itself admin-level ADB Shell access.
- Attackers use an AI-assisted agent to steal sensitive data, including usernames, passwords, two-factor authentication codes, and touchscreen input.
- Researchers have identified 162 infected apps linked to attackers in China, primarily targeting financial platforms like WeChat Pay and Alipay.
- Because the malware installs hidden secondary files, a full factory reset is the only reliable method to completely remove the infection from a device.

### Why it Matters
This threat highlights the critical danger of granting broad accessibility permissions to untrusted applications, which can bypass standard Android security protections. Users must remain vigilant against phishing attempts and avoid downloading apps from unofficial websites to prevent total device compromise.
