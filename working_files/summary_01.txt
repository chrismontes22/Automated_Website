Abandoned Popcorn Time apps had to be disabled to prevent a zombie army
============================================================

Security researchers have neutralized a massive botnet risk by hijacking abandoned domains associated with the pirated streaming application Popcorn Time to remotely disable the software’s vulnerable update mechanism.

### Key Points
- Eye Security discovered that the "Time4Popcorn" fork remained active on millions of Windows, macOS, and Android devices worldwide.
- The software’s unencrypted update process allowed attackers to execute arbitrary code with full system privileges on infected machines.
- Researchers registered abandoned domains to push a benign update that disabled the insecure service on Windows and macOS systems.
- Over 2 million update downloads were distributed by March before the domains were transferred to the non-profit Shadowserver Foundation.
- The security team bypassed potential malware flags from antivirus software to ensure the disabling script could reach affected devices.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the significant security risks posed by abandoned software that continues to communicate with expired or hijacked web domains. Users should audit their devices for legacy applications to prevent them from being exploited as part of a global botnet or surveillance network.
