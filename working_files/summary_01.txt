I stopped using free VPNs after I learned what they do with your traffic
============================================================

Many free VPN services compromise user privacy by leaking data, tracking browsing habits, or selling personal information to third parties, necessitating caution when selecting a secure connection tool.

### Key Points
- Research from MVPNalyzer indicates that over 80% of free Android VPNs send user data to known advertising and tracking domains.
- Common security failures include leaking DNS queries, exposing IPv6 traffic, and transmitting data in plaintext without encryption.
- Providers like Urban VPN have been caught harvesting user data, including AI chat logs, even when the application is disabled.
- Trustworthy free VPNs, such as Proton VPN and Windscribe, typically feature open-source clients, independent security audits, and transparent business models.
- Self-hosting a VPN using WireGuard on a virtual private server offers a secure alternative that eliminates reliance on third-party providers.

### Why it Matters
Using unreliable free VPNs can expose sensitive metadata and browsing history to malicious actors or data brokers rather than protecting it. Choosing audited, transparent services or self-hosted solutions is essential for maintaining genuine digital privacy and security.
