Can AI break encryption? OpenAI’s new math has cryptographers divided
============================================================

OpenAI recently released 722 mathematical manuscripts generated by artificial intelligence, sparking expert debate over whether advanced models could eventually compromise the encryption systems securing global digital communications.

### Key Points
- OpenAI published 722 AI-generated math papers, though three were retracted shortly after release due to inaccuracies.
- Cryptographers like Matthew Green warn that AI could potentially identify mathematical shortcuts, threatening the security of public-key encryption algorithms.
- Research from ETH Zurich and Anthropic shows AI models are rapidly improving at cryptanalysis, including finding weaknesses in quantum-resistant schemes like HAWK.
- Experts like Yehuda Lindell and Bart Preneel argue there is currently no evidence that AI has broken any widely deployed cryptographic standards.
- Security professionals emphasize that stealing encryption keys remains a more immediate and practical threat than the theoretical mathematical collapse of encryption.

### Why it Matters
Public-key cryptography serves as the foundation for secure online commerce, banking, and private messaging. If AI models successfully discover fundamental mathematical flaws, it could force a massive, high-risk global infrastructure upgrade to protect sensitive digital data.
