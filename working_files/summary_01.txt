Switzerland begins testing alternatives to Microsoft 365 to protect its "most sensitive data" and strengthen digital sovereignty
============================================================

The Swiss federal government has launched a pilot program to test alternatives to Microsoft 365, aiming to enhance digital sovereignty and protect sensitive data from potential foreign interference.

### Key Points
- The trial will transition 3,000 workstations, representing 7% of federal administrative staff, to non-Microsoft software suites.
- Professor Matthias Stürmer of the Bern University of Applied Sciences cited concerns regarding data leaks and rising licensing costs as primary drivers for the shift.
- Switzerland joins other European nations, including France, in seeking to reduce reliance on American-made software for government operations.
- Officials are evaluating sovereign solutions like "Euro-Office" that offer collaborative document editing while keeping data under local control.
- The pilot remains an exploratory phase, and the government may choose to retain Microsoft 365 if the alternative software fails to meet performance requirements.

### Why it Matters
This move highlights a growing trend among European governments to prioritize digital sovereignty by reducing dependence on major American technology providers. If successful, the transition could set a precedent for other nations to migrate sensitive administrative infrastructure toward localized, independent software ecosystems.
