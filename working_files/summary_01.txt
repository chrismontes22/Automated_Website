Inside the race to train robots on human work
============================================================

Startups are paying thousands of workers globally to record their daily physical tasks, creating massive datasets intended to train humanoid robots for future labor in homes and factories.

### Key Points
- Companies like Microagi, Mecka AI, and Human Archive use head-mounted cameras and sensor-equipped gloves to capture human movements, force, and physical interactions.
- Demand for robot training data has surged tenfold in the past year, with robotics firms seeking up to 100,000 hours of annotated footage to improve machine reliability.
- Global data collection efforts span over 30 countries, with workers performing tasks ranging from cooking and cleaning to repairing jet engines.
- The AI training dataset market is projected to grow from $11.9 billion this year to nearly $49.8 billion by 2031.
- Robotics companies raised $33.4 billion in the first half of 2026, driven by investor interest in automating labor and addressing workforce shortages.

### Why it Matters
The race to build functional humanoid robots is currently bottlenecked by a lack of real-world training data, turning human labor into a critical commodity for AI development. While this creates immediate income for global workers, it simultaneously accelerates the automation of the very tasks they are being paid to record.
