LLMs Aren't Inevitable
============================================================

The widespread adoption of large language models is not an inevitable technological progression, but rather a contingent choice that fails to meet the criteria for overwhelming competitive necessity.

### Key Points
- A technology is only "inevitable" if it provides massive, unavoidable advantages that force competitors to adopt it or face obsolescence.
- Historical examples like firearms demonstrate true inevitability, whereas tools like the magnetic compass remained useful but optional.
- LLMs currently lack the capability to perform high-level, complex work, limiting their utility to mediocre or low-stakes tasks.
- The author compares LLMs to chemical weapons, which persist as niche tools for weak actors but offer no strategic advantage to high-performing organizations.
- Social resistance and the high cost of filtering AI-generated "slop" may eventually neutralize the perceived benefits of LLM integration.

### Why it Matters
- Understanding the limitations of AI helps businesses avoid over-investing in tools that provide only marginal efficiency gains rather than true competitive superiority. By recognizing that LLMs are not an inevitable replacement for human expertise, organizations can prioritize high-quality, human-led work to maintain a distinct market advantage.
