Suspected Chinese hackers posed as US AI insiders, Proofpoint says
============================================================

Chinese hackers identified as TA419 are impersonating prominent American AI experts to infiltrate email accounts belonging to policy analysts at think tanks, universities, and major law firms.

### Key Points
- Security firm Proofpoint linked the TA419 hacking group to China based on specific malware, server infrastructure, and strategic targeting patterns.
- Attackers posed as high-profile figures, including former White House official Lynne Parker and employees from the AI company Anthropic.
- The campaign targeted fewer than ten individuals involved in AI regulation, export controls, and national strategy development.
- Hackers utilized phishing tactics, including requests to join fictitious policy panels, to distribute malware or harvest user credentials.
- Operations have been active since at least 2025, focusing on organizations across the United States and Japan.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a shift in state-sponsored cyber espionage toward influencing the development of international AI policy rather than simple intellectual property theft. By targeting key regulatory influencers, these actors aim to gain strategic insight into how the United States and its allies formulate future technology controls.
