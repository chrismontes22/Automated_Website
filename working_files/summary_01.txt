PHP: The three letters that shut down sites all over the internet today
============================================================

Critical vulnerabilities discovered in the PHP programming language have forced major websites, government agencies, and gaming services offline as experts warn of potential unauthenticated remote code execution.

### Key Points
- OpenAI and security firm Wiz identified two critical PHP vulnerabilities, designated CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, using advanced AI models.
- The flaws allow attackers to execute unauthorized code, access sensitive databases, and gain full control over servers without requiring administrator credentials.
- Affected platforms include Wikipedia, DeviantArt, 4chan, and various government portals in the Netherlands and Finland, alongside services like Pokémon Home.
- PHP powers approximately 69.3% of all websites, making the potential attack surface for these vulnerabilities exceptionally large across the global internet.
- Security agencies, including the NSA and the UK’s National Cyber Security Center, have advised organizations to take vulnerable systems offline if immediate patching is not possible.

### Why it Matters
These vulnerabilities represent a significant shift in cybersecurity, as AI-assisted research has successfully uncovered complex, long-standing flaws that were previously considered too difficult to exploit. Because PHP underpins a vast majority of the web, the risk of widespread data breaches and server hijacking remains high until hosting providers and site administrators apply necessary security updates.
