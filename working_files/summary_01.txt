AI scammers are scouring obituaries to target widows — one lost $7,000 after being told her late husband evaded taxes
============================================================

Fraudsters are increasingly using artificial intelligence to scan public obituaries for personal information, enabling them to target grieving widows with sophisticated extortion schemes and financial scams.

### Key Points
- Scammers use AI to rapidly extract names, locations, and family details from online obituaries to build convincing, personalized fraud scripts.
- One victim lost $7,000 after being falsely told she needed to pay to cover her late husband’s alleged tax evasion.
- Fraudsters leverage AI tools to generate deepfake audio and video, allowing them to contact thousands of potential victims within minutes.
- FBI data shows Americans aged 60 and older reported $7.7 billion in cyber-enabled crime losses in 2025, a 59% increase from the previous year.
- The average loss per elderly scam case reached $38,500, highlighting the growing financial threat posed by automated, data-driven fraud campaigns.

### Why it Matters
This trend demonstrates how readily available public data can be weaponized by AI to exploit vulnerable populations during periods of personal crisis. As these automated scams become more efficient and harder to detect, they pose a significant risk to the financial security of older Americans and necessitate increased vigilance regarding personal information shared online.
