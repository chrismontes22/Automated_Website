Security Bite: The record $6.3B Binance heist started with a fake Homebrew install on Mac
============================================================

Hackers stole $6.3 billion from the cryptocurrency exchange Binance after compromising a developer’s Mac through social engineering tactics and a malicious coding assignment disguised as a job interview.

### Key Points
- Attackers successfully exfiltrated $6.3 billion from Binance, marking the largest financial heist in history.
- The breach originated on an Apple silicon Mac, evidenced by malware targeting the /opt/homebrew directory.
- Hackers utilized a two-phase social engineering campaign, starting with a malicious Homebrew installer in June and a fake job interview in September.
- Security experts suspect North Korean hackers orchestrated the attack by tricking a DevOps engineer into running a malicious coding script.
- Binance managed to freeze approximately $3 billion of the stolen funds following the October 29 discovery of the theft.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the growing vulnerability of enterprise environments where developers frequently use macOS for high-stakes financial infrastructure. It demonstrates that even sophisticated organizations remain susceptible to basic social engineering, emphasizing the need for stricter endpoint security beyond standard operating system warnings.
