Android 17 has some new tricks to keep your web traffic private
============================================================

Google is introducing significant network security upgrades in Android 17, including Encrypted Client Hello support and new protections against malicious SMS Blaster attacks to enhance user privacy.

### Key Points
- Android 17 implements Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) to encrypt DNS server lookups and prevent third-party tracking of visited websites.
- New carrier-integrated defenses allow mobile networks to block low-security 2G connections, mitigating risks from SMS Blaster and "stingray" surveillance devices.
- The update restricts app access to local network information to prevent device fingerprinting and the identification of vulnerable hardware.
- Certificate Transparency is now enabled by default to ensure devices verify the authenticity of SSL certificates during server communication.

### Why it Matters
These updates represent a major shift in mobile privacy by closing long-standing loopholes in DNS traffic and cellular network security. By broadly adopting ECH, Google aims to set a new industry standard that encourages wider server-side support for encrypted web browsing.
