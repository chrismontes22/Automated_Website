Antino Backdoor Uses Outlook and OneDrive for C2 in China-Nexus Espionage Campaign
============================================================

A China-nexus threat actor identified as UAT-11587 is deploying a sophisticated Rust-based backdoor called Antino to target government and policy organizations across Asia and the Middle East.

### Key Points
- Cisco Talos identified the UAT-11587 cluster, which has compromised 16 entities across eight countries, including Taiwan, India, and the Philippines.
- The Antino backdoor uses Microsoft 365 services, specifically Outlook and OneDrive, as a command-and-control channel to evade traditional network detection.
- Attackers utilize highly tailored spear-phishing emails that spoof Gmail attachment previews to bypass security filters and deceive high-profile targets.
- The malware employs DLL sideloading via a legitimate Microsoft-signed binary, "GatherOsState.exe," to maintain persistence on infected Windows systems.
- Evidence linking the campaign to China includes Simplified Chinese metadata, specific Cargo registry paths, and targeting themes consistent with regional geopolitical interests.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a growing trend of threat actors abusing legitimate cloud infrastructure to conduct stealthy, long-term espionage against government and diplomatic entities. By leveraging trusted platforms like Microsoft 365, attackers significantly complicate detection efforts for security teams relying on traditional traffic monitoring.
