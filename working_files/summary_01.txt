This Week in Security: FBI Gets Hacked, Muse Vulnerable to ClickFix, Popular Rust Developers at Risk, and New Attacks Against RSA
============================================================

The cybercriminal group ShinyHunters has compromised the FBI’s jobs website via an Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability, exfiltrating 2 TB of sensitive data from AWS GovCloud instances in the process.

### Key Points
- ShinyHunters exploited a zero-day vulnerability in Oracle PeopleSoft to breach the FBI’s recruitment portal and AWS GovCloud.
- Meta’s Muse agent for macOS contains a critical flaw allowing attackers to hijack microphone, screen recording, and authentication tokens via malicious terminal commands.
- North Korean state-linked hackers are targeting Rust language maintainers with sophisticated phishing lures to facilitate supply chain attacks on the Cargo repository.
- The hacker known as NightmareEclipse released "BigDiskBuster," a proof-of-concept exploit that disables Windows Defender updates.
- Researchers identified a new attack method against RSA encryption that significantly reduces the computational difficulty required to break 1024-bit, 2048-bit, and 4096-bit keys.

### Why it Matters
These incidents highlight a growing trend of high-stakes vulnerabilities affecting both government infrastructure and widely used consumer software. As attackers refine methods like "ClickFix" and supply chain poisoning, organizations and individual users face increased risks of data theft and system compromise.
