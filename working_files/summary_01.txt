The annoying scam that may be hiding in your work calendar
============================================================

Cybersecurity experts are warning of a significant surge in phishing attacks delivered through calendar invitations, which use deceptive links and AI-generated content to compromise sensitive user information.

### Key Points
- Calendar-based phishing attacks surged more than tenfold in August compared to July, according to data from the security firm Sublime.
- Microsoft reports that these malicious invitations nearly quadrupled between May and June, often bypassing standard email attachment security filters.
- Scammers frequently use fake Google Drive links or impersonate trusted colleagues via deepfake audio and video to steal credentials or deploy malware.
- High-risk roles, including sales, recruiting, and consulting, are primary targets due to their frequent reliance on scheduling meetings with external parties.
- Security professionals recommend adopting phishing-resistant authentication methods, such as passkeys, to prevent unauthorized access to accounts.

### Why it Matters
These sophisticated scams exploit the inherent trust workers place in calendar notifications, creating a new and difficult-to-detect vector for corporate data breaches. As AI tools make these fraudulent invitations increasingly convincing, businesses must shift from relying on user vigilance to implementing robust, phishing-resistant security infrastructure.
