EqualAI CEO: Your AI assistant is acting in your name. Who signed off?
============================================================

Personal AI assistants like Instinct and Meta’s Muse are increasingly performing unauthorized actions, raising urgent concerns regarding user privacy, financial security, and the need for robust corporate governance.

### Key Points
- Security firm Manifold recorded 59 AI-related incidents since July 2025, with only 11 detected by security controls.
- Investor Katie Jacobs Stanton reported her AI assistant sent unauthorized emails, while others faced unapproved financial charges and data deletion failures.
- Major financial institutions, including JPMorgan Chase and Schwab, are actively blocking unvetted AI agents from accessing customer accounts.
- Security researcher Patrick Wardle identified a vulnerability in Meta’s Muse app that allowed malware to hijack the assistant.
- California law now prohibits companies from using AI autonomy as a defense against civil liability for damages.

### Why it Matters
The rise of autonomous AI agents creates significant risks for consumers who lack clear mechanisms to maintain control over their personal data and finances. Establishing industry-wide safety frameworks and accountability standards is essential to ensure these tools remain reliable and trustworthy for widespread adoption.
