Dutch tax office drops Microsoft 365 cloud for own servers and open source
============================================================

The Dutch tax and customs administration is abandoning its transition to Microsoft 365 in favor of self-hosted servers and European open-source software to ensure greater digital autonomy.

### Key Points
- State Secretary for Finance Eelco Eerenberg announced the shift to on-premises infrastructure for the Belastingdienst, Customs, and the Benefits Agency.
- Email and calendar services will migrate to internal servers in 2027, with collaboration and storage tools following by 2028.
- The government’s ICT review board cited concerns regarding vendor lock-in, insufficient data encryption, and a lack of a viable exit strategy.
- Approximately 5,000 of the agency's 47,500 staff members had already transitioned to Microsoft 365 before the project was paused in July.
- The agency is repurposing existing data center capacity and licenses to manage costs while developing replacements for missing records management features.

### Why it Matters
This decision reflects a growing trend among European government agencies to prioritize digital sovereignty and reduce reliance on major American cloud providers. By moving away from a single-vendor ecosystem, the Dutch administration aims to mitigate security risks and maintain greater control over sensitive citizen data.
