Personal Computing 2.0: It's time for a personal computing revolution
============================================================

The proposed Personal Computing 2.0 paradigm seeks to shift digital power back to individual users by prioritizing data ownership, local control, and interoperability over centralized Big Tech ecosystems.

### Key Points
- Personal Computing 2.0 advocates for storing data locally rather than in the cloud to prevent vendor lock-in and subscription-based access restrictions.
- The model promotes cross-platform compatibility, allowing users to integrate various AI agents and software tools regardless of the underlying hardware or provider.
- By leveraging open-weight AI models, users can run powerful intelligence locally on personal devices like laptops or Raspberry Pis.
- The framework aims to solve modern privacy concerns by returning to the foundational principle that a user’s data should reside entirely under their own control.
- Advanced serverless computing and AI-driven interfaces are intended to make complex programming and data management accessible to non-engineers.

### Why it Matters
This shift represents a direct challenge to the current "closed" AI and SaaS-dominated landscape that has increasingly restricted user autonomy. By reclaiming data sovereignty, individuals can potentially mitigate the risks of platform dependency and foster a more creative, personalized digital environment.
