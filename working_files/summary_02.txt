Iran threatens Gulf data centres: How exposed is the region’s internet?
============================================================

Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei has warned that regional data centers and undersea internet cables could face attacks if hostilities between Iran and the United States escalate further.

### Key Points
- Over 90 percent of data capacity between Europe and Asia relies on undersea cables passing through the narrow Bab al-Mandeb strait.
- The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has identified cloud infrastructure belonging to major firms like Google, Microsoft, and Oracle as potential targets.
- Amazon Web Services reported physical damage to facilities in the UAE and Bahrain following drone strikes earlier this year.
- Cargo ship incidents and regional conflict have previously caused significant undersea cable disruptions, impacting internet connectivity across the Middle East and South Asia.
- Gulf nations are currently pursuing alternative land-based data routes through Iraq to reduce reliance on vulnerable maritime infrastructure.

### Why it Matters
These threats jeopardize the ambitious digital transformation strategies of Gulf states that rely on deep integration with American technology companies. By targeting critical infrastructure, Iran is effectively turning regional economic stability into a geopolitical bargaining chip in its ongoing standoff with the United States.
