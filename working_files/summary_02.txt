Canada's Bill C-22: VPN and tech firms urge the government to 'fix' the bill before it becomes law
============================================================

Major privacy providers including Windscribe, NordVPN, and ExpressVPN are threatening to exit Canada if the government passes Bill C-22, which mandates weakened encryption and increased user metadata retention.

### Key Points
- A coalition of tech firms and civil liberties groups signed an open letter demanding significant amendments to the proposed Lawful Access Act.
- Bill C-22 would require digital service providers to create backdoors into encrypted systems and store user metadata for up to six months.
- Cybersecurity experts warn that mandated backdoors create systemic vulnerabilities that could be exploited by criminal actors and foreign adversaries.
- Windscribe CEO Yegor Sak stated that the legislation makes Canada an untenable operating environment, prompting the company to consider relocating its headquarters.
- Major tech entities, including Google, have joined the opposition, arguing the bill establishes a dangerous surveillance infrastructure that threatens global digital security.

### Why it Matters
The potential exodus of top-tier privacy providers could leave Canadian consumers with limited access to secure digital tools and encrypted communication services. This standoff highlights a growing global conflict between government surveillance mandates and the fundamental right to private, secure online infrastructure.
