US long-term borrowing costs ease after government steps in
============================================================

The United States national debt has officially surpassed $40 trillion, doubling over the last decade due to sustained federal spending and rising interest costs on government borrowing.

### Key Points
- The national debt reached $40.05 trillion as of August 18, significantly outpacing previous Congressional Budget Office projections for fiscal year 2026.
- The US debt-to-GDP ratio currently stands at 125.8%, ranking among the highest of the world's major economies.
- Treasury Department officials announced plans to double bond buyback operations to $4 billion to provide liquidity and stabilize long-term borrowing costs.
- Yields on 30-year Treasury bonds recently hit 5.34%, the highest level in nearly two decades, impacting consumer mortgage and credit card rates.
- The Federal Reserve continues to monitor inflation risks, with policymakers signaling that further interest rate hikes may be necessary to reach a 2% inflation target.

### Why it Matters
The rapid accumulation of federal debt increases the government's interest burden, which can constrain future fiscal policy and influence broader economic stability. Sustained high borrowing costs may eventually limit consumer access to affordable credit and increase the risk of long-term financial market volatility.
