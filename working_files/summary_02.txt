FBI tip line and veterans crisis site among 350 federal web addresses exposing visitors to network spies, senator warns
============================================================

Senator Ron Wyden is urging federal agencies to adopt Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) to protect user privacy by masking the specific websites visited by citizens on federal domains.

### Key Points
- Senator Wyden identified 350 federal web addresses, including FBI and VA portals, that currently fail to use ECH to hide site names from network observers.
- ECH encrypts the Server Name Indication (SNI) field, preventing third parties from logging which specific subpages a user accesses during an encrypted HTTPS session.
- The senator requested that CISA and the OMB mandate ECH implementation and require it as a condition for future federal cloud and network service contracts.
- Agencies have until November 20, 2026, to provide an unclassified response and a formal action plan regarding these privacy upgrades.
- While some providers like Cloudflare support ECH, others like Amazon Web Services currently do not, complicating the proposed federal procurement requirements.

### Why it Matters
Implementing ECH would prevent internet service providers and malicious actors from tracking sensitive user activity, such as visits to whistleblower hotlines or crisis counseling services. This initiative forces a broader conversation about whether the federal government should use its massive purchasing power to compel private cloud vendors to adopt modern privacy standards.
