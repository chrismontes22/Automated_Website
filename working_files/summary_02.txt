These seaside apartments were a ghost city - then police say scammers moved in
============================================================

Malaysia’s $100 billion Forest City project has become a hub for transnational cryptocurrency scam syndicates, as high vacancy rates and modern infrastructure attract criminal operations seeking anonymity.

### Key Points
- Malaysian police recently arrested 335 individuals across 32 Forest City properties for running international investment and love scams.
- The seized assets included over 1,500 mobile phones and 300 computers used to facilitate fraudulent activities from residential apartments.
- Originally designed for 700,000 residents, the project remains largely vacant, with current occupancy estimated at less than 5,000 people.
- Experts suggest criminal syndicates are migrating to Malaysia after facing increased pressure and government crackdowns in Cambodia.
- The project was developed by Country Garden and Danga 88 but stalled due to the pandemic and China’s property market slump.

### Why it Matters
The transformation of Forest City into a base for organized crime illustrates how failing mega-projects can inadvertently provide cover for sophisticated, multinational fraud networks. By embedding themselves within legitimate-looking commercial developments, these syndicates exploit low occupancy and modern connectivity to operate with minimal oversight.
