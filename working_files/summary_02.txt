This Week in Security: New Spectre Attacks, Crushing Quantity of Linux Vulns, Google Gets Too Much AI, and Hacking Lawnmowers
============================================================

New research reveals that JIT compilers can be exploited to bypass existing Spectre-v2 mitigations, potentially exposing sensitive data by tricking processors into executing cached, vulnerable instruction sets.

### Key Points
- Researchers successfully tested the JIT-based Spectre-v2 attack against Firefox’s SpiderMonkey, the Linux kernel’s eBPF, and Python’s GraalVM.
- The attack uses self-modifying code to force CPUs to execute cached instructions, effectively bypassing kernel-level security patches designed to stop branch prediction manipulation.
- High-security environments, such as iOS and macOS, already mitigate similar risks by disabling JIT compilation entirely.
- While the attack is currently a research-level proof of concept, it highlights the evolving nature of speculative execution vulnerabilities in modern computing.

### Why it Matters
This discovery demonstrates that even well-patched systems remain vulnerable to sophisticated side-channel attacks that target the fundamental way processors handle speculative execution. It underscores the ongoing tension between the performance benefits of JIT compilation and the security risks inherent in complex, high-speed computing architectures.
