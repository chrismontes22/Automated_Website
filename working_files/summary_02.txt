This Week in Security: Critical DNS Keys Rotated, Hacking Freezers Isn’t Cool, OpenAI Spies On Itself, and Advanced Hardware Wallet Hacks
============================================================

The global internet infrastructure successfully completed a critical DNSSEC root key rollover on October 11, 2026, marking a significant update to the system’s underlying chain of trust.

### Key Points
- The DNSSEC root key, which validates DNS responses to prevent spoofing, was updated following a year-long distribution process that began in January 2025.
- A ransomware recovery firm CEO faces fraud charges for allegedly charging victims $18 million while secretly paying attackers only $8 million in ransom.
- Internet-connected Danfoss refrigeration controllers were exploited in France and at U.S. military bases, causing food spoilage due to easily guessable "admin" passwords.
- Hardware implants in Ledger Nano X wallets have enabled the theft of approximately $102 million in cryptocurrency by capturing recovery seeds directly from device screens.
- An OpenAI employee was charged with espionage after using the company's own AI to help design and hide listening devices within office meeting rooms.

### Why it Matters
These events highlight the persistent vulnerability of both legacy internet protocols and modern consumer hardware to sophisticated, targeted attacks. As security threats evolve from simple software exploits to complex physical supply-chain tampering, users and organizations must adopt more rigorous verification processes to maintain digital and physical integrity.
