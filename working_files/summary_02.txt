Ring Gave Itself a Security Makeover Called TAKE. Here’s What’s Actually Changing
============================================================

Ring is introducing a new encryption standard called Throw Away the Key Encryption this September to improve data transparency and security for its cloud-based camera video analysis.

### Key Points
- The new Throw Away the Key Encryption (TAKE) standard uses rotating keys to secure video data processed by Ring’s artificial intelligence features.
- Ring will use a one-time key to access and analyze footage for enabled features, then immediately delete that key to prevent further access.
- Users retain a master key for their cloud storage, which remains accessible for up to 180 days depending on the subscription plan.
- The update will be enabled by default for all Ring subscribers starting in September.
- Existing end-to-end encryption options remain available for users who prefer to disable cloud-based AI features entirely for increased privacy.

### Why it Matters
This update addresses long-standing privacy concerns regarding how Ring accesses and utilizes customer video data for its AI-driven analysis tools. By implementing a more transparent, time-limited access model, the company aims to balance the demand for smart home features with the necessity of protecting sensitive user footage.
