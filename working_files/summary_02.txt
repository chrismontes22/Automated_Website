'Nine out of ten people prefer our version of the internet over Google's' — Proton Founder Andy Yen on age verification, Big Tech, AI, and the future of digital privacy
============================================================

Proton CEO Andy Yen is scaling the privacy-focused company into a comprehensive ecosystem of 11 products to challenge Google and Silicon Valley’s model of surveillance-based digital services.

### Key Points
- Proton, founded in 2014, now offers a suite of privacy-first tools including encrypted email, VPN, and the Lumo AI chatbot.
- The company operates under a non-profit-owned structure, which Yen claims aligns financial incentives with user privacy rather than data monetization.
- To mitigate regulatory risks, Proton has diversified its operations, including basing its Lumo AI development in Germany.
- Yen argues that age verification mandates threaten digital anonymity and should only be implemented via device-side processing if required by law.
- The company aims to replace Big Tech dominance by providing an integrated, open-source alternative that avoids the surveillance capitalism business model.

### Why it Matters
Proton’s expansion represents a significant test of whether a privacy-first business model can achieve the scale necessary to compete with established tech giants. By building an ecosystem rather than standalone tools, the company is attempting to shift market standards toward user-centric data protection and away from ad-driven surveillance.
