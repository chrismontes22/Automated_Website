Flock worker calls police on reporter filming public camera installation
============================================================

Flock Safety faces mounting public and legal scrutiny as critics challenge the privacy implications of its widespread automated license plate reader network and the company's response to public documentation.

### Key Points
- Flock Safety operates a network of approximately 120,000 AI-powered license plate readers used by law enforcement agencies across the United States.
- Multiple jurisdictions have recently canceled contracts or implemented bans on the technology due to growing concerns regarding privacy and potential misuse.
- Investigative reporters and content creators have been detained by police after Flock employees called 911 to report being filmed in public spaces.
- The company maintains that its technology captures "plain view" information and asserts that employees may contact law enforcement if they feel threatened or harassed.
- Security researchers have previously identified encryption vulnerabilities in some Flock cameras, fueling further debate over the company's data collection practices.

### Why it Matters
The controversy highlights a deepening tension between private surveillance technology providers and the public's right to document activities in shared spaces. As these tools become standard for law enforcement, the debate over whether such systems prioritize public safety or infringe upon individual privacy continues to influence local government policy and community trust.
