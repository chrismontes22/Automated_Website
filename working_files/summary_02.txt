Encryption in the 1790s
============================================================

The Jefferson Disk, a historical encryption device often attributed to Thomas Jefferson, utilized a system of rotating wheels to secure communications through complex, permutation-based letter arrangements and cipher keys.

### Key Points
- The Jefferson Disk system uses a series of disks on an axle, each featuring randomized alphabet sequences to encrypt and decrypt messages.
- While often associated with Thomas Jefferson in the 1790s, the device's origins likely predate his work and were independently developed by others like Etienne Bazeries.
- The U.S. military adopted the concept in 1922, utilizing the M-94 cipher device with 25 aluminum disks until 1942.
- Later iterations, such as the M-138A, improved security by using 100 interchangeable strips, allowing users to select 30 for more complex key combinations.
- Modern cryptographic analysis confirms that the disk-based system is highly secure, provided the specific wheel order and arrangement remain unknown to unauthorized parties.

### Why it Matters
This historical encryption method demonstrates the evolution of mechanical cryptography before the widespread adoption of electronic rotor machines like Enigma. Understanding these early systems highlights the foundational principles of key-based security that continue to influence modern data protection and information theory.
