Alphabet’s chief investment officer responds to Jefferies warning that the most likely outcome of the AI boom is massive U.S. capital destruction
============================================================

Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta are projected to spend over $720 billion on AI infrastructure this year, fueling a massive industry-wide capital expenditure surge for long-term growth.

### Key Points
- Alphabet expects 2026 capital expenditures between $195 billion and $205 billion, more than double its 2025 spending.
- Private credit firms like Blue Owl, Apollo, and KKR have provided over $200 billion in loans to support AI-related companies and neocloud operators.
- Alphabet reported negative free cash flow in Q2 2026 for the first time since its 2004 IPO to prioritize AI investment.
- Analysts warn that the rise of cheaper, open-weight models from China could commoditize the AI market and lead to significant capital destruction.
- Alphabet President Ruth Porat maintains that heavy investment is necessary to avoid competitive disadvantages and capture long-term economic value.

### Why it Matters
The massive capital outlay highlights a high-stakes gamble on AI's future profitability despite growing concerns regarding market overcapacity and economic risk. Companies that fail to integrate AI-driven innovation risk long-term obsolescence, forcing firms to balance aggressive infrastructure spending against potential financial instability.
