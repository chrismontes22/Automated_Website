Inside our months-long investigation into Kevin O’Leary’s Utah data center debacle
============================================================

**Public backlash and political fallout have stalled Stratos, a massive proposed AI data center project in Utah spearheaded by television personality Kevin O’Leary.**

### Key Points
- The Stratos project, also known as Wonder Valley, aimed to build a 40,000-acre AI campus requiring nine gigawatts of power.
- Local residents and activists successfully organized against the development, citing concerns over water usage, environmental impact, and lack of transparency.
- The project bypassed standard local oversight by utilizing the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA), which has the power to override local land-use regulations.
- Kevin O’Leary faced significant criticism for his public rhetoric, including a retracted claim that project opponents were Chinese spies.
- The development is currently stalled due to ongoing litigation and a referendum challenge filed by local residents.

### Why it Matters
- This conflict highlights a growing national trend where local democratic processes are clashing with the rapid, speculative infrastructure demands of the AI industry. As hyperscalers race to build massive data centers, they are increasingly encountering organized community resistance that threatens to disrupt the speed and profitability of the AI buildout.
