Coding Is Not Solved – Alex Ewerlöf Notes
============================================================

Software engineering remains a complex discipline requiring human accountability, as current AI models struggle with logic, reliability, and the non-functional requirements essential for production-grade software systems.

### Key Points
- AI models are stochastic and lack the accountability required for critical sectors like healthcare, finance, and defense.
- Coding is not a "solved" problem because maintenance, security, and scalability represent the majority of software development costs.
- LLMs often exhibit "jagged intelligence," making them inconsistent and difficult to debug compared to deterministic code.
- Over-reliance on AI for code generation can lead to "AI overdose," where developers lose the ability to reason about system behavior.
- Effective engineering requires deep understanding and ownership, which cannot be fully offloaded to automated tools without risking service degradation.

### Why it Matters
The push to replace human engineering with AI-generated code risks prioritizing short-term velocity over long-term system reliability and security. Businesses that fail to maintain human oversight and accountability may face significant technical debt and operational failures when automated systems inevitably encounter logical errors.
