World War I Coded Message Appears Cracked, Finally
============================================================

The AI model GPT-6 Astra has successfully decrypted a century-old German ADFGVX cipher message from 1918 that remained unsolved by human codebreakers for over one hundred years.

### Key Points
- The ADFGVX cipher was a complex World War I encryption method using a 6x6 grid and transposition keys.
- French cryptographer Georges Painvin originally cracked the code in 1918, though some intercepted messages remained undeciphered.
- The newly decoded message, dated November 27, 1918, details the movements of the British ship HMS Canterbury near Sevastopol.
- The message utilized the German keyword "TRUPPENVERSCHIEBUNG," suggesting the key was in use earlier than historians previously believed.

### Why it Matters
This breakthrough demonstrates the evolving capability of artificial intelligence to solve historical cryptographic puzzles that once required immense human effort. It highlights how modern machine learning models can provide new insights into military history by processing complex, long-standing data gaps.
