Sam Altman says the economy is adapting to AI slower than he expected
============================================================

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman admits that corporate adoption of generative AI is moving slower than expected as businesses continue relying on familiar software tools and established operational routines.

### Key Points
- Sam Altman stated that the economic disruption predicted following the 2023 release of GPT-4 has not materialized as quickly as initially anticipated.
- Corporate customers remain loyal to existing software providers and traditional workflows rather than immediately pivoting to new AI-driven alternatives.
- Altman compared the current slow transition to the early 2000s, when consumers continued visiting Blockbuster despite the emergence of Netflix’s DVD delivery model.
- The CEO acknowledged that changing long-standing human behavior is significantly more difficult than technology developers often assume during the product design phase.
- Previous industry forecasts, including those from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, suggested AI would rapidly eliminate large portions of white-collar work.

### Why it Matters
The slower-than-expected adoption cycle challenges the aggressive growth narratives that previously triggered significant volatility in software-as-a-service stock valuations. This reality check suggests that established companies may maintain their market dominance longer than investors feared during the initial generative AI boom.
