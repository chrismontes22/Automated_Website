Iran is demanding that major tech companies pay sovereignty fees and undergo inspections for undersea internet cables passing through its territorial waters in the Strait of Hormuz.

### Key Points
- Iran claims legal jurisdiction over undersea cables, including the FALCON and Gulf Bridge International systems, based on their placement on the Iranian seabed.
- The government is requiring Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon to obtain passage permits and comply with new environmental and monitoring standards.
- Officials threatened that regional data centers and AI facilities, valued at over $2 trillion, could be destroyed if military conflict in the region escalates.
- More than 99% of global international data traffic currently relies on the network of undersea cables that Iran is now seeking to regulate.

### Why it Matters
This move represents a significant escalation in geopolitical control over critical digital infrastructure, potentially allowing Iran to disrupt or tax global internet traffic. If enforced, these demands could force major tech firms into a precarious legal position, complicating the stability of international data flows and increasing operational costs for global networks.
