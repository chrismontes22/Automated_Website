Musicians-turned-detectives are hunting for AI grifters
============================================================

Electronic dance music producers are increasingly calling out peers for using generative AI tools like Suno to create and pass off algorithmically derived tracks as original human-made art.

### Key Points
- EDM producers Max “H4RRIS” Harris and Nihil Young are leading efforts to identify and expose AI-generated music within the electronic dance music scene.
- Critics argue that AI platforms like Suno and Udio rely on copyrighted material and produce low-quality audio characterized by artifacts like white noise and melodic stuttering.
- Despite backlash, AI-generated music is gaining mainstream traction, with tracks appearing on Billboard charts and labels signing AI-powered avatars to multi-million dollar deals.
- Industry data suggests AI content is proliferating rapidly, with some platforms reporting that machine-generated tracks now account for over 50 percent of new uploads.
- Professional musicians report that the rise of generative tools is negatively impacting their income and creating a culture of mistrust regarding the authenticity of digital audio.

### Why it Matters
The rise of AI-generated music is fundamentally challenging the definition of artistic labor and threatening the livelihoods of professional producers and sound engineers. As these tools become more sophisticated and integrated into streaming platforms, the industry faces a growing crisis of authenticity that complicates copyright enforcement and consumer trust.
