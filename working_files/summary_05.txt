Oura pulls $15bn stock market listing days after announcement
============================================================

Smart ring manufacturer Oura has officially postponed its planned initial public offering on the Nasdaq, citing significant uncertainty and challenging conditions within the current global stock market environment.

### Key Points
- Oura halted plans to raise $2.2 billion through an IPO that would have valued the health-tech company at approximately $15 billion.
- The company reported a pre-tax profit of $70 million on $1.2 billion in sales for the nine-month period ending June 30.
- CEO Tom Hale stated the company has the flexibility to wait for a more favorable market window before pursuing a public listing.
- Rising interest rates, global trade tensions, and inflation have recently caused multiple firms, including Holtec International, to delay their market debuts.
- Oura is currently facing a class action lawsuit alleging false advertising regarding the accuracy of its sleep-tracking technology, though the company denies these claims.

### Why it Matters
The postponement highlights a cooling IPO market as macroeconomic pressures and rising interest rates force companies to reconsider their valuation expectations. This trend signals a cautious approach among private firms that are prioritizing financial stability over immediate public market entry during periods of heightened economic volatility.
