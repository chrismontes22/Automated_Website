Mercor is embracing old-school Big Tech interview strategies that even Google and Microsoft are ditching
============================================================

Mercor has shifted its hiring strategy away from AI-assisted take-home assignments, opting instead for traditional whiteboarding sessions to better evaluate a candidate's fundamental judgment and technical problem-solving skills.

### Key Points
- Head of Product Osvald Nitski confirmed the $10 billion startup now prioritizes whiteboarding to test systems design, statistical knowledge, and experimental reasoning.
- The company aims to prevent candidates from relying on AI tools like Claude to generate work rather than demonstrating independent decision-making abilities.
- This approach contrasts with industry trends where companies like Google, Microsoft, and various AI startups are increasingly integrating AI tools into their interview processes.
- Many tech firms now favor work trials and "vibe coding" assessments to gauge how effectively candidates utilize AI coding assistants in real-world scenarios.

### Why it Matters
This shift highlights a growing debate in the tech industry regarding whether to embrace AI-assisted workflows or prioritize foundational human expertise during recruitment. By rejecting AI-heavy assessments, Mercor is positioning itself against the broader Silicon Valley trend of integrating generative tools into standard technical evaluations.
