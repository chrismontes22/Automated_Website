AI labs are fueling a wild market for your Slack DMs
============================================================

AI labs are aggressively purchasing internal business data, including Slack messages and documents, to train autonomous AI agents, creating a booming but controversial market for workplace information.

### Key Points
- Annual spending on data for AI training is projected to grow from under $1 billion in 2023 to nearly $6.5 billion by 2027.
- Startups like Micro1 are offering businesses between $100,000 and $2 million to license anonymized internal records for model development.
- Tech giants including Meta and SpaceX have faced internal backlash for attempting to harvest employee keystrokes and communications for AI training.
- Data brokers are using high-pressure tactics, such as 48-hour "exploding offers," to acquire proprietary information from small and midsize companies.
- The lack of federal privacy laws has created an ethically gray area regarding whether employee consent is required for the sale of workplace data.

### Why it Matters
The commodification of internal business records transforms mundane workplace communications into a high-value asset, potentially creating a new, significant revenue stream for companies. However, this trend raises urgent questions about employee privacy, intellectual property rights, and the potential for security breaches when sensitive corporate data is sold to third-party developers.
