Cognitive Mercantilism: AI panic resembles fears of early capitalism
============================================================

The current anxiety surrounding artificial intelligence mirrors historical mercantilism, as elites attempt to hoard intelligence as a finite resource rather than embracing its potential for widespread economic expansion.

### Key Points
- Joyce Appleby’s *The Relentless Revolution* argues that capitalism emerged by shifting from a zero-sum, scarcity-based worldview to one driven by expanding consumer demand.
- Cognitive mercantilism treats intelligence as a fixed, scarce commodity that must be controlled by elites to maintain power and prevent social disorder.
- AI is lowering the cost of cognitive production, potentially triggering a "cognitive industrious revolution" where individuals voluntarily increase intellectual effort to access new opportunities.
- AI Malthusianism incorrectly assumes that an abundance of intelligence will lead to human redundancy, ignoring the historical elasticity of demand for new goods and services.
- The transition to an intelligence-rich economy may shift labor politics from protecting existing jobs to securing a fair share of expanding cognitive consumption opportunities.

### Why it Matters
This perspective reframes the AI debate from a fear of automation-driven job loss to a model of economic growth driven by the democratization of intelligence. By viewing AI as a catalyst for new forms of demand, society can move toward a more generative future rather than clinging to restrictive, scarcity-based policies.
