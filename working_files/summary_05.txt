The People of Utah vs. Kevin O’Leary
============================================================

Celebrity investor Kevin O’Leary’s plan to build the world’s largest AI data center in rural Utah has stalled following intense public backlash and a series of political repercussions.

### Key Points
- The "Wonder Valley" project, later renamed Stratos, proposed a 40,000-acre data center campus in Box Elder County powered by a 9-gigawatt gas plant.
- Local residents and activists organized a massive opposition movement after learning the project was fast-tracked by state officials with minimal public input.
- O’Leary faced widespread criticism and a defamation lawsuit after baselessly accusing project opponents of being funded by the Chinese Communist Party.
- The political fallout led to the primary election defeats of several key supporters, including state Senate President J. Stuart Adams and two county commissioners.
- As of late August, the project remains stalled with no announced tenants, no land transfers, and ongoing legal challenges regarding a public referendum.

### Why it Matters
The controversy highlights the growing friction between the rapid, speculative expansion of AI infrastructure and the concerns of local communities regarding water usage, environmental impact, and transparency. This case serves as a cautionary tale for developers and policymakers, demonstrating that bypassing public engagement can trigger significant political instability and jeopardize multi-billion-dollar industrial projects.
