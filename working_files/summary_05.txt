SEC to review Nasdaq bitcoin options approval after CME challenge
============================================================

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has suspended its approval of Nasdaq’s bitcoin index options following a formal legal challenge regarding regulatory jurisdiction filed by the CME Group.

### Key Points
- The SEC paused the approval of Nasdaq’s cash-settled bitcoin index options, known as QBTC, pending a formal review of the decision.
- CME Group challenged the approval on June 11, arguing that bitcoin is a commodity under the exclusive jurisdiction of the CFTC.
- Nasdaq’s proposed product would compete directly with existing bitcoin futures and options markets currently operated by the CME.
- The SEC has invited interested parties to submit public comments regarding the jurisdictional dispute by the August 24 deadline.
- If the CME’s argument prevails, Nasdaq may be required to register as a CFTC-regulated venue or redesign its contracts to track securities instead.

### Why it Matters
This dispute highlights a significant regulatory friction point between the SEC and the CFTC regarding the oversight of crypto-based financial derivatives. The final ruling will determine whether securities exchanges can list commodity-linked products, potentially reshaping the competitive landscape for digital asset trading platforms.
