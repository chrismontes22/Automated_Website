Japanese Arcade Machines Have Been Taken Down By A Major Hack Of Sega’s Services
============================================================

Sega Fave has suspended its ALL.net and Aime online services following a security breach, forcing popular Japanese arcade games like Taiko no Tatsujin offline since November 1.

### Key Points
- Sega Fave confirmed unauthorized third-party access to the ALL.net network and Aime NFC card system.
- Affected titles include major arcade games such as Bandai Namco’s Taiko no Tatsujin and Sega’s Initial D: The Arcade.
- Sega reports that no customer payment information or personal data was compromised during the security incident.
- Arcades across Japan are currently operating machines in limited offline modes or blocking access entirely while the investigation continues.
- This breach follows a previous service outage in late October intended to address a specific PHP exploit.

### Why it Matters
The outage disrupts the core functionality of Japan’s competitive arcade scene, which relies on these networks for leaderboards, save data, and online matchmaking. While no data was stolen, the prolonged downtime highlights the vulnerability of centralized gaming infrastructure and the significant operational impact on physical arcade businesses.
