Gateway 2000's hilariously bad ads in the 90s (Part II)
============================================================

Gateway 2000 transitioned from a bootstrapped, quirky computer brand to a corporate giant during the 1990s, fueled by massive, pun-heavy magazine advertising campaigns and a successful public offering.

### Key Points
- Gateway 2000 grew from a Sioux City startup to a $3.7 billion company by 1995, driven by aggressive mail-order sales and international expansion.
- Founder Ted Waitt utilized unconventional, multi-page magazine ad inserts featuring barnyard humor and elaborate costumes to build brand awareness.
- The company went public on the Nasdaq in 1993, helping Waitt become one of America’s first tech billionaires.
- Despite high revenue, the brand faced increasing customer complaints, leading to expanded support services and longer warranties to retain its base.
- By 1997, the company began shedding its iconic Holstein cow branding and moved its headquarters from Iowa to San Diego.

### Why it Matters
The evolution of Gateway 2000’s marketing reflects the broader shift of 1990s tech companies from scrappy, personality-driven startups to detached, corporate entities. This transition highlights the risks of abandoning a unique brand identity in favor of a more conventional, polished image during periods of rapid market expansion.
