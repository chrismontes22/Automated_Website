A Court Ruling Killed ‘Click to Cancel.’ NYC Just Brought It Back
============================================================

New York City has implemented a new "click-to-cancel" law requiring businesses to make canceling subscriptions as simple as the initial sign-up process for all local residents.

### Key Points
- The executive order, which took effect October 1, applies to services including gym memberships, cellphone contracts, and monthly app subscriptions.
- New Yorkers can file formal complaints against non-compliant businesses through a dedicated city web portal managed by human staff members.
- Companies found in violation of the new regulation face potential fines of up to $525 per incident.
- Similar consumer protection laws regarding subscription cancellations are currently active in states including California, Maryland, and Colorado.
- The Federal Trade Commission is currently reviewing public comments on "negative option" billing practices, though a national policy remains stalled.

### Why it Matters
This local mandate creates a significant regulatory burden for companies that rely on difficult cancellation processes to retain subscribers. It serves as a potential model for other jurisdictions and increases pressure on federal regulators to establish a uniform national standard for subscription management.
