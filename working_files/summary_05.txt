I made my mesh Wi-Fi less wireless, and it finally got faster
============================================================

Connecting mesh Wi-Fi satellite nodes to a primary router via Ethernet cables, known as wired backhaul, significantly improves network speeds by eliminating common wireless signal performance penalties.

### Key Points
- Wireless backhaul in mesh systems often causes a 50% speed reduction because nodes operate in half-duplex mode, alternating between sending and receiving data.
- Wired backhaul uses Ethernet cables to link satellite nodes directly to the main router, bypassing the interference and signal loss inherent in wireless connections.
- Users can implement wired backhaul by connecting nodes to an unmanaged switch, such as the Netgear 5-Port Gigabit Ethernet switch, to maintain full network management.
- Testing with a Google Nest Wi-Fi Pro system showed that switching from wireless to wired backhaul increased speeds from half of the network's capacity to over 900Mbps.
- While wired backhaul offers superior performance, it requires the physical installation of Ethernet cables, which may be challenging for users without existing home networking infrastructure.

### Why it Matters
Transitioning to a wired backhaul configuration allows users to maximize the performance of their mesh networking hardware by removing the inherent limitations of wireless signal hops. This approach provides a reliable, high-speed connection that is essential for bandwidth-intensive tasks like gaming and streaming, even in homes with large floor plans.
