Your Business Model Should Be Built to Absorb Change. Here’s What That Requires.
============================================================

Building a durable business requires entrepreneurs to prioritize organizational flexibility and contingency planning to effectively navigate unpredictable external disruptions like regulatory shifts, new technologies, and changing economic conditions.

### Key Points
- Durable business models must be designed to absorb external shocks, including tariff fluctuations, tax policy changes, and industry-wide regulatory updates.
- Over-optimizing for efficiency can eliminate necessary excess capacity, leaving companies without the optionality required to pivot during market disruptions.
- Resilience involves maintaining a financial runway that allows for simultaneous investment in current operations and future-focused innovation during transitions.
- Management teams should model multiple timelines for capital needs, specifically preparing for scenarios where development costs rise or commercialization is delayed.
- Successful companies balance calculated risk-taking with strategic contingency planning to ensure they remain competitive when initial growth assumptions prove incorrect.

### Why it Matters
Maintaining operational flexibility allows companies to adapt to unforeseen challenges without sacrificing long-term innovation for short-term survival. Businesses that preserve optionality are better positioned to invest during economic downturns, ultimately emerging stronger than competitors who lack the resources to pivot.
