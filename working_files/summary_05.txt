'Profits won.' The child safety trial against Meta kicks off in federal court
============================================================

A landmark federal trial has begun in Oakland, California, where a coalition of states accuses Meta of designing Instagram and Facebook to be addictive and harmful to young users.

### Key Points
- The lawsuit alleges Meta prioritized profits over child safety by implementing features like infinite scroll and algorithms that encourage compulsive use.
- States claim Meta violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by collecting data on users under 13 without parental consent.
- Former Meta engineer and whistleblower Arturo Béjar testified that the company downplayed internal data regarding harmful content and youth safety.
- Meta denies the allegations, arguing that it has implemented meaningful safety tools and that the states are misrepresenting internal company communications.
- The trial, presided over by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, is expected to last approximately six weeks.

### Why it Matters
This case represents a significant shift in legal strategy by focusing on platform design rather than user-generated content, potentially bypassing traditional Section 230 protections. A ruling against Meta could force fundamental changes to social media business models and set a major precedent for how technology companies are held accountable for the mental health of minors.
