How the Wisconsin governor’s race became a data center hate-off
============================================================

Wisconsin gubernatorial candidates Tom Tiffany and David Crowley are clashing over the rapid expansion of data centers, debating the environmental impact and tax incentives of the state's AI infrastructure.

### Key Points
- Republican Tom Tiffany accuses Democrat David Crowley of prioritizing corporate interests over Wisconsin’s farmland and natural resources.
- Crowley argues for stricter local oversight and environmental regulations while highlighting Tiffany’s past support for federal tax cuts benefiting Big Tech.
- Wisconsin’s Legislative Fiscal Bureau projects the state could forgo $1.5 billion in sales tax revenue during the construction of certified data center projects.
- Both candidates have avoided calling for a statewide moratorium, partly due to strong support for data center construction from Wisconsin building trades unions.
- Data center projects, such as the one in Port Washington, can significantly expand local tax bases, offering potential relief for post-industrial municipalities.
- A July Marquette Law School poll indicated that only 5 percent of Wisconsin voters consider data centers their most important issue.

### Why it Matters
The debate highlights a growing political tension between the economic benefits of hyperscale development and the public's increasing skepticism toward the environmental and fiscal costs of AI infrastructure. As both candidates navigate this divide, their platforms reflect the broader national struggle to balance corporate investment with local community interests and labor union priorities.
