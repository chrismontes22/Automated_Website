Welcome to the 'Wild West' of AI in schools: Research is scarce, but experiments abound
============================================================

Dayton Public Schools and other districts are increasingly deploying AI chatbots and robots in classrooms despite limited research, clear federal guidance, or standardized metrics for measuring student outcomes.

### Key Points
- Dayton Public Schools paid SchoolAI $72,600 to pilot "Jordan," a social-emotional learning chatbot designed to engage students at an alternative school.
- A Stanford University review of over 800 academic papers found that research on the efficacy and potential harms of AI in K-12 education remains extremely limited.
- The Salamanca City Central School District paused plans to use a $58,000 humanoid robot named "Sally" following community concerns regarding data privacy and student interaction.
- Major districts, including New York City and Los Angeles, have implemented bans or strict limitations on student-facing AI tools to mitigate potential risks.
- Experts suggest that while AI could transform education, there is an urgent need for state and federal leadership to establish safety and evaluation standards.

### Why it Matters
The rapid, decentralized adoption of AI in schools creates a "Wild West" environment where vulnerable students are often the subjects of unproven technological experiments. Without standardized metrics or federal oversight, districts struggle to balance the necessity of preparing students for an AI-driven future against the risks of cognitive stunting and data privacy breaches.
