Your secrets aren’t safe with this cute robot toy — they’re on Chinese servers ready to be hacked
============================================================

The Eilik robot toy, manufactured by Energize Lab, contains severe security vulnerabilities that allow unauthorized access to private user conversations, personal profiles, and remote control of the devices.

### Key Points
- Security researcher Sammy Azdoufal discovered that Energize Lab stores 13 million user conversations on unprotected Chinese servers.
- The database includes sensitive personal information for over 68,000 active robots, including names, ages, and locations of more than 31,000 children.
- Hackers can remotely hijack the robot’s personality and LLM, forcing the device to speak inappropriate or harmful content to users.
- Despite company privacy policies claiming data is encrypted and deleted, researchers found unencrypted audio recordings and photos stored at public web addresses.
- Energize Lab has failed to address multiple inquiries regarding these security breaches or implement necessary patches to protect its global user base.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the significant privacy and safety risks associated with internet-connected toys that lack basic cybersecurity protections. The exposure of sensitive data and the ability for third parties to manipulate device behavior pose a direct threat to consumer safety, particularly for children.
