Personal Data for Millions of California Students Stolen in Massive Breach
============================================================

Millions of current and former California public school students have had their personal data compromised following a significant security breach at the Educational Testing Service.

### Key Points
- The breach exposed sensitive information including names, dates of birth, and addresses for students who took state-mandated standardized tests.
- Compromised records date back to 2006, affecting both current students and those who have since left the California public school system.
- Unauthorized access occurred between May 22 and September 18, 2026, via a single compromised login credential used to copy files.
- Educational Testing Service (ETS) has implemented strengthened access controls and enhanced monitoring to prevent future unauthorized data access.
- Affected individuals are eligible for 24 months of free identity-protection services and identity-restoration assistance.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the systemic risks associated with outsourcing sensitive student data to third-party vendors that manage large-scale information repositories. The breach underscores the critical need for robust authentication and monitoring safeguards to prevent single points of failure from impacting millions of individuals.
