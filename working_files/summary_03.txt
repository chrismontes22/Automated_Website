The Real Lives of Online Romance Scammers
============================================================

Carlos Barragán’s new book, The Yahoo Boys, explores the lives of Nigerian cybercriminals, examining how poverty and global inequality drive young men to exploit digital connections for survival.

### Key Points
- The term "Yahoo Boy" refers to West African fraudsters who use fake online personas to manipulate victims in the U.S., U.K., and other English-speaking nations.
- Scams often involve romance fraud, sextortion, and fake cryptocurrency investments, frequently targeting lonely individuals seeking human connection.
- Economic instability in Nigeria, including high youth unemployment and the legacy of colonial-era corruption, has fueled the rise of these illicit digital economies.
- A 2023 estimate from Nigeria’s Economic and Financial Crimes Commission suggested that approximately 70% of students in the country are involved in some form of cybercrime.
- The scam economy operates as a complex global supply chain, often involving the trade of gift cards and digital assets across international borders.

### Why it Matters
- This narrative highlights how the global commodification of human connection and extreme wealth inequality create a symbiotic relationship between isolated Western victims and desperate Nigerian scammers. By framing these crimes as a byproduct of late-stage capitalism, the book challenges readers to view the "Yahoo Boy" phenomenon as a reflection of systemic failures rather than mere individual criminality.
