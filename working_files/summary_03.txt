This bill could reshape crypto in America -- and it's sparking a major battle
============================================================

The U.S. Senate blocked the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act on Tuesday, stalling a major Trump-backed legislative effort to establish the first comprehensive regulatory framework for the cryptocurrency industry.

### Key Points
- The bill failed a procedural vote with only 49 senators in favor, falling short of the 60-vote threshold required to advance.
- Legislation proposed shifting primary oversight of the crypto sector from the Securities and Exchange Commission to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission.
- Democratic opposition centered on concerns regarding ethics clauses and potential conflicts of interest involving President Trump’s personal crypto business ventures.
- Community banking leaders lobbied against the bill, arguing that provisions allowing crypto firms to pay interest on stablecoins would unfairly threaten traditional banking deposits.
- Senator Thom Tillis filed a motion to allow for a potential reconsideration of the bill, though its future remains uncertain ahead of upcoming elections.

### Why it Matters
The defeat of the Clarity Act leaves the cryptocurrency industry in a state of regulatory limbo, subject to shifting enforcement priorities between different presidential administrations. This legislative impasse highlights the ongoing tension between industry advocates seeking clear legal standards and critics concerned about consumer protections and financial stability.
