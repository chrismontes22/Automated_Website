Does Inclusive Finance Really Need Stablecoins?
============================================================

Financial expert Michael Wiegand argues that stablecoins are not the most effective solution for global financial inclusion, favoring traditional mobile-based payment systems and data-driven lending models instead.

### Key Points
- Global financial inclusion has improved significantly, with account ownership in low- and middle-income countries rising from 42% in 2011 to 75% today.
- Stablecoins require smartphones and high digital literacy, whereas existing mobile money systems function on basic phones, making them more accessible to the unbanked.
- Cross-border payment platforms like Wise and Remitly currently offer better value and accessibility for low-income recipients than stablecoin-based transfers.
- Data-driven lending, such as the Michu product in Ethiopia, allows banks to provide loans based on transaction history rather than traditional collateral.
- The G20 Roadmap for Cross-Border Payments promotes linking domestic instant-payment systems, such as the Nexus network, to improve international transfer efficiency.

### Why it Matters
Prioritizing stablecoins over proven mobile-money infrastructure risks ignoring the practical technological barriers faced by the world's 1.3 billion unbanked adults. Focusing on interoperable payment systems and data-backed financial products offers a more scalable and equitable path toward genuine global financial inclusion.
