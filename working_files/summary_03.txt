I pitted my dream PC from 2017 against an RTX 5090 monster in 2026 in a fight to the death - here's what happened
============================================================

A performance comparison between a 2017-era high-end gaming PC and a 2026 flagship system highlights the massive evolution in hardware capabilities and the shifting economics of PC gaming.

### Key Points
- A 2017-era system featuring an Intel Core i7-7700K and Nvidia GTX 1080 Ti cost approximately £1,700, which now barely covers the price of a single flagship GPU.
- Modern hardware, such as the Nvidia RTX 5090, delivers up to five times the performance of the 1080 Ti in synthetic benchmarks and demanding 4K gaming scenarios.
- The 2017 system remains capable of running older titles at high frame rates but struggles with modern, ray-tracing-heavy games that require newer AI-assisted upscaling technologies.
- Significant bottlenecks in the older machine include its 16GB of DDR4 RAM and limited 250GB SSD, which are insufficient for modern Windows 11 installations and contemporary game sizes.
- The GTX 1080 Ti maintains a legendary reputation for longevity, proving that a well-balanced enthusiast PC can remain functional and enjoyable nearly a decade after its release.

### Why it Matters
The widening gap between 2017 and 2026 hardware illustrates how flagship graphics cards have transitioned from being the centerpiece of a complete "dream" build to becoming luxury components priced higher than entire systems once were. This shift reflects both the rapid advancement of rendering technologies like DLSS and frame generation and the increasing financial barrier to entry for top-tier PC gaming.
