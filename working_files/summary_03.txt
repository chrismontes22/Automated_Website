The winners and losers of the new H-1B era
============================================================

The Trump administration’s restrictive H-1B visa policies and proposed $100,000 fees are reshaping the labor market, favoring high-salary tech specialists while creating significant hurdles for startups and outsourcing firms.

### Key Points
- The March 2026 H-1B lottery saw a 38% decline in applications, largely attributed to a proposed $100,000 fee currently facing legal challenges.
- A new wage-weighted selection process prioritizes higher-paid applicants, benefiting roles in generative AI like data scientists and research scientists.
- Major tech companies, including Nvidia, Anthropic, and OpenAI, remain resilient, while outsourcing firms like Infosys, TCS, and Cognizant saw certified applications drop by 30%.
- Small startups and accounting firms are struggling to navigate the increased costs and compliance requirements of the current immigration regime.
- Certified H-1B applications for graduates from top-ranked universities fell by 14% between 2025 and 2026.

### Why it Matters
These policy shifts are fundamentally altering the U.S. talent pipeline by favoring established, capital-rich corporations over smaller innovators and entry-level professionals. This environment may stifle startup growth and reduce the availability of early-career opportunities, potentially impacting long-term economic competitiveness in the technology sector.
