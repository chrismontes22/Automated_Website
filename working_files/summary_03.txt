We asked our Gen Z and millennial reporters and editors about AI agents. Here's where they split.
============================================================

Business Insider reporters from Gen Z and millennial generations express differing levels of skepticism and optimism regarding the adoption of AI agents for personal and professional tasks.

### Key Points
- AI agents are designed to perform multi-step tasks like booking travel or managing schedules, moving beyond the capabilities of standard chatbots.
- Gen Z staff cited significant concerns regarding data privacy, potential scams, and the loss of personal agency when delegating tasks to automated software.
- Millennial editors expressed cautious optimism, viewing AI agents as potential tools to reduce screen time and automate tedious professional workflows.
- Practical testing of Meta’s Muse AI agent revealed current limitations, including failures with two-factor authentication and complex logistical tasks.
- Pew Research Center data indicates that Americans under age 30 are increasingly skeptical of the overall benefits provided by artificial intelligence.

### Why it Matters
The divide between generational attitudes highlights the significant trust barriers that developers must overcome to achieve widespread consumer adoption of agentic AI. As these tools evolve, their ability to balance efficiency with data security will determine whether they become essential productivity assistants or remain niche technologies.
