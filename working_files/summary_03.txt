China's biggest apps are inviting the West closer into its insular internet economy
============================================================

Major Chinese technology companies are increasingly opening their digital ecosystems to international users through new payment integrations and global platform expansions to drive growth beyond domestic markets.

### Key Points
- PayPal announced on August 11 that U.S. users can now make QR code payments at millions of Weixin Pay merchants across China.
- The youth-focused video platform Bilibili has launched an international app and is actively recruiting creators in the U.S., Japan, and Europe.
- Chinese tech firms are seeking international expansion as domestic consumption stagnates and competition for global market share intensifies.
- The lifestyle app Xiaohongshu, known internationally as RedNote, has gained significant traction among foreign users seeking alternatives to other social media platforms.
- Moonshot AI recently released its Kimi K3 open-weight model, offering Western enterprises a lower-cost alternative for advanced AI tasks like coding and reasoning.

### Why it Matters
These developments signal a strategic shift as Chinese tech giants move to integrate their services into the global digital economy to offset slowing growth at home. By lowering barriers for international tourists and courting foreign creators, these companies are positioning themselves as competitive, globally accessible alternatives to Western platforms.
