The next-gen Xbox won’t cost you $1,800. It can’t. Can it?
============================================================

**Microsoft’s upcoming "Helix" console could retail between $1,200 and $1,800 by 2028, forcing the company to consider new business models to maintain affordability for its mass-market gaming audience.**

### Key Points
- Reports suggest the next-generation Xbox, codenamed "Helix," may carry a premium price tag due to rising costs for high-end RAM and storage components.
- Microsoft executives, including Asha Sharma and Phil Spencer, have previously acknowledged that mass-market consumers cannot afford multi-thousand-dollar gaming hardware.
- Potential strategies to offset costs include bundling the console with long-term Xbox Game Pass subscriptions or shifting toward cloud-based rental models.
- The "Helix" is expected to be part of a broader "family of devices," potentially allowing third-party partners like Asus to release more affordable, varied hardware options.
- Industry experts suggest that current-generation consoles like the Xbox Series X and S may remain relevant longer as developers focus on scaling games across diverse hardware tiers.

### Why it Matters
The potential shift toward a high-priced, premium console signals a fundamental change in how Microsoft approaches the gaming market, moving away from traditional hardware subsidies. By diversifying its hardware ecosystem and emphasizing subscription services, the company aims to balance the high cost of cutting-edge technology with the need to keep gaming accessible to a wider audience.
