Counterfeit HDDs are flooding the market — here's how to spot one
============================================================

Seagate recently seized 700 counterfeit hard drives in Malaysia as scammers increasingly pass off used, low-capacity storage devices as new, high-capacity products across major online retail platforms.

### Key Points
- Seagate confiscated approximately 700 counterfeit drives in Malaysia that were being sold as new high-capacity surveillance hardware.
- Scammers are manipulating firmware, S.M.A.R.T. data, and proprietary FARM logs to disguise used desktop drives from manufacturers like Seagate, WD, and Toshiba.
- Consumers are advised to use the ValiDrive tool to verify actual storage capacity, as fake drives often report false sizes in standard system software.
- Experts recommend avoiding unusually low-priced listings and purchasing exclusively from authorized retailers to mitigate the risk of receiving tampered hardware.
- Physical inspection of packaging, including checking for missing manufacturer stickers and suspicious production dates, remains a primary defense for buyers.

### Why it Matters
The proliferation of sophisticated counterfeit storage poses a significant risk to data integrity and hardware reliability for both individual consumers and enterprise users. As scammers bypass traditional verification methods, the burden of authentication shifts to the buyer, potentially leading to widespread data loss and financial fraud.
