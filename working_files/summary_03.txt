Seniors Lose More Money to Investment Scams Than Any Other Fraud
============================================================

The Federal Trade Commission reported that adults aged 60 and older lost $3 billion to fraud in 2025, marking a significant fivefold increase over the past five years.

### Key Points
- Investment scams accounted for over $1 billion in losses, representing a 42% increase compared to the previous year.
- Adults aged 80 and older reported the highest median losses, with many victims targeted via social media platforms.
- Common fraud tactics include tech support, romance, government impersonation, and sweepstakes scams.
- The FTC’s "Never Ever" campaign warns that legitimate agencies never demand payment via cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or gift cards.
- Victims can access support through the Department of Justice’s National Elder Fraud Hotline and the FTC’s Consumer Sentinel Network.

### Why it Matters
The rapid rise in financial fraud targeting older adults threatens the retirement security of a vulnerable demographic facing increasingly sophisticated cyber scams. These trends highlight an urgent need for enhanced consumer education and stronger collaboration between law enforcement and private-sector partners to mitigate systemic financial risks.
