The US Threw the First Punch in the Global Robot Wars. How Will China Respond?
============================================================

The Federal Communications Commission has banned the import and sale of foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots, citing national security risks and potential surveillance concerns regarding critical infrastructure.

### Key Points
- The FCC added foreign-made robots to its "Covered List," prohibiting new models from entering the U.S. market while exempting existing products.
- China currently dominates the sector, accounting for 90% of the 13,000 humanoid robots shipped globally last year.
- Major Chinese robotics firms impacted by the policy include Agibot, Unitree, and UBTech.
- The U.S. government claims these mobile sensor platforms could be used to surveil citizens or be remotely commandeered by foreign intelligence services.
- Chinese officials have condemned the move as discriminatory and warned of potential countermeasures that could disrupt global supply chains.

### Why it Matters
This policy shift intensifies the ongoing technological arms race between the U.S. and China by shielding domestic manufacturers like Tesla and Boston Dynamics from cheaper foreign competition. However, experts warn that restricting access to affordable Chinese hardware could inadvertently slow American innovation and increase costs for researchers and developers.
