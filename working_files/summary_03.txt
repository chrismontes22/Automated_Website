You Know GDPR Is Good Based on Who Hates It
============================================================

The European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) has emerged as a global standard for privacy, filling a regulatory void left by the United States’ inaction on surveillance capitalism.

### Key Points
- The GDPR, implemented on May 25, 2018, mandates strict consent and data transparency, forcing global tech companies to adopt European privacy standards.
- The "Brussels Effect" describes how the EU’s massive market size compels international corporations to comply with its regulations to maintain access to European consumers.
- Surveillance capitalism, as defined by Shoshana Zuboff, treats human behavioral data as raw material for profit, a practice currently unchecked by comprehensive federal U.S. law.
- The EU’s approach treats privacy as a fundamental human right, contrasting with the U.S. legal framework that largely views data protection as a market-based issue.
- Enforcement mechanisms, such as those triggered by activist Max Schrems, have successfully challenged international data agreements and forced industry-wide policy changes.

### Why it Matters
The lack of federal U.S. privacy legislation has allowed the EU to become the world's primary regulator for the digital economy. This shift highlights a significant decline in American regulatory influence and suggests that global data standards will continue to be dictated by Brussels rather than Washington.
