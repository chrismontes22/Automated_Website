Waymo and other autonomous vehicle developers are rapidly expanding global robotaxi operations, challenging traditional taxi services and professional driving roles in major cities like London and Tokyo.

### Key Points
- Waymo is expanding into over 20 new cities following a $16 billion investment, while tripling its fleet size in US hubs like Dallas and Miami.
- Wuhan, China, currently leads in robotaxi density, with Baidu’s Apollo Go deploying thousands of driverless vehicles that operate 30% cheaper than human-driven services.
- Data from the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety shows driverless vehicles have a 68% lower crash rate than human drivers over 50 million miles.
- Tokyo is integrating autonomous pilots to address a severe labor shortage, while London serves as a critical test for AI navigation in complex urban environments.
- Operational metrics indicate one robotaxi can replace four human drivers by eliminating fatigue and enabling 24/7 service with lower maintenance costs.

### Why it Matters
The transition toward autonomous fleets signals a fundamental shift in the global transportation economy, threatening the viability of traditional professional driving careers. As AI technology proves its safety and efficiency, cities must navigate the social and economic consequences of replacing human labor with automated, round-the-clock transit systems.
