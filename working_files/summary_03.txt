Ledger Confirms Spy Chip Implant Behind Wallet Crypto Thefts
============================================================

Ledger has confirmed that hardware wallets sold by the Malaysian reseller CryptoBilis were compromised with malicious implants designed to steal cryptocurrency by transmitting private keys via cellular networks.

### Key Points
- Ledger identified tampered Ledger X devices distributed through the Southeast Asian retailer CryptoBilis.
- Attackers replaced original batteries with smaller versions to accommodate malicious circuitry, including an Arm Cortex-M4 microcontroller and an LTE module.
- The implants exfiltrate sensitive wallet data using a tiny eSIM, allowing unauthorized parties to drain user funds.
- Ledger warns that its "Genuine Check" feature cannot detect these hardware modifications if the device's original Secure Element remains intact.
- The company advises customers to purchase hardware wallets exclusively through authorized channels to avoid counterfeit or modified products.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a critical supply chain vulnerability where physical tampering can bypass standard digital security verification methods. It serves as a stark reminder for users to prioritize purchasing security hardware directly from manufacturers to mitigate the risk of sophisticated, hardware-level fraud.
