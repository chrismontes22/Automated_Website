Trump administration says Microsoft ‘abused’ worker visa program and is cutting off access
============================================================

The Trump administration has suspended Microsoft and other major tech firms from a federal immigration program, citing allegations of visa fraud and the displacement of American workers.

### Key Points
- Vice President JD Vance accused Microsoft of abusing the permanent labor certification program, which is intended for roles where domestic workers are unavailable.
- The administration also suspended Adobe, Cognizant, and Infosys from the program as part of a broader crackdown on foreign worker visa usage.
- Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling reported that Microsoft and Adobe requested nearly 3 million foreign workers, resulting in over 230,000 H-1B visa approvals.
- Microsoft stated that 80% of its recent H-1B filings were to extend existing employee statuses rather than hiring new foreign arrivals.
- The company maintains that its H-1B filings represent only 1% of its U.S. workforce and that it pays these employees competitive, industry-standard wages.

### Why it Matters
This policy shift signals a significant escalation in the administration's efforts to restrict foreign labor access for major technology companies. The move could force firms to restructure their hiring practices and may lead to increased operational costs as they navigate stricter federal oversight of visa programs.
