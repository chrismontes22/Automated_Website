Where every US governor stands on AI and the data-center boom
============================================================

US governors are increasingly shaping the national AI landscape through diverse policies, ranging from aggressive safety regulations and "kill switch" proposals to aggressive economic incentives for data-center infrastructure.

### Key Points
- Governors in states like California, New York, and Oregon are exploring "kill switch" mechanisms to disable advanced AI systems during critical safety failures.
- Many states, including Texas, Michigan, and Maryland, have implemented frameworks requiring data-center developers to cover their own energy and infrastructure costs to protect local ratepayers.
- Several governors, such as those in Alaska, North Dakota, and Wyoming, are actively courting AI investment by highlighting their states' cold climates for cooling efficiency and business-friendly policies.
- Legislative actions across the country include bans on Chinese-affiliated AI platforms, protections against deepfakes in elections, and the establishment of state-level AI task forces.
- Tennessee became the first state to pass legislation protecting artists and musicians from unauthorized AI voice and likeness cloning via the ELVIS Act.

### Why it Matters
The fragmented state-level response to AI and data-center expansion creates a complex regulatory patchwork that forces tech companies to navigate varying compliance standards across the US. As governors balance the promise of economic growth against concerns over energy consumption and public safety, their individual policies are effectively serving as a testing ground for future federal oversight.
