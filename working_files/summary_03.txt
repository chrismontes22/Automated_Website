Secret documents reveal donors behind $330 million for Trump's ballroom
============================================================

Internal documents reveal that major corporations and wealthy donors contributed $333 million to President Trump’s White House ballroom project, often while navigating significant federal regulatory and contract decisions.

### Key Points
- Nvidia, Apple, and billionaire Charles Ergen each committed $25 million, the highest donation amount recorded in the internal fundraising spreadsheet.
- The project has raised $333 million from private donors, with at least $77 million coming from contributors who requested anonymity.
- Several donors, including Salesforce and EchoStar, secured major government contracts or favorable regulatory outcomes shortly before or after pledging millions to the project.
- The daughter of a pardoned international banker, Julio Herrera Velutini, committed $2.5 million to the ballroom fund following her father's legal plea deal.
- Cryptocurrency investors, including the Winklevoss brothers, pledged over $18 million to the project amid a period of loosened federal regulations for the industry.
- Donations are routed through the Trust for the National Mall, a nonprofit that is not required to publicly disclose its contributors.

### Why it Matters
The fundraising arrangement has drawn scrutiny from ethics experts and lawmakers concerned about the potential for influence-peddling and transactional politics. Because the project operates through a private nonprofit, it bypasses standard campaign finance transparency, raising questions about the intersection of private wealth and federal government decision-making.
