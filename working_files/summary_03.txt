North Korea used job interviews to deploy malware on 30,000 devices during coding tests — WaterPlum group loots $10.7 million in crypto and plants persistent RATs
============================================================

Security agencies from four nations have issued a joint warning regarding the North Korean "WaterPlum" group, which uses fake job postings to distribute malware and steal cryptocurrency.

### Key Points
- The WaterPlum cyber group has infected over 30,000 devices across 100 countries using malware hidden in fake coding assignments.
- Attacks have resulted in the compromise of 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets, leading to documented losses of $10.71 million.
- Amazon has blocked more than 1,800 suspected North Korean job applications since April 2024 to prevent unauthorized network access.
- Stolen credentials and data are used to infiltrate Western companies, with the DPRK government reportedly generating $500 million annually through these IT schemes.
- Security experts recommend that job seekers use isolated virtual machines for interviews and verify all job openings directly through official company websites.

### Why it Matters
These operations provide a critical revenue stream for the North Korean government while creating significant security vulnerabilities for global technology firms. By compromising individual devices, attackers gain a persistent foothold that can be used to infiltrate corporate networks and steal sensitive intellectual property.
