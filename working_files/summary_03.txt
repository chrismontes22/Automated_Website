Does AI need an antitrust exemption so it doesn’t kill everyone????
============================================================

Former DOJ antitrust chief Jonathan Kanter argues that AI companies should not receive antitrust exemptions, emphasizing that firms must remain liable for safety failures rather than coordinating to stifle competition.

### Key Points
- Jonathan Kanter, former head of the DOJ Antitrust Division, warns that AI companies seeking regulatory exemptions may be attempting to form cartels or secure regulatory capture.
- Kanter asserts that existing antitrust laws do not prevent companies from collaborating on legitimate safety repositories or threat-sharing initiatives.
- The discussion highlights that AI firms should be held to strict product liability standards, similar to other industries, rather than being allowed to slow innovation to protect IPO valuations.
- Kanter dismisses the argument that domestic monopolies are necessary to compete with China, advocating instead for a competitive market that fosters innovation.
- The interview notes a political realignment where both progressives and some conservatives are increasingly skeptical of Big Tech's market dominance.

### Why it Matters
The debate over AI regulation centers on whether the government should grant industry-wide exemptions to address safety concerns or enforce existing liability laws to ensure accountability. This shift reflects a broader move away from the hands-off regulatory approach of the past, signaling a potential transformation in how the U.S. manages the intersection of national security, corporate power, and technological innovation.
