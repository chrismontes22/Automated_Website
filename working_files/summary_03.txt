Russia Was Not Ready For the Data Center War It Started With Ukraine
============================================================

Ukrainian drone strikes against three major Yandex data centers have triggered widespread digital service outages across Russia, highlighting the vulnerability of the nation's centralized technological infrastructure.

### Key Points
- Ukrainian forces targeted three Yandex data centers in a retaliatory campaign following Russian strikes on Ukrainian telecommunications and power infrastructure.
- The outages have disrupted essential Russian services, including digital banking, payment apps, e-commerce, ride-hailing, and navigation platforms.
- Yandex, often described as "Russia's Google," holds approximately 70 percent of the domestic search market and provides critical cloud computing services to major corporations.
- Pro-Kremlin commentators have expressed concerns that Russia’s centralized digital economy is ill-equipped to withstand a sustained "data center war."
- Independent reports suggest that Ukraine’s decentralized digital architecture may offer greater resilience against similar long-range strike campaigns.

### Why it Matters
The disruption of Yandex services demonstrates the significant risks posed to modern economies when centralized digital infrastructure becomes a primary target in military conflicts. This escalation suggests that critical civilian technology platforms are increasingly vulnerable to retaliatory strikes, potentially destabilizing daily commerce and essential public services.
