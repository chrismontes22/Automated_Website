Analysis-China draws 'red lines' around its economic model ahead of EU, US trade talks
============================================================

China is doubling down on its state-led industrial development model, rejecting Western criticism of its trade surplus and manufacturing policies ahead of critical upcoming negotiations with global partners.

### Key Points
- Chinese leadership confirmed policy continuity, prioritizing targeted support for advanced industries over the consumer-focused stimulus requested by international trading partners.
- The Ministry of Commerce released a position paper dismissing Western concerns regarding industrial overcapacity as being rooted in ulterior motives and logical flaws.
- Premier Li Qiang rebranded the "China shock 2.0" narrative, framing the country's manufacturing output as a global "China opportunity 2.0" for the international economy.
- Beijing faces an October deadline to resolve ongoing trade disputes with Brussels, alongside planned face-to-face meetings between President Xi Jinping and Donald Trump.
- The Communist Party journal Qiushi defended low domestic consumption as a historically justified outcome of China's investment-led development strategy.

### Why it Matters
This firm stance signals that Beijing is drawing a red line against external pressure to restructure its economy before entering high-stakes trade talks. The refusal to pivot toward consumer-led growth risks further trade friction and potential retaliatory measures from Western nations concerned about domestic industrial stability.
