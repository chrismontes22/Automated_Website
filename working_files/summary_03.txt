Did Substack Fix the Internet?
============================================================

Substack co-founder Hamish McKenzie explores the platform's role in modern media in his new book, detailing how subscription-based models aim to empower writers and decentralize digital content distribution.

### Key Points
- Substack, launched in 2017 by Hamish McKenzie and Chris Best, allows creators to monetize content through direct reader subscriptions.
- The platform takes a 10% revenue share while positioning itself as a neutral host, avoiding the editorial oversight common in traditional media.
- High-profile writers like Bari Weiss and Matthew Yglesias have helped the platform gain significant cultural influence and financial success.
- Critics, including professor Sarah Brouillette, argue that such platforms force writers to prioritize personal branding and content efficiency over traditional journalism.
- Substack has expanded beyond text to include multimedia features like "Notes," increasingly resembling the social media networks it originally sought to disrupt.

### Why it Matters
Substack represents a significant shift in the creator economy by moving media power from centralized advertising-based platforms to individual, subscription-funded authors. While it provides a viable livelihood for many writers, it also risks homogenizing content as creators adapt their work to satisfy algorithmic demands and audience expectations.
