Condé Nast, Hearst among 300 media execs to push federal ‘bad bots’ bill on AI scraping
============================================================

Over 300 news publishing executives are meeting with Congress in Washington D.C. to advocate for the Stealth Bot Prohibition Act to curb unauthorized AI content scraping.

### Key Points
- Executives from major organizations including Condé Nast, Hearst, and USA Today are participating in the News/Media Alliance-led lobbying effort.
- The proposed legislation would mandate that AI crawlers clearly identify themselves and their purpose, preventing them from disguising traffic to bypass publisher blocks.
- Cloudflare data indicates that bots now account for over 60% of total internet traffic, with AI-specific scraping increasing sixfold throughout 2025.
- The bill aims to create a transparent marketplace where publishers can control their content and negotiate fair licensing agreements with AI companies.
- A similar measure, the Stealth Crawler Prohibition Act, was successfully passed in New York earlier this year.

### Why it Matters
This legislative push represents a critical attempt by the news industry to regain control over intellectual property in an era where automated scraping threatens traditional revenue models. By forcing transparency, publishers hope to shift the economics of AI training from unauthorized data harvesting toward a sustainable, licensed content ecosystem.
