How months of work on the Clarity Act all fell apart
============================================================

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act has stalled in the U.S. Senate following bipartisan opposition, leaving the crypto industry’s primary goal of comprehensive market structure reform currently out of reach.

### Key Points
- The bill failed a key procedural vote in the Senate this month due to intense partisan friction and election-year political maneuvering.
- Democrats blocked the legislation over concerns regarding President Donald Trump’s personal financial ties to the crypto sector, specifically his $1.4 billion in earnings from crypto ventures.
- Negotiations collapsed after a last-ditch effort to include an ethics amendment was abruptly halted by staff for Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott.
- The industry faced internal setbacks, including a months-long delay caused by Coinbase’s public withdrawal of support for the Senate’s version of the bill in January.
- Despite the House of Representatives passing its own version of the bill with significant bipartisan support in 2025, the Senate chose to draft separate, piecemeal legislation.

### Why it Matters
The failure of this legislation leaves the $3 trillion crypto sector in a persistent regulatory gray zone, forcing firms to rely on fragmented guidance from the SEC and CFTC rather than durable federal law. This outcome highlights the growing difficulty of passing industry-specific policy when it becomes entangled with broader political ethics debates and the high-stakes environment of a midterm election cycle.
