California’s billionaire tax faces a precarious road from rich opposition
============================================================

California voters are deciding on a proposed five percent tax on billionaire assets, a ballot measure that has become the most expensive campaign in state history.

### Key Points
- Tech billionaires, including Sergey Brin and Tony Xu, have donated over $300 million to defeat the proposed tax on their assets.
- Supporters of the measure have raised only $32 million, leading to a significant disparity in advertising and voter outreach efforts.
- Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg reportedly considered moving out of California to avoid the tax, prompting the company to create a new share class.
- Opponents have introduced competing ballot measures that could legally negate the billionaire tax if they receive more votes.
- California Governor Gavin Newsom and other prominent Democrats oppose the measure, citing concerns that it is poorly written and could drive wealth out of the state.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a growing political divide between progressive and moderate factions within the Democratic Party regarding wealth taxation and corporate influence. The outcome serves as a high-stakes test for future economic policies and demonstrates how massive private spending can shape public perception of ballot initiatives.
