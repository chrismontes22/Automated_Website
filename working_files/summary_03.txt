'Hands off our NHS data:'Inside the nationwide movement against Palantir’s £330m NHS deal
============================================================

Protesters across the UK held demonstrations on October 1, 2026, demanding the government terminate its £330 million contract with Palantir Technologies for managing sensitive NHS patient health data.

### Key Points
- Demonstrations occurred in over 30 UK cities, including London and Bristol, to oppose Palantir’s role in the NHS Federated Data Platform (FDP).
- Critics cite concerns over Palantir’s history with US immigration enforcement and military operations, fearing potential misuse of UK health data.
- The £330 million FDP contract, awarded in 2023, faces scrutiny over claims of poor system performance and lack of transparent evidence regarding efficiency gains.
- Over 44,000 people have filed legal objections to their data being processed by the platform, which currently lacks an opt-out mechanism for patients.
- Political pressure is mounting as 76% of Labour voters and various parliamentary committees urge the government to utilize a 2027 break clause.

### Why it Matters
The controversy highlights a growing public conflict between the need for modernized digital healthcare infrastructure and the demand for data sovereignty and ethical corporate partnerships. As the government weighs the contract's renewal, the outcome will set a significant precedent for how the UK manages public trust and private sector involvement in sensitive national services.
