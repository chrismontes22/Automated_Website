'SNL' takes aim at Kevin O’Leary and his Utah data center: 'I'm going to make the future go away’
============================================================

"Saturday Night Live" parodied investor Kevin O'Leary and his controversial Stratos Project data center in Utah, highlighting growing public opposition to large-scale artificial intelligence infrastructure developments across America.

### Key Points
- Comedian James Austin Johnson portrayed Kevin O'Leary in a sketch mocking the investor's plans for a massive data center in Utah.
- The real-world Stratos Project faced significant local backlash, forcing O'Leary to reduce the proposed site size from 40,000 to 20,000 acres.
- Critics cite concerns regarding the facility's high water consumption, environmental impact, and a perceived lack of transparency from developers.
- A Utah advocacy group filed a defamation lawsuit against O'Leary and Fox News following comments linking project opponents to the Chinese Communist Party.
- A May Gallup poll indicated that more than half of Americans oppose the construction of AI data centers within their local communities.

### Why it Matters
The sketch highlights the widening cultural and political divide between tech developers and local residents regarding the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure. As companies race to build power-intensive facilities, the resulting disputes over land use and resource management are increasingly influencing public policy and legal landscapes.
