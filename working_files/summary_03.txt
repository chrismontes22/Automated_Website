‘Outlook Cloudy’: Fed Rate Hike Pits Bulls Against Bears Over Year-End Rally Odds
============================================================

Investors are debating the potential for a year-end market rally as the Federal Reserve’s recent interest rate hike fuels concerns over corporate borrowing costs and economic growth.

### Key Points
- The S&P 500 has historically declined by an average of 1.1% during the final two weeks of September before rebounding in October.
- Macro Risk Advisors CEO Dean Curnutt warned that rising interest rates could trigger a market pullback of up to 10% by increasing corporate borrowing costs.
- Goldman Sachs shares fell 4% after CEO David Solomon reported softer fixed-income trading activity, signaling potential impacts from higher rates.
- Citadel Securities remains optimistic, noting that the S&P 500 has historically gained 5.6% during midterm election years between late September and the end of the year.
- Data from Goldman Sachs indicates that while markets often dip during initial rate-hiking cycles, the S&P 500 has historically posted an average 12-month gain of 9%.

### Why it Matters
The Federal Reserve's interest rate policy serves as a critical indicator for market stability and corporate profitability in the coming months. Investors are weighing these macroeconomic pressures against historical trends to determine if the current volatility will lead to a sustained year-end rally.
