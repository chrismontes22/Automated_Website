5 Reasons why a foldable iPad Duo is a BAD Idea
============================================================

Apple recently unveiled its first foldable phone, sparking industry speculation about whether the company will expand its foldable hardware lineup to include a tablet-sized "iPad Duo."

### Key Points
- Apple’s foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, follows a decade of development and a shift toward a hardware and services-focused business model.
- Industry experts argue that foldable tablets lack a clear market, as evidenced by the limited success of devices like the Huawei MateBook Fold.
- Engineering a large-scale foldable device presents significant cost and durability challenges, particularly regarding hinge reliability and the high price of large, flexible OLED displays.
- Current tablet and laptop users prioritize portability and affordability, whereas a foldable tablet would likely exceed a $3,000 price point without offering unique utility.
- The success of the affordable MacBook Neo suggests that consumers currently favor cost-effective computing over experimental, high-priced foldable form factors.

### Why it Matters
The potential development of a foldable iPad highlights the tension between Apple’s history of premium innovation and the current market demand for accessible, practical technology. If Apple pursues this category, it risks creating a niche, cost-prohibitive product that could cannibalize existing iPad and MacBook sales while complicating the software ecosystem for developers.
