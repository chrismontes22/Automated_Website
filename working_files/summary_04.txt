Time is a-changin’: say goodbye to the leap second
============================================================

The International Bureau of Weights and Measures has voted to retire the leap second by May 20, 2025, to prevent potential technical infrastructure failures caused by inconsistent time synchronization.

### Key Points
- The BIPM will officially end the use of leap seconds on World Metrology Day, May 20, 2025.
- The decision was expedited due to concerns that subtracting time to match Earth's rotation could disrupt critical digital infrastructure.
- Major technology companies, including Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon, have publicly supported the move to eliminate the adjustment.
- Future timekeeping will allow atomic and astronomical time to drift until a full "leap hour" is required, an event not expected for approximately 3,000 years.
- Previous leap second additions have caused significant service outages for platforms such as Reddit, LinkedIn, Yelp, and Foursquare.

### Why it Matters
Eliminating the leap second removes a source of instability for global digital systems that rely on precise, continuous time synchronization. This shift simplifies infrastructure management for major tech firms and prevents the unprecedented risks associated with attempting to subtract time from atomic clocks.
