Why the current tech backlash feels different
============================================================

**The Verge’s *Decoder* podcast addresses listener feedback on AI skepticism, the "software brain" thesis, and the evolving nature of tech industry accountability in a polarized political climate.**

### Key Points
- Host Nilay Patel explains that his "software brain" critique focuses on the limitations of viewing all human activity through a database-driven, automated framework.
- The show highlights a growing bipartisan backlash against surveillance technologies like Flock cameras, noting that public resistance often transcends algorithmic echo chambers.
- Patel discusses the challenges of interviewing politicians and CEOs, emphasizing that his goal is to maintain journalistic independence despite intense media training and PR pressure.
- The episode addresses the "liberal bubble" perception of platforms like Bluesky, noting that social products must prioritize growth and broad appeal to remain viable.
- The production team confirms that *The Verge* and its parent company, PMX Global, are involved in active antitrust litigation against Google regarding AI search summaries.

### Why it Matters
This discussion underscores the widening gap between the tech industry’s push for rapid AI integration and the public’s demand for agency, control, and transparency. By analyzing these tensions, the podcast highlights how consumer skepticism is forcing a realignment in how both media outlets and political figures approach the influence of big tech.
