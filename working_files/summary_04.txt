Here Are the Tell-Tale Signs a Startup Founder Used AI to Apply to Y Combinator: ‘It Feels Like Being Lied to’
============================================================

Y Combinator leaders report a significant rise in startup applications written by artificial intelligence, evidenced by increased response lengths and the frequent use of specific stylistic markers.

### Key Points
- Y Combinator application lengths have increased by 60% over the past three years, suggesting widespread use of AI tools by founders.
- The use of the word "wedge" in applications surged from less than 1% in spring 2025 to over 20% by summer 2026.
- Em dashes, particularly those with surrounding spaces, appeared in more than 50% of applications by summer 2025.
- Partners Tyler Bosmeny and Pete Koomen identified these trends as clear indicators of AI-generated content following the release of models like Anthropic’s Opus 4.
- Co-founder Paul Graham noted that founders are increasingly adopting a "hard-hitting journalistic style" that feels inauthentic compared to traditional founder communication.

### Why it Matters
The reliance on AI for accelerator applications complicates the evaluation process for investors who prioritize authentic founder insights and original thinking. This trend forces firms like Y Combinator to reconsider how they assess startup potential when standard application responses become increasingly homogenized by automated writing tools.
