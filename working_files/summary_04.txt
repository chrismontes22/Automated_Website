What AI Anxiety Looks Like in China
============================================================

Despite China’s national mandate to lead in artificial intelligence by 2035, the country faces widespread public anxiety and economic instability as workers fear displacement by rapidly advancing AI technologies.

### Key Points
- The Chinese government has prioritized AI integration across its economy and society, aiming for full implementation by 2035.
- Workers across various sectors, including entertainment and voice acting, report losing jobs to AI systems that replicate their likenesses and professional data.
- Many Chinese businesses have struggled with AI adoption, with nearly 70 percent of small firms failing to move beyond initial trials due to high costs and complexity.
- Popular open-source AI tools like OpenClaw have faced backlash from users who reported security risks, unauthorized actions, and financial losses.
- Beijing is attempting to balance technological dominance with social stability, recently signaling that mass layoffs caused by AI may be legally and politically unacceptable.

### Why it Matters
The rapid, state-led push for AI in China creates a significant tension between the government's desire for global technological supremacy and the need to maintain domestic social order. This balancing act highlights the broader risks of AI-driven labor displacement, suggesting that even authoritarian regimes must eventually confront the political consequences of widespread economic disruption.
