CoreWeave’s debt is becoming the whole AI industry’s problem
============================================================

CoreWeave bonds have entered distressed territory as investor concerns grow regarding the company's massive debt load and the long-term financial sustainability of the broader artificial intelligence infrastructure buildout.

### Key Points
- CoreWeave bonds recently hit distressed status, defined by yields exceeding government bonds by 1,000 basis points.
- The company reported over $35 billion in debt and a loss exceeding $1 billion during the first half of the year.
- Cofounder Brannin McBee stated intentions to pursue investment-grade status to access insurance capital, fueling investor fears of further borrowing.
- CoreWeave holds $103.7 billion in remaining performance obligations, with Microsoft, OpenAI, and Nvidia accounting for nearly 75% of second-quarter revenue.
- Market volatility has spread to other "neocloud" firms, with Oracle shares and bonds experiencing significant declines following the news.

### Why it Matters
The financial instability of CoreWeave highlights the high-stakes risks associated with the capital-intensive AI infrastructure market. If these companies cannot maintain profitability while servicing massive debt, it could trigger broader market contagion across the technology and credit sectors.
