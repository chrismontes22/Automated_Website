The AI trade is already sketching its playbook for a possible apocalypse
============================================================

Investors are recalibrating their portfolios as concerns over AI safety and potential development slowdowns shift market focus toward cybersecurity, established software platforms, and away from hardware-heavy infrastructure providers.

### Key Points
- Cybersecurity stocks surged on Monday, with CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks rising 14% and 13% respectively, as investors prioritized digital defense.
- Software companies including Adobe, ServiceNow, and Workday gained between 5% and 7% as a slower AI rollout eases pressure on their existing business models.
- Hyperscalers Alphabet and Meta rose 3% each, benefiting from their deep cash reserves and established market positions during the period of industry uncertainty.
- The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 6% and the Roundhill Memory ETF fell 7% as investors trimmed exposure to companies reliant on rapid infrastructure expansion.
- The S&P 500 declined 0.5% overall, reflecting a broader rotation in market sentiment rather than a complete exit from the artificial intelligence sector.

### Why it Matters
This shift indicates that the AI investment narrative is evolving from a focus on rapid hardware buildouts to a more cautious, security-oriented phase. Investors are prioritizing companies with defensive moats and established cash flows, suggesting that the long-term viability of AI remains intact despite changing regulatory and safety expectations.
