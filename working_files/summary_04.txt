Microsoft shuts down its beloved maker space
============================================================

Microsoft is winding down its internal "The Garage" program, a long-standing initiative that provided dedicated maker spaces for employees to collaborate on experimental hardware and software projects.

### Key Points
- The Garage program, which operated for 17 years, is closing its centralized maker spaces across the globe starting October 23rd.
- The initiative served as an incubator for successful products, including the Xbox Adaptive Controller and the Seeing AI accessibility application.
- Microsoft plans to move away from dedicated physical workshops, claiming the "hacker mindset" is now integrated into daily team operations.
- The closure follows other recent internal cutbacks, including the shuttering of the company’s employee library earlier this year.
- Future plans for the annual employee hackathon remain uncertain as the company reimagines the program to fit the current technology landscape.

### Why it Matters
The closure of The Garage signals a shift in Microsoft’s corporate culture as the company moves away from centralized, physical innovation hubs toward more integrated, team-based workflows. This transition reflects broader cost-cutting measures and a strategic pivot that may impact how employees develop and prototype experimental technologies in the future.
