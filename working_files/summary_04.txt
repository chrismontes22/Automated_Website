Google takes down a massive Android TV botnet, but infected devices remain dangerous
============================================================

Google has dismantled a massive malware operation involving Videostrong Technology that compromised over 1.3 million Android TV boxes by embedding a persistent backdoor directly into device firmware.

### Key Points
- Google identified the XCPE malware, which grants attackers system-level privileges to install apps, capture keystrokes, and reroute internet traffic.
- The malware was pre-installed by manufacturer Videostrong Technology on various Android-based devices, including TV boxes, karaoke machines, and digital signage.
- Because the malicious code resides in a protected firmware partition, it automatically reinstalls itself upon device reboot, rendering standard app removal ineffective.
- Google collaborated with international law enforcement and security firms to disable command-and-control servers, potentially impacting 10 million additional compromised devices.
- Google has banned Videostrong from producing certified Android devices and implemented stricter firmware integrity checks for all manufacturers.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a significant supply chain vulnerability where hardware manufacturers intentionally embed malware at the firmware level. Consumers using affected devices may need to replace their hardware entirely, as the deep-seated nature of the infection makes standard security software unable to fully remediate the threat.
