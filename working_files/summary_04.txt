An Amazon data center could have the worst polluting power plant in the country
============================================================

Amazon is investing in a new natural-gas power plant in Pecos County, Texas, to supply electricity to its data center, raising concerns over potential greenhouse gas emissions.

### Key Points
- The GW Ranch facility is permitted to emit up to 33 million tons of CO2 annually, potentially exceeding the output of the largest U.S. coal plants.
- The plant will utilize 35 natural-gas turbines to generate 7.65 gigawatts of electricity specifically for Amazon’s new data center campus.
- Amazon confirmed it purchased the site and plans to source power from the facility, which will initially operate independently of the Texas power grid.
- The project faces scrutiny regarding Amazon’s Climate Pledge, which aims for carbon neutrality by 2040 despite rising emissions driven by artificial intelligence infrastructure.
- Amazon stated the facility is designed to eventually connect to the grid and may incorporate future solar energy and battery storage solutions.

### Why it Matters
This project highlights the growing tension between the massive energy demands of AI development and corporate sustainability commitments. It underscores a broader industry trend where major tech companies are increasingly building private power infrastructure to bypass grid limitations, potentially complicating national climate goals.
