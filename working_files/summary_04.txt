Apple Sued by Customers Who Lost Combined $1.8 Million Through Fake Bitcoin Wallet in App Store
============================================================

Three Apple customers filed a federal lawsuit in California seeking damages after losing $1.8 million to a fraudulent Bitcoin wallet application disguised as the legitimate Sparrow Wallet service.

### Key Points
- Plaintiffs James Ramirez, Christopher Ellis, and Jalen Delgado lost a combined $1.8 million in Bitcoin between May and August 2025.
- The lawsuit alleges Apple failed to properly vet apps, violating the California Consumers Legal Remedies Act despite marketing the App Store as a secure environment.
- Sparrow Wallet is a desktop-only application and does not offer an official iOS version, yet multiple spoof apps have appeared on the App Store.
- Developer Craig Raw claims Apple ignored repeated reports regarding fake apps and previously threatened to terminate his developer account for attempting to warn users.
- The plaintiffs are seeking full reimbursement for their stolen cryptocurrency assets and damages for Apple's alleged negligence.

### Why it Matters
This litigation challenges the efficacy of Apple’s "walled garden" security model and its legal responsibility for fraudulent content distributed through its platform. The outcome could force stricter app review protocols and redefine the extent of Apple's liability regarding consumer protection in the digital marketplace.
