Big tech is quietly preparing for Democratic scrutiny as Trump tries to crack down on free speech
============================================================

Major technology companies are increasingly resisting ICE data requests as they prepare for potential Congressional investigations into their compliance with government surveillance of critics and journalists.

### Key Points
- ICE has utilized administrative subpoenas, which do not require judicial approval, to obtain user data from platforms including Google, Meta, Reddit, TikTok, and X.
- The Department of Homeland Security has expanded its definition of doxing to include names and titles, facilitating broader surveillance of protestors and journalists.
- Tech companies are updating their terms of service to increase transparency regarding how user data is shared with federal authorities.
- Lawmakers, including Sen. Ron Wyden and Rep. Pramila Jayapal, have accused ICE of using these subpoenas to suppress free speech and intimidate citizens.
- Tech executives are currently delaying or pushing back on certain data requests to mitigate legal risks ahead of potential Democrat-led Congressional inquiries.

### Why it Matters
These developments highlight a growing tension between federal law enforcement surveillance tactics and the privacy responsibilities of major technology platforms. As political scrutiny intensifies, companies are forced to balance legal compliance with the risk of future legislative investigations into their data-sharing practices.
