Store-bought hardware crypto wallets had secret chips installed that enabled stealing crypto recovery phrases
============================================================

An investigation has revealed that compromised Ledger hardware wallets, modified with malicious eSIM and LTE modules to steal recovery phrases, have been sold by retailers across Asia and Europe.

### Key Points
- Attackers have siphoned at least $93 million from users by installing hidden PCBs that capture private keys during the initial device setup process.
- Compromised units have been identified at retailers including Cryptobilis in Southeast Asia and MediaMarkt locations in Germany.
- Evidence suggests the malicious hardware modifications are being mass-produced, rather than hand-assembled, indicating a sophisticated supply chain interception.
- Ledger advises users who purchased devices from unauthorized or suspicious resellers in the last 90 days to avoid setup and move assets to a new wallet.
- The company provides official documentation and visual guides on its website to help users verify the physical integrity of their hardware wallet components.

### Why it Matters
This discovery highlights a critical vulnerability in the hardware wallet supply chain, proving that even "cold storage" devices can be compromised before reaching the end user. It underscores the necessity of purchasing security hardware exclusively from official manufacturers to mitigate the risk of sophisticated, pre-installed hardware backdoors.
