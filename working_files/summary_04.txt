Meta's historic $18 billion settlement may mean little for the plight of teens
============================================================

Meta has reached a historic $18 billion settlement with US states to resolve claims that its app designs harm children by implementing new teen safety and usage restrictions.

### Key Points
- Meta will introduce two-hour daily time limits, overnight notification silences, and interruptions to infinite scrolling for teen users.
- The company agreed to hide like counts by default, limit beauty filters, and provide options for non-algorithmic content feeds.
- This settlement represents the largest payout by a Big Tech company to state attorneys general regarding platform safety concerns.
- Meta is calling on competitors like TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar safety standards to ensure consistent protections across the industry.
- Privacy advocates, including the ACLU, warn that mandatory age verification requirements could lead to increased data collection and potential First Amendment violations.

### Why it Matters
This settlement sets a significant legal precedent that could force other social media platforms to overhaul their design features to prioritize teen mental health. While the agreement aims to curb digital addiction, it also raises complex questions about the balance between protecting minors and maintaining user privacy across the internet.
