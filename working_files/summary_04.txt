Journalist Calls Out 'Collective Amnesia' of Schools' Romance With Big Tech Over AI
============================================================

New York Times reporter Natasha Singer examines how Big Tech companies influence American public education in her new book, Coding Kids: Big Tech's Battle to Remake Public Schools.

### Key Points
- Natasha Singer’s book investigates how major technology firms have historically shaped and captured computer science and AI curricula in U.S. public schools.
- The narrative highlights the role of Code.org and its leader, Hadi Partovi, in promoting tech-focused education over the past thirteen years.
- Major corporations including Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are currently competing to integrate AI tools and software licenses into school districts nationwide.
- Singer argues that society suffers from "collective amnesia" regarding the recurring cycles of tech industry promises to revolutionize education through various digital tools.
- The book cites research contributions from long-time Slashdot user "theodp" and consumer advocate Ralph Nader.

### Why it Matters
This investigation highlights the ongoing tension between corporate interests and public education as schools increasingly adopt AI tools despite growing parental distrust of Big Tech. It challenges stakeholders to critically evaluate whether these recurring technological integrations truly benefit students or primarily serve the strategic expansion of tech companies.
