Lights out, Instagram off? The changes to Meta for teens could be a big deal
============================================================

Meta has agreed to a $17.1 billion settlement to resolve a federal lawsuit, implementing significant safety reforms on Facebook and Instagram to reduce social media addiction among minors.

### Key Points
- Meta will implement a default two-hour daily time limit and block nighttime usage for users under 18.
- New restrictions include disabling notifications during school hours and hiding public like counts on posts.
- The company must provide non-personalized feed options and ban cosmetic procedure filters for teen accounts.
- Meta is required to improve age verification systems and submit to regular oversight by an independent auditor.
- California Attorney General Rob Bonta led a coalition of 50 states and territories to secure the agreement.
- Meta is calling on industry competitors like TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar safety frameworks.

### Why it Matters
This settlement establishes a significant legal precedent for holding major technology companies accountable for the design features that impact youth mental health. By forcing industry-wide changes, the agreement aims to mitigate clinical dependency and sleep disruption among adolescents while pressuring other platforms to adopt similar safety standards.
