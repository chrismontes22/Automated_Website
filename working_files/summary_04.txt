This Week in Security: Flock Cameras Are Old, Microsoft Patches Patches, and Researchers attack SSH
============================================================

Security researchers have exposed critical vulnerabilities in Flock surveillance cameras, revealing outdated software, hard-coded API credentials, and the unauthorized collection of personal data from unsuspecting citizens.

### Key Points
- Flock cameras run on an obsolete Android 8.1 version with unpatched kernel vulnerabilities dating back to 2018.
- Analysis shows the devices store API keys and decryption keys in plain text, allowing unauthorized access to captured video.
- Evidence suggests the cameras capture video of people and vehicles, contradicting company claims that they only record license plates.
- A single camera captured 1.6 million photos of 50,000 vehicles in just 21 days, highlighting extensive data collection.
- Flock has dismissed these findings, stating they cannot perform a security assessment based on incomplete reports from third parties.

### Why it Matters
These findings highlight the significant privacy and security risks posed by the widespread deployment of internet-connected surveillance hardware with poor maintenance standards. When manufacturers fail to patch known vulnerabilities or secure sensitive data, they create permanent backdoors that can be exploited to monitor public spaces and compromise personal information.
