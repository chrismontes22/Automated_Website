Google Gets to Keep Its Adtech Stack Intact. Does The Trade Desk’s Pitch Lose its Punch?
============================================================

Google’s adtech business remains intact following a federal court ruling, potentially weakening the competitive market position of independent demand-side platform The Trade Desk and its open-web strategy.

### Key Points
- A federal judge ruled that Google will not be forced to break up its adtech stack, instead requiring limited operational changes to increase auction transparency.
- The Trade Desk built its business model as a neutral alternative to Google’s vertically integrated ecosystem, relying on tools like Unified ID 2.0 and OpenPath.
- Meta’s AI assistant, Muse, has become the leading source of AI-agent web traffic, generating over double the requests of all other measured agents combined.
- AMD announced plans to acquire AI startup World Labs for $8.2 billion in stock, while Samsung is investing $1 billion into the AI-infrastructure firm Helix.
- High-profile executive shifts include Roger Lynch leaving Condé Nast to become CEO of Mattel and the departure of three top BBDO New York leaders.

### Why it Matters
The court's decision to leave Google’s adtech empire structurally intact challenges the long-term growth narrative of independent competitors like The Trade Desk. As Google maintains its market dominance, these independent firms must pivot their strategies toward emerging sectors like connected TV and agentic advertising to remain relevant.
