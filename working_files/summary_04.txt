North Korea’s hackers using AI for attacks, cybersecurity firm says
============================================================

North Korean state-linked hacking group Kimsuky is utilizing artificial intelligence to automate and scale sophisticated spear-phishing attacks targeting military, diplomatic, and academic sectors, according to a new report.

### Key Points
- South Korean cybersecurity firm Genians identified Kimsuky’s use of AI to generate malicious documents disguised as legitimate research reports and invitations.
- The hackers utilize offline, open-source tools including Ollama, GPT-4All, and Msty to run large language models without triggering internet-based security detections.
- This shift allows threat actors to produce high-quality, deceptive content rapidly, significantly increasing the efficiency of large-scale social engineering campaigns.
- North Korean state-backed groups have a history of cyber operations, including the theft of over $2 billion in cryptocurrency during the first nine months of 2025.

### Why it Matters
The integration of generative AI into cyber warfare lowers the technical barrier for entry, allowing threat actors to execute complex attacks with greater speed and volume. This development signals a broader shift in the global threat landscape where AI-supported social engineering is becoming a standard, highly effective tool for state-sponsored espionage.
