Online scams cost Americans almost $150B last year – 7x more than reported
============================================================

The Consumer Federation of America estimates that online scams cost Americans $148.2 billion last year, a figure significantly higher than official reports due to widespread underreporting of cybercrime.

### Key Points
- The Consumer Federation of America estimates total annual losses from cyber scams reached $148.2 billion, seven times higher than FBI-reported figures.
- Only 14% of financial fraud victims report their losses to law enforcement, leading to a massive discrepancy in official crime statistics.
- Cryptocurrency scams accounted for over half of all reported losses, totaling approximately $11.4 billion in documented incidents.
- Meta platforms are the primary vectors for these scams, with Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp hosting 87% of reported fraudulent activity.
- Scammers are increasingly utilizing AI-driven deepfake technology to impersonate family members and create fraudulent documentation to deceive victims.

### Why it Matters
The massive gap between reported and actual losses highlights a significant challenge for law enforcement in tracking the true scale of digital financial crime. As AI tools lower the barrier for sophisticated fraud, consumers must adopt proactive security measures to protect their assets from increasingly convincing impersonation tactics.
