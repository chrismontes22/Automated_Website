Who Is Planting Backdoors in Ledger Crypto Wallets?
============================================================

Sophisticated hardware implants discovered in Ledger crypto wallets sold by authorized retailers have enabled a coordinated heist, resulting in the theft of approximately $102 million in digital assets.

### Key Points
- Attackers embedded custom-built antennas and LTE modules into Ledger Nano X, Flex, and Stax devices to intercept and transmit user recovery seed phrases.
- Compromised wallets were identified at authorized resellers, including the Malaysian firm CryptoBilis and the European electronics chain MediaMarkt.
- Blockchain research firm Bitquery confirmed that at least 315 accounts were drained of Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron during the recent coordinated attack.
- Security researchers, including Joe Grand and Mark Karpelès, identified multiple generations of increasingly sophisticated, mass-produced hardware implants.
- Data extracted from the implants suggests the stolen seed phrases were transmitted to command-and-control servers hosted in Hong Kong.
- Competitor SatoshiLabs has begun reducing its list of authorized Trezor resellers as a preventive measure against similar supply-chain vulnerabilities.

### Why it Matters
This breach highlights a critical failure in the physical supply chain for hardware security devices, undermining the trust users place in authorized retail channels. The incident forces a reevaluation of security protocols for crypto storage and may lead to stricter distribution controls across the entire hardware wallet industry.
