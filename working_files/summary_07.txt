Face Recognition Is Becoming the Norm for Dating Apps
============================================================

Tinder and other major dating platforms are mandating biometric face-scanning tools to combat a surge in AI-generated deepfakes, romance scams, and fraudulent accounts affecting millions of users.

### Key Points
- Tinder launched mandatory "Face Check" verification for new members in October 2025, with plans to expand the requirement to existing users in the US and UK.
- FBI data indicates that US romance scams resulted in over $4 billion in losses between 2015 and 2025.
- More than a dozen dating apps now utilize 3D authentication, liveness checks, or video selfies to verify user identities.
- Tools for Humanity partnered with Tinder in April to offer optional iris-scanning verification through its "World ID" system.
- Utah’s Digital Identity law, effective May 2025, establishes a "duty of loyalty" to limit data collection and protect user privacy against invasive surveillance practices.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward biometric verification creates a new industry standard that prioritizes security but raises significant concerns regarding long-term privacy and the normalization of mass surveillance. While these tools aim to reduce fraud, experts warn that biometric data is uniquely vulnerable and that "proof of human" systems may offer a false sense of security against sophisticated AI actors.
