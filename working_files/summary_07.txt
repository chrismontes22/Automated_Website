Flying cars are coming. You’ll need $300K and the space to fly it.
============================================================

Alef Aeronautics and Tesla are advancing flying car technology, though experts warn that regulatory hurdles and infrastructure limitations make widespread urban adoption unlikely for the foreseeable future.

### Key Points
- Alef Aeronautics aims to deliver its first flying car by 2025, with a projected retail price of $300,000.
- Tesla plans to begin production of its Roadster, featuring an optional "full flight system," in 2028.
- Alef Aeronautics has logged approximately 1,000 test flights since 2015, according to CEO Jim Dukhovny.
- Experts like UC Berkeley professor Alexandre Bayen argue that mass transit via flying cars remains generations away due to regulatory and infrastructure challenges.
- Current industry focus is shifting toward elite, short-distance travel over water or obstacles rather than daily urban commuting.

### Why it Matters
The development of flying cars highlights a growing divide between high-end technological innovation and the practical realities of modern transportation infrastructure. While these vehicles may offer niche solutions for remote travel, significant regulatory and safety barriers suggest they will remain luxury items rather than viable mass-transit alternatives for the general public.
