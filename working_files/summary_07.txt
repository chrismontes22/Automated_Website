Big Tech Wants to Harvest Your Thoughts
============================================================

Major technology companies are developing consumer neurotechnology products that monitor brain activity, sparking urgent debates regarding the protection of mental privacy and the establishment of fundamental neurorights.

### Key Points
- Tech giants including Apple, Meta, and Snap are actively developing wearable devices capable of interpreting human brain activity.
- Researchers at the University of California successfully enabled a paralyzed patient to communicate at 78 words per minute using a brain-computer interface.
- A University of Texas study demonstrated that AI models can translate fMRI brain scans into coherent words and sentences after training on podcast audio.
- Neuroscientists are advocating for "neurorights" to safeguard personal agency and mental privacy as noninvasive brain-scanning technology enters the consumer market.

### Why it Matters
The transition of neurotechnology from clinical settings to the mass consumer market creates significant risks regarding the unauthorized collection and exploitation of sensitive neural data. Establishing legal frameworks for mental privacy is essential to prevent the misuse of cognitive information by corporations and other entities.
