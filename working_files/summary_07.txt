XBOX made generational leaps with each console — So, what happens next?
============================================================

**Microsoft is celebrating the 25th anniversary of the Xbox brand this November, marking a quarter-century of hardware evolution from the original 2001 console to the modern Series X.**

### Key Points
- The original 2001 Xbox featured an Intel Pentium III CPU, 64 MB of RAM, and an 8–10 GB hard drive.
- The Xbox 360 introduced a 3.2 GHz tri-core IBM PowerPC CPU and transitioned to ATI-designed graphics architecture.
- The Xbox One shifted to an AMD-based x86-64 architecture, incorporating 8 GB of RAM and a 500 GB hard drive.
- Current Xbox Series X consoles utilize AMD Zen 2 CPUs, RDNA 2 GPUs, and 1 TB NVMe SSD storage.
- Microsoft plans to release a special "Xbox Series X25" edition in November to commemorate the 25th anniversary.
- Future hardware, codenamed Project Helix, is expected to leverage AI-assisted upscaling and custom AMD silicon.

### Why it Matters
- Tracking the technical progression of Xbox consoles highlights the industry's shift toward PC-like architectures and the increasing reliance on digital media over physical discs. As manufacturing costs rise due to component shortages and AI-driven demand, the next generation of gaming hardware faces significant pricing uncertainty for consumers.
