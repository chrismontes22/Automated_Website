Poland asks EU to fine Meta €250 million, urges action against scams and false ads
============================================================

Poland has requested that the European Commission impose a €250 million fine on Meta Platforms for failing to remove fraudulent advertisements from its social media services.

### Key Points
- Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski cited tests by CERT Polska identifying 122 fraudulent advertisements on Meta platforms.
- Data shows Meta failed to remove 106 of the reported fraudulent ads, representing an 86.8% inaction rate.
- The Polish government is seeking a €250 million penalty to serve as a deterrent against deceptive advertising practices.
- Minister Gawkowski plans to lobby European leaders at the upcoming G20 summit to form a unified front against Meta’s content moderation policies.
- A Warsaw appellate court ruled in April 2024 that Meta holds responsibility for the advertisements hosted on its platforms.

### Why it Matters
This request highlights growing regulatory pressure on Meta regarding its accountability for user-generated content and deceptive advertising. If the European Commission pursues the fine, it could set a significant legal precedent for how tech giants are held liable for fraudulent activity across the European Union.
