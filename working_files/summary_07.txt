How Meta tried, and failed, to grab its employees' data for AI
============================================================

Meta suspended its Model Capability Initiative after employees revolted against the mandatory program, which tracked keystrokes and mouse movements to train artificial intelligence models on human computer usage.

### Key Points
- The Model Capability Initiative (MCI) aimed to collect employee data to help AI models replicate human tasks like using keyboard shortcuts and dropdown menus.
- Over 1,800 Meta employees signed a petition against the program, citing concerns over privacy, sensitive data exposure, and potential job displacement.
- Internal backlash intensified after a security leak revealed that collected data was accessible across the company, contradicting leadership's claims of strict control.
- Meta’s Chief Technology Officer, Andrew Bosworth, initially defended the program as a necessary tool for AI development before the company eventually suspended it.
- The program remains suspended, with Meta stating that any future iteration would be strictly opt-in for staff.

### Why it Matters
This conflict highlights the growing tension between corporate AI ambitions and employee privacy rights in the workplace. It serves as a significant case study on how internal labor activism can successfully challenge data-harvesting practices at major technology firms.
