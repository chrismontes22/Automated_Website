Brave’s Browser Hides Your Real Email When You’re Signing Up for Websites
============================================================

Brave has launched a new email alias feature for its browser, allowing users to generate five free, anonymous addresses to prevent tracking and protect against data breaches.

### Key Points
- Brave users can now create five managed dummy email addresses that forward messages to their primary inbox.
- The feature is designed to prevent ad tech companies from using email addresses as persistent identifiers to track user activity across the web.
- Users must sign up for a Brave account and navigate to the browser's settings to generate and manage their aliases.
- Brave utilizes Opaque technology to ensure the company does not learn or store user account passwords.
- All alias-related data is encrypted at rest, and emails are deleted from Brave’s servers within seconds of delivery.

### Why it Matters
This feature provides a practical tool for users to minimize their digital footprint and reduce the risk of their primary email being exposed in data breaches. By limiting the distribution of personal identifiers, it challenges the data-harvesting practices commonly employed by major advertising platforms.
