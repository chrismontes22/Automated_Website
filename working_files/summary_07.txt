xAI’s last-minute scramble to stop Minnesota’s anti-nudification app law
============================================================

xAI has filed a lawsuit against Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison to block a new state law that imposes strict penalties on companies providing "nudification" image-editing software.

### Key Points
- The Minnesota law, signed by Governor Tim Walz, takes effect August 1 and allows civil penalties of up to $500,000 per violation.
- xAI argues the statute is unconstitutionally overbroad and violates First Amendment protections for artistic or consensual content.
- The lawsuit claims xAI already employs sophisticated technical controls to prevent the generation of nonconsensual or explicit imagery.
- A report by the Center for Countering Digital Hate previously found Grok generated millions of sexualized images, including thousands depicting minors, in early 2026.
- xAI contends that existing federal and state statutes are sufficient to address the nonconsensual dissemination of deepfakes without the new, punitive state-level restrictions.

### Why it Matters
This legal challenge highlights the ongoing tension between rapid AI development and state-level efforts to regulate the proliferation of nonconsensual deepfake content. The outcome could set a significant precedent for how broadly governments can restrict generative AI tools while balancing free speech rights against public safety concerns.
