Hackers Had A Live Feed Of Every ID This Verification Company Scanned. For Over A Year.
============================================================

A massive data breach at Louisiana-based IDScan.net exposed over 153 million driver’s license scans, with hackers maintaining a live, year-long feed of sensitive identity verification records.

### Key Points
- The breach involves more than 153 million driver’s licenses from the United States and Canada, currently being sold on a dark web service called Nexus.
- IDScan.net provides identity and age verification services for major corporations, including Hertz, FedEx, Target, and numerous cannabis dispensaries.
- Hackers were actively siphoning new data in real time, adding approximately 400,000 records to the database on the day the breach was publicly disclosed.
- The FBI’s New Orleans field office has launched an official inquiry into the source of the leaked images.
- Compromised data includes high-profile individuals, such as the U.S. Secretary of Defense, whose license was listed for sale on the platform.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the inherent security risks associated with centralized identity and age verification systems, which create massive, high-value targets for cybercriminals. The breach demonstrates that even companies claiming strict regulatory compliance can suffer long-term, undetected data exfiltration, putting millions of individuals at risk of permanent identity theft.
