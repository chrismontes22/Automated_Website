Flock Wants The Most Detailed Map of Its Surveillance Cameras Taken Offline
============================================================

A new cybersecurity map reveals that Flock Safety operates over 300,000 surveillance devices nationwide, significantly exceeding the 120,000 cameras the company previously reported to the public this summer.

### Key Points
- Cybersecurity researcher Joshua Michael identified 300,000 Flock devices, including cameras, acoustic sensors, and networking equipment, using data leaked from the company's own servers.
- The dataset includes specific locations for sensitive sites, such as an "FBI Pilot Camera" at the FBI headquarters and various devices inside detention centers.
- Michael discovered a vulnerability in November 2025 that allowed unauthenticated access to Flock’s ArcGIS geographic data, which the company allegedly failed to disclose as a breach.
- Flock Safety has publicly denied experiencing any data breaches, despite evidence that its internal device database was accessible and exfiltrated by researchers.
- The company has recently attempted to suppress the map by filing a trademark infringement complaint through a third-party firm.

### Why it Matters
The discrepancy between Flock’s public statements and the actual scale of its surveillance network raises significant concerns regarding corporate transparency and national security. This expansive infrastructure, which tracks the movements of federal agents and citizens alike, highlights the potential risks of private companies managing vast, sensitive datasets without adequate oversight.
