What does the advent of powerful AI models mean for mathematicians like me?
============================================================

Bowdoin College professor Jennifer Taback explores how integrating frontier AI models into mathematical research and teaching can enhance human productivity while necessitating new academic standards for the discipline.

### Key Points
- Jennifer Taback, a professor at Bowdoin College, uses AI to refine mathematical proofs, reorganize research papers, and overcome the isolation of working in a small department.
- The author argues that AI serves as an effective collaborative partner for brainstorming and improving technical writing, despite the current prohibition of AI as a co-author.
- AI integration is forcing significant changes in classroom assessment, moving away from traditional methods toward new pedagogical strategies for undergraduate education.
- The author highlights the disparity between R1 research universities and smaller liberal arts colleges regarding the resources available to adapt to AI-driven teaching requirements.
- Taback calls for the academic community to establish transparent standards for AI use in research, tenure, and publication to support both senior and junior faculty.

### Why it Matters
The integration of AI into mathematics challenges traditional definitions of proof and authorship, requiring institutions to redefine how they evaluate research and teaching. Broadening the conversation beyond elite research universities is essential to ensure that faculty at all institutions can effectively adapt to these technological shifts.
