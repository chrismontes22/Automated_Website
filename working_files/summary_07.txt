I ditched my cloud password manager for KeePassXC, and I feel more secure than ever
============================================================

Users are increasingly migrating from cloud-based password managers like LastPass to local, open-source alternatives like KeePassXC to gain greater control over their sensitive data and security protocols.

### Key Points
- KeePassXC allows users to store password databases locally in a .kdbx file, eliminating reliance on third-party cloud servers.
- The software supports advanced encryption standards, including AES-256 and ChaCha20, alongside key derivation functions like Argon2.
- Users can synchronize local databases across devices using third-party tools like Syncthing or compatible mobile apps such as Strongbox and KeePassDX.
- Transitioning to a local manager requires users to manage their own backups, often utilizing the 3-2-1 strategy to prevent data loss.
- KeePassXC offers high customizability, including support for custom entry fields, plugins, and portable installation options for advanced workflows.

### Why it Matters
Shifting to local password management reflects a growing trend of users prioritizing data sovereignty over the convenience of cloud-based services following high-profile security breaches. While this approach demands more technical responsibility, it mitigates the risk of centralized server attacks and ensures users maintain full ownership of their digital credentials.
