Meta Made 43M Misleading Statements, New Mexico Jury Finds, Including on Its Cambridge Analytica Response
============================================================

A New Mexico jury found Facebook liable for over 43 million violations of the state's Unfair Practices Act regarding deceptive privacy protections and the Cambridge Analytica data scandal.

### Key Points
- A jury determined Facebook made 26 misleading statements to consumers regarding how their personal information was handled and shared with third parties.
- The verdict follows a 2021 lawsuit filed by New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez concerning the 2013 Cambridge Analytica data breach.
- The state is seeking the maximum civil penalty of $5,000 per violation, which could result in billions of dollars in total fines.
- Judge Francis Mathew will determine the final financial penalties and potential court-ordered mandates for Meta to audit its data management practices.

### Why it Matters
This verdict represents a significant legal escalation in state-level oversight of how major technology companies handle user privacy and data security. The potential for multi-billion dollar penalties sets a major precedent for future consumer protection litigation against social media platforms.
