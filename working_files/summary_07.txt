New York school pauses plan to deploy humanlike AI robot teacher after backlash
============================================================

The Salamanca City Central School District has paused the deployment of an AI-powered humanoid robot following public backlash regarding data privacy concerns and the manufacturer's corporate affiliations.

### Key Points
- The district purchased a $60,000 humanoid robot named "Sally" from the Las Vegas-based company Realbotix.
- Critics, including the New York State United Teachers union, raised concerns over Realbotix's corporate ties to a manufacturer of hyper-realistic sex dolls.
- State Education Commissioner Betty Rosa questioned the robot's role as a "tutoring platform" and requested further clarification on student data security.
- District officials maintain the robot is intended to support STEAM education and will not record audio, video, or collect personal student information.
- The pilot program remains on hold while the district negotiates enhanced data privacy agreements with state education officials.

### Why it Matters
This controversy highlights the growing tension between school districts seeking to integrate emerging technology and stakeholders concerned about student privacy and the role of AI in classrooms. The situation serves as a case study for how corporate associations and ethical concerns can derail educational technology initiatives, even when districts emphasize that such tools are meant to supplement rather than replace human educators.
