What gold's nearly 30% crash from all-time highs tells us about markets and the economy
============================================================

Gold prices are on track for their worst annual performance in over a decade, falling 9% this year as investors pivot away from the metal toward higher-yielding assets.

### Key Points
- Gold is trading below $4,000 an ounce, marking a 29% decline from its January peak.
- Rising Treasury yields and a stronger US dollar have increased the opportunity cost of holding gold.
- Global demand for gold bars, coins, and jewelry has dropped significantly, with year-over-year declines of 19% and 17% respectively.
- Investors are liquidating gold holdings to cover margin calls and raise cash following the recent correction in AI-related stocks.
- Markets are pricing in a 75% probability of further Federal Reserve interest rate hikes before the end of the year.

### Why it Matters
The breakdown of gold's traditional role as a safe-haven asset suggests that market sentiment has shifted toward extreme risk-off behavior. This trend indicates that investors are prioritizing liquidity and yield over traditional hedges, potentially signaling a prolonged period of stagnation for the precious metal.
