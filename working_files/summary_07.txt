Does an 'Elite' chip really matter? I put two Android flagships to the test to find out
============================================================

Benchmark testing comparing the Motorola Razr Ultra 2026 and Razr Fold reveals that newer base-model Snapdragon chips can outperform older "Elite" processors in sustained thermal and AI tasks.

### Key Points
- The Snapdragon 8 Elite in the Razr Ultra 2026 leads in raw single-core CPU and short-burst gaming performance.
- The Snapdragon 8 Gen 5 in the Razr Fold demonstrates superior on-device AI processing capabilities.
- The Razr Ultra 2026 failed the 3DMark Wild Life Extreme stress test due to overheating, indicating thermal management issues.
- The Razr Fold completed the 20-minute stress test with 56.5% stability, proving more reliable under sustained heavy workloads.
- Benchmark results suggest that "Elite" branding does not guarantee better real-world performance across all device form factors.

### Why it Matters
These findings highlight that consumers should prioritize overall thermal efficiency and specific use cases over premium chip branding when selecting a smartphone. Relying solely on marketing labels can be misleading, as a newer base-model processor may offer better stability and AI functionality than an older, high-performance chip that struggles with heat dissipation.
