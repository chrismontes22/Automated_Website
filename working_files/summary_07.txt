AI Made Building Your Own Software Easier Than Ever — But That Doesn’t Mean You Should. Here’s What It Could Cost You.
============================================================

While artificial intelligence has made software development accessible to non-engineers, businesses must now carefully weigh the long-term risks of building internal tools versus purchasing reliable, vendor-supported software solutions.

### Key Points
- AI tools have significantly lowered the technical barriers to entry, allowing individuals without formal engineering backgrounds to build functional software applications.
- Self-built software requires internal teams to manage ongoing maintenance, security, and 24/7 reliability, responsibilities typically handled by professional vendors.
- Relying on third-party vendors provides the benefit of collective experience, as products improve through feedback and edge cases identified by a broad customer base.
- Internal development is best suited for low-stakes, contained projects like internal dashboards where the potential impact of a system failure is minimal.
- Customer-facing technology carries high reputational risks, making it safer to rely on established vendors with dedicated infrastructure and security compliance teams.

### Why it Matters
The ease of AI-assisted coding creates a false sense of security that can lead companies to underestimate the operational costs and catastrophic risks of maintaining custom software. Businesses must establish clear guardrails to distinguish between safe internal experimentation and critical customer-facing systems that require professional-grade reliability.
