The Sudden Return of Effective Altruism
============================================================

The philosophy of effective altruism is facing renewed scrutiny as its adherents clash with the Trump administration and grapple with internal divisions regarding AI safety and moral priorities.

### Key Points
- Effective altruism (EA) posits that resources should be allocated to maximize objective "returns," such as lives saved or future technological progress.
- The movement has gained significant influence through the patronage of Silicon Valley figures including Elon Musk, Peter Thiel, and Dustin Moskovitz.
- Critics argue that "longtermism," a radical offshoot of EA, justifies the hoarding of wealth and power by prioritizing hypothetical future lives over immediate human needs.
- The TESCREAL framework identifies EA as part of a broader ideological cluster that includes transhumanism, singularitarianism, and rationalism.
- Internal conflicts have emerged between those focused on practical humanitarian aid and those prioritizing the development of artificial superintelligence.

### Why it Matters
The rise of effective altruism has provided a moral framework that allows tech oligarchs to justify the concentration of wealth and the pursuit of potentially dangerous AI development. By framing their actions as essential for the long-term survival of humanity, these figures can effectively bypass democratic oversight and ignore immediate social crises.
