Retiree, 70, lost over $2,000 in an Amazon impersonation scam. How fraudsters got him ‘hook, line and sinker’
============================================================

A 70-year-old retiree lost over $2,400 to sophisticated fraudsters posing as Amazon employees who used psychological manipulation and personal details to gain his trust and access his accounts.

### Key Points
- Dorian Jones, a 70-year-old retiree, lost approximately $2,400 across 12 separate bank transactions due to an Amazon impersonation scam.
- Scammers utilized multi-person verification tactics and claimed the victim's IP address was compromised to create a false sense of urgency.
- The Federal Trade Commission reports that nearly 96,000 consumers were targeted by Amazon impersonators in a single year, resulting in $27 million in total losses.
- Adults aged 60 and older are disproportionately affected, with a median loss of $1,500 compared to $814 for younger victims.

### Why it Matters
These scams highlight the increasing sophistication of social engineering tactics used to exploit vulnerable populations by mimicking trusted corporate entities. As financial fraud evolves, consumers must remain vigilant against unsolicited calls that demand immediate action or access to personal banking information.
