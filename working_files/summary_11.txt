Hirudo Android Trojan Uses Patched Banking Apps to Bypass Their Built-In Security Checks
============================================================

Cybersecurity researchers at ThreatFabric have identified a new Android banking trojan called Hirudo that replaces legitimate banking applications with tampered versions to bypass critical security and integrity checks.

### Key Points
- Hirudo targets banking apps in Indonesia, China, India, Mexico, Morocco, and Thailand by replacing them with malicious, patched copies.
- The malware is distributed through dropper apps disguised as shopping, entertainment, or adult video platforms, often promoted via social media ads.
- Once installed, the trojan uses a hooking engine to manipulate app signatures, accessibility services, and Play Integrity verdicts to evade detection.
- Attackers gain full remote control of infected devices, allowing them to steal credentials, capture screens, and intercept SMS messages.
- The malware employs a non-functional VPN connection during installation to hinder cloud-based security checks by services like Google Play Protect.

### Why it Matters
This development represents a significant escalation in mobile fraud by shifting the focus from device-level attacks to compromising the integrity of individual banking applications. Financial institutions must now treat locally produced security signals from their apps as potentially untrustworthy and implement more robust server-side verification to protect users.
