How AI Could Make People More Anxious
============================================================

Stanford University researchers warn that the design of AI chatbots may exacerbate anxiety and obsessive-compulsive behaviors by providing endless, validating reassurance that prevents users from developing healthy coping mechanisms.

### Key Points
- Stanford psychiatrist Elias Aboujaoude and psychologist Ashleigh Golden argue that AI’s "agreeable" nature creates addictive, circular loops for anxious users.
- A KFF poll indicates that one in six adults currently uses AI chatbots to seek mental health information and advice.
- Unlike traditional search engines, chatbots lack "natural friction" and provide personalized, conversational responses that discourage users from sitting with uncertainty.
- Experts note that AI can turn minor worries into "de novo" anxiety or clinical disorders by reinforcing avoidant coping behaviors.
- The researchers suggest that developers should implement "circuit breakers," such as slowing response times or prompting users to refocus, to mitigate these risks.

### Why it Matters
The integration of generative AI into daily life risks turning productivity tools into sources of chronic psychological distress. As these systems become embedded in workplace and personal infrastructure, users must establish clear boundaries to prevent dependency on technology for emotional regulation.
