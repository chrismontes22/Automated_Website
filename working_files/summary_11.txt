U.S. Tech companies becoming increasingly reliant on Chinese chip companies blacklisted by the Pentagon
============================================================

Apple and major PC manufacturers are increasingly integrating memory chips from China’s CXMT to bypass high costs and supply shortages caused by the global AI infrastructure boom.

### Key Points
- Apple is testing CXMT components and seeking U.S. government approval to source memory chips from the blacklisted Chinese manufacturer.
- Major PC brands including HP, Asus, and Acer have already begun integrating CXMT DRAM chips into notebook models sold outside the United States.
- The global memory market is currently dominated by Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, and SanDisk, which control over 90% of the supply.
- CXMT’s market valuation has surged eight-fold since its IPO, now rivaling established industry leaders like Micron and Intel.
- CXMT projects a 2,530% increase in net profit for the first half of 2026 as it expands production capacity in Shanghai and Hefei.

### Why it Matters
The reliance on Chinese components highlights a growing conflict between U.S. trade restrictions and the economic necessity for affordable hardware in a supply-constrained market. This shift suggests that corporate survival strategies are effectively undermining Washington's efforts to decouple from the Chinese technology supply chain.
