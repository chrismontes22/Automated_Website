Amid RAM price hikes, Microsoft reportedly raises Windows license cost, forcing OEMs to hike prices even further on hardware
============================================================

Microsoft has reportedly increased Windows licensing fees for hardware manufacturers by 7% to 10%, marking a significantly higher annual price hike than the company’s typical single-digit adjustments.

### Key Points
- Microsoft is raising Windows license fees for original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) by 7% to 10% as of July.
- The price increase is notably higher than the standard single-digit annual adjustments previously implemented by the company.
- Hardware manufacturers are expected to pass these additional costs onto consumers, further increasing retail laptop prices.
- The fee hikes apply exclusively to OEMs and do not affect the $99 or $199 retail prices for individual Windows 11 Home and Pro licenses.
- Microsoft declined to comment on the report, which was originally published by the Taiwanese outlet United Daily News.

### Why it Matters
These licensing hikes arrive during a global component shortage that has already driven significant price increases across the entire personal computer market. By raising costs for manufacturers, Microsoft may further exacerbate the affordability crisis for consumers already facing higher prices for flagship and mid-range laptops.
