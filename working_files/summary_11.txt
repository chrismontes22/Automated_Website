PHP is in the foundation of 70% of websites – here’s what it is and why a flaw in the software is a major hacking threat
============================================================

Global organizations are rushing to patch a critical vulnerability in the PHP programming language after hackers began exploiting the flaw to gain unauthorized control over web servers.

### Key Points
- The vulnerability, discovered in August 2026 using an OpenAI model, affects the core PHP system that powers approximately 70% of all websites.
- Hackers are actively using an exploit tool called PHP2Shell to gain direct command-line access to servers and steal sensitive data.
- The U.S. National Security Agency and various cybersecurity vendors issued urgent warnings after news of the flaw leaked before official patches were released.
- While everyday internet users do not need to take action, website administrators and hosting providers must update their PHP software immediately to secure their systems.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the dual-use nature of artificial intelligence, which can accelerate both the discovery of critical software flaws and the development of malicious exploits. Because PHP serves as a foundational component of the modern web, the rapid patching of these systems is essential to prevent widespread data breaches and operational disruptions.
