A new dawn for digital rights? What Burnham’s 'do better' promise could mean for your privacy
============================================================

New British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has initiated a major government shake-up, canceling the national digital ID plan and restructuring the technology department to prioritize economic efficiency and reform.

### Key Points
- Prime Minister Andy Burnham scrapped the proposed "BritCard" digital ID, citing a desire to reallocate £600 million in annual savings toward cost-of-living support.
- The Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) was dismantled and merged into the Business Department during Burnham's first day in office.
- The government is currently reviewing a £330 million NHS contract with Palantir, with critics urging a shift toward locally developed data infrastructure.
- Despite privacy concerns, Burnham maintains his support for regulating social media access for children under 16, signaling continuity with previous administration policies.

### Why it Matters
Burnham’s early decisions suggest that economic pragmatism, rather than purely ideological shifts, will drive the government's approach to digital policy and surveillance. This strategy creates a unique opening for privacy advocates to influence future tech regulations by framing human rights protections as fiscally responsible alternatives to expensive, controversial projects.
