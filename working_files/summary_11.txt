No standing still: Zero Trust and cybersecurity
============================================================

Rising cyberattacks and data breaches across major industries are prompting businesses to adopt Zero Trust security frameworks to better protect sensitive information from increasingly sophisticated digital threats.

### Key Points
- Recent high-profile breaches include a £1.9 billion incident at Jaguar Land Rover and the theft of data from 2.7 million passengers at WestJet and Collins Aerospace.
- While 96% of companies plan to incorporate Zero Trust strategies, only 35% have successfully reached the implementation stage.
- The Zero Trust philosophy, popularized by John Kindervag, operates on the principle of "never trust, always verify" to limit unauthorized access during a breach.
- Global cybersecurity spending is projected to reach $377 billion by 2028, reflecting a 12.2% annual growth rate in security investments.
- Consumer trust in corporate data handling has dropped significantly, with no industry sector currently maintaining an approval rating above 50%.

### Why it Matters
Adopting a Zero Trust model is essential for businesses to mitigate the severe financial and reputational damage caused by modern, AI-driven cyberattacks. By shifting from perimeter-based security to granular, data-centric verification, organizations can better protect their assets and restore declining consumer confidence in their digital infrastructure.
