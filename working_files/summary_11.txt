Who Owns the Truth About Your Customer-Facing Automation Claims?
============================================================

Public companies are increasingly citing AI risks in annual filings, yet many lack concrete plans to address these disclosures, drawing heightened scrutiny from the Securities and Exchange Commission.

### Key Points
- AI risk mentions in annual corporate filings surged from 4% in 2020 to 43% in 2024, according to research from Maastricht University.
- A review of over 7,800 filings revealed that two-thirds of companies highlight AI benefits while omitting critical risks like service outages.
- The SEC initiated its first AI-washing enforcement action against Presto Automation for misleading investors about its drive-thru voice ordering technology.
- Presto Automation claimed high "non-intervention" rates while relying on offshore human agents in India and the Philippines to complete the majority of orders.
- Regulators are now applying established standards of accuracy and internal control to AI, penalizing companies that fail to substantiate customer-facing claims.

### Why it Matters
The shift toward regulatory enforcement means that vague or unsubstantiated claims about AI performance now pose significant legal and reputational risks to public companies. By establishing clear, defensible metrics for customer-facing AI, organizations can move beyond empty marketing language and satisfy the growing demand for transparency from investors and regulators alike.
