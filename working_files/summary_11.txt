Trump Administration Sides With OpenAI in Publishers’ Copyright Lawsuits
============================================================

The Trump administration filed a statement of interest in federal court supporting OpenAI’s argument that using copyrighted news content to train artificial intelligence models constitutes fair use.

### Key Points
- The U.S. government filed its statement in the Southern District of New York regarding lawsuits from The New York Times and Ziff Davis.
- Federal officials argue that requiring licenses for training data could hinder domestic AI development and provide a competitive advantage to foreign adversaries.
- The filing suggests that AI models promote competition by allowing smaller entities to challenge the dominance of established media corporations.
- The New York Times criticized the administration's stance, arguing that AI companies should compensate creators for the content used to build their products.
- OpenAI faces ongoing legal challenges from various publishers and authors, including George R.R. Martin, over the unauthorized use of copyrighted works.

### Why it Matters
This legal intervention highlights a growing tension between the protection of intellectual property and the rapid expansion of the domestic artificial intelligence industry. A judicial ruling in favor of fair use could significantly lower barriers to entry for AI developers while potentially reducing revenue streams for traditional media organizations.
