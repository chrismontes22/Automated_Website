‘The fixes are architectural, not bigger pipes’: OpenSSL President on what businesses can expect and how to prepare for a post-quantum internet
============================================================

The internet is undergoing a critical transition to post-quantum cryptography to protect sensitive data from future decryption threats posed by the inevitable development of powerful quantum computing technology.

### Key Points
- Quantum computers threaten current public-key encryption, prompting a global shift toward more robust, quantum-resistant cryptographic standards.
- Larger digital signatures required for post-quantum security may cause network congestion and increased latency, particularly on mobile and satellite connections.
- OpenSSL is developing open-source cryptographic libraries to facilitate this transition, focusing on interoperability and system-wide agility.
- Regulatory bodies, including those in the U.S., EU, and Australia, have established deadlines for organizations to adopt quantum-safe protocols by 2030 or 2035.
- Experts advise businesses to prioritize inventory management and cryptographic agility over purchasing specific "quantum-safe" products.

### Why it Matters
This migration is essential because hackers are currently harvesting encrypted data to decrypt it once quantum technology becomes commercially viable. Organizations must act now to secure long-term assets, as the shift requires a fundamental redesign of digital infrastructure rather than simple software updates.
