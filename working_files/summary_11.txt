Military funding, AI and more: Congress returns for short stint before bolting again
============================================================

Congress returns to Washington for a brief pre-election session to address military funding, Russia sanctions, and new artificial intelligence regulations before the upcoming November midterm elections.

### Key Points
- Lawmakers face a packed agenda including the National Defense Authorization Act and potential legislation regarding cryptocurrency and Name, Image and Likeness (NIL) rights.
- The House plans to vote on a Russia sanctions bill and several anti-fraud measures, including the creation of a National Fraud Enforcement Division.
- Congressional committees will host high-level officials, including FBI Director Kash Patel and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, for scheduled hearings this week.
- Legislative leaders are managing a shortened schedule, with the House session limited to one week and the Senate planning for up to three weeks of business.
- Bipartisan pressure is mounting to address AI security concerns following recent reports, with lawmakers demanding information from OpenAI.

### Why it Matters
The current legislative push sets the stage for a complex lame duck session by deferring major budget and fiscal decisions until after the midterm elections. Failure to resolve these priorities now could lead to a significant backlog of must-pass legislation, potentially impacting national defense and federal agency operations.
