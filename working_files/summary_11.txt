'Not a theoretical risk,' feds warn as attackers use AI-made code to hack critical infrastructure controllers
============================================================

Recent developments in the technology sector highlight significant advancements in AI hardware, critical cybersecurity threats, and ongoing updates to open-source software platforms and Linux desktop environments.

### Key Points
- Waymo is developing custom 5nm machine learning accelerators to improve robocar performance, targeting over 1,000 TOPS with ultra-low latency.
- Cerebras introduced its CS-4 rack systems, which double per-chip performance and increase rack density by three times for AI workloads.
- EQT acquired a majority stake in Swiss cybersecurity firm Acronis, valuing the company at more than $3.5 billion.
- Microsoft failed to fully patch a zero-day vulnerability in on-premises SharePoint, leaving systems exposed to active exploitation.
- Debian released versions 13.6 and 12.15, marking the final support for the x86-32 architecture in the long-running Linux distribution.
- Hackers at the DEF CON conference are expanding the Franklin project to harden critical infrastructure and voting systems.

### Why it Matters
These updates reflect a broader industry trend where hardware manufacturers are aggressively optimizing AI infrastructure to maintain competitive advantages in performance and energy efficiency. Simultaneously, the persistence of critical software vulnerabilities and ransomware threats underscores the growing necessity for robust security measures across both enterprise and open-source ecosystems.
