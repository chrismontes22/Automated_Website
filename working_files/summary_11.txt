Group Policy hijacked: PAYLOAD ransomware weaponizes Active Directory GPO
============================================================

A Middle Eastern manufacturing firm suffered a major security breach in April 2026 where attackers weaponized Active Directory Group Policy Objects to disrupt operations without using traditional ransomware encryption.

### Key Points
- Threat actors gained domain admin-equivalent access and deployed a malicious Group Policy Object (GPO) named "PAYLOAD" at the domain root.
- The attack disabled local administrator accounts, altered desktop wallpapers, and enforced ransom banners across all domain-joined Windows workstations.
- No malicious binaries or encryption processes were detected on endpoints, allowing the attack to bypass standard file-based security and EDR detection tools.
- Data exfiltration occurred from file servers, with stolen information subsequently published on the dark web.
- The attack remained dormant for one day after GPO creation, detonating only when endpoints rebooted and applied the new policy configurations.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights a growing trend of "encryptionless extortion," where attackers leverage trusted administrative infrastructure to cause operational chaos rather than relying on file-locking malware. Because the attack lives entirely within Active Directory, traditional endpoint security solutions remain blind to the threat, necessitating a shift toward directory service auditing and strict privileged access governance.
