Michael Hudson: The AI Crash Is Coming, And You Will Pay For It
============================================================

Economists Michael Hudson and Radhika Desai analyze the financial risks and geopolitical implications of artificial intelligence, contrasting American proprietary models with China’s more efficient, open-source implementation strategies.

### Key Points
- The U.S. AI sector faces a potential bubble, with companies borrowing trillions to fund research while struggling to generate sustainable profits.
- China focuses on "automatic intelligence" to solve finite, practical tasks, whereas U.S. firms prioritize monopoly rent-seeking and speculative "superintelligence" claims.
- Massive energy and water consumption by AI data centers is driving up utility costs and sparking local opposition in the United States.
- Experts warn that the inevitable AI market crash may lead to significant taxpayer-funded bailouts, mirroring past financial crises.
- Legal and ethical concerns persist regarding AI surveillance, data privacy, and the lack of accountability for automated decision-making errors.

### Why it Matters
The current trajectory of AI development in the West risks creating a fragile, debt-fueled monopoly that prioritizes rent extraction over genuine technological or social utility. If these speculative investments fail, the resulting economic fallout could force governments to prioritize corporate bailouts over essential public infrastructure and environmental stability.
