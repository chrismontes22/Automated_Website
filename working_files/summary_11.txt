Chrome’s new rules ruined ad blockers. Here are 4 things that still work
============================================================

Google has officially phased out Manifest V2 technology in Chrome, rendering many traditional ad blockers obsolete and forcing users to seek alternative solutions for managing web advertisements.

### Key Points
- Google’s transition to the Manifest V3 standard has made it impossible to use advanced extensions like the original uBlock Origin within the Chrome browser.
- AdGuard offers a robust, system-wide alternative that blocks ads and trackers across apps and browsers, with personal plans starting at $2.49 per month.
- uBlock Origin Lite is a new, Manifest V3-compliant version that provides basic ad-clutter removal but lacks the advanced filtering capabilities of its predecessor.
- Users can configure Private DNS services like NextDNS to block ads at the network level, which minimizes resource usage and works across multiple devices.
- Switching to privacy-focused browsers like Brave or Firefox allows users to retain advanced ad-blocking features that are no longer supported by Chrome.

### Why it Matters
The shift to Manifest V3 significantly limits the ability of browser extensions to intercept and block web traffic, fundamentally changing how users manage their online privacy. This transition forces a trade-off between staying within the Google ecosystem and maintaining the granular control over ad-blocking that many power users require.
