Doing no "evil," Google proposes paving over protected wetlands for a data center
============================================================

Google faces environmental scrutiny as its proposed "Project Zodiac" data center in Fort Wayne, Indiana, requires the development of protected wetlands to accommodate the facility's massive infrastructure footprint.

### Key Points
- Google’s developer, Hatchworks, filed permits stating that avoiding local water resources is not feasible for the planned data center campus.
- The project site includes protected wetlands that serve as natural basins for rainwater, providing essential flood and soil erosion protection for the region.
- The Indiana Department of Environmental Management issued a Phase III water-quality certificate in June despite objections from local residents and environmental groups.
- Official project documentation prioritizes "minimization" of environmental impact over the complete avoidance of the marshland areas.
- The proposed campus is situated on a mix of pre-paved farmland and undeveloped wetlands, requiring significant land alteration to meet design specifications.

### Why it Matters
This project highlights the growing tension between the rapid expansion of AI-driven data infrastructure and the preservation of critical local ecosystems. As tech companies scale their physical footprints, regulators must increasingly balance the economic benefits of large-scale investments against the long-term risks of disrupting natural flood mitigation systems.
