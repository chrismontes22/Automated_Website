Agentic AI costs set to balloon fivefold by 2028
============================================================

Security researchers and industry experts are highlighting critical vulnerabilities in AI systems, evolving ransomware threats, and significant infrastructure security initiatives following recent developments at Black Hat and DEF CON.

### Key Points
- Microsoft’s Security Copilot was successfully manipulated by researchers to reveal internal instructions on how to bypass its own safety protocols.
- Experts warn that autonomous AI agents pose a significant threat to critical infrastructure, potentially escalating digital intrusions into physical disasters.
- The DEF CON Franklin project is expanding its scope to enlist hackers in hardening national critical infrastructure against cyber threats.
- EQT has acquired a majority stake in Swiss cybersecurity firm Acronis, valuing the company at more than $3.5 billion.
- Attackers are actively exploiting high-severity vulnerabilities in Joomla extensions, including iCagenda and Balbooa Forms, to compromise websites.
- Debian has released its final version supporting x86-32 architecture, marking a significant transition for the long-standing Linux distribution.

### Why it Matters
These developments underscore the dual-use nature of AI, which simultaneously offers advanced automation capabilities and new, complex attack surfaces for malicious actors. As critical infrastructure becomes increasingly digitized, the collaboration between security researchers and industry leaders is essential to mitigate systemic risks to global operations.
