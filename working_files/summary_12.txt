Nikkei data breach affected over 1.8 million records
============================================================

Japanese media giant Nikkei confirmed a data breach involving 1.83 million records after unauthorized actors accessed a cloud development environment using stolen credentials on October 7.

### Key Points
- Attackers compromised a cloud service used for system development, exposing 1.83 million records.
- Approximately 150,000 records contained sensitive personal data, including names, email addresses, postal addresses, phone numbers, or birth dates.
- The remaining 1.68 million leaked items consist of non-identifiable information.
- Nikkei confirmed that no credit card details or financial information were compromised in the incident.
- The company reported the breach to Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission on October 11.
- Nikkei has blocked the compromised credentials and is currently notifying all affected individuals.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights the ongoing vulnerability of corporate cloud development environments to credential-based attacks. As Nikkei faces its third major security disclosure since 2022, the breach underscores the critical need for robust multi-factor authentication and stricter access controls to protect user data.
