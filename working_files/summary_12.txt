Flock seeks to have security researchers' map of Flock cameras taken down
============================================================

Security researcher Joshua Michael created a public map of 335,701 Flock surveillance cameras after exploiting an unauthenticated API vulnerability to exfiltrate the company's device location database.

### Key Points
- Researcher Joshua Michael accessed Flock’s device database in November 2025 by querying an unauthenticated ArcGIS endpoint.
- The resulting "Flock Surveillance Map" website currently lists the precise locations of 335,701 cameras.
- Flock fixed the underlying security vulnerability in January 2026 after Michael publicly disclosed his findings.
- The surveillance network covers sensitive areas, including the Pentagon, CIA Headquarters, and FBI Headquarters, with high recording probabilities for nearby residents.
- Flock has issued a trademark infringement complaint against the map website, demanding its removal rather than addressing the data exfiltration.

### Why it Matters
This incident highlights significant privacy and national security concerns regarding the widespread deployment of private surveillance networks near sensitive government facilities. It also raises questions about corporate transparency, as the company’s legal response focuses on trademark claims rather than the potential exposure of its massive device database.
