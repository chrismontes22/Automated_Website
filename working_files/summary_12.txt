Met Police apologises for data breach involving alleged Al Fayed victims
============================================================

The Metropolitan Police has apologized after inadvertently exposing the email addresses of approximately 140 survivors of sexual abuse linked to the late Harrods owner Mohamed Al Fayed.

### Key Points
- The data breach occurred when the Metropolitan Police failed to use blind copy (BCC) while sending an update regarding Operation Cornpoppy to survivors.
- Operation Cornpoppy is an ongoing investigation into individuals who allegedly facilitated or enabled sexual offending by Mohamed Al Fayed.
- The force has referred itself to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) following the incident.
- Investigators confirmed that seven suspects in their 70s and 80s have been interviewed under caution as part of the probe.
- This incident follows a recent ICO enforcement notice issued to the Metropolitan Police regarding systemic failures in handling sensitive personal data.

### Why it Matters
This breach undermines trust in the Metropolitan Police among vulnerable victims who have already faced significant barriers to reporting sexual abuse. It highlights ongoing concerns regarding the force's ability to protect sensitive information, potentially discouraging other survivors from coming forward to assist with criminal investigations.
