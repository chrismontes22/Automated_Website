Attackers Combine ChatGPT Feature Abuse With ClickFix to Deliver Trojan Malware
============================================================

Cybersecurity researchers at Huntress have identified a malicious campaign using fake ChatGPT CustomGPTs and ClickFix social engineering tactics to infect user devices with remote access trojan malware.

### Key Points
- Attackers created a fraudulent CustomGPT named "Plus 5.6" that appeared in sponsored Google search results to impersonate legitimate ChatGPT services.
- The campaign utilizes a "ClickFix" attack, tricking users into pasting malicious commands into their systems under the guise of a Cloudflare CAPTCHA verification.
- Victims are prompted to download a malicious Microsoft Software Installer that sideloads code through a legitimate, Canon-signed application.
- The deployed remote access trojan allows attackers to monitor systems, capture audio and video, and exfiltrate sensitive data to external servers.
- Huntress confirmed at least 40 infections and reported the activity to OpenAI, which removed the initial malicious CustomGPT on September 25.

### Why it Matters
This campaign highlights a growing trend of cybercriminals weaponizing trusted AI platforms and brand names to bypass traditional security defenses. Users should remain vigilant, as no legitimate website will ever require manual command execution to verify human identity.
