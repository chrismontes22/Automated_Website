'AI refuser' quit her dream job, and hopes others follow
============================================================

Gabrielle Boyle resigned from her position at the AFL after the organization denied her request to opt out of using Microsoft’s Copilot AI assistant in the workplace.

### Key Points
- Gabrielle Boyle, a former AFL participation manager, resigned on June 26, three days before the league mandated the use of Microsoft 365 Copilot.
- The AFL informed Boyle in writing that employees do not have the right to refuse access to AI systems, citing compliance requirements.
- Boyle expressed concerns regarding data privacy, the environmental impact of data centers, and the potential for AI to influence human cognition.
- The AFL’s AI policy, which was finalized in March, permits the use of tools like ChatGPT, Salesforce Agentforce, and Claude Code.
- Australian labor laws currently provide limited protections for employees seeking to opt out of workplace AI, as employers generally own the equipment and data produced.

### Why it Matters
This case highlights a growing tension between rapid corporate AI adoption and employee autonomy in the absence of clear legal frameworks. It underscores the challenge for organizations in managing workforce resistance and the potential for talent loss when change management processes fail to address staff concerns.
