What Is Happening With India’s Unified Payments Interface (UPI), the World’s Largest Real-Time Payments System?
============================================================

India’s National Payments Corporation has postponed the introduction of a 0.4% merchant fee on Unified Payments Interface transactions, sparking debate over potential US influence on domestic policy.

### Key Points
- The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) planned a 0.4% fee on UPI transactions exceeding 2,000 rupees starting October 15.
- The rollout is now delayed until after the Diwali festival to avoid disrupting peak consumer spending.
- UPI, launched in 2016, currently processes over 24 billion monthly transactions and has remained free for users and merchants since inception.
- US trade representatives have previously criticized India’s digital payment policies for allegedly favoring domestic players over firms like Visa and Mastercard.
- Walmart’s PhonePe and Alphabet’s Google Pay currently control approximately 80% of the UPI market share by transaction value.

### Why it Matters
The introduction of fees threatens to increase costs for Indian businesses and consumers, potentially slowing the rapid adoption of digital payments. This policy shift reflects broader geopolitical tensions as the US government pushes for a level playing field for American financial firms within India’s massive digital economy.
