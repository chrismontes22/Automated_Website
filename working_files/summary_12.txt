Silicon Valley AI Academy Seeks ‘Main Character’ Students
============================================================

Venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz has launched a $42 million, two-year San Francisco-based academy designed to train young students for careers in an AI-driven technology landscape.

### Key Points
- The Horowitz Andreessen Academy will enroll its first class of approximately 50 students in the fall of 2027.
- The program targets individuals aged 16 to 22 and replaces traditional grades and homework with hands-on startup experience and project-based learning.
- Founding partners include major tech companies such as OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, Palantir, and Anthropic.
- Faculty members will consist of industry leaders, including CEOs Sam Altman, Brian Armstrong, and Dylan Field.
- While the inaugural year is tuition-free, subsequent years will charge fees comparable to elite private universities.

### Why it Matters
This initiative represents a significant challenge to the traditional higher education model by prioritizing industry-specific skills over academic degrees. Its success could influence how future tech talent is cultivated and whether elite firms continue to bypass conventional university pipelines in favor of specialized, corporate-backed training programs.
