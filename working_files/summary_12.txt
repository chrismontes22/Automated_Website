Netflix can secretly make your 4K TV look worse, and your streaming box is why
============================================================

Netflix streaming quality depends on your hardware's certification and security standards, meaning bargain-bin streaming boxes often fail to deliver 4K resolution despite their advertised technical specifications.

### Key Points
- Netflix restricts video quality based on device certification and hardware-level security, often limiting uncertified or budget Android boxes to standard definition.
- Google’s Widevine DRM is a primary gatekeeper, where L1 certification is required for high-definition playback, while L3-only devices are restricted to lower resolutions.
- Streaming devices must support specific hardware-based codecs like HEVC or AV1 and maintain HDCP compliance to successfully decode and display 4K content.
- Netflix maintains a specific list of supported devices, and hardware that lacks official certification may lose access to high-quality streams regardless of its technical specs.
- Licensing costs for DRM, patent royalties for codecs, and secure hardware engineering often lead manufacturers of cheap streaming boxes to cut corners.

### Why it Matters
Consumers often overspend on 4K televisions while neglecting the streaming hardware that acts as the primary gateway for content delivery. Understanding these hardware requirements helps users avoid purchasing budget devices that cannot actually support the high-definition services they intend to use.
