This week on Tom's Hardware Premium: September 12, 2026 — Benchmarking Qwen 3.8, the splintered compute economy and AI breakthroughs
============================================================

Tom’s Hardware explores the latest industry shifts, including OpenAI’s new GPT-6 Astra model, semiconductor supply chain constraints, and the evolving landscape of high-performance AI hardware and research.

### Key Points
- OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra, a frontier-level AI model that claims to solve the complex Navier-Stokes mathematical problem amid ongoing plagiarism and alignment controversies.
- Japanese firm Ajinomoto reports a 30% price increase for ABF substrates, a critical component for AI accelerators produced by Nvidia, Intel, and AMD.
- TSMC, Intel, and Samsung are collaborating with ASML to transition toward 6×12-inch photomasks to improve High-NA EUV lithography efficiency.
- Benchmarking tests of the Qwen 3.8 27B model across various hardware, including the RTX 5090 and Mac Mini, highlight the importance of configuration over raw output.
- IFA 2024 revealed a market shift toward expensive Agentic AI PCs, leaving a significant void in the $1,000 enthusiast price point for standard consumer systems.

### Why it Matters
The rapid expansion of AI infrastructure is creating significant bottlenecks in the semiconductor supply chain while simultaneously driving up costs for consumer hardware. These developments suggest a fractured market where high-end innovation increasingly prioritizes enterprise-grade performance over accessible, mid-range computing solutions.
