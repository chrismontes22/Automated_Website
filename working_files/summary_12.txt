We have the most powerful technology in history, and we’re using it to stop hiring young people
============================================================

U.S. employers are increasingly using artificial intelligence to reduce hiring and cut costs, prompting calls for policy reforms to protect infrastructure, healthcare affordability, and entry-level job opportunities.

### Key Points
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported that AI was cited in 120,136 of the 573,195 U.S. job cuts announced through September.
- Stanford economists found that hiring for 22- to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% lower than in less-exposed sectors.
- Data centers are driving significant electricity price hikes, with the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis estimating they caused $9.3 billion in grid costs.
- Successful AI deployments in water management, such as San Jose Water’s sensor network, have saved hundreds of millions of gallons of treated water.
- New Jersey’s Data Center Fair Share Act and Oregon’s hospital price caps serve as legislative models for mitigating the economic impact of AI and rising costs.

### Why it Matters
The current deployment of AI is prioritizing corporate efficiency and headcount reduction over public infrastructure stability and workforce development. Without policy intervention, the concentration of resources in data centers and the displacement of junior workers threaten long-term economic mobility and the affordability of essential utilities for households.
