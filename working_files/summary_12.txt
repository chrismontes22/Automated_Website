What Are Prediction Markets—and Can They Really Predict the Future?
============================================================

Prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket are gaining mainstream influence as forecasting tools, though their rapid growth raises significant questions regarding regulatory oversight, insider trading, and market integrity.

### Key Points
- Prediction markets allow users to trade binary contracts on real-world outcomes, including political elections, financial trends, and weather events.
- Leading platforms Kalshi and Polymarket operate under different models, with Kalshi functioning as a federally regulated exchange and Polymarket utilizing decentralized blockchain oracles.
- Proponents argue these markets aggregate information more efficiently than traditional polls, while critics warn of risks involving market manipulation and gambling-like speculation.
- Regulatory challenges persist, with ongoing legal disputes over whether these platforms should be governed by federal commodities law or state-level gambling regulations.
- Insider trading remains a primary concern, highlighted by a 2026 CFTC enforcement action against a trader who used nonpublic information to profit from presidential speech content.
- Global trading volume on major platforms surged from under $5 billion in September 2025 to approximately $24 billion by April 2026.

### Why it Matters
These platforms are evolving from niche experiments into significant components of the modern information ecosystem, increasingly utilized by financial institutions and news organizations to gauge public sentiment. Their long-term viability depends on whether they can establish transparent, enforceable standards that distinguish legitimate forecasting from illicit insider trading and unregulated gambling.
