Founders Are Told to Chase Easy Markets. After 20 Years in Healthcare, I Think That Advice Is Backwards.
============================================================

Founders entering complex industries like healthcare should prioritize deep market understanding over rapid sales cycles to build durable competitive advantages that protect against future market commoditization.

### Key Points
- Markets with high barriers to entry, such as healthcare and defense, offer long-term protection against competitors once a company successfully navigates procurement and regulatory requirements.
- HIMSS data indicates that nearly 60% of healthcare technology purchases involve five or more stakeholders, requiring tailored value propositions for clinicians, finance leads, and payers.
- A 2026 B2B Go-to-Market report found that 24% of commercial spending is not linked to revenue, highlighting a widespread lack of strategic discipline among competitors.
- Successful market entry requires mapping every stakeholder’s incentives, specifically identifying what actions will lead to their professional promotion or blame within their organization.
- Companies should treat compliance reviews and committee approvals as formal stages of the sales process rather than afterthoughts to avoid stalled momentum.

### Why it Matters
Prioritizing speed often leads to rapid commoditization, as easy-to-enter markets allow competitors to replicate features and drive down prices quickly. By mastering the complexities of difficult industries, companies create a defensible "moat" that rewards patient, well-informed strategies over short-term revenue gains.
