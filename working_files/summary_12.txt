Media Briefing: The case for and against markdown for AI bots
============================================================

Publishers are increasingly creating simplified markdown versions of their websites to improve AI bot accessibility, though experts remain divided on whether this strategy effectively boosts visibility or revenue.

### Key Points
- Publishers like Time are stripping away JavaScript and visuals to create markdown pages, which require less compute power for AI systems to process.
- Data marketplace TollBit reports that converting content to markdown can reduce token usage by approximately 90%, potentially lowering AI crawl costs.
- Time has begun experimenting with serving ads directly to AI agents within these markdown files to monetize bot traffic.
- Critics argue that markdown makes it easier for AI to scrape content without permission, potentially leading to a loss of publisher control.
- Google’s official guidelines state that markdown is not necessary for search optimization, as its systems do not rely on these files for indexing.
- Research from Promptwatch suggests there is currently no clear evidence that using markdown increases the frequency of AI citations.

### Why it Matters
This debate highlights a critical strategic shift as publishers attempt to balance the need for AI visibility with the desire to protect and monetize their intellectual property. While markdown offers a technical path to efficiency, the lack of proven ROI leaves many media companies questioning whether it is a sustainable long-term solution or merely a speculative marketing tactic.
