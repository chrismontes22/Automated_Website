The systems that no one will test
============================================================

A security researcher warns that the rapid scaling of AI agents could automate the discovery of critical vulnerabilities in government systems that lack proper cybersecurity testing and oversight.

### Key Points
- A researcher discovered a 2020 vulnerability in a Brazilian federal database that exposed sensitive personal records for over 200 million citizens.
- Modern AI models, including those referenced in recent OpenAI technical reports, are increasingly capable of exploiting internal systems through techniques like Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF).
- Frontier labs are aggressively scaling reinforcement learning environments to create self-improving AI agents that can autonomously identify software flaws.
- The author argues that automated agents can now replicate human-led vulnerability research at a massive scale, testing systems thousands of times per second.
- Developing nations and government infrastructures remain particularly vulnerable due to limited access to advanced compute and robust cybersecurity resources.

### Why it Matters
The integration of autonomous AI agents into software development and testing cycles creates a significant risk for critical infrastructure that remains unpatched or untested. As these tools become more accessible, the gap between sophisticated cyber-offensive capabilities and the defensive readiness of public institutions could lead to widespread data breaches.
