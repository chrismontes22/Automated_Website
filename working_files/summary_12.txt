Reed Jobs says Silicon Valley’s 'energy' has moved to San Francisco thanks to post-pandemic office prices
============================================================

Reed Jobs suggests that San Francisco’s pandemic-era real estate decline catalyzed a shift in the tech industry's center of gravity from the South Bay toward the city.

### Key Points
- Reed Jobs identified a migration of tech energy from Palo Alto to San Francisco, highlighting companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Chai Discovery.
- Pandemic-driven vacancies lowered commercial real estate costs, allowing startups to establish operations in the city more affordably.
- Data from Cresa shows startups are clustering in neighborhoods like the Design District, where office space costs approximately $59.53 per square foot.
- Tech firms are increasingly favoring smaller, mixed-use urban neighborhoods over the traditional Financial District or sprawling suburban campuses.
- Reed Jobs, founder of the oncology-focused venture firm Yosemite, cited the city's renewed momentum as the primary reason for basing his company there.

### Why it Matters
This shift signals a broader trend of tech innovation moving away from traditional corporate campuses toward dense, urban environments that offer flexibility and lower overhead. By prioritizing affordable, reconfigurable spaces, the next generation of AI startups is fundamentally reshaping San Francisco's commercial real estate market.
