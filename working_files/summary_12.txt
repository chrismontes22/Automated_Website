‘Indentured servants’: US green card move will hit thousands of IT workers
============================================================

The United States government has suspended major tech companies from the PERM program, halting green card sponsorship for foreign workers to combat alleged visa fraud and labor displacement.

### Key Points
- The Department of Labor will stop processing new and pending permanent labor certification applications for Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, Wipro, HCL Technologies, and Capgemini.
- Vice President JD Vance accused these firms of using the PERM program to replace American workers with lower-paid foreign staff, citing Microsoft’s recent hiring and layoff data.
- The PERM program is a critical pathway for H-1B visa holders to transition to permanent residency, a process currently backlogged with over 1.2 million applicants.
- Indian nationals, who comprise approximately 70 percent of H-1B visa holders, are expected to be the most significantly impacted by this policy shift.
- India’s Ministry of External Affairs formally criticized the move, labeling Vance’s rhetoric regarding "indentured servants" as offensive and detrimental to bilateral economic cooperation.

### Why it Matters
This policy change creates significant uncertainty for thousands of skilled foreign professionals already residing in the U.S. who now face potential roadblocks to permanent residency. It also signals a more aggressive stance by the administration toward corporate immigration practices, likely forcing major tech firms to re-evaluate their long-term talent acquisition and retention strategies.
