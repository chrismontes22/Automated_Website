'It’s not red, it’s magenta': How ThinkPad designers gaslit IBM to save the iconic TrackPoint
============================================================

IBM design leaders secured the iconic red color of the ThinkPad TrackPoint by creatively labeling the component as magenta to bypass strict corporate safety regulations regarding emergency controls.

### Key Points
- The TrackPoint, a central keyboard input stick, was introduced by IBM in 1992 as a signature feature of the ThinkPad laptop line.
- IBM’s internal safety standards reserved the color red exclusively for emergency power shutdown switches, prohibiting its use on other hardware components.
- Design Program Director Tom Hardy and his team bypassed these regulations by documenting the TrackPoint color as magenta to avoid safety department scrutiny.
- The design team leveraged the support of industrial designer Richard Sapper and graphic designer Paul Rand to defend the aesthetic choice against corporate opposition.
- A pre-planned advertising campaign featuring the red nub forced the safety team to eventually approve the color for production.

### Why it Matters
This story highlights how creative defiance and strategic internal maneuvering can establish a lasting brand identity in the competitive technology market. By successfully navigating corporate bureaucracy, the design team ensured the TrackPoint became a recognizable, iconic element that continues to define the ThinkPad user experience today.
