Paywall violation ‘eviscerates’ fair use defense: What OpenAI-NYT twist means for publishers
============================================================

Unsealed internal documents from The New York Times’ lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft reveal executives privately acknowledged that AI products pose an existential threat to the publishing industry.

### Key Points
- Internal filings suggest OpenAI and Microsoft executives recognized AI models as substitutive products that could undermine the economic viability of news organizations.
- Evidence of deliberate paywall circumvention by OpenAI may violate the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, weakening the company's fair-use legal defense.
- Legal experts argue that existing licensing deals between AI firms and other publishers prove a market price for news content already exists.
- The case could set a major legal precedent, potentially forcing AI companies to adopt standardized, paid licensing models for training data.
- Both parties have requested summary judgment from Judge Sidney Stein, seeking a resolution based on current discovery evidence rather than a jury trial.

### Why it Matters
This litigation serves as a critical test for the future of AI training, potentially shifting the industry from unauthorized data scraping to a formal, paid licensing market. A ruling in favor of the publisher could grant news organizations significant leverage, forcing tech giants to compensate creators for the content used to build their AI models.
