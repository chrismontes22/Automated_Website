The 'Big Bad Theory'
============================================================

Hollywood is increasingly casting Silicon Valley tech billionaires as the primary villains in film and television, reflecting a growing societal "techlash" against the industry's immense power.

### Key Points
- Recent productions like "Cupertino," "The Social Reckoning," and "The Dropout" depict tech founders as megalomaniacal antagonists rather than faceless corporate entities.
- The CBS legal procedural "Cupertino" has gained significant viewership by focusing on the conflict between everyday citizens and powerful Silicon Valley giants.
- Scholars note that these narratives mirror Cold War-era fears, replacing external threats with tech leaders who prioritize financial gain over ethical or human concerns.
- Unlike historical "mad scientist" tropes, modern tech villains are often modeled after real-world figures like Elon Musk, Sam Altman, and Elizabeth Holmes.
- Data suggests that audiences, including older demographics, are increasingly receptive to stories that portray tech executives as the source of modern societal inconveniences.

### Why it Matters
This shift in storytelling signals a broader cultural reckoning with the ubiquity of Big Tech and the perceived arrogance of its leadership. By humanizing the "villain" as a recognizable tech mogul, these narratives tap into widespread class rage and provide a cathartic outlet for public frustration with the digital economy.
