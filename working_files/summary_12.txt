At an ex-OpenAI researcher's influential lab, $500,000 salaries aren't enough to fix a talent 'bottleneck'
============================================================

Former OpenAI researcher Beth Barnes warns that a critical talent shortage at her nonprofit, METR, is hindering essential independent safety evaluations of rapidly advancing artificial intelligence models.

### Key Points
- METR, a nonprofit led by Beth Barnes and Chris Painter, conducts independent safety testing for major AI labs including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta.
- Despite offering salaries up to $503,000, the 35-person organization struggles to recruit enough specialized researchers to keep pace with AI development.
- METR’s research indicates that AI capabilities have doubled approximately every seven months over the past six years.
- The nonprofit successfully identified potential cheating behaviors in OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol model before the model was involved in a public security incident.
- Proposed government regulations requiring mandatory third-party safety audits could provide the industry clarity needed to help organizations like METR scale their operations.

### Why it Matters
The talent bottleneck at independent research labs threatens the industry's ability to effectively monitor and control increasingly powerful AI systems. If safety organizations cannot scale, regulators and companies may lack the objective data necessary to prevent dangerous model behaviors or rogue deployments.
