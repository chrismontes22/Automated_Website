CrowdStrike links South Korean bank breaches to AI tools and a China suspect
============================================================

Cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike identified a potential suspect in a series of recent cyberattacks targeting at least nine South Korean financial institutions using artificial intelligence tools.

### Key Points
- CrowdStrike reports that an attacker used AI models like Anthropic’s Claude Code and the Chinese tool ARTEX to breach South Korean banks.
- The investigation suggests the perpetrator may be a 26-year-old individual based in China’s Guangdong province, though no formal charges have been filed.
- South Korean police launched a formal investigation on October 6 into the data breaches affecting major firms including Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, and Hana Bank.
- Shinhan Bank confirmed that the personal data of approximately 25,000 customers was exposed during the attacks, which occurred between late September and early October.
- Evidence of the attacker's identity was discovered in open server folders containing AI chat logs where the user discussed selling stolen Korean financial data.

### Why it Matters
These attacks highlight the growing risk of malicious actors leveraging generative AI tools to identify security vulnerabilities and automate cybercriminal activities. The incident underscores an urgent need for financial institutions to strengthen data protections against increasingly sophisticated, AI-assisted threats.
