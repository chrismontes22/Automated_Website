A Classic Photoshop Workflow Versus Adobe's New AI Tools
============================================================

Adobe Photoshop’s recent integration of AI-driven features and updated adjustment layers has introduced workflow inconsistencies, prompting professional photographers to seek more reliable, manual editing alternatives for complex projects.

### Key Points
- Photoshop’s new AI-assisted editor creates flattened document copies and can result in reduced image sharpness compared to traditional editing methods.
- The new "Light" adjustment layer replaces legacy Brightness/Contrast tools, creating potential compatibility issues when reopening older files or maintaining consistent tonal values.
- Adobe’s generative AI features utilize a credit-based system, with costs ranging from one credit for Firefly fills to 40 credits for partner models like Google’s Gemini.
- Professional workflows remain more stable by using stamped layers converted to smart objects, which allow for non-destructive editing via the Camera Raw Filter.
- Users can disable the AI-assisted editor interface through the Preferences menu if they prefer to maintain a traditional, layer-based Photoshop workflow.

### Why it Matters
These changes highlight a growing tension between Adobe’s push for automated, AI-first tools and the precision requirements of professional photography workflows. Relying on proprietary AI models can introduce hidden costs and unpredictable results, making manual, non-destructive techniques essential for maintaining long-term project consistency.
